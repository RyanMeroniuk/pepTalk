# Glycine
## Foods That Enhance Absorption

### Direct Absorption Support
- **Glycine is very well absorbed on its own** — as the simplest amino acid, it uses multiple transport pathways and does not require special co-factors for intestinal absorption
- **Warm water or herbal tea** — dissolving glycine in warm (not boiling) liquid improves dissolution and may slightly speed absorption. Glycine is heat-stable, unlike glutamine.
- **Small amount of carbohydrate** — a teaspoon of honey or a small piece of fruit can enhance amino acid uptake via insulin-mediated transport

### Synergistic Nutrients (Enhance Glycine's Effects)

#### For Collagen Synthesis
- **Vitamin C** — absolutely essential. Vitamin C is required for prolyl hydroxylase and lysyl hydroxylase, the enzymes that stabilize collagen structure. Without vitamin C, collagen synthesis fails regardless of glycine availability. Take 250-500 mg vitamin C with glycine/collagen doses.
- **Proline/Hydroxyproline** — the other key collagen amino acids. Collagen supplements or bone broth provide these alongside glycine.
- **Copper** — required for lysyl oxidase, which cross-links collagen fibers for structural integrity. Food sources: oysters, dark chocolate, cashews, liver.
- **Zinc** — supports fibroblast function and collagen deposition. Food sources: oysters, beef, pumpkin seeds.
- **Silica** — supports collagen cross-linking and connective tissue structure. Food sources: cucumbers, oats, horsetail tea.

#### For Sleep Enhancement
- **Magnesium-rich foods** — magnesium and glycine are synergistic for sleep. Both support GABA activity and muscle relaxation. Food sources: pumpkin seeds, dark chocolate, almonds, spinach, avocado.
- **Tart cherry juice** — natural melatonin source; combined with glycine, covers multiple sleep pathways (temperature, melatonin, GABA)
- **Kiwi** — shown to improve sleep quality independently; the serotonin and antioxidant content complements glycine's mechanisms

#### For Glutathione Production
- **Cysteine-rich foods** — cysteine is the other rate-limiting glutathione precursor. Food sources: eggs, garlic, onions, cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, Brussels sprouts), whey protein.
- **Selenium-rich foods** — selenium is a cofactor for glutathione peroxidase. Food sources: Brazil nuts (1-2 per day), sardines, eggs.
- **Sulfur-rich foods** — support overall transsulfuration pathway. Food sources: garlic, onions, eggs, cruciferous vegetables.

### Optimal Pairing Meals
- **Evening collagen drink:** 10 g collagen + 3-5 g glycine + 250 mg vitamin C + warm water with lemon — covers collagen synthesis and sleep
- **Bone broth with liver:** Glycine from broth + copper/zinc from liver + vitamin C from a side of red peppers — complete collagen support stack
- **Pre-bed snack:** Kiwi + 3 g glycine in warm water + magnesium glycinate capsule — triple-pathway sleep support
