# Glycine
## Things to Avoid

### Behaviors That Counteract Glycine's Sleep Benefits
- **Blue light exposure before bed** — screens (phones, tablets, TVs, computers) suppress melatonin production. Glycine works through non-melatonin pathways but works best when melatonin signaling is also intact. Use blue light blockers or stop screens 1 hour before bed.
- **Hot showers/baths right before bed** — glycine promotes sleep by lowering core temperature. A hot shower raises core temperature temporarily. If you prefer a hot shower, take it 90 minutes before bed so your body has time to cool down (the post-shower temperature drop can actually complement glycine).
- **Exercising within 2 hours of bedtime** — raises core temperature and sympathetic nervous system activity, opposing glycine's temperature-lowering and calming effects. Morning or afternoon exercise is ideal.
- **Inconsistent sleep schedule** — glycine supports the circadian system, but an erratic schedule undermines it. Maintain consistent bed and wake times.
- **Warm bedroom** — keep the bedroom cool (65-68°F / 18-20°C) to complement glycine's thermoregulatory sleep mechanism

### Substances That Interfere
- **Excessive stimulant use** — caffeine, nicotine, and pre-workout stimulants counteract glycine's calming and sleep-promoting effects
- **Strychnine** — a glycine receptor antagonist (extremely unlikely exposure but worth noting for completeness — some traditional medicine preparations have contained trace amounts)
- **High-dose zinc supplements at bedtime** — zinc and glycine compete for some absorption pathways. If taking high-dose zinc, separate from glycine by 2 hours. Magnesium glycinate is fine (glycine is the carrier).

### Supplement Interactions to Monitor
- **High-dose individual amino acid supplements** — taking many single amino acids simultaneously can create absorption competition. Space glycine from other amino acid megadoses by 30-60 minutes.
- **5-HTP or L-Tryptophan at high doses** — both are serotonin precursors. Glycine also modulates serotonin. Combining high doses of all three could theoretically overshoot serotonin levels, though this is rare. Start with conservative doses of each.

### Lifestyle Factors
- **Chronic stress without management** — cortisol interferes with sleep onset regardless of glycine supplementation. Glycine helps, but is not a substitute for addressing root-cause stress.
- **Shift work** — disrupts circadian rhythm fundamentally. Glycine can still help with sleep quality when you do sleep, but cannot fully compensate for circadian misalignment.
- **Excessive alcohol consumption** — depletes glycine stores (used for alcohol detoxification in the liver), reduces sleep quality, and accelerates collagen breakdown. Triple negative.
