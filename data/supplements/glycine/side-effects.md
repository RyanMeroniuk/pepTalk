# Glycine
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild)
- **Soft stools** — at doses above 10-15 g, some people experience loose stools. This is the most commonly reported side effect and is dose-dependent.
- **Mild nausea** — rare at standard doses (3-5 g); more common at very high doses (15+ g), especially on an empty stomach
- **Mild sedation** — glycine's inhibitory neurotransmitter activity can cause drowsiness; this is a feature for nighttime use but could be a side effect if taken during the day at higher doses

### Uncommon
- **Vivid dreams** — some users report more vivid or memorable dreams, likely related to sleep architecture changes (enhanced slow-wave sleep)
- **Morning grogginess** — very rare; unlike sedative sleep aids, glycine typically improves morning alertness. If it occurs, reduce dose or take earlier in the evening.

### Very Rare
- **Allergic reactions** — extremely rare with a pure amino acid
- **Hypotension** — theoretical at very high doses due to peripheral vasodilation; not reported at standard supplemental doses

### Safety Profile
- **Exceptionally safe** — glycine has one of the best safety profiles of any supplement
- **No known toxicity** at doses up to 60 g/day in short-term clinical trials (schizophrenia research)
- **No tolerance or dependence** — does not lose effectiveness; no withdrawal effects
- **No drug-like hangover** — unlike benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, or antihistamines, glycine does not impair morning function
- **Safe for long-term use** — no adverse effects reported with chronic supplementation at 3-10 g/day
- **GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe)** by the FDA
- **No impact on liver or kidney function** at standard supplemental doses
- **No impact on hormones** — does not affect testosterone, estrogen, cortisol, or thyroid hormones at standard doses

### Populations That Should Use Caution
- **People on clozapine** — glycine may interact with clozapine (see Drug Interactions)
- **Severe hypotension** — theoretical concern at very high doses due to vasodilation
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — safe at food-equivalent doses; high-dose supplementation (10+ g/day) lacks specific safety data, though no concerns have been identified
