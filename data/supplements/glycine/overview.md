# Glycine
## Overview

Glycine is arguably the most underappreciated amino acid in human biology. Despite being classified as "non-essential" (the body can synthesize it), mounting evidence suggests humans are chronically deficient — we produce about 3 g/day but need 10-15 g/day for optimal collagen synthesis, detoxification, and neurotransmitter function.

### Why It Matters

- **Sleep quality:** Glycine is one of the most effective and well-studied natural sleep aids. It lowers core body temperature, modulates NMDA receptors, and increases serotonin in the prefrontal cortex — improving sleep onset, sleep quality, and next-day alertness without grogginess.
- **Collagen synthesis:** Every third amino acid in collagen is glycine (Gly-X-Y repeating sequence). Without adequate glycine, collagen production is rate-limited regardless of how much collagen protein you consume.
- **Detoxification:** Glycine is required for Phase II liver detoxification (glycine conjugation) and is a component of glutathione (the body's master antioxidant).
- **Neuroprotection:** Acts as an inhibitory neurotransmitter in the brainstem and spinal cord; also a co-agonist at NMDA receptors in the brain, supporting cognitive function.
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Glycine activates glycine-gated chloride channels on immune cells (macrophages, neutrophils), reducing inflammatory cytokine production.

### Who Benefits Most

- **Poor sleepers** — glycine at 3 g before bed consistently improves subjective and objective sleep quality
- **Athletes and active individuals** — collagen synthesis support, joint health, recovery
- **Anyone taking collagen supplements** — collagen without extra glycine may be suboptimal
- **People using peptides for healing** — glycine supports the tissue repair that peptides like BPC-157 and TB-500 initiate
- **Older adults** — declining collagen production, increased need for detoxification support
- **Those with high toxic burden** — glycine is essential for liver conjugation pathways
- **High-stress individuals** — calming neurotransmitter effects, cortisol modulation
