# Glycine
## Foods to Avoid

### Foods That Interfere with Glycine's Sleep Benefits
- **Caffeine (after 2 PM)** — caffeine blocks adenosine receptors, counteracting glycine's sleep-promoting effects. While glycine works through different mechanisms (temperature, GlyR), caffeine's stimulatory effects can overpower them. Cut caffeine by early afternoon at the latest.
- **Alcohol before bed** — while alcohol is sedating, it fragments sleep architecture, suppresses REM sleep, and causes rebound wakefulness. This directly opposes glycine's sleep quality benefits. Avoid alcohol within 3 hours of bedtime.
- **High-sugar meals at dinner** — blood sugar spikes and crashes disrupt sleep onset and cause nighttime awakenings. Glycine improves sleep quality, but sugar undoes it.
- **Spicy foods at dinner** — can raise core body temperature and cause reflux, working against glycine's core temperature-lowering mechanism

### Foods That May Compete with Glycine Absorption
- **Very large protein meals taken simultaneously** — high-dose amino acid mixtures can compete for intestinal transport. This is a minor concern — glycine uses multiple transporters — but for the sleep protocol, taking glycine separately from a large protein meal is ideal.
- **High-dose BCAA supplements** — branched-chain amino acids compete for the same large neutral amino acid transporter (LAT1). While glycine primarily uses other transporters (GLYT1, PAT1), very high BCAA doses could theoretically reduce uptake. Separate by 1 hour if taking both.

### Foods That Undermine Glycine's Collagen Benefits
- **High-sugar/high-glycemic diet** — promotes advanced glycation end products (AGEs) that cross-link and damage existing collagen. Building new collagen with glycine while destroying it with sugar is counterproductive.
- **Excessive alcohol** — depletes vitamin C, which is essential for collagen hydroxylation. Also promotes collagen-damaging free radicals.
- **Processed seed oils (high omega-6)** — promote chronic inflammation that accelerates collagen breakdown. Soybean oil, corn oil, sunflower oil, canola oil in excess.
- **Charred/blackened meats** — heterocyclic amines and AGEs directly damage collagen structures

### Foods That Deplete Glycine
- **No specific foods deplete glycine directly**, but high metabolic demand for detoxification (alcohol, medications, environmental toxins) redirects glycine away from collagen and glutathione synthesis toward conjugation pathways in the liver
