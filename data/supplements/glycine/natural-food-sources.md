# Glycine
## Natural Food Sources

### Highest Concentration (Collagen-Rich Foods)
- **Bone broth (beef, chicken)** — 1-3 g per cup depending on preparation time and bone density. 24-hour slow-cooked beef bone broth is richest.
- **Gelatin** — ~22 g glycine per 100 g (gelatin is ~33% glycine by weight). 1 tablespoon provides ~1.5 g glycine.
- **Pork skin (chicharrones/pork rinds)** — ~3.5 g per 100 g; one of the highest whole-food sources
- **Chicken skin** — ~2.5 g per 100 g
- **Connective tissue meats** — oxtail, short ribs, shanks, cheeks — any cut rich in connective tissue provides more glycine than lean cuts

### Animal Proteins
- **Beef** — ~1.5 g per 100 g (varies by cut; collagen-rich cuts have more)
- **Chicken** — ~1.2 g per 100 g
- **Turkey** — ~1.1 g per 100 g
- **Pork** — ~1.0 g per 100 g
- **Salmon** — ~1.0 g per 100 g (with skin provides more)
- **Shrimp** — ~1.5 g per 100 g (shellfish are glycine-rich)
- **Crab** — ~1.3 g per 100 g
- **Eggs** — ~0.4 g per large egg

### Dairy
- **Parmesan cheese** — ~0.5 g per 30 g serving
- **Cottage cheese** — ~0.3 g per 100 g
- **Milk** — ~0.1 g per cup

### Plant Sources
- **Seaweed/nori** — ~1.0 g per 100 g (dry weight)
- **Spinach** — ~0.3 g per 100 g
- **Soybeans** — ~0.9 g per 100 g (dry)
- **Pumpkin seeds** — ~0.7 g per 100 g
- **Sesame seeds** — ~0.7 g per 100 g
- **Spirulina** — ~3.0 g per 100 g (dry weight; small serving sizes limit practical intake)

### Important Notes
- **The "glycine gap":** The body synthesizes ~3 g/day and a typical omnivore diet provides ~3-5 g/day, totaling ~6-8 g/day. Estimated optimal intake is 10-15 g/day for full collagen and glutathione support. This gap justifies supplementation.
- **Nose-to-tail eating** is the ancestral solution — our ancestors consumed connective tissue, skin, organs, and bones (as broth), which are all glycine-rich. Modern diets emphasize lean muscle meat, which is relatively low in glycine.
- **Bone broth is the single best food source** — it provides glycine, proline, hydroxyproline, glutamine, and gelatin in a bioavailable matrix.
- **Collagen powder** provides ~3.3 g glycine per 10 g serving and counts as a food-form source
