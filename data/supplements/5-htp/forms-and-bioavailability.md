# 5-HTP
## Forms and Bioavailability

### Griffonia simplicifolia Extract (Standard Source)
- **Where it comes from:** Most commercial 5-HTP is extracted from the seeds of Griffonia simplicifolia, a West African plant naturally rich in 5-HTP
- **What to look for:** Standardized 5-HTP content, pharmaceutical/USP-grade, third-party tested for purity. The 1989 EMS event associated with contaminated l-tryptophan underlines why quality sourcing of serotonin precursors matters

### Immediate-Release vs Time-Release
- **Immediate-release capsules/tablets:** Most common; rapid rise in plasma 5-HTP. May cause more GI side effects due to a fast peripheral serotonin surge
- **Enteric-coated or time-release:** Designed to slow release and reduce GI upset (a major practical issue with 5-HTP). May be gentler on the stomach

### Bioavailability and Peripheral Conversion
- **Crosses the blood-brain barrier directly** without competing with large neutral amino acids — a key advantage over l-tryptophan (which must compete for transport)
- **Peripheral conversion limits brain delivery:** Aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (AADC) converts much of an oral dose to serotonin in peripheral tissues before it reaches the brain. This both reduces CNS delivery and drives GI side effects
- **Carbidopa (clinical):** In research/clinical settings, 5-HTP is sometimes co-administered with carbidopa to block peripheral conversion, raising the fraction reaching the brain and reducing GI effects (not a typical OTC combination)
- **Vitamin B6 cofactor:** Conversion of 5-HTP to serotonin requires B6; adequate B6 status supports the pathway, and some formulas include it

### Form Comparison: 5-HTP vs Tryptophan
- **5-HTP:** Downstream, bypasses rate-limiting step, no LNAA competition, potent at milligram doses, more GI side effects from peripheral serotonin
- **L-tryptophan:** Upstream, rate-limited, competes for transport, gentler but needs gram-level doses, partly diverted to kynurenine/niacin
- They are alternatives — **never combine them** (additive serotonin-syndrome risk)

### Capsules vs Powder
- **Capsules/tablets:** Standard, precise, convenient; enteric-coated options help GI tolerance
- **Powder:** Available but less common; bitter, and the fast release may worsen nausea

### Storage
- Store sealed, cool, and dry, away from light and moisture
