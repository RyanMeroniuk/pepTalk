# 5-HTP
## Foods That Enhance Absorption

### 5-HTP Does Not Depend on Food the Way Tryptophan Does
- A key advantage of 5-HTP over l-tryptophan: it crosses the blood-brain barrier **without competing** with large neutral amino acids. This means, unlike tryptophan, you do NOT need to take 5-HTP away from protein to get it into the brain
- Absorption itself is reasonably efficient; the main practical issue is GI tolerance (nausea from peripheral serotonin), not absorption

### Vitamin B6 (Key Cofactor)
- **Vitamin B6 (pyridoxal-5-phosphate)** is the cofactor for converting 5-HTP into serotonin. Adequate B6 supports efficient conversion, and many 5-HTP products include B6
- B6-rich foods: poultry, fish, bananas, potatoes, chickpeas, fortified foods

### A Little Food to Reduce Nausea
- Taking 5-HTP with a small amount of food can blunt the nausea that peripheral serotonin causes. This is about tolerance, not absorption enhancement
- A small carbohydrate snack in the evening is a common pairing for sleep use

### Supporting Nutrients for the Serotonin Pathway
- **Magnesium** and adequate overall micronutrient status support neurotransmitter function and relaxation, complementing 5-HTP's calming/sleep effects
- A nutrient-adequate, anti-inflammatory diet supports serotonin signaling generally (chronic inflammation diverts the upstream tryptophan pool toward the kynurenine pathway)

### What to Avoid Pairing It With
- Do **not** pair 5-HTP with other serotonergic supplements/drugs to "boost" it — combining with l-tryptophan, St. John's Wort, or serotonergic medications increases serotonin-syndrome risk, not benefit (see drug-interactions)

### Practical Pairing
- **Simplest approach:** Take 5-HTP with adequate vitamin B6 (in the product or diet) and a small amount of food to reduce nausea. For sleep, an evening dose with a small carbohydrate snack works well
- No protein-timing maneuvering is needed (that is a tryptophan concern, not a 5-HTP one)
