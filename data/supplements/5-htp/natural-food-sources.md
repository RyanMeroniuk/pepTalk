# 5-HTP
## Natural Food Sources

### 5-HTP Is Not Present in Meaningful Amounts in Common Foods
- 5-HTP is an intermediate the body makes from tryptophan; it is **not found in appreciable quantities in ordinary foods**. You cannot meaningfully raise 5-HTP by eating regular meals
- The one notable natural source is the seed of the West African plant **Griffonia simplicifolia**, which is where commercial 5-HTP supplements are extracted from — not a food eaten in typical diets

### The Dietary Route Is Tryptophan (the Precursor)
- The body produces 5-HTP from **L-tryptophan**, an essential amino acid found in protein foods. So the "food source" for 5-HTP is really tryptophan-rich food, which the body then converts (via the rate-limiting tryptophan hydroxylase step) into 5-HTP
- **Tryptophan-rich foods include:** turkey, chicken, eggs, dairy/cheese, fish, soy/tofu, pumpkin and squash seeds, nuts, legumes, and oats (see the l-tryptophan entry for detail)

### Why Eating Tryptophan Foods ≠ Taking 5-HTP
- Dietary tryptophan must pass through the regulated tryptophan hydroxylase step and competes with other amino acids for brain transport, so eating tryptophan-rich food raises serotonin only modestly and gently
- Supplemental 5-HTP bypasses that rate-limiting step and the amino-acid competition, producing a more direct, potent serotonergic effect than any food could
- This is precisely why 5-HTP is taken as a supplement rather than obtained from diet — and why its serotonin-syndrome cautions apply to the supplement, not to eating turkey

### Griffonia simplicifolia
- The seeds naturally contain a high concentration of 5-HTP, which is why this plant is the commercial source. It is not a culinary food; it is processed into standardized supplement extracts

### Practical Takeaway
- There is no practical food source of 5-HTP itself. A tryptophan-rich diet supports the body's own gentle serotonin production; targeted 5-HTP effects come only from the Griffonia-derived supplement, which carries the serotonergic safety considerations detailed elsewhere
