# 5-HTP
## Overview

5-HTP (5-hydroxytryptophan) is a naturally occurring amino acid derivative that the body produces from the essential amino acid tryptophan as the immediate precursor to serotonin. As a supplement, it is typically extracted from the seeds of the West African plant Griffonia simplicifolia. 5-HTP occupies a pivotal position in the serotonin pathway (tryptophan → 5-HTP → serotonin → melatonin): it sits just downstream of l-tryptophan and just upstream of serotonin, and crucially it **bypasses the rate-limiting enzyme (tryptophan hydroxylase)** and crosses the blood-brain barrier without competing with other amino acids. This makes 5-HTP a more direct and potent way to raise serotonin than tryptophan — which is exactly why it is used for mood, sleep, and appetite/satiety, and also why it must be treated with caution. **The most important point about 5-HTP is its serotonin-syndrome risk: combining it with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, or other serotonergic drugs can cause a potentially life-threatening excess of serotonin and must be avoided unless supervised by a physician.** Because 5-HTP raises serotonin throughout the body (not just the brain), peripheral side effects and the need for cofactors (and often carbidopa in clinical settings) are relevant. It is conceptually related to l-tryptophan, gaba, saffron, and melatonin in mood/sleep contexts, but should never be stacked with another serotonin precursor. This content is educational and research-oriented, not medical advice.
