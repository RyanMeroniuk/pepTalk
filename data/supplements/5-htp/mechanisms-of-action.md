# 5-HTP
## Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Direct Serotonin Synthesis (Primary Mechanism)
- 5-HTP is decarboxylated by aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (AADC) directly into **serotonin (5-HT)**
- Unlike tryptophan, 5-HTP **bypasses the rate-limiting enzyme tryptophan hydroxylase** — the step the body uses to regulate serotonin production. This is why 5-HTP raises serotonin more directly, potently, and with less built-in regulation than l-tryptophan
- The result is increased serotonin availability, underlying effects on mood, sleep, and appetite

### 2. Blood-Brain Barrier Transport (No LNAA Competition)
- 5-HTP crosses the blood-brain barrier **without competing** with large neutral amino acids (unlike tryptophan, which must compete for transport)
- This means 5-HTP's brain entry is not blocked by dietary protein, making it more reliable and more potent per mg than tryptophan

### 3. Peripheral vs Central Serotonin (Why Carbidopa Matters Clinically)
- AADC is present throughout the body, so a large fraction of oral 5-HTP is converted to serotonin **in peripheral tissues** before reaching the brain
- Peripheral serotonin contributes to side effects (especially GI — nausea, cramping) and limits how much reaches the CNS
- In clinical research, 5-HTP is sometimes given with **carbidopa** (a peripheral decarboxylase inhibitor) to block peripheral conversion, increasing the fraction reaching the brain and reducing GI effects

### 4. Downstream Melatonin Synthesis (Sleep)
- Serotonin made from 5-HTP can be converted to **melatonin** in the pineal gland (in darkness), supporting sleep timing — the basis for 5-HTP's use as a sleep aid

### 5. Appetite and Satiety
- Serotonin signaling influences satiety and reduces appetite/carbohydrate cravings; this is the mechanism behind 5-HTP's studied effects on appetite and weight

### 6. Cofactor Dependence
- The decarboxylation of 5-HTP to serotonin requires **vitamin B6 (pyridoxal-5-phosphate)** as a cofactor. Adequate B6 status supports efficient conversion

### 7. ⚠️ Mechanistic Basis of Serotonin Syndrome
- Because 5-HTP directly and potently increases serotonin, combining it with drugs that also raise serotonin (SSRIs/SNRIs blocking reuptake, MAOIs blocking breakdown) can drive serotonin to dangerous levels — the mechanistic basis for **serotonin syndrome** (see drug-interactions and side-effects)
