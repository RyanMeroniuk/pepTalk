# Alpha-GPC
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild)
- **Headache** — paradoxically, both too much AND too little acetylcholine can cause headaches. If Alpha-GPC causes headache, reduce the dose. If you're taking racetams without Alpha-GPC, the headache is from choline depletion.
- **GI discomfort** — mild nausea, stomach upset, or diarrhea at higher doses (600+ mg). Taking with food usually resolves this.
- **Insomnia / sleep disruption** — cholinergic stimulation can interfere with sleep onset if taken too late in the day. Avoid dosing after 3 PM.
- **Heartburn** — occasional reports, likely related to the glycerophosphate component

### Uncommon
- **Dizziness** — rare at standard doses; more common at 1200 mg/day
- **Excessive salivation** — acetylcholine stimulates salivary glands; very rare at supplemental doses
- **Skin rash** — very rare allergic/sensitivity reaction
- **Fishy body odor** — choline can be metabolized to trimethylamine (TMA), which has a fishy smell. Rare at standard Alpha-GPC doses; more common with choline bitartrate. See TMAO section below.
- **Vivid dreams** — acetylcholine is involved in REM sleep. Some users report more vivid or intense dreams.

### The TMAO Concern (Important Context)
- **The concern:** Choline is metabolized by gut bacteria into trimethylamine (TMA), which the liver converts to trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO). TMAO has been associated with increased cardiovascular disease risk in some epidemiological studies.
- **The nuance:**
  - The TMAO-CVD link is observational, not proven causal. Many researchers believe TMAO is a marker of poor gut health or high animal product intake, not a direct cause of CVD.
  - Choline is an ESSENTIAL nutrient — deficiency causes fatty liver, cognitive decline, and neural tube defects. Avoiding choline to reduce TMAO is not rational.
  - Alpha-GPC is largely absorbed in the small intestine BEFORE reaching colonic bacteria, potentially producing LESS TMA/TMAO than choline bitartrate or dietary choline sources that reach the colon.
  - The dose of choline from typical Alpha-GPC supplementation (120-240 mg choline from 300-600 mg Alpha-GPC) is modest compared to dietary choline from eggs (147 mg per egg) or liver (356 mg per 100 g).
- **Practical guidance:**
  - TMAO concern should NOT prevent you from taking Alpha-GPC at standard doses
  - Maintain a healthy gut microbiome (fiber, probiotics, diverse diet) — this influences how much TMA gut bacteria produce
  - If concerned, periodically test TMAO levels with a comprehensive metabolic panel
  - Some evidence suggests resveratrol, garlic, and certain probiotics may reduce TMA production

### Cholinergic Excess (Overdose Signs — Very High Doses)
- **Symptoms of excessive acetylcholine (SLUDGE mnemonic):** Salivation, Lacrimation (tearing), Urination, Diarrhea, GI distress, Emesis (vomiting)
- **This would require extremely high doses** — well beyond normal supplementation
- If you experience multiple SLUDGE symptoms, reduce dose immediately

### Safety Profile
- **Well-tolerated at standard doses** (300-600 mg/day) in the vast majority of users
- **Clinical trials at 1200 mg/day** for 6+ months showed minimal adverse effects in elderly populations
- **Safe for long-term use** — used as a daily prescription medication in Europe without significant long-term adverse effects
- **No hepatotoxicity or nephrotoxicity** at studied doses
- **No hormonal disruption** beyond the acute, exercise-dependent GH increase

### Populations That Should Use Caution
- **People with depression on acetylcholinesterase inhibitors** (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) — additive cholinergic effect. Consult prescribing physician.
- **People with Parkinson's disease** — dopaminergic-cholinergic balance is critical; adding choline may alter this balance
- **People with asthma** — acetylcholine constricts bronchial smooth muscle; excessive cholinergic stimulation could theoretically worsen asthma
- **Pregnant/breastfeeding women** — choline is essential during pregnancy (adequate intake: 450 mg/day pregnant, 550 mg/day lactating). Alpha-GPC can contribute to meeting this requirement, but specific supplemental safety data is limited.
