# Alpha-GPC
## Natural Food Sources

Alpha-GPC itself is found in trace amounts in foods, but dietary choline (which serves the same acetylcholine-precursor function) is available from several food sources. Alpha-GPC is a naturally occurring phospholipid found in small quantities in organ meats and dairy.

### Foods Containing Alpha-GPC (Trace Amounts)
- **Organ meats (liver, brain)** — highest natural Alpha-GPC content
- **Dairy products (milk, yogurt, cheese)** — contain glycerophosphocholine as a natural component
- **Wheat germ** — trace amounts
- **Soy lecithin** — contains phospholipid forms of choline including some glycerophosphocholine

### Best Dietary Choline Sources (All Forms)
- **Beef liver** — 356 mg choline per 100 g (the single richest food source)
- **Chicken liver** — 290 mg per 100 g
- **Eggs (whole)** — 147 mg per large egg (concentrated in the yolk — ~126 mg per yolk)
- **Beef** — 80-100 mg per 100 g
- **Chicken breast** — 85 mg per 100 g
- **Salmon** — 80 mg per 100 g
- **Cod** — 83 mg per 100 g
- **Shrimp** — 80 mg per 100 g
- **Turkey** — 70 mg per 100 g
- **Pork** — 65 mg per 100 g

### Plant-Based Choline Sources
- **Soybeans** — 107 mg per 100 g
- **Quinoa** — 70 mg per cup (cooked)
- **Broccoli** — 63 mg per cup (cooked)
- **Brussels sprouts** — 63 mg per cup (cooked)
- **Cauliflower** — 47 mg per cup (cooked)
- **Peanuts** — 52 mg per 100 g
- **Shiitake mushrooms** — 58 mg per cup (cooked)
- **Kidney beans** — 45 mg per cup (cooked)
- **Wheat germ** — 152 mg per 100 g

### Adequate Intake (AI) for Choline
- **Men:** 550 mg/day
- **Women:** 425 mg/day
- **Pregnant women:** 450 mg/day
- **Lactating women:** 550 mg/day
- **Over 90% of Americans don't meet adequate choline intake** — making supplementation particularly relevant

### Important Notes
- **Dietary choline is primarily phosphatidylcholine and free choline** — these forms are converted to acetylcholine in the body but cross the blood-brain barrier less efficiently than Alpha-GPC
- **Eggs are the most practical dietary choline source** — 2-3 eggs/day provides ~300-450 mg choline, covering most of the adequate intake
- **Liver is the richest source** but many people don't eat organ meats regularly
- **Vegetarians and vegans** are at higher risk of choline deficiency — consider supplementation
- **The gap between dietary choline and the amount needed for nootropic effects** is significant. Food choline supports basic metabolic needs; supplemental Alpha-GPC provides the concentration needed for enhanced cognitive and athletic performance.
- **You cannot get meaningful Alpha-GPC doses from food alone** — supplementation is the only practical way to achieve nootropic doses
