# Alpha-GPC
## Drug Interactions

### Moderate Interactions (Use with Caution)
- **Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil/Aricept, rivastigmine/Exelon, galantamine/Razadyne)** — these drugs prevent acetylcholine breakdown. Alpha-GPC increases acetylcholine production. Combining them could lead to excessive cholinergic activity (bradycardia, excessive salivation, GI distress, diarrhea). If combining, do so only under medical supervision with dose adjustments.
- **Muscarinic agonists (bethanechol, pilocarpine)** — these drugs directly activate muscarinic acetylcholine receptors. Adding Alpha-GPC increases acetylcholine further, potentially causing additive cholinergic effects. Use with caution.
- **Scopolamine (motion sickness patches)** — scopolamine is a muscarinic antagonist (anticholinergic). Alpha-GPC directly opposes its mechanism. They work against each other. Avoid combining for cognitive purposes; if using scopolamine for nausea, be aware that Alpha-GPC may reduce its effectiveness.
- **Atropine and other anticholinergics** — direct pharmacological opposition. Alpha-GPC increases ACh; anticholinergics block ACh receptors. Combining reduces the effectiveness of both.

### Mild Interactions (Monitor)
- **Tricyclic antidepressants (amitriptyline, nortriptyline, imipramine)** — TCAs have anticholinergic side effects. Alpha-GPC may partially counteract these side effects (which could be beneficial for dry mouth, constipation, cognitive dulling) but could also alter the drug's therapeutic profile. Discuss with prescribing psychiatrist.
- **Antihistamines (first-generation: diphenhydramine/Benadryl, chlorpheniramine)** — first-gen antihistamines are anticholinergic. Alpha-GPC may partially counteract their sedative/anticholinergic effects. Second-generation antihistamines (cetirizine, loratadine) have minimal anticholinergic activity and are fine.
- **Beta-blockers** — acetylcholine slows heart rate (parasympathetic). Combined with beta-blockers (which also slow heart rate), there is a theoretical additive bradycardic effect. At standard supplemental Alpha-GPC doses this is very unlikely to be clinically significant, but monitor if you have a very low resting heart rate.
- **Cholinergic medications for myasthenia gravis (pyridostigmine)** — additive cholinergic effect. Medical supervision required.

### Positive Interactions (Beneficial)
- **Racetam nootropics (piracetam, aniracetam, oxiracetam)** — Alpha-GPC is the preferred choline source to stack with racetams. Racetams increase ACh turnover; Alpha-GPC prevents depletion. Eliminates "racetam headache."
- **Huperzine A (at conservative doses)** — huperzine A inhibits acetylcholinesterase (preventing ACh breakdown). Combined with Alpha-GPC (increasing ACh production), this provides a "produce more, break down less" approach. Use conservative doses of each.
- **L-Theanine + Caffeine** — synergistic nootropic stack. Alpha-GPC adds cholinergic memory support to the alert calm focus of L-Theanine + caffeine.
- **Omega-3 fatty acids / fish oil** — DHA supports neuronal membranes; Alpha-GPC supports the phospholipid components. Complementary neuroprotection.
- **Bacopa monnieri** — complementary memory mechanisms. Bacopa supports memory consolidation (long-term storage); Alpha-GPC supports encoding (learning).

### Contraindications
- **No absolute contraindications** at standard supplemental doses for healthy individuals
- Exercise caution with acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (medical supervision required)
- Avoid combining with anticholinergic drugs if the goal is cognitive enhancement (pharmacological opposition)

### Safety Notes
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — choline is essential during pregnancy and lactation. Alpha-GPC can contribute to meeting choline requirements. Supplemental doses should be discussed with OB/GYN, but choline itself is a recommended nutrient during pregnancy.
- **Asthma** — acetylcholine constricts bronchial smooth muscle. While standard Alpha-GPC doses are unlikely to worsen asthma, patients with severe asthma should start with lower doses and monitor.
- **Cardiac conditions** — acetylcholine's parasympathetic (vagal) effects include slowing heart rate. In people with significant bradycardia or heart block, use Alpha-GPC with caution and medical awareness.
- **Parkinson's disease** — the dopaminergic-cholinergic balance is disrupted in Parkinson's. Adding choline could theoretically worsen motor symptoms by shifting the balance further toward cholinergic dominance. Consult neurologist.
- **Surgery** — Alpha-GPC's cholinergic effects could theoretically interact with anesthetic agents (succinylcholine, neostigmine). Discontinue 1-2 weeks before surgery or inform anesthesiologist.
