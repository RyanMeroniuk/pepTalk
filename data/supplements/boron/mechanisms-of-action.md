# Boron
## Mechanisms of Action

- **SHBG reduction and free-testosterone increase (key hormonal mechanism):** In human studies, supplemental boron lowers sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG). Because SHBG binds and inactivates circulating testosterone, reducing it raises the *free* (bioavailable) testosterone fraction. This is the same core mechanism that makes boron and tongkat-ali complementary
- **Reduced estradiol / favorable androgen-to-estrogen ratio:** The same boron studies report decreased estradiol alongside increased free testosterone, shifting the sex-hormone balance toward androgens. The exact mechanism is not fully defined but may involve effects on steroid hormone metabolism and binding
- **Reduced inflammation (lower hs-CRP and inflammatory cytokines):** Boron supplementation has been associated with reductions in inflammatory markers such as high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) and certain cytokines, which may contribute to its bone and metabolic benefits
- **Vitamin D metabolism support:** Boron appears to increase the half-life and bioactivity of vitamin D, raising circulating active vitamin D in some studies. This links boron to calcium absorption and bone mineralization, and pairs it functionally with vitamin-d3
- **Calcium and magnesium handling / bone mineralization:** Boron influences the metabolism of calcium, magnesium, and phosphorus, reducing urinary calcium and magnesium loss and supporting bone mineral density. This underlies its traditional role in bone and joint health
- **Enzyme and cell-signaling effects:** Boron (as boric acid/borate) reversibly binds to compounds containing cis-hydroxyl groups, allowing it to modulate the activity of various enzymes and influence steroid hormone and energy-substrate metabolism
- **Antioxidant support:** Boron may support the body's antioxidant defenses (e.g., glutathione-related activity), contributing to its anti-inflammatory and tissue-protective effects
