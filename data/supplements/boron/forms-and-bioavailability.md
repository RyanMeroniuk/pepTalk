# Boron
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **General bioavailability:** Boron is exceptionally well absorbed regardless of form. Nearly all ingested boron (>85-90%) is absorbed in the gut and converted to/circulated as boric acid, then excreted renally. Because absorption is so high across forms, the choice of salt matters less than for many minerals — the main differences are cost, label dose accuracy, and tolerability

- **Boron citrate:**
  - **What it is:** Boron bound to citric acid — one of the most common and inexpensive supplement forms
  - **Characteristics:** Well absorbed, well tolerated, widely available, affordable
  - **Best for:** General testosterone/bone support; a sensible default

- **Boron glycinate (boron bisglycinate / chelate):**
  - **What it is:** Boron chelated to the amino acid glycine
  - **Characteristics:** Marketed for gentle absorption and good tolerability. Because boron is already highly bioavailable, the practical advantage over citrate is modest, but it is a fine, well-tolerated option
  - **Best for:** Those who prefer chelated minerals or have sensitive digestion

- **Calcium fructoborate:**
  - **What it is:** A naturally-occurring sugar-borate complex (the form found in some fruits/vegetables), often studied for inflammation and joint/bone outcomes
  - **Characteristics:** Some of the inflammation and bone research uses this form. Well absorbed
  - **Best for:** Bone, joint, and anti-inflammatory-focused protocols

- **Boron aspartate:**
  - Boron bound to aspartic acid; sometimes sold in combination with citrate and glycinate as a "tri-boron" complex. Well absorbed

- **Sodium tetraborate / borax (food-grade):**
  - **Note:** Some individuals use very small amounts of food-grade borax dissolved in water as a cheap boron source. While chemically a boron source, dosing precision is poor and the practice is not recommended over standardized supplements. Industrial/cleaning borax is NOT for consumption
  - **Best for:** Not recommended — use a standardized supplement instead

- **Dose reference (elemental boron):** Labels should state *elemental* boron content. Typical supplements provide 3-10 mg elemental boron per dose. The adult tolerable upper intake level is 20 mg/day

- **Quality notes:** Choose products listing elemental boron content clearly, from a reputable third-party-tested brand. Avoid using industrial borax products as a boron source
