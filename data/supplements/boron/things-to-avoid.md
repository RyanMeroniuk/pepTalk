# Boron
## Things to Avoid

- **Exceeding the 20 mg/day upper limit:** This is the most important rule. The adult tolerable upper intake level for boron is 20 mg/day. Standard protocols (3-10 mg/day) are well below this. Megadosing offers no added benefit and risks boric-acid toxicity (nausea, vomiting, headache, lethargy, skin flushing)
- **Using industrial/cleaning-grade borax as a supplement:** Some online advice suggests dosing household borax. Industrial borax is not food-grade, dosing is imprecise, and contamination is possible. Use a standardized boron supplement with a clearly stated *elemental* boron content
- **Confusing elemental boron with compound weight:** A "10 mg boron citrate" capsule is not 10 mg of elemental boron. Always read the *elemental* boron amount on the label to dose correctly and stay under the limit
- **Use during pregnancy or breastfeeding:** Avoid supplemental boron beyond normal dietary intake — high boron exposure shows reproductive/developmental toxicity in animal studies. Dietary boron from food is fine
- **Use with significant kidney disease without medical guidance:** Boron is cleared renally; impaired kidneys may clear it more slowly. Consult a physician
- **Use with hormone-sensitive conditions without oversight:** Because boron raises free testosterone and lowers estradiol, those with prostate cancer, prostate conditions, or hormone-sensitive cancers should consult a physician
- **Expecting large hormonal swings:** Boron's effect is modest — it frees existing testosterone by lowering SHBG rather than producing large amounts of new hormone. It is a supporting player, best used within a stack
- **Stacking many testosterone items at once without a plan:** Combining boron simultaneously with tongkat-ali, shilajit, and fadogia-agrestis makes it impossible to attribute effects or side effects — and fadogia-agrestis carries serious toxicity concerns and almost no human data. Add one variable at a time
- **Ignoring the basics:** Boron supports, but cannot replace, the lifestyle fundamentals (sleep, resistance training, adequate fat/protein, stress management) that drive testosterone and bone health
