# Boron
## Natural Food Sources

Unlike many supplement compounds, boron is genuinely abundant in a plant-rich diet. A varied diet of fruits, vegetables, nuts, and legumes typically provides ~1-3 mg/day.

### Richest Sources (Fruits & Dried Fruits)
- **Raisins** — among the richest common sources (~2-4 mg per 100 g)
- **Prunes (dried plums)** — very high; also a classic bone-health food
- **Dried apricots** — high boron content
- **Avocado** — one of the richest fresh sources (~1-2 mg per fruit)
- **Apples, pears, grapes** — good fresh-fruit sources (boron concentrates in fruit)
- **Dates** — high in boron

### Nuts & Legumes
- **Almonds, hazelnuts, peanuts** — solid sources
- **Beans and lentils** — contribute meaningful boron
- **Chickpeas** — good legume source

### Vegetables
- **Broccoli and leafy greens** — moderate sources
- **Potatoes** (with skin) — contribute boron

### Beverages
- **Wine, cider, and beer** — contain boron (from the fruit/grain); not a recommended source given alcohol's downsides
- **Coffee** — a surprisingly significant contributor to total boron intake in many diets simply due to volume consumed

### Context
- **Typical dietary intake:** ~1-3 mg/day on a plant-rich diet; lower on diets poor in fruits, vegetables, and nuts
- **Why supplement:** The hormonal-support dose (~6-10 mg/day) is higher than diet typically provides, so a supplement is used to reach that range. For general adequacy and bone support, a fruit-, vegetable-, and nut-rich diet may suffice
- **Soil dependence:** Plant boron content depends on soil boron levels, so dietary intake varies by region and growing conditions
