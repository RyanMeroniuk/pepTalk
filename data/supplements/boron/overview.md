# Boron
## Overview

Boron is an ultratrace mineral that, despite being needed in only milligram quantities, exerts surprisingly meaningful effects on hormone metabolism, bone health, and mineral balance. It is obtained naturally from a plant-rich diet — fruits, vegetables, nuts, and legumes — and circulates in the body largely as boric acid. Boron earned its place in the "natural testosterone" conversation because of a frequently cited human study in which roughly 10 mg/day of boron for one week significantly raised free testosterone, lowered estradiol, and reduced sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) — the protein that binds and inactivates testosterone. By lowering SHBG, boron increases the bioavailable (free) testosterone fraction, a mechanism it shares with the sibling supplement tongkat-ali. Beyond hormones, boron supports bone mineralization, influences calcium and magnesium handling, and appears to enhance the activity of vitamin D, making it a quiet but useful player in skeletal health. It is inexpensive, well-tolerated at sensible doses (typically 3-10 mg/day, well under the 20 mg/day upper limit), and is frequently included in testosterone-support and bone-health stacks alongside magnesium, vitamin-d3, and the broader testosterone cluster of tongkat-ali and shilajit.
