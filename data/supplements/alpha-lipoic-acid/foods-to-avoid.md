# Alpha-Lipoic Acid
## Foods to Avoid

- **Any food taken simultaneously with ALA:** This is the most important point. Food reduces ALA absorption by 40-60%. Do not take ALA with meals, snacks, or caloric beverages. Take 30-60 minutes before eating on a completely empty stomach
- **Excessive alcohol:** Alcohol depletes glutathione (the antioxidant ALA helps regenerate), damages the liver, and increases oxidative stress. Chronic alcohol use also depletes thiamine, and ALA may increase thiamine requirements. This combination is particularly problematic
- **High-sugar and high-glycemic foods:** While ALA helps manage blood sugar, consuming excessive sugar and refined carbohydrates creates a metabolic burden that overwhelms ALA's glucose-lowering effects. Reduce sugar intake to maximize ALA's blood sugar benefits
- **Processed foods with heavy metal contamination:** Some processed foods (certain rice products, fish from polluted waters, contaminated spices) contain heavy metals. While ALA chelates metals, it redistributes them before eliminating them. Minimizing new metal intake reduces the chelation burden
- **Excessive dairy at ALA dosing time:** Calcium in dairy products may interact with ALA's metal chelation properties. Space dairy consumption away from ALA supplementation
- **High-iron meals concurrent with ALA (chelation concern):** ALA chelates iron. While this can be beneficial for iron overload, it can be problematic for iron-deficient individuals. If iron-deficient, do not take ALA with iron-rich meals or iron supplements — space them 2-3 hours apart
- **Energy drinks and high-caffeine beverages:** These can affect blood sugar regulation and may interact with ALA's glucose-lowering effects, potentially causing unpredictable blood sugar swings
