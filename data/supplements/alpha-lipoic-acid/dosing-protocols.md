# Alpha-Lipoic Acid
## Dosing Protocols

- **General antioxidant support:** 100-300mg/day racemic ALA, or 50-150mg/day R-ALA
- **Blood sugar management:** 300-600mg/day racemic ALA, or 150-300mg/day R-ALA. The majority of diabetes studies used 600mg/day racemic ALA
- **Diabetic neuropathy:** 600mg/day racemic ALA (the ALADIN trial dose). Some studies used up to 1800mg/day, but side effects increased without proportional benefit. 600mg/day is the sweet spot
- **Weight management:** 300-600mg/day racemic ALA. Higher doses (1200-1800mg/day) showed greater weight loss in some trials but with more GI side effects
- **Heavy metal chelation:** Typically part of a protocol (e.g., Cutler protocol for mercury). Doses range from 12.5-200mg R-ALA every 3-4 hours around the clock for 3 days on, 4 days off. This is specialized and should be done under practitioner guidance
- **Neuroprotection / cognitive support:** 300-600mg/day racemic ALA or 150-300mg/day R-ALA
- **Timing (critical — empty stomach):** Take ALA 30-60 minutes before a meal on an empty stomach. Food reduces absorption by 40-60%. If splitting doses, take before breakfast and before dinner
- **Split dosing:** ALA has a short half-life (~30 min for R-ALA, ~60 min for racemic). For sustained benefit, split the daily dose into 2-3 doses taken before meals. Example: 200mg three times daily before meals (600mg total racemic)
- **R-ALA dose equivalence:** R-ALA is roughly twice as potent as racemic ALA. 150mg R-ALA is approximately equivalent to 300mg racemic ALA in terms of bioactive R-enantiomer content
- **Starting dose:** Begin with 100-200mg/day and increase over 1-2 weeks to assess tolerance, especially regarding blood sugar effects
- **Onset of effects:** Antioxidant effects begin immediately. Blood sugar improvements are noticeable within 1-2 weeks. Neuropathy improvements require 3-5 weeks. Weight management effects emerge over 8-12 weeks
- **Upper limit:** Doses up to 1800mg/day racemic ALA have been used in clinical trials. Higher doses increase GI side effects without proportionally greater benefits for most indications
