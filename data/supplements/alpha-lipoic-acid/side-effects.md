# Alpha-Lipoic Acid
## Side Effects

- **Gastrointestinal discomfort:** The most common side effect. Nausea, heartburn, stomach pain, and diarrhea occur in 10-20% of users, especially at doses above 600mg/day. Taking on an empty stomach (required for absorption) may contribute. Starting at low doses and titrating up helps
- **Hypoglycemia (important for diabetics):** ALA lowers blood sugar through AMPK activation and improved insulin sensitivity. Diabetics on insulin or sulfonylureas are at risk of hypoglycemia. Blood sugar monitoring is essential when starting ALA. Medication doses may need reduction
- **Skin rash / allergic reaction:** Uncommon. Contact dermatitis or generalized rash has been reported. Discontinue if rash develops
- **Headache:** Occasionally reported, usually mild and transient
- **Metallic taste:** Some users report a metallic taste in the mouth, which may relate to ALA's sulfur content or metal chelation activity
- **Body odor change:** ALA is a sulfur compound. Some users notice a change in body odor or urine smell, similar to what occurs with asparagus. Not harmful but can be noticeable
- **Hypotension:** ALA may lower blood pressure modestly. Combined with antihypertensive medications, this could cause dizziness in sensitive individuals
- **Mineral depletion (with chronic high-dose use):** ALA chelates metals including essential minerals (zinc, copper, iron) along with toxic metals. Long-term high-dose use without mineral monitoring or repletion could theoretically cause deficiencies. Supplement with a multimineral on off-days if using ALA for chelation
- **Thiamine depletion (theoretical):** ALA may increase thiamine (B1) metabolism. Alcohol users and those with marginal thiamine status should ensure adequate B1 intake alongside ALA. In rare cases, severe thiamine deficiency combined with ALA could worsen neurological symptoms
- **Insulin autoimmune syndrome (very rare):** Extremely rare reports (primarily in Japanese populations with genetic predisposition) of ALA triggering insulin autoantibodies, causing severe hypoglycemia. This is essentially limited to individuals with HLA-DRB1*0406
- **Skin sensitivity to sun:** Some users report increased photosensitivity. Use sunscreen as appropriate
