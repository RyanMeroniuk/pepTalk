# Alpha-Lipoic Acid
## Lifestyle Recommendations

- **Monitor blood sugar if diabetic or prediabetic:** ALA meaningfully lowers blood sugar. Track fasting glucose and post-meal glucose when starting ALA. Work with your physician to adjust diabetes medications if needed. This is especially important when combining ALA with exercise and fasting, both of which also lower blood sugar
- **Exercise regularly:** Exercise increases mitochondrial function, reduces oxidative stress long-term, and improves insulin sensitivity — all complementary to ALA's mechanisms. ALA may enhance exercise recovery through its antioxidant effects. Aim for 150+ minutes moderate cardio + 2-3 resistance sessions per week
- **Support detoxification pathways:** ALA chelates heavy metals and supports glutathione. Help your body clear mobilized toxins by staying well hydrated (2-3 liters water daily), sweating regularly (sauna, exercise), ensuring adequate fiber intake (25-35g/day), and supporting liver health
- **Eat a nutrient-dense diet:** ALA recycles vitamins C, E, and glutathione. Ensure adequate dietary intake of these through colorful fruits, vegetables, nuts, seeds, and quality protein sources. ALA amplifies a good diet; it cannot replace one
- **Manage environmental toxin exposure:** Minimize exposure to heavy metals and environmental chemicals. Filter drinking water, choose lower-mercury fish (sardines, anchovies, wild salmon over tuna, swordfish), avoid lead paint exposure, and use glass or stainless steel instead of plastic for food storage
- **Prioritize sleep:** Oxidative stress increases with sleep deprivation. ALA's antioxidant recycling is more effective when the body has lower baseline oxidative burden from adequate sleep
- **Reduce alcohol consumption:** Alcohol depletes glutathione, increases oxidative stress, and requires thiamine — all counteracting ALA's benefits. Minimize or eliminate alcohol to maximize ALA's effectiveness
- **Include cruciferous vegetables daily:** Broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage, and Brussels sprouts provide sulforaphane, which activates Nrf2 alongside ALA. The combination provides powerful upregulation of endogenous antioxidant enzymes
- **Consider periodic sauna use:** Sweating is a route for heavy metal excretion. When using ALA for metal chelation, infrared sauna (3-4 sessions/week) can support metal elimination through sweat
- **Supplement with a B-complex:** ALA increases the metabolic demand for B vitamins, particularly B1 (thiamine) and biotin. A quality B-complex ensures these cofactors are not depleted
