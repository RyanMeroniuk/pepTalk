# Alpha-Lipoic Acid
## Things to Avoid

- **Taking ALA with food (critical):** Absorption drops 40-60% when taken with meals. This is the single biggest mistake people make with ALA. Always take on an empty stomach, 30-60 minutes before eating
- **Using racemic ALA when R-ALA is available:** The S-enantiomer in racemic ALA is largely inactive and may compete with R-ALA for absorption. R-ALA or stabilized R-ALA (Na-R-ALA) is the preferred form for anyone serious about results
- **Ignoring blood sugar effects in diabetics:** ALA can significantly lower blood sugar. Diabetics on insulin, sulfonylureas, or other glucose-lowering medications MUST monitor blood glucose closely and may need medication dose reductions. Hypoglycemic episodes can be dangerous
- **High-dose chelation without guidance:** ALA's heavy metal chelation properties can mobilize metals from tissues into the bloodstream. Without proper protocol (e.g., Andy Cutler protocol: frequent small doses around the clock), ALA can redistribute metals to more sensitive tissues including the brain. Never do high-dose ALA chelation without practitioner guidance
- **Ignoring mineral depletion during long-term use:** ALA chelates essential minerals (zinc, copper, iron) along with toxic metals. Long-term use at high doses without mineral supplementation can cause deficiencies. Monitor mineral levels and supplement accordingly
- **Thiamine (B1) deficiency:** ALA may increase thiamine utilization. Individuals who are thiamine-depleted (alcoholics, those with malabsorption, bariatric surgery patients) should ensure adequate B1 before starting ALA. Co-supplement with a B-complex
- **Combining with other blood sugar-lowering agents without monitoring:** The additive hypoglycemic effect of ALA + diabetes medications + fasting or exercise can cause dangerous blood sugar drops. Monitor, adjust, and communicate with your physician
- **Starting with high doses:** Begin with 100-200mg/day and titrate up over 1-2 weeks. Starting at 600mg/day can cause significant GI distress and blood sugar drops
- **Using ALA without antioxidant network support:** ALA regenerates vitamins C, E, and glutathione. If these cofactors are severely depleted, ALA may generate pro-oxidant radicals during redox cycling. Ensure basic nutritional adequacy
- **Storing improperly:** R-ALA, especially unstabilized forms, is sensitive to heat, light, and moisture. Store in a cool, dark, dry place. Refrigeration is recommended for R-ALA
