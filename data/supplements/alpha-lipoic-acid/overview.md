# Alpha-Lipoic Acid
## Overview

Alpha-lipoic acid is a naturally occurring organosulfur compound that holds a unique position among antioxidants: it is the only one that functions in both water-soluble and fat-soluble environments. This "universal antioxidant" property allows ALA to protect every compartment of the cell — from the aqueous cytoplasm to lipid-rich cell membranes to the blood-brain barrier.

- **Why it matters:** Most antioxidants work in only one environment. Vitamin C is water-soluble. Vitamin E is fat-soluble. ALA works in both, and it also regenerates vitamins C, E, glutathione, and CoQ10 — essentially recycling the entire antioxidant network
- **R-ALA vs racemic ALA:** ALA exists as two mirror-image forms (enantiomers): R-ALA (the naturally occurring, bioactive form) and S-ALA (synthetic, less active). Most supplements contain racemic ALA (50/50 R and S). R-ALA is 10-40x more potent than S-ALA and is the preferred supplemental form
- **Mitochondrial cofactor:** ALA is a required cofactor for pyruvate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase — two critical enzymes in the Krebs cycle. Without ALA, mitochondrial energy production is impaired
- **Blood sugar regulation:** ALA is one of the best-studied supplements for blood sugar management. It enhances glucose uptake by activating AMPK and promoting GLUT4 translocation to cell membranes. It is prescribed for diabetic neuropathy in Europe
- **Heavy metal chelation:** ALA (and its reduced form DHLA) chelates mercury, arsenic, cadmium, and other heavy metals. It crosses the blood-brain barrier, making it one of the few chelating agents that can address brain-accumulated metals
- **Neuroprotection:** ALA crosses the blood-brain barrier, protects neurons from oxidative damage, and may slow progression of neurodegenerative diseases
- **Extensive clinical evidence:** Hundreds of clinical trials support ALA's benefits for diabetic neuropathy, blood sugar control, weight management, and antioxidant protection
