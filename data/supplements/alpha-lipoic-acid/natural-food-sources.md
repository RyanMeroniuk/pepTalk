# Alpha-Lipoic Acid
## Natural Food Sources

ALA is found in foods bound to protein (as lipoyllysine). Dietary amounts are much lower than supplemental doses.

- **Animal sources (higher concentrations):**
  - **Beef kidney** — 2.64mg per 100g (the richest known dietary source)
  - **Beef heart** — 1.51mg per 100g
  - **Beef liver** — 0.86mg per 100g
  - **Pork kidney** — 2.12mg per 100g
  - **Pork heart** — 1.23mg per 100g
  - **Beef (muscle meat)** — 0.10-0.50mg per 100g
- **Vegetables:**
  - **Spinach** — 0.94mg per 100g (highest among vegetables)
  - **Broccoli** — 0.71mg per 100g
  - **Tomato** — 0.56mg per 100g
  - **Brussels sprouts** — 0.39mg per 100g
  - **Peas** — 0.39mg per 100g
  - **Rice bran** — 0.24mg per 100g
- **Other sources:**
  - **Brewer's yeast** — 0.33mg per 100g
  - **Potatoes** — 0.13mg per 100g
  - **Carrots** — 0.10mg per 100g

**Important context:** The richest food source (beef kidney) provides about 2.6mg per 100g serving. A typical therapeutic dose is 300-600mg/day. You would need to eat approximately 12-23 kg (26-50 lbs) of beef kidney daily to reach therapeutic levels. Dietary sources contribute trace amounts but cannot substitute for supplementation at therapeutic doses. The R-ALA in food is protein-bound and has different absorption kinetics than free-form supplemental ALA.
