# Zinc
## Things to Avoid

### Taking Zinc on an Empty Stomach
- **Why:** Causes nausea, stomach cramps, and sometimes vomiting in many people
- **Solution:** Always take zinc with a meal or snack containing protein

### Taking Zinc at the Same Time as Other Mineral Supplements
- **Why:** Zinc competes with iron, calcium, and copper for absorption via shared intestinal transporters (DMT1, ZIP4)
- **Solution:** Stagger mineral supplements by at least 2 hours. Take zinc with breakfast, iron at lunch, calcium at dinner, for example.

### Chronic High-Dose Zinc Without Copper
- **Why:** This is the most common mistake with zinc supplementation. Zinc induces metallothionein production in intestinal cells, which preferentially binds copper, trapping it in the cell and preventing absorption. Over 6-8 weeks, this leads to copper deficiency — which causes anemia, immune dysfunction, and potentially irreversible neurological damage.
- **Solution:** Always supplement copper (2-3mg/day) when taking zinc above 25mg/day. Maintain a zinc-to-copper ratio of 10:1 to 15:1.

### Using Zinc Oxide for Oral Supplementation
- **Why:** Zinc oxide has the lowest bioavailability of any common zinc form (~5-8% absorption). It is cheap to manufacture, so it appears in many budget supplements and multivitamins, but you absorb very little of it.
- **Solution:** Choose zinc picolinate, bisglycinate, citrate, or gluconate instead

### Taking Zinc With Quinolone or Tetracycline Antibiotics
- **Why:** Zinc chelates these antibiotics, dramatically reducing their effectiveness
- **Solution:** Separate zinc from these medications by at least 2 hours before or 4-6 hours after the antibiotic

### Excessive Zinc Supplementation (>40mg/day chronic)
- **Why:** Exceeding the UL chronically increases risk of copper deficiency, HDL cholesterol reduction, immune suppression, and GI distress
- **Solution:** Keep daily zinc intake at or below 40mg for long-term use. Short-term higher doses (cold protocol) are acceptable for up to 2 weeks.

### Taking Zinc Right Before Bed
- **Why:** Some individuals report vivid dreams, insomnia, or restless sleep when taking zinc in the evening, possibly due to zinc's influence on neurotransmitter activity
- **Solution:** Take zinc with breakfast or lunch instead

### Nasal Zinc Sprays
- **Why:** The FDA issued warnings in 2009 after over 130 reports of anosmia (permanent loss of smell) linked to intranasal zinc products (e.g., Zicam nasal gel). These products were recalled.
- **Solution:** Use zinc lozenges for cold treatment, never nasal zinc products

### Smoking
- **Why:** Cadmium in cigarette smoke competes with zinc for absorption and displaces zinc from metallothioneins and enzymes. Smokers have significantly lower zinc levels than non-smokers.
- **Solution:** If unable to quit, increase zinc intake by 30-50%
