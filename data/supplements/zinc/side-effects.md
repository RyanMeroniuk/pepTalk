# Zinc
## Side Effects

### Common Side Effects
- **Nausea and stomach upset** — The most common complaint, especially with zinc sulfate or oxide on an empty stomach. Taking with food significantly reduces this.
- **Metallic taste** — Particularly with zinc lozenges; temporary and resolves after stopping
- **Headache** — Occasionally reported at doses above 30mg/day
- **Reduced appetite** — Paradoxically, while zinc deficiency reduces appetite, excessive zinc can also decrease appetite

### Serious Side Effects (High Dose / Long-Term)

- **Copper deficiency** — The most clinically significant risk of chronic zinc supplementation
  - Occurs at doses above 30-40mg/day taken for more than 6-8 weeks without copper co-supplementation
  - Symptoms: anemia (microcytic or sideroblastic), neutropenia, fatigue, pale skin, nerve damage, myeloneuropathy
  - Can mimic B12 deficiency neurologically
  - **Prevention:** Always supplement copper (2-3mg/day) when taking zinc above 25mg/day

- **Iron absorption interference** — Zinc competes with iron for absorption; high-dose zinc can reduce iron status over time

- **HDL cholesterol reduction** — Doses above 50mg/day have been associated with reduced HDL ("good") cholesterol levels

- **Immune suppression** — Paradoxically, excessive zinc (>100mg/day chronic) impairs immune function rather than enhancing it

- **Prostate cancer concern** — One observational study (Leitzmann et al., 2003) found supplemental zinc above 100mg/day was associated with increased prostate cancer risk; however, this finding has not been consistently replicated

### Toxicity Thresholds
- **Acute toxicity:** Single doses above 200mg can cause severe nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal cramps
- **Chronic toxicity:** Regular intake above 40mg/day (UL) increases risk of copper deficiency and other adverse effects
- **Lethal dose:** Extremely rare; zinc has low acute toxicity, but ingestion of zinc chloride (from pennies minted after 1982) has caused fatalities in rare cases
- **Zinc fume fever:** Inhaled zinc oxide fumes (occupational exposure) cause flu-like symptoms — not relevant to oral supplementation

### Interactions That Cause Side Effects
- **Zinc + antibiotics (quinolones, tetracyclines):** Zinc chelates these drugs and reduces their absorption — separate by 2+ hours
- **Zinc + penicillamine:** Zinc reduces absorption of this medication used for Wilson's disease and rheumatoid arthritis
- **Zinc on an empty stomach:** Most GI side effects occur because zinc was taken without food
