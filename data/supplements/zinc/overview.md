# Zinc
## Overview

Zinc is an essential trace mineral involved in virtually every aspect of cellular metabolism. It is the second most abundant trace mineral in the body after iron and is present in every cell.

### Why It Matters
- **Immune function:** Zinc is critical for both innate and adaptive immunity. Zinc-deficient individuals are significantly more susceptible to infections.
- **Protein synthesis and wound healing:** Required for DNA synthesis, cell division, and tissue repair.
- **Testosterone and hormonal health:** Zinc is essential for testosterone production and reproductive health in both men and women.
- **Taste and smell:** Zinc deficiency is a common cause of anosmia (loss of smell) and dysgeusia (altered taste).
- **Antioxidant defense:** Component of superoxide dismutase (Cu/Zn-SOD), a key antioxidant enzyme.
- **Gene expression:** Zinc finger proteins regulate transcription of thousands of genes.

### Who Needs It Most
- **Vegetarians and vegans** — Plant-based diets are high in phytates, which bind zinc and reduce absorption by up to 50%
- **Athletes and heavy exercisers** — Zinc is lost through sweat (up to 1mg/hour during intense exercise)
- **Older adults** — Absorption decreases with age; ~35-45% of adults over 60 have inadequate zinc intake
- **Pregnant and lactating women** — Increased demand for fetal development and milk production
- **People with GI disorders** — Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, and short bowel syndrome impair zinc absorption
- **Heavy alcohol consumers** — Alcohol reduces zinc absorption and increases urinary excretion
- **Men focused on hormonal optimization** — Zinc is directly linked to testosterone levels

### Signs of Deficiency
- Frequent illness or slow wound healing
- Hair loss or thinning
- Loss of taste or smell
- Skin lesions, acne, or dermatitis
- Poor appetite
- Brain fog or difficulty concentrating
- Low testosterone or reduced libido
- White spots on fingernails (leukonychia)
