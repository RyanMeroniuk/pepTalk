# Zinc
## Foods to Avoid (When Taking Zinc)

### Phytate-Rich Foods
- **What:** Whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, and soy products
- **Examples:** Wheat bran, brown rice, lentils, chickpeas, kidney beans, tofu, corn tortillas
- **Why:** Phytic acid (inositol hexaphosphate) binds zinc in the GI tract, forming insoluble zinc-phytate complexes that cannot be absorbed. Phytates are the single most significant dietary inhibitor of zinc absorption.
- **How much impact:** Phytates can reduce zinc absorption by 40-60%
- **Mitigation:** Do not need to eliminate these foods — just avoid consuming them at the same time as zinc supplements. Soaking, sprouting, or fermenting these foods reduces phytate content significantly.

### Calcium-Rich Foods and Supplements
- **What:** Milk, cheese, yogurt, calcium-fortified foods, calcium supplements
- **Why:** Calcium competes with zinc for absorption transporters in the intestinal lumen, particularly when phytates are also present. The combination of calcium + phytates is more inhibitory than either alone.
- **How much impact:** 600mg calcium taken with a meal can reduce zinc absorption by 50% in the presence of phytates
- **Mitigation:** Separate calcium supplements and zinc supplements by at least 2 hours. A glass of milk with a zinc supplement is usually fine on its own, but avoid dairy + grains + zinc together.

### High-Iron Foods and Iron Supplements
- **What:** Iron supplements, fortified cereals, red meat in very large quantities
- **Why:** Non-heme iron and zinc compete for the same DMT1 (divalent metal transporter 1) receptor in the small intestine. When taken in supplemental form at high doses, iron significantly reduces zinc absorption.
- **How much impact:** Iron supplements (>25mg) taken simultaneously with zinc reduce zinc absorption by up to 50%
- **Mitigation:** Take iron and zinc supplements at different times of day. Iron from food sources at normal dietary levels does not significantly impair zinc absorption.

### Coffee and Tea
- **What:** Coffee, black tea, green tea
- **Why:** Tannins and polyphenols in coffee and tea can bind minerals including zinc, forming insoluble complexes. Caffeine may also slightly increase urinary zinc excretion.
- **How much impact:** Moderate — less significant than phytates but still relevant
- **Mitigation:** Wait at least 1 hour after taking zinc before drinking coffee or tea

### Alcohol
- **What:** Beer, wine, spirits
- **Why:** Alcohol reduces zinc absorption in the intestine, increases urinary zinc excretion, and impairs the liver's ability to release zinc from storage
- **How much impact:** Chronic alcohol use is one of the most common causes of zinc deficiency in developed countries
- **Mitigation:** Limit alcohol consumption; if drinking, do not take zinc supplements with alcoholic beverages

### High-Dose Folate (Folic Acid) Supplements
- **What:** Folic acid supplements above 800mcg
- **Why:** High-dose folic acid may interfere with zinc absorption by forming insoluble complexes, though the evidence is debated
- **How much impact:** Modest; primarily a concern at supplemental doses above 800mcg
- **Mitigation:** Take folic acid and zinc at different times of day if taking high-dose folic acid
