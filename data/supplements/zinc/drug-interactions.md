# Zinc
## Drug Interactions

### Major Interactions (Avoid Concurrent Use or Separate Significantly)

**Quinolone antibiotics (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, moxifloxacin):**
- **Interaction:** Zinc chelates quinolones in the GI tract, forming insoluble complexes and reducing antibiotic absorption by up to 50%
- **Management:** Take zinc at least 2 hours before or 6 hours after quinolone antibiotics

**Tetracycline antibiotics (doxycycline, minocycline, tetracycline):**
- **Interaction:** Same chelation mechanism as quinolones; zinc significantly reduces tetracycline absorption and efficacy
- **Management:** Separate by at least 2 hours before or 4-6 hours after

**Penicillamine (Cuprimine, Depen):**
- **Interaction:** Zinc reduces penicillamine absorption; used for Wilson's disease, cystinuria, and rheumatoid arthritis
- **Management:** Separate by at least 2 hours. Note: zinc is sometimes intentionally used as an alternative copper-lowering therapy for Wilson's disease.

**Cephalexin and cephalosporin antibiotics:**
- **Interaction:** Zinc may reduce absorption of some cephalosporins
- **Management:** Separate by at least 2 hours

### Moderate Interactions

**Thiazide diuretics (hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone):**
- **Interaction:** Thiazides increase urinary zinc excretion by 60%, potentially leading to zinc depletion with chronic use
- **Management:** Monitor zinc levels; may need to supplement 15-25mg/day to compensate

**ACE inhibitors (lisinopril, enalapril, ramipril):**
- **Interaction:** ACE inhibitors may reduce zinc levels; zinc depletion may contribute to ACE inhibitor-associated cough and taste changes
- **Management:** Supplementing zinc (15-30mg/day) may reduce ACE inhibitor side effects

**Bisphosphonates (alendronate, risedronate):**
- **Interaction:** Zinc may reduce bisphosphonate absorption
- **Management:** Separate by at least 2 hours

**Proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole, esomeprazole):**
- **Interaction:** PPIs reduce stomach acid, which is required for zinc dissolution and absorption. Chronic PPI use is associated with zinc deficiency.
- **Management:** Use a chelated form of zinc (picolinate, bisglycinate) that does not require stomach acid for absorption; consider higher doses

**H2 blockers (famotidine, ranitidine):**
- **Interaction:** Similar to PPIs — reduced stomach acid impairs zinc absorption, though to a lesser degree
- **Management:** Take zinc with food; consider chelated forms

**Oral contraceptives:**
- **Interaction:** Birth control pills may reduce serum zinc levels
- **Management:** Women on oral contraceptives should ensure adequate zinc intake (15mg/day)

**Corticosteroids (prednisone, prednisolone):**
- **Interaction:** Chronic corticosteroid use increases zinc excretion
- **Management:** Supplement with 15-25mg zinc/day during long-term corticosteroid therapy

### Minor Interactions

**Zinc + Iron supplements:**
- **Interaction:** Competitive absorption; take separately by 2+ hours
- **Note:** Dietary iron at normal levels does not significantly interact

**Zinc + Copper supplements:**
- **Interaction:** Competitive absorption at the intestinal level
- **Note:** Combined formulas are fine; if taking separately, space by 2 hours

**Zinc + Calcium supplements:**
- **Interaction:** Calcium may reduce zinc absorption, especially in the presence of phytates
- **Note:** Dietary calcium has minimal impact; supplemental calcium (500mg+) should be separated

### Pregnancy Considerations
- **FDA Category:** Zinc is generally recognized as safe during pregnancy at recommended doses
- **RDA during pregnancy:** 11mg/day (age 19+), 12mg/day (age 14-18)
- **Upper limit during pregnancy:** 40mg/day (age 19+), 34mg/day (age 14-18)
- **Importance:** Zinc deficiency during pregnancy is associated with low birth weight, preterm delivery, preeclampsia, and complications in labor
- **Caution:** Do not exceed the UL during pregnancy without medical supervision
- **Note:** Many prenatal vitamins contain zinc (typically 11-15mg); check total intake from all sources before adding a separate zinc supplement

### Breastfeeding
- **RDA during lactation:** 12mg/day
- **Zinc is secreted in breast milk; adequate intake is important for infant development
- **Most prenatal/postnatal vitamins provide adequate zinc; additional supplementation is rarely needed
