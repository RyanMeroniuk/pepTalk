# Vitamin K2
## Dosing Protocols

### General Maintenance (MK-7)
- **General health / D3 co-supplementation:** 100-200 mcg MK-7 per day
- **Per 5,000 IU vitamin D3:** 100-200 mcg MK-7 (commonly cited ratio)
- **Per 10,000 IU vitamin D3:** 200-300 mcg MK-7
- **Cardiovascular protection focus:** 200-360 mcg MK-7 per day
- **Bone health maintenance:** 180-200 mcg MK-7 per day

### Therapeutic Dosing (MK-7)
- **Active osteoporosis / osteopenia:** 200-360 mcg MK-7 per day, combined with D3 (5,000 IU), calcium (from food ideally, 1,000-1,200 mg/day), and magnesium (300-400 mg/day)
- **Existing arterial calcification:** 360 mcg MK-7 per day (based on clinical trials showing reduction in arterial stiffness at this dose over 3 years)
- **Post-menopausal women:** 180-360 mcg MK-7 per day for bone and cardiovascular protection

### Therapeutic Dosing (MK-4)
- **Japanese osteoporosis protocol:** 45 mg/day (15 mg three times daily with meals). This is a pharmacological dose, proven in multiple clinical trials. Available as a prescription drug in Japan (Glakay).
- **General MK-4 supplementation:** 1-5 mg/day in divided doses (2-3x daily) for tissue-specific support
- **Brain health protocol:** 1-5 mg MK-4/day (MK-4 preferentially accumulates in brain tissue)
- **Testosterone support:** 1-5 mg MK-4/day (based on animal data; human studies pending)

### Combined MK-7 + MK-4 Protocol
- **Comprehensive approach:** MK-7 100-200 mcg/day (once, for sustained systemic levels) + MK-4 1-5 mg/day (divided, for tissue-specific effects). This provides both long-duration systemic K2 activity and tissue-specific benefits.

### D3+K2 Pairing Guidelines
- **Rule of thumb:** For every 1,000 IU of vitamin D3, take approximately 20-40 mcg of MK-7
- **Standard pairing:** D3 5,000 IU + MK-7 100-200 mcg — the most common protocol
- **Higher D3 doses:** D3 10,000 IU + MK-7 200-300 mcg
- **Always include magnesium:** 300-400 mg/day (glycinate, malate, or threonate) — required as a cofactor for both D3 activation and K2-dependent enzyme function

### Upper Limits
- **No established UL for K2.** There is no tolerable upper intake level (UL) set by any regulatory body for vitamin K2.
- **MK-7 up to 360 mcg/day** has been used in 3-year clinical trials without adverse effects.
- **MK-4 up to 45 mg/day** has been used in Japanese clinical trials for years without toxicity.
- **K2 does not cause hypercoagulation.** Unlike K1, supplemental K2 at these doses does not make blood clot "more than normal." Coagulation proteins have limited binding capacity and are fully carboxylated at relatively low K intake.

### Timing and Administration
- **Take with a fat-containing meal:** K2 is fat-soluble. Absorption is significantly enhanced when taken with dietary fat (butter, eggs, avocado, olive oil, cheese).
- **Best time:** With lunch or dinner (largest fat-containing meals). Can also be taken with breakfast if it contains adequate fat.
- **MK-7:** Once daily (long half-life provides 72-hour sustained levels)
- **MK-4:** Divided into 2-3 doses per day with meals (short 1-2 hour half-life)
- **Can be taken with D3:** K2 and D3 are commonly taken together in the same meal. Many supplements combine them.
- **Not time-sensitive relative to other supplements:** K2 does not compete for absorption with other vitamins or minerals.

### Special Populations
- **Pregnancy:** K2 supplementation (100-200 mcg MK-7) supports fetal bone and dental development. K2 deficiency during pregnancy and early childhood is associated with dental crowding and poor enamel. Generally considered safe.
- **Children and adolescents:** 45-90 mcg MK-7/day to support bone growth and dental development.
- **Chronic kidney disease patients:** High risk of vascular calcification with profound K2 deficiency. Supplementation (90-360 mcg MK-7) is being actively studied and is recommended by many nephrologists, but monitor in context of overall medication regimen.
- **Post-bariatric surgery:** Fat malabsorption increases risk of K2 deficiency. Consider higher doses or emulsified forms.

### Monitoring
- **No routine clinical test** for K2 status is widely available.
- **Research markers:** Undercarboxylated osteocalcin (ucOC) and dephospho-uncarboxylated MGP (dp-ucMGP) reflect K2 functional status but are not standard clinical tests.
- **Indirect markers:** Bone density scans (DEXA), coronary artery calcium (CAC) scores, and arterial stiffness measurements can track long-term outcomes.
- **Osteocalcin ratio (ucOC/cOC):** Available through some functional medicine labs. A high ratio indicates K2 insufficiency.
