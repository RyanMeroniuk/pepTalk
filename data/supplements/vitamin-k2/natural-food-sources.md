# Vitamin K2
## Natural Food Sources

### Highest Sources (K2 MK-7)
- **Natto (fermented soybeans):** 3.5 oz (100g) = ~1,000 mcg MK-7. The single richest food source of K2 by a massive margin. A traditional Japanese food with a strong flavor and sticky, stringy texture that many Westerners find challenging. Even 1 tablespoon (~15g) provides ~150 mcg MK-7.
- **Note:** Natto is the primary reason Japanese populations have lower rates of osteoporosis and cardiovascular disease despite lower calcium intake than Western countries.

### Moderate Sources (K2 MK-4 and mixed MKs)
- **Goose liver pate:** 3.5 oz = 369 mcg (primarily MK-4)
- **Hard cheeses (aged, European):**
  - **Gouda:** 3.5 oz = 73 mcg (mixed MK-7, MK-8, MK-9)
  - **Jarlsberg:** 3.5 oz = 65-80 mcg (MK-7, MK-8, MK-9 — Jarlsberg has been specifically studied for K2 content)
  - **Edam:** 3.5 oz = 49 mcg
  - **Brie:** 3.5 oz = 50 mcg
  - **Munster:** 3.5 oz = 80 mcg
  - **Blue cheese:** 3.5 oz = 36 mcg
  - **Cheddar:** 3.5 oz = 10-20 mcg (lower than European varieties)
- **Egg yolks (pasture-raised):** 1 large = 30-40 mcg MK-4 (pasture-raised contain 3-6x more than conventional due to grass/insect diet of hens)
- **Egg yolks (conventional):** 1 large = 5-10 mcg MK-4
- **Butter (grass-fed):** 1 tablespoon = 15 mcg MK-4 (grass-fed contains 3-5x more than grain-fed)
- **Butter (conventional):** 1 tablespoon = 2-5 mcg MK-4
- **Chicken liver:** 3.5 oz = 14 mcg MK-4
- **Chicken thigh (dark meat, with skin):** 3.5 oz = 27 mcg MK-4
- **Ground beef:** 3.5 oz = 8 mcg MK-4
- **Pork sausage:** 3.5 oz = 5-10 mcg MK-4
- **Beef liver:** 3.5 oz = 11 mcg MK-4

### Fermented Foods (variable K2 content)
- **Sauerkraut:** Variable, ~5 mcg per cup (depends on fermentation bacteria)
- **Kimchi:** Variable, ~5-10 mcg per cup
- **Miso:** ~10 mcg per 3.5 oz
- **Cheese curds:** Variable, depends on bacterial cultures used

### Dairy Fat Sources (grass-fed is key)
- **Ghee (grass-fed):** 1 tablespoon = 10-15 mcg MK-4
- **Cream (heavy, grass-fed):** 1 tablespoon = 3-5 mcg MK-4
- **Full-fat yogurt:** Variable, ~5 mcg per cup

### Key Notes
- **Grass-fed / pasture-raised matters enormously.** Animals consuming green grass convert K1 from grass into MK-4 in their tissues. Grain-fed animals produce significantly less K2 in their meat, eggs, butter, and milk. This is the single biggest variable in food K2 content.
- **Western diets are K2-poor.** Unless you regularly eat natto, aged European cheeses, pasture-raised eggs, and grass-fed butter, dietary K2 intake is likely inadequate (estimated 10-50 mcg/day in typical Western diets vs. 200-400 mcg/day needed for optimal bone and vascular health).
- **K1-rich foods (green vegetables) are NOT significant K2 sources.** The body converts K1 to MK-4 very inefficiently (~5-25%). Kale, spinach, and broccoli provide K1 for coagulation but negligible K2 for bone and arterial health.
- **Fermented dairy vs. unfermented:** Bacterial fermentation of dairy (cheese-making) produces K2 (MK-8, MK-9). Unfermented dairy (milk, cream, butter) contains only MK-4 from the animal's tissue conversion.
- **Supplementation is typically necessary** for most people to achieve the 100-200 mcg MK-7 daily intake associated with optimal outcomes.
