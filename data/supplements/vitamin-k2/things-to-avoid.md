# Vitamin K2
## Things to Avoid

### Substances That Deplete or Block K2 Function
- **Warfarin (Coumadin) — CRITICAL:** Warfarin blocks vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKOR), preventing K2 recycling. This means warfarin not only thins blood (intended) but also inactivates the calcium-regulating proteins (osteocalcin, MGP) that K2 activates (unintended). Long-term warfarin use is strongly associated with arterial calcification and osteoporosis. This is a known "collateral damage" of warfarin therapy. Do not supplement K2 without physician guidance if on warfarin.
- **Broad-spectrum antibiotics:** Destroy gut bacteria that produce vitamin K2 (MK-7 through MK-13 in the colon). While colonic K2 absorption is limited, prolonged antibiotic courses can contribute to K2 depletion. Supplement during and after antibiotic therapy.
- **Cholestyramine and colesevelam (bile acid sequestrants):** Bind bile acids and fat-soluble vitamins, reducing K2 absorption. Separate supplementation by 4-6 hours.
- **Orlistat (Xenical/Alli):** Blocks fat absorption, which directly reduces K2 uptake. Supplement K2 at least 2 hours before or after orlistat.
- **Excessive vitamin A supplementation:** Very high-dose vitamin A (>25,000 IU/day retinol) may antagonize vitamin K2 function. Vitamins A, D, and K2 work best in balance — excessive A without proportional D and K2 can impair K2-dependent protein activation.
- **Excessive vitamin E supplementation:** High-dose vitamin E (>800 IU/day alpha-tocopherol) may interfere with vitamin K metabolism and increase bleeding risk, particularly in those with low K status. Keep vitamin E in the 200-400 IU range when supplementing K2.

### Behaviors That Reduce K2 Status
- **Avoiding dietary fat / very low-fat diets:** K2 is fat-soluble. Fat-free or extremely low-fat diets impair both K2 absorption from food and K2 supplement uptake. Include healthy fats at every meal.
- **Choosing grain-fed over grass-fed animal products:** Grain-fed meat, eggs, butter, and dairy contain dramatically less K2 (MK-4) than grass-fed counterparts. The K2 content of animal foods directly reflects the animal's K1 intake from green plants.
- **Relying on K1 for K2 needs:** Many people assume that eating spinach and kale (K1-rich) provides K2. The conversion rate is only 5-25% — woefully inadequate for optimal K2-dependent protein activation. K1 and K2 are distinct and not interchangeable.
- **Ignoring the D3-K2 relationship:** Taking vitamin D3 without K2 increases calcium absorption without directing it to bones. This can accelerate arterial calcification, the opposite of the intended health benefit. Every D3 supplement regimen should include K2.
- **Chronic digestive issues without compensation:** Conditions that impair fat absorption — celiac disease, Crohn's disease, SIBO, bile insufficiency, gallbladder removal — reduce K2 absorption. Use emulsified or micellized K2 forms and consider higher doses.

### Medications That Impair K2 Function (beyond warfarin)
- **Cephalosporin antibiotics (ceftriaxone, cefotetan):** Some cephalosporins contain the NMTT side chain that directly inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase (the same target as warfarin), potentially causing vitamin K-dependent coagulopathy and reduced K2 function.
- **Salicylates (high-dose aspirin >3g/day):** Very high-dose aspirin has mild anticoagulant effects and may interfere with vitamin K metabolism at extreme doses. Standard low-dose aspirin (81-325 mg) does not meaningfully interact with K2.
- **Some statins:** There is emerging evidence that statins may interfere with menaquinone synthesis. The clinical significance is not yet established, but statin users may benefit from K2 supplementation.

### General Cautions
- **Do not abruptly start or stop K2 supplementation if on warfarin.** Any change in vitamin K intake must be communicated to the prescribing physician and accompanied by INR monitoring.
- **If switching from warfarin to a DOAC (Eliquis, Xarelto),** K2 supplementation can typically be started, as DOACs do not work through the vitamin K pathway. Confirm with your cardiologist.
- **Store K2 supplements properly.** MK-7 can degrade with heat, light, and certain mineral interactions. Keep in a cool, dark place in the original container.
