# Vitamin K2
## Classification

- **Category:** Fat-soluble vitamin / calcium-directing hormone cofactor
- **Also known as:** Menaquinone (MK-n, where n = number of isoprenoid side chain units)
- **Vitamin group:** Fat-soluble (A, D, E, K)
- **Chemical class:** Menaquinones — a family of related compounds distinguished by the length of their isoprenoid side chain
- **Primary subtypes:**
  - **MK-4 (menaquinone-4):** Short-chain form, found in animal foods, rapid absorption, shorter half-life (~1-2 hours), tissue-specific effects
  - **MK-7 (menaquinone-7):** Long-chain form, primarily from bacterial fermentation (natto), longer half-life (~72 hours), most effective at sustained blood levels and most studied for supplementation
  - **MK-8, MK-9, MK-10:** Found in fermented cheeses, less studied, likely contribute to dietary K2 intake
- **Distinct from K1 (phylloquinone):** Vitamin K1 is found in green leafy vegetables and primarily supports blood coagulation. K2 (menaquinone) has distinct roles in calcium metabolism, bone health, and cardiovascular protection that K1 does not efficiently perform.
- **Essential nutrient:** Yes — required for activation of vitamin K-dependent proteins including osteocalcin (bone) and matrix Gla protein (arteries)
- **Endogenous production:** Limited — gut bacteria produce some MK-7 through MK-13, but absorption from the colon is poor and insufficient to meet physiological needs
