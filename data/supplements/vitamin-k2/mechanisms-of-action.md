# Vitamin K2
## Mechanisms of Action

- **Gamma-carboxylation of vitamin K-dependent proteins (VKDPs):** Vitamin K2 serves as an essential cofactor for the enzyme gamma-glutamyl carboxylase (GGCX). This enzyme adds a carboxyl group to specific glutamic acid residues in VKDPs, converting them from inactive (undercarboxylated) to active (carboxylated) forms. Without K2, these proteins remain inactive and cannot perform their calcium-regulating functions.
- **Osteocalcin activation (bone metabolism):** K2 carboxylates osteocalcin, a protein produced by osteoblasts. Carboxylated osteocalcin (cOC) has high affinity for hydroxyapatite (the calcium-phosphate mineral in bone) and directs calcium deposition into the bone matrix. Undercarboxylated osteocalcin (ucOC) cannot bind calcium effectively, leading to reduced bone mineralization. Elevated ucOC is a marker of K2 insufficiency and an independent predictor of fracture risk.
- **Matrix Gla protein (MGP) activation (vascular protection):** K2 carboxylates MGP, the body's most potent inhibitor of vascular calcification. Carboxylated MGP binds calcium crystals forming in arterial walls and prevents their growth. Dephospho-uncarboxylated MGP (dp-ucMGP) — the inactive form — is a strong predictor of cardiovascular disease and mortality. Without K2, MGP remains inactive and arteries calcify progressively.
- **Vitamin K cycle (recycling):** After K2 donates electrons during carboxylation, it is oxidized to vitamin K epoxide. The enzyme vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKOR) recycles it back to the active reduced form. This cycle allows a small amount of K2 to carboxylate many proteins. Warfarin blocks VKOR, which is why it inhibits both coagulation (K1-dependent) and calcium regulation (K2-dependent).
- **Anti-inflammatory effects:** K2 (particularly MK-7) reduces inflammatory markers including IL-6, TNF-alpha, and C-reactive protein (CRP). It inhibits NF-kB signaling in vascular smooth muscle cells, reducing inflammation-driven calcification. MK-4 also suppresses inflammatory gene expression through interaction with the steroid and xenobiotic receptor (SXR/PXR).
- **Mitochondrial electron carrier (MK-4):** MK-4 can function as a mitochondrial electron carrier, supporting ATP production and protecting against oxidative stress. This may explain MK-4's tissue-specific effects in the brain and other high-energy organs.
- **Gene expression regulation (MK-4):** MK-4 acts as a ligand for the steroid and xenobiotic receptor (SXR/PXR), a nuclear receptor that regulates genes involved in bone metabolism, inflammation, and xenobiotic detoxification. This is a unique mechanism not shared by MK-7.
- **Insulin sensitivity:** K2 may improve insulin sensitivity through osteocalcin signaling. Carboxylated osteocalcin (activated by K2) stimulates beta-cell proliferation and insulin secretion in the pancreas, and increases adiponectin expression in fat cells, improving glucose metabolism.
- **Testosterone and reproductive function (MK-4):** MK-4 is concentrated in the testes and supports steroidogenesis. Animal studies show MK-4 stimulates testosterone production through SXR/PXR activation and CYP11A1 enzyme upregulation. MK-4 supplementation increased testosterone levels in animal models.
- **Synergy with vitamin D3:** Vitamin D3 upregulates production of both osteocalcin and MGP. However, these proteins are produced in their inactive (undercarboxylated) forms. K2 is required to carboxylate and activate them. D3 increases the demand for K2 — supplementing D3 without K2 increases the pool of inactive calcium-regulatory proteins, which is counterproductive.
