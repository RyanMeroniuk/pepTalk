# Vitamin K2
## Drug Interactions and Contraindications

### CRITICAL INTERACTION: Warfarin (Coumadin)
- **Severity:** Major — potentially life-threatening
- **Mechanism:** Warfarin works by inhibiting vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKOR), the enzyme that recycles vitamin K. Supplementing K2 provides additional substrate for VKOR, directly opposing warfarin's anticoagulant effect. This can reduce INR, increase clotting risk, and potentially cause thromboembolic events (stroke, pulmonary embolism, deep vein thrombosis).
- **Recommendation:** Do NOT start, stop, or change K2 supplementation without physician approval and INR monitoring if on warfarin. If K2 is used, the dose must be consistent daily (never vary) and INR must be checked frequently to adjust warfarin dosing.
- **The warfarin paradox:** Warfarin prevents blood clots (beneficial) but also inactivates K2-dependent proteins in bone and arteries (harmful). Long-term warfarin use is associated with accelerated arterial calcification (50-60% higher CAC scores) and increased osteoporosis and fracture risk. This is a recognized "off-target" effect of warfarin.
- **Transitioning to DOACs:** Patients switching from warfarin to direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) can typically begin K2 supplementation, as DOACs do not work through the vitamin K pathway. Discuss with cardiologist.

### Does NOT Interact With: Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs)
- **Apixaban (Eliquis):** Factor Xa inhibitor — does not involve vitamin K pathway. K2 supplementation is safe.
- **Rivaroxaban (Xarelto):** Factor Xa inhibitor — no vitamin K interaction. K2 safe.
- **Dabigatran (Pradaxa):** Direct thrombin inhibitor — no vitamin K interaction. K2 safe.
- **Edoxaban (Savaysa/Lixiana):** Factor Xa inhibitor — no vitamin K interaction. K2 safe.
- **Clinical significance:** The safety of K2 with DOACs is one of the advantages of newer anticoagulants over warfarin. Patients on DOACs can and should optimize K2 status for bone and cardiovascular health.

### Other Anticoagulant and Antiplatelet Medications
- **Heparin and low-molecular-weight heparins (enoxaparin/Lovenox):** Do not work through vitamin K. K2 does not interfere with their anticoagulant activity. K2 supplementation is generally safe.
- **Aspirin (low-dose, 81-325 mg):** Works through COX-1 platelet inhibition, not vitamin K. No meaningful interaction with K2. Safe to use together.
- **Clopidogrel (Plavix):** P2Y12 platelet receptor blocker — no vitamin K interaction. K2 safe.
- **High-dose aspirin (>3g/day):** Very high doses may have mild vitamin K-antagonizing effects, but this is clinically uncommon.

### Medications That Reduce K2 Levels
- **Broad-spectrum antibiotics (fluoroquinolones, cephalosporins, penicillins):** Destroy gut bacteria that produce some K2 forms. Extended antibiotic courses can reduce K2 status. Supplement K2 during and after antibiotic therapy.
- **Cephalosporins with NMTT side chain (cefotetan, cefoperazone, moxalactam):** These specific antibiotics directly inhibit vitamin K epoxide reductase (same mechanism as warfarin), potentially causing vitamin K-dependent coagulopathy. K2 supplementation may actually be beneficial during use of these antibiotics (under physician guidance).
- **Cholestyramine (Questran) and colesevelam (Welchol):** Bile acid sequestrants that bind fat-soluble vitamins in the gut. Significantly reduce K2 absorption. Take K2 at least 4-6 hours before or after these medications.
- **Orlistat (Xenical/Alli):** Lipase inhibitor that blocks fat absorption, reducing K2 uptake by ~30%. Take K2 at least 2 hours before or after orlistat.
- **Mineral oil laxatives:** Dissolve and remove fat-soluble vitamins from the gut. Separate K2 supplementation by 4-6 hours.

### Medications Potentially Affected by K2
- **Calcitonin (Miacalcin):** Both calcitonin and K2 influence calcium metabolism, but through different mechanisms. No adverse interaction expected; may be complementary for osteoporosis.
- **Bisphosphonates (alendronate/Fosamax, risedronate/Actonel, zoledronic acid/Reclast):** K2 and bisphosphonates work through different mechanisms — bisphosphonates inhibit osteoclast (bone-resorbing) activity while K2 activates osteocalcin (bone-building). They are potentially synergistic. No adverse interaction. Some studies suggest combination therapy improves bone density more than either alone.
- **Denosumab (Prolia):** RANKL inhibitor for osteoporosis. No K2 interaction. May be complementary.
- **Hormone replacement therapy (HRT):** Estrogen supports bone density; K2 supports calcium deposition into bone. Complementary mechanisms with no adverse interaction.
- **Statins:** Emerging evidence suggests statins may interfere with menaquinone (K2) synthesis. K2 supplementation may be particularly beneficial for statin users. No adverse drug interaction.

### Contraindications
- **Unstable anticoagulation on warfarin:** If INR is difficult to control, adding K2 supplementation further complicates management. Stabilize anticoagulation first.
- **Acute thrombotic event while on warfarin:** Do not add K2 during active treatment of acute thrombosis with warfarin.

### Cautions (NOT Contraindications)
- **Thrombophilia (Factor V Leiden, prothrombin mutation):** K2 does not cause excessive clotting in healthy individuals, and clinical evidence does not show increased thrombotic risk with K2 supplementation. However, consult hematologist as a precaution.
- **Mechanical heart valves on warfarin:** These patients require strict INR control. K2 supplementation is generally not recommended due to the critical importance of consistent anticoagulation in this population.
- **Prosthetic heart valves on DOACs:** K2 supplementation is generally safe since DOACs do not involve the vitamin K pathway.

### Safe Combinations
- **Vitamin D3:** Not only safe but strongly recommended — the D3+K2 pairing is foundational
- **Calcium (from food or supplements):** K2 directs this calcium to bones — this is a beneficial interaction
- **Magnesium:** Safe and recommended cofactor for D3-K2 axis
- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** Safe; fat content may enhance K2 absorption
- **Vitamin A (retinol, at reasonable doses):** Works synergistically with D3 and K2 for fat-soluble vitamin balance. Keep A, D, and K2 in proportion.
- **Iron supplements:** No interaction
- **B-complex:** No interaction
- **Most common supplements and medications** (outside of warfarin) have no concerning interactions with vitamin K2
