# Calcium Alpha-Ketoglutarate (Ca-AKG)
## Overview

Alpha-ketoglutarate (AKG) is a central intermediate in the TCA cycle (Krebs cycle), the core metabolic pathway that produces cellular energy. Beyond energy, AKG is a required substrate or cofactor for a large family of enzymes — including those that hydroxylate collagen (supporting connective tissue) and those that regulate DNA and histone methylation (influencing the epigenome). AKG levels decline with age, and it has emerged as a longevity candidate after a 2020 mouse study (Shahmirzadi et al.) showed that calcium AKG (Ca-AKG) extended healthspan and modestly extended lifespan, compressing the period of late-life morbidity. A single small, uncontrolled human study using a Ca-AKG-based formulation (the "Rejuvant" study, Demidenko et al., 2021) reported a reduction in biological age by DNA-methylation clock — an intriguing but preliminary finding that needs replication in controlled trials. Ca-AKG is the supplemental form of choice (the calcium salt is more stable than the free acid) and is a notable ingredient in Bryan Johnson's Blueprint protocol.

### Why It Matters

- **Energy metabolism:** As a TCA-cycle intermediate, AKG sits at the heart of cellular energy production and links carbohydrate, fat, and amino-acid metabolism.
- **Collagen synthesis:** AKG is a required co-substrate for prolyl and lysyl hydroxylase enzymes that build stable collagen, relevant to skin, bone, and connective tissue (and a natural pairing with collagen-peptides).
- **Epigenetic regulation:** AKG is a co-substrate for TET and JmjC-domain enzymes that modify DNA/histone methylation, tying it to epigenetic-clock biology and the longevity rationale.
- **Longevity evidence (preliminary):** Mouse healthspan/lifespan data plus one small uncontrolled human biological-age study — promising, but not yet confirmed by controlled human trials.
- **Bone/calcium contribution:** The calcium salt also delivers some calcium, of minor nutritional note.

### Who Might Be Interested

- **Longevity-focused users** — interested in metabolic and epigenetic aging (with the caveat that human data is one small uncontrolled study)
- **People focused on connective tissue / skin / bone** — given AKG's role in collagen synthesis (often paired with collagen-peptides)
- **Those building a metabolic longevity stack** — alongside NMN and other healthy-aging compounds
