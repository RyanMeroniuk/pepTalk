# Calcium Alpha-Ketoglutarate (Ca-AKG)
## Natural Food Sources

Alpha-ketoglutarate is produced inside the body as a TCA-cycle intermediate, so it is not primarily a "dietary" nutrient in the way vitamins are. Dietary contributions come indirectly, through nutrients and amino acids that the body converts into AKG.

### No Significant Direct Food Source
- **AKG is not concentrated in foods as a free compound in meaningful amounts** — unlike spermidine (wheat germ) or quercetin (onions), you cannot meaningfully "eat" supplemental-level AKG directly
- The body generates AKG endogenously from glucose metabolism and from amino acids (especially glutamate/glutamine) via the TCA cycle and transamination

### Foods That Support Endogenous AKG Production
- **Glutamine/glutamate-rich proteins** — meat, fish, eggs, dairy, and legumes provide glutamine and glutamate, which transaminate into AKG
- **Bone broth and collagen-rich foods** — provide amino acids that feed the same metabolic pool and complement AKG's collagen-cofactor role
- **Vegetables and whole foods** — supply the substrates (carbohydrates, amino acids) the TCA cycle uses to generate AKG

### Cofactor Foods for AKG-Dependent Enzymes
- **Vitamin C-rich foods (citrus, peppers, kiwi, strawberries)** — vitamin C works alongside AKG as a cofactor for collagen-building prolyl/lysyl hydroxylases
- **Iron-containing foods** — these dioxygenase enzymes are iron-dependent, so adequate (not excessive) iron status supports their function

### Important Context
- **Endogenous AKG production declines with age**, which is part of the longevity rationale for supplementation
- **Diet cannot realistically deliver supplemental-level AKG directly** — the 1-2 g/day used in studies is achieved through supplementation, not food
- **A protein-adequate, whole-food diet supports the body's own AKG production** and supplies the cofactors (vitamin C, iron) that AKG-dependent enzymes need
- **Supplementation (as Ca-AKG)** is the practical route to research-level intake
