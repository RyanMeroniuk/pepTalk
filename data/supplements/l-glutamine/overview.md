# L-Glutamine
## Overview

L-Glutamine is the most abundant free amino acid in the human body, serving as a critical fuel source for rapidly dividing cells — particularly enterocytes (intestinal lining cells), immune cells (lymphocytes, macrophages), and fibroblasts involved in wound healing.

### Why It Matters

- **Gut barrier integrity:** Glutamine is the primary metabolic fuel for enterocytes. Without adequate supply, the intestinal lining breaks down, leading to increased intestinal permeability ("leaky gut"), endotoxemia, and systemic inflammation.
- **Immune function:** Lymphocytes and macrophages are highly dependent on glutamine for proliferation and cytokine production. Plasma glutamine drops significantly during critical illness and intense training, correlating with immune suppression.
- **Muscle preservation:** As the most abundant amino acid in muscle, glutamine plays a role in nitrogen transport, anti-catabolism, and glycogen replenishment.
- **Nitrogen shuttle:** Glutamine carries nitrogen between tissues, acting as a non-toxic ammonia transporter — critical for acid-base balance in the kidneys.

### Who Benefits Most

- **People with gut issues:** IBS, IBD (Crohn's, ulcerative colitis), leaky gut, SIBO, post-antibiotic gut repair
- **Athletes and heavy trainers:** Prevents exercise-induced immune suppression, supports recovery
- **Post-surgical or critically ill patients:** Demand skyrockets during physiological stress
- **Anyone on peptides for gut healing:** Synergizes powerfully with BPC-157 and KPV
- **People under chronic stress:** Cortisol depletes glutamine stores rapidly
- **Those with sugar/alcohol cravings:** Glutamine can reduce cravings by serving as an alternative fuel for the brain
