# L-Glutamine
## Foods to Avoid

### Foods That Undermine Glutamine's Gut-Healing Effects
- **Alcohol** — directly damages the intestinal lining and depletes glutamine stores; counterproductive to any gut-healing protocol. Even moderate alcohol consumption increases intestinal permeability.
- **Refined sugar (high amounts)** — feeds pathogenic gut bacteria and yeast (Candida), promotes inflammation, and undermines gut barrier repair. Small amounts of glucose enhance absorption, but high-sugar meals negate the benefits.
- **Highly processed foods** — emulsifiers (polysorbate 80, carboxymethylcellulose), artificial sweeteners, and preservatives directly damage gut barrier integrity
- **Artificial sweeteners (sucralose, aspartame)** — shown to disrupt gut microbiome composition and increase intestinal permeability in some studies
- **Gluten (if sensitive)** — triggers zonulin release, which opens tight junctions — directly opposes glutamine's tight junction-strengthening effect. Relevant for celiac, NCGS, and active gut-healing protocols.
- **Dairy (if intolerant)** — casein and lactose can provoke inflammation in sensitive individuals, counteracting gut repair

### Foods That Interfere with Glutamine Metabolism
- **Excessive protein in a single meal** — amino acids compete for the same intestinal transporters. Taking glutamine with a very large protein meal (50+ g) may reduce its preferential uptake. Take glutamine between meals for gut-targeted delivery.
- **Very hot foods/beverages** — glutamine degrades rapidly in heat. Never mix glutamine powder into hot soup, coffee, or tea. Let liquids cool to room temperature first.

### Timing Conflicts
- **High-fat meals** — slow gastric emptying, which delays glutamine reaching the small intestine. For gut-healing, take glutamine on an empty stomach or with a light, low-fat snack.
- **Coffee on an empty stomach** — coffee increases gastric acid and cortisol, which can increase glutamine demand. If taking glutamine in the morning, take it first, wait 15-20 minutes, then have coffee.

### Foods That Deplete Glutamine
- **High-sugar diet overall** — chronic high sugar intake promotes systemic inflammation, increasing glutamine consumption by immune cells and leaving less for gut repair
- **Excessive alcohol** — liver uses glutamine to process alcohol metabolites; chronic drinking creates a persistent glutamine deficit
