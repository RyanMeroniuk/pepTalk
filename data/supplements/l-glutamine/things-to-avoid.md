# L-Glutamine
## Things to Avoid

### Behaviors That Deplete Glutamine
- **Overtraining without recovery** — intense exercise depletes plasma glutamine by 20-30%. Training hard without adequate rest creates a chronic glutamine deficit that suppresses immunity and slows gut repair.
- **Chronic psychological stress** — cortisol catabolizes muscle tissue to liberate glutamine for immune and gut cells. Unmanaged stress = constant glutamine drain.
- **Sleep deprivation** — poor sleep elevates cortisol and inflammatory cytokines, both of which increase glutamine consumption. Undermines any gut-healing protocol.
- **Fasting too aggressively** — prolonged fasting depletes glutamine stores as the body uses it for gluconeogenesis. If doing extended fasts, consider supplementing glutamine.

### Substances That Interfere
- **NSAIDs (chronic use)** — ibuprofen, aspirin, naproxen directly damage the gut lining. Glutamine can help mitigate this, but chronic NSAID use overwhelms the protective effect. If you must use NSAIDs, co-supplement with glutamine.
- **Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)** — omeprazole, lansoprazole, etc. alter gut pH and microbiome composition, reducing the environment in which glutamine works best
- **Antibiotics (broad-spectrum)** — destroy beneficial gut bacteria that work synergistically with glutamine to maintain barrier function. If on antibiotics, increase glutamine dose and add probiotics (spaced 2 hours apart).
- **Excessive alcohol** — directly toxic to enterocytes and depletes glutamine stores. Avoid entirely during gut-healing protocols.
- **Corticosteroids (chronic use)** — prednisone, dexamethasone, etc. increase protein catabolism and glutamine consumption while suppressing gut mucosal immunity

### Supplement Conflicts
- **Mixing glutamine in hot liquids** — heat rapidly degrades glutamine into pyroglutamic acid and ammonia. Always use cool or room-temperature water.
- **Pre-mixing glutamine and leaving it** — glutamine degrades in solution within hours. Always mix fresh and drink immediately.
- **Taking glutamine with massive protein doses** — amino acid transporter competition reduces preferential glutamine uptake. For gut-targeted delivery, take between meals.

### Lifestyle Factors
- **Smoking** — increases oxidative stress and systemic inflammation, draining glutamine for antioxidant defense (glutathione production)
- **Excessive endurance exercise without fueling** — running or cycling for hours on empty depletes glutamine and suppresses immunity for up to 72 hours post-exercise ("open window" theory)
