# L-Glutamine
## Lifestyle Recommendations

### Optimize Glutamine's Gut-Healing Effects
- **Eat an anti-inflammatory diet** — whole foods, vegetables, healthy fats, clean protein. The less inflammatory burden on the gut, the more glutamine can go toward repair rather than defense.
- **Include bone broth regularly** — 1-2 cups/day provides glutamine, glycine, collagen, and gelatin in a synergistic matrix. Homemade is ideal (simmer bones 12-24 hours).
- **Chew food thoroughly** — mechanical digestion reduces the burden on the gut lining and improves nutrient absorption. 20-30 chews per bite.
- **Manage meal spacing** — give the gut 3-4 hours between meals for the migrating motor complex (MMC) to sweep debris. Constant grazing impairs gut cleanup.

### Stress Management (Critical for Glutamine Conservation)
- **Practice daily stress reduction** — meditation, breathwork (4-7-8 breathing, box breathing), yoga, or nature walks. Cortisol is the number one glutamine thief.
- **Cold exposure (controlled)** — cold showers or ice baths reduce systemic inflammation, preserving glutamine for repair. Start with 30 seconds of cold water at end of shower.
- **Prioritize social connection** — loneliness and social isolation elevate cortisol and inflammatory markers

### Sleep Optimization
- **Get 7-9 hours of quality sleep** — gut repair happens primarily during sleep. Growth hormone release during deep sleep supports mucosal regeneration.
- **Take evening glutamine dose 30 min before bed** — supports overnight gut repair and can promote relaxation via GABA conversion
- **Maintain consistent sleep/wake times** — circadian rhythm regularity supports gut motility and repair cycles

### Exercise Guidelines
- **Moderate exercise supports glutamine** — 30-60 minutes of moderate activity increases circulation and nutrient delivery to the gut
- **Avoid overtraining** — more than 90 minutes of intense exercise without fueling depletes plasma glutamine by 20-30%
- **Supplement around training** — 5 g pre-workout + 5 g post-workout prevents exercise-induced glutamine depletion
- **Include rest days** — glutamine replenishment and gut repair require recovery time

### Hydration
- **Drink adequate water** — dehydration reduces blood flow to the gut and impairs nutrient delivery. Aim for 0.5 oz per pound of body weight daily.
- **Add electrolytes** — sodium enhances glutamine transport. A pinch of sea salt in water or an electrolyte supplement supports uptake.

### Gut-Specific Habits
- **Take probiotics** — beneficial bacteria and glutamine work synergistically for gut barrier maintenance. Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium strains are most studied.
- **Eat prebiotic-rich foods** — garlic, onions, leeks, asparagus, green bananas feed beneficial bacteria
- **Minimize antibiotic use** — only when truly necessary; always co-supplement with glutamine and probiotics if antibiotics are required
- **Avoid eating when stressed** — the sympathetic ("fight or flight") state diverts blood away from the gut, impairing digestion and glutamine utilization. Eat in a calm, parasympathetic state.
