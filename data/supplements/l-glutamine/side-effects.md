# L-Glutamine
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild)
- **Bloating or gas** — typically transient, occurs when starting high doses; usually resolves within 3-5 days
- **Mild GI discomfort** — more common at doses above 15 g in a single sitting; splitting doses eliminates this for most people
- **Constipation** — occasional reports at higher doses; increase water intake

### Uncommon
- **Headache** — rare, possibly related to glutamate conversion in sensitive individuals
- **Dizziness** — very rare at standard doses
- **Mild nausea** — typically only with very high single doses (15+ g at once)

### The Cancer Concern (Important Context)
- **The concern:** Glutamine is avidly consumed by rapidly dividing cells, including cancer cells. Many cancers upregulate glutamine transporters and become "glutamine-addicted" (a phenomenon called glutamine dependency or glutaminolysis).
- **The nuance:** This does NOT mean supplemental glutamine causes cancer. There is no clinical evidence that glutamine supplementation increases cancer risk in healthy individuals.
- **What the research actually shows:**
  - Cancer cells will scavenge glutamine from muscle and plasma regardless of supplementation
  - Depleting glutamine harms healthy immune cells (which also need glutamine to fight cancer) faster than it starves tumors
  - Several oncology studies have used glutamine supplementation (30 g/day) to reduce chemotherapy side effects (mucositis, neuropathy) without worsening cancer outcomes
- **Practical guidance:** If you have an active cancer diagnosis, discuss glutamine supplementation with your oncologist. For healthy individuals and those in remission, standard supplemental doses are not a concern.

### Populations That Should Use Caution
- **Active cancer patients** — discuss with oncologist (see above)
- **Hepatic encephalopathy / severe liver disease** — glutamine metabolism produces ammonia; impaired liver function may lead to ammonia accumulation
- **Reye's syndrome** — avoid due to ammonia metabolism concerns
- **Mania / bipolar disorder** — theoretical concern that glutamate conversion could exacerbate excitatory neurotransmission; limited evidence but worth noting
- **MSG sensitivity** — glutamine converts to glutamate; those with severe MSG reactions should start with low doses and monitor

### Safety Profile
- **Generally Recognized as Safe (GRAS)** at doses up to 40 g/day in clinical studies
- No kidney toxicity at standard supplemental doses in healthy individuals
- No liver toxicity at standard supplemental doses in individuals with normal liver function
- Safe for long-term use (years) at maintenance doses (5-10 g/day)
