# L-Glutamine
## Forms and Bioavailability

### L-Glutamine (Free Form Powder)
- **Most common and cost-effective form**
- Mixes easily in water (tasteless to mildly sweet)
- Well-absorbed orally — ~30% is consumed by the gut on first pass (this is actually a feature for gut-healing protocols, not a bug)
- **Unstable in liquid** — decomposes to pyroglutamic acid and ammonia in solution over time; mix and drink immediately
- Best for: gut healing, general supplementation, high-dose protocols

### L-Glutamine Capsules
- **Convenient but impractical at therapeutic doses** — you'd need 10-20 capsules to hit 5-10 g
- Typical capsule: 500 mg - 1000 mg per cap
- Best for: low-dose maintenance (1-2 g/day), travel

### L-Alanyl-L-Glutamine (Sustamine)
- **Dipeptide form** — glutamine bonded to alanine
- **More stable in solution** than free-form glutamine; doesn't degrade as quickly
- **Better absorbed** in some studies — higher plasma glutamine levels per gram vs free-form
- ~35% more bioavailable than free-form L-glutamine in hydration studies
- **More expensive** per gram of glutamine equivalent
- Best for: intra-workout hydration, athletic performance

### Glutamine Peptides (from hydrolyzed wheat or whey)
- Glutamine bound within short peptide chains
- Better stability than free-form
- May contain gluten traces if derived from wheat — **not suitable for celiac or gluten-sensitive individuals**
- Absorption is comparable to free-form
- Best for: people who want moderate stability without the Sustamine price premium

### Key Bioavailability Notes
- **First-pass gut extraction is high** (~30-35%) — enterocytes consume glutamine avidly, which means oral dosing is ideal for gut-healing purposes
- **For systemic effects** (muscle, immune), higher doses are needed to compensate for gut extraction
- **Empty stomach absorption** is fastest, but glutamine is well-absorbed with food too
- **Heat degrades glutamine** — do not add to hot beverages or cook with it
