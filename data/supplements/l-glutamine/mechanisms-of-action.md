# L-Glutamine
## Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Enterocyte Fuel & Gut Barrier Maintenance
- **Primary energy substrate** for intestinal epithelial cells (enterocytes), more important than glucose for these cells
- Maintains tight junction protein expression (claudin-1, occludin, ZO-1), preventing paracellular permeability
- Stimulates intestinal cell proliferation via mTOR and MAPK signaling pathways
- Activates heat shock protein (HSP70) expression, which protects gut lining from stress-induced damage

### 2. Immune Cell Metabolism
- **Lymphocytes and macrophages** use glutamine at rates comparable to or exceeding glucose utilization
- Required for nucleotide synthesis (purines and pyrimidines) in rapidly dividing immune cells
- Fuels the hexosamine biosynthetic pathway, essential for immune cell activation and cytokine production
- Supports T-cell proliferation and B-cell antibody production

### 3. Nitrogen Transport & Ammonia Detoxification
- Carries ammonia (as a non-toxic amide group) from peripheral tissues to the liver and kidneys
- In the kidney, glutaminase cleaves glutamine to glutamate + NH4+, which is excreted — critical for acid-base homeostasis
- In the liver, feeds into the urea cycle for ammonia disposal

### 4. Glutathione Precursor
- Glutamine is converted to glutamate, which is one of the three amino acids in glutathione (glutamate + cysteine + glycine)
- Supports the body's master antioxidant system, particularly under oxidative stress

### 5. Gluconeogenic Substrate
- Can be converted to alpha-ketoglutarate and enter the TCA cycle
- Serves as a gluconeogenic precursor, contributing to blood glucose maintenance during fasting or intense exercise
- This is why glutamine can reduce sugar cravings — it provides an alternative energy substrate for the brain

### 6. mTOR Signaling & Muscle Protein Synthesis
- Activates mTOR pathway in intestinal and muscle cells
- While not as potent as leucine for muscle protein synthesis, glutamine supports the anabolic environment by preventing catabolism and maintaining nitrogen balance

### 7. GABA Precursor (Neurological)
- Glutamine crosses the blood-brain barrier and is converted to glutamate (excitatory) or GABA (inhibitory) depending on tissue needs
- This dual role supports both cognitive function and calm/relaxation
