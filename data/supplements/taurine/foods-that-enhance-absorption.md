# Taurine
## Foods That Enhance Absorption

### The Reality: Taurine Is Already Well Absorbed
- **Taurine has high oral bioavailability on its own** — it is absorbed efficiently via the TauT transporter regardless of food. There is no strong need to "stack" it with anything to absorb it.
- **The main optimization is avoiding transporter saturation** — splitting large doses (3+ g) does more for retention than any food pairing

### Cysteine and Methionine (Precursor Support)
- **Taurine is synthesized from cysteine** (which derives from methionine)
- **Adequate dietary sulfur amino acids** (eggs, poultry, fish, legumes for cysteine/methionine) support endogenous taurine production
- **Vitamin B6 (pyridoxal-5-phosphate)** is a cofactor for the enzyme (CSAD) that converts cysteinesulfinic acid to taurine — adequate B6 supports synthesis
- **Foods rich in B6:** poultry, fish, potatoes, bananas, chickpeas

### Sodium Context
- **The TauT transporter is sodium- and chloride-dependent** — normal dietary sodium is more than sufficient; no need to add salt
- This is the same transport family used by other osmolytes

### Practical Pairings
- **With or without food** — both work. Taking with food reduces the rare GI upset.
- **With a meal containing protein** — supplies cysteine/methionine for ongoing endogenous synthesis, supporting overall taurine status
- **Evening dosing** — pairs well with magnesium and glycine for a calm/sleep stack (see peptide-synergies), leveraging taurine's mild GABAergic effect

### What Doesn't Matter
- **Hot vs cold liquid** — taurine is stable and water-soluble
- **Acidic beverages** — do not degrade taurine
- **Fat content of the meal** — irrelevant to taurine absorption (it is water-soluble)

### Bottom Line
- **Don't overthink it.** Taurine absorbs well on its own. Ensure adequate protein and B6 for endogenous synthesis, split large doses, and take it consistently.
