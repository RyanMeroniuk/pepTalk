# Taurine
## Drug Interactions

### Moderate Interactions (Use with Caution / Monitor)
- **Antihypertensives (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers, beta-blockers, diuretics)** — taurine independently lowers blood pressure. Combining with blood-pressure medications can have an additive effect, potentially causing hypotension. Monitor blood pressure and consult your physician before combining.
- **Insulin and oral hypoglycemics (metformin, sulfonylureas)** — taurine improves insulin sensitivity and can lower blood glucose. Combined use may increase the risk of hypoglycemia; monitor blood glucose closely.
- **Lithium** — taurine may have mild diuretic-like effects on the kidney, which could theoretically alter lithium clearance and blood levels. Use caution and monitor lithium levels with medical supervision.

### Mild Interactions (Monitor)
- **CNS depressants / sedatives (benzodiazepines, GABAergic agents)** — taurine has weak GABA-A and glycine receptor activity; theoretically additive calming effects. Clinically minor at supplement doses, but be aware if combining with sedatives.
- **Anticoagulants / blood thinners (warfarin)** — no well-established direct interaction, but as with any supplement, inform your physician; monitor if combining.

### Timing-Sensitive Notes
- **Separate from medications with narrow therapeutic windows when uncertain** — taking taurine a few hours apart from drugs like lithium or levothyroxine avoids theoretical absorption or clearance concerns, though no strong taurine-specific timing conflict is documented
- **Levothyroxine / thyroid meds** — no direct interaction documented; general best practice is to take thyroid medication on an empty stomach separate from other supplements, so keep taurine to a different time of day

### Positive / Neutral Interactions
- **Magnesium** — complementary cardiovascular and calming effects; magnesium taurate combines both
- **Caffeine** — no harmful interaction; taurine's calming profile may smooth caffeine's stimulant edge (the basis for their pairing in energy products)
- **Creatine** — no interaction; complementary performance support

### Population Cautions
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — taurine is essential in development and present in breast milk, but supplemental high-dose safety data are limited; consult an OB/GYN
- **Kidney disease** — the kidneys regulate taurine excretion; impaired renal function warrants medical guidance
- **Liver disease** — taurine is involved in bile acid conjugation; generally considered supportive, but discuss with a hepatologist in significant liver disease
- **Bipolar disorder** — caution given energy-drink case reports (confounded by caffeine); discuss with prescriber

### Safety Notes
- **Always inform healthcare providers** of taurine supplementation, especially if taking cardiovascular, diabetes, lithium, or sedative medications
- **This is educational information, not medical advice** — medication interactions should be reviewed with a pharmacist or physician
