# Taurine
## Benefits

### Cardiovascular (Strong Evidence)
- **Lowers blood pressure** — meta-analyses (e.g., Waldron et al., 2018) report reductions of ~3 mmHg systolic and ~1.5 mmHg diastolic, with larger effects in hypertensive individuals at doses of 1-3 g/day
- **Supports cardiac function** — improves contractility and exercise tolerance in heart failure patients in some trials; taurine is highly concentrated in heart tissue
- **Improves endothelial function** — enhances nitric oxide availability and vascular reactivity
- **Reduces sympathetic overactivity** — lowers resting heart rate and sympathetic tone

### Metabolic (Good Evidence)
- **Lowers fasting glucose and improves insulin sensitivity** — particularly in people with type 2 diabetes or metabolic syndrome
- **Reduces triglycerides** — consistent triglyceride-lowering effect across several trials, partly via enhanced bile acid conjugation
- **May reduce total and LDL cholesterol** — modest effects in some populations
- **Supports beta-cell protection** — reduces oxidative stress on pancreatic islets in preclinical models

### Exercise & Performance (Moderate Evidence)
- **Reduces exercise-induced muscle damage** — lower creatine kinase and lactate dehydrogenase in some studies
- **May improve endurance performance** — modest improvements in time-to-exhaustion at ~1-3 g doses
- **Reduces perceived fatigue and oxidative stress** post-exercise
- **Note:** effects are smaller and less reliable than creatine for strength/power; taurine is more of a supportive adjunct

### Nervous System
- **Mild calming/anxiolytic effect** — via weak GABA-A and glycine receptor agonism
- **Neuroprotection** — protects against excitotoxicity and supports neuronal osmoregulation in preclinical models
- **Retinal health** — taurine is essential for retinal photoreceptor function; deficiency causes retinal degeneration (well-documented in cats)

### Longevity & Aging (Contested — Read Carefully)
- **The 2023 *Cell* study** (Singh et al.) showed taurine supplementation extended lifespan and healthspan in mice and worms and that taurine levels decline with age across several species
- **The 2025 NIH study** (Fernández et al., *Science*) found that in humans, blood taurine did NOT reliably decrease with age and varied enormously between individuals — directly undercutting taurine as an aging biomarker
- **Honest summary:** the animal longevity data are intriguing but the human aging-biomarker claim is not supported. Do not take taurine expecting a proven anti-aging effect in humans. Take it for its well-established cardiometabolic benefits.

### Other
- **Hepatoprotection** — supports bile flow and may protect against fatty liver in preclinical and small human studies
- **Eye fatigue / vision support** — preliminary, based on taurine's retinal role
