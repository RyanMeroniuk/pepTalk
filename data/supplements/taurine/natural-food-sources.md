# Taurine
## Natural Food Sources

### Highest Concentration (Shellfish and Seafood)
- **Scallops** — ~800 mg per 100 g; among the richest dietary sources
- **Mussels** — ~650 mg per 100 g
- **Clams** — ~520 mg per 100 g
- **Octopus** — ~520 mg per 100 g
- **Tuna (dark meat)** — ~250-350 mg per 100 g
- **Mackerel** — ~200 mg per 100 g

### Good Sources (Meat and Poultry)
- **Dark chicken meat (thigh/leg)** — ~150-200 mg per 100 g
- **Turkey (dark meat)** — ~300 mg per 100 g
- **Beef** — ~40-80 mg per 100 g
- **Pork** — ~50-60 mg per 100 g
- **Lamb** — ~50 mg per 100 g

### Moderate Sources
- **Dairy** — small amounts; cow's milk ~6 mg per 100 mL (human breast milk is much higher, reflecting taurine's importance in development)
- **Eggs** — modest amounts

### Plant Foods
- **Essentially zero** — taurine is virtually absent from plant foods. This is why vegans and vegetarians have lower dietary intake and lower plasma taurine.

### Important Context
- **Typical omnivorous intake:** ~40-400 mg/day depending on diet (high seafood diets at the upper end)
- **Vegan/vegetarian intake:** Near zero from food; rely on endogenous synthesis from cysteine
- **Cooking method matters** — boiling leaches water-soluble taurine into the cooking liquid; broths and soups retain it, while discarded cooking water loses it
- **Cats vs humans** — cats cannot synthesize taurine at all and require dietary taurine (it is added to cat food); humans can synthesize it but at limited rates, making it conditionally essential

### Supplementation vs Food
- **To reach the studied 1-3 g/day** purely from food would require very large quantities of shellfish daily — impractical and expensive
- **Synthetic supplemental taurine is identical** to dietary taurine and vegan-friendly
- **Vegans/vegetarians especially** may benefit from supplementation given negligible dietary intake
