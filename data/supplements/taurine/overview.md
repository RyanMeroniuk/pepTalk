# Taurine
## Overview

Taurine is a conditionally essential sulfur-containing amino acid that is among the most abundant free amino acids in the human body, concentrated in the heart, muscle, brain, and retina. Unlike most amino acids, it is not used to build proteins — instead it acts as a cellular regulator, supporting osmoregulation (cell volume), calcium handling, membrane stabilization, antioxidant defense, and bile acid conjugation. Decades of research point to consistent, well-supported benefits for blood pressure, blood glucose, and triglycerides, plus a strong safety record. Taurine drew major attention from a 2023 study in the journal *Cell* (Singh et al.) that linked declining taurine levels to aging across multiple species and showed supplementation extended healthspan and lifespan in mice and worms — but this longevity angle should be read cautiously. A 2025 NIH-led human study (Fernández et al., *Science*) found that circulating taurine did NOT reliably decline with age in humans and varied widely between individuals, undercutting the idea that low taurine is a clean biomarker of aging. The cardiometabolic benefits remain solid; the anti-aging story is unsettled.

### Why It Matters

- **Cardiovascular support:** Meta-analyses show taurine supplementation modestly lowers both systolic and diastolic blood pressure and supports healthy cardiac contractility and calcium handling.
- **Glucose and lipids:** Improves insulin sensitivity and lowers fasting glucose and triglycerides in several human trials, particularly in people with metabolic dysfunction.
- **Cellular regulation:** Acts as an osmolyte (regulates cell volume), an antioxidant, and a membrane stabilizer — not as a protein building block.
- **Exercise:** May modestly reduce muscle damage markers and fatigue and support endurance, though effects are smaller than for creatine.
- **Safety:** Excellent tolerability across studies up to 3 g/day and beyond; the body readily excretes excess.

### Who Benefits Most

- **People with elevated blood pressure** — among the most consistent benefits in the literature
- **People with metabolic syndrome, prediabetes, or type 2 diabetes** — glucose and triglyceride improvements
- **Vegans and vegetarians** — have lower dietary intake (taurine is concentrated in meat, fish, and shellfish) and lower plasma levels
- **Athletes** — modest support for endurance and recovery
- **Those on the "longevity supplement" trend** — but should understand the human aging evidence is contested (see 2025 NIH study)
