# Melatonin
## Overview

Melatonin is the body's master circadian hormone, produced by the pineal gland in response to darkness. It serves as a **chronobiotic** — a timekeeper that signals to every cell in the body that it is nighttime — rather than a sedative. This distinction is critical and widely misunderstood.

**Why it matters:**

- **Circadian synchronization** — Melatonin does not force sleep. It opens the "sleep gate" by signaling to the brain and body that the biological night has begun. It shifts the timing of sleep onset and helps anchor the circadian clock.
- **Antioxidant powerhouse** — Melatonin is one of the most potent endogenous antioxidants, scavenging free radicals directly and upregulating antioxidant enzymes (superoxide dismutase, glutathione peroxidase, catalase).
- **Immune modulation** — Melatonin influences T-cell activity, cytokine production, and has documented anti-inflammatory effects.
- **Neuroprotection** — Crosses the blood-brain barrier freely and protects neurons from oxidative damage, excitotoxicity, and neuroinflammation.
- **Gut health** — The gut produces 400x more melatonin than the pineal gland. Enteric melatonin supports gut motility, mucosal integrity, and reduces gastric acid secretion.
- **Declining with age** — Endogenous melatonin production drops significantly with aging, contributing to disrupted sleep architecture and increased oxidative stress in older adults.

**The "more is not better" myth:**

Most commercial melatonin supplements are sold at 3mg, 5mg, or even 10mg doses. Research consistently shows that **physiological doses (0.3-1mg)** are more effective than supraphysiological doses for improving sleep onset and quality. Higher doses can cause next-day grogginess, vivid dreams or nightmares, desensitize melatonin receptors, and paradoxically disrupt sleep architecture. The goal is to mimic the body's natural melatonin pulse, not overwhelm the system.
