# Melatonin
## Natural Food Sources

- **Tart cherries (Montmorency variety)** — The richest commonly consumed food source of melatonin. Tart cherry juice concentrate contains approximately 13.5 ng/g of melatonin. Studies show drinking tart cherry juice increases urinary melatonin metabolites (6-sulfatoxymelatonin) and improves sleep duration by ~84 minutes.
- **Pistachios** — Exceptionally high melatonin content (233 mcg per gram), making them the highest known food source. Just two pistachios provide a physiologically relevant dose. However, the melatonin is bound within the food matrix and bioavailability is uncertain.
- **Walnuts** — Contain 3.5 ng/g of melatonin. Consumption increases blood melatonin levels and total antioxidant capacity.
- **Goji berries** — Contain melatonin and have been shown to improve sleep quality in traditional medicine research.
- **Tomatoes** — Contain measurable melatonin, with varieties and ripeness affecting concentration. Cherry tomatoes have higher concentrations.
- **Grapes (and red wine)** — Grape skins contain melatonin, particularly in Nebbiolo, Merlot, and Cabernet Sauvignon varieties. However, alcohol's sleep-disrupting effects far outweigh any melatonin benefit from wine.
- **Rice and barley** — Germinated seeds contain significant melatonin. Black rice and red rice have higher concentrations than white rice.
- **Mushrooms** — Several species contain melatonin, including Agaricus bisporus (white button mushroom) and various medicinal mushrooms.
- **Eggs** — Contain melatonin, particularly from hens exposed to longer dark periods. Pastured/free-range eggs may have higher levels.
- **Fish** — Salmon and sardines contain melatonin, with farm-raised salmon showing higher levels than wild-caught in some studies.
- **Milk (night-milked)** — Cows milked at night produce milk with significantly higher melatonin content ("night milk"). Commercially available in some countries.
- **Peppers** — Both sweet and hot peppers contain melatonin.
- **Corn** — Contains melatonin, with sweet corn varieties having the highest concentrations among cereal grains.
- **Oats** — Contain melatonin and melatonin precursor tryptophan.

**Melatonin precursor foods (tryptophan-rich):**

The body synthesizes melatonin from tryptophan via the pathway: Tryptophan -> 5-HTP -> Serotonin -> Melatonin. Foods high in tryptophan support endogenous melatonin production:

- **Turkey and chicken** — Classic tryptophan sources (~250-300 mg per 100g)
- **Pumpkin seeds** — 576 mg tryptophan per 100g
- **Spirulina** — 929 mg tryptophan per 100g (highest per weight)
- **Soy products (tofu, tempeh)** — ~230 mg per 100g
- **Cheese (Parmesan, cheddar)** — ~320 mg per 100g
- **Bananas** — Moderate tryptophan plus vitamin B6, which is a cofactor for serotonin-to-melatonin conversion
