# Melatonin
## Dosing Protocols

**Critical principle: Less is more. Physiological doses (0.3-1mg) outperform supraphysiological doses (3-10mg) for sleep quality in most people.**

- **General sleep onset support** — 0.3-0.5mg immediate-release, 30-60 minutes before desired bedtime. Start at 0.3mg and increase only if needed. This mimics the body's natural melatonin pulse without receptor desensitization.
- **Difficulty staying asleep** — 0.5-1mg extended-release, 30-60 minutes before bed. Or use a combination IR/ER product (e.g., 0.3mg IR + 0.5mg ER).
- **Jet lag (eastward travel)** — 0.5mg at the target destination bedtime, starting 1 day before travel and continuing for 3-5 days after arrival. For crossing 5+ time zones, some protocols use 1-3mg.
- **Jet lag (westward travel)** — 0.5mg at the destination bedtime. Less critical than eastward travel since the body adapts more easily to phase delays.
- **Shift work** — 0.5-1mg before the desired sleep period, combined with blackout curtains and light avoidance. Extended-release preferred for daytime sleeping.
- **Delayed sleep phase disorder (DSPD)** — 0.3-0.5mg taken 4-6 hours before the desired (earlier) bedtime. This leverages melatonin's phase-advance properties. Timing is more important than dose.
- **Children (under physician supervision only)** — 0.5-1mg, 30 minutes before bed. Common in autism spectrum and ADHD management. Must be supervised by a pediatrician.
- **Older adults (55+)** — 0.3-2mg extended-release. Endogenous production is significantly reduced with age, making supplementation more justifiable. The European-approved Circadin protocol is 2mg ER.
- **Antioxidant/neuroprotective use** — Higher doses (3-10mg) are used in clinical research contexts for neuroprotection, migraine prevention, and cancer adjunctive therapy. These are not standard sleep doses and should be discussed with a physician.

**Timing guidelines:**

- **Chronobiotic effect (clock shifting):** Take 4-6 hours before desired sleep onset
- **Acute sleep onset effect:** Take 30-60 minutes before bed
- **Consistency matters:** Take at the same time every night to reinforce the circadian signal
- **Darkness required:** Melatonin works synergistically with dim light exposure. Bright light after taking melatonin will suppress its effects.

**Signs you are taking too much:**

- Morning grogginess or "melatonin hangover"
- Vivid, disturbing, or unusually intense dreams
- Waking up at 2-3 AM (paradoxical waking from receptor desensitization)
- Daytime sleepiness
- Headaches upon waking
