# Melatonin
## Things to Avoid

- **Blue and green light exposure after sunset** — Light in the 450-520nm wavelength range (blue/green) is the most potent suppressor of melatonin production. Even brief exposure (10-15 minutes) to screens, overhead LED lighting, or fluorescent lights after dark can suppress melatonin by 50-80% and delay its onset by 1-3 hours. **Use blue-light-blocking glasses (amber/orange lenses), enable Night Shift/Night Light on devices, and switch to warm dim lighting (< 2700K, < 10 lux) 2 hours before bed.**
- **Bright overhead lighting at night** — Melanopsin-containing retinal ganglion cells that suppress melatonin are most sensitive to light from above. Overhead lighting is worse than table lamps or floor lamps at the same brightness. **Switch to low-positioned, dim, warm lighting in the evening.**
- **Late-night intense exercise** — Vigorous exercise raises core body temperature, cortisol, and norepinephrine, all of which oppose melatonin's effects. **Stop intense exercise 3-4 hours before bedtime.** Gentle stretching, yoga, or walking are fine.
- **Irregular sleep-wake schedules** — Melatonin is a clock signal. Varying bedtime and wake time by more than 30-60 minutes disrupts the circadian rhythm and reduces the effectiveness of melatonin supplementation. **Maintain a consistent sleep schedule, even on weekends** (social jet lag is a real phenomenon).
- **Napping too late in the day** — Naps after 3 PM reduce homeostatic sleep pressure (adenosine accumulation), making it harder for melatonin to facilitate sleep onset at night. **If napping, limit to 20-30 minutes before 2 PM.**
- **Tobacco/nicotine** — Nicotine is a stimulant that raises cortisol and norepinephrine. Additionally, smoking induces CYP1A2, the primary enzyme that metabolizes melatonin, causing melatonin to be cleared 50-60% faster. **Smokers may need to address this factor before melatonin supplementation is effective.**
- **Beta-blockers** — Atenolol, metoprolol, and propranolol suppress endogenous melatonin production by blocking the beta-1 adrenergic receptors on the pineal gland that trigger melatonin synthesis. If you take beta-blockers and have sleep issues, discuss melatonin supplementation with your prescriber.
- **NSAIDs at night** — Ibuprofen, aspirin, and naproxen can suppress nocturnal melatonin production by inhibiting prostaglandins involved in the melatonin synthesis pathway. If pain relief is needed at bedtime, acetaminophen has less impact on melatonin.
- **Stress and cortisol elevation** — Cortisol and melatonin are reciprocally regulated. Chronic stress elevates evening cortisol, which directly suppresses melatonin release. **Address evening stress through meditation, breathwork, journaling, or other wind-down practices.**
- **Hot environments** — Melatonin works partly by cooling the core body temperature. A hot bedroom (> 67-68 degrees Fahrenheit / 19-20 degrees Celsius) fights this mechanism. **Keep the bedroom cool (60-67 degrees Fahrenheit / 15-19 degrees Celsius).**
