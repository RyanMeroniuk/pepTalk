# Melatonin
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Oral immediate-release (IR)** — Most common form. Rapidly absorbed, peak plasma levels in 20-60 minutes. Mimics the initial spike of natural melatonin secretion. **Best for difficulty falling asleep.** Duration of action is approximately 3-4 hours. Oral bioavailability is only 3-33% due to extensive first-pass hepatic metabolism (CYP1A2).
- **Oral extended-release (ER) / sustained-release (SR)** — Designed to release melatonin over 6-8 hours, mimicking the natural melatonin secretion curve. **Best for difficulty staying asleep or frequent nighttime awakenings.** Circadin (2mg ER) is the only pharmaceutical-grade extended-release melatonin, approved in Europe for adults over 55. Some formulations combine IR + ER for both sleep onset and maintenance.
- **Sublingual tablets/drops** — Absorbed through the oral mucosa, bypassing first-pass liver metabolism. Higher bioavailability than oral (~50-60%). Faster onset (10-20 minutes). **Best for rapid sleep onset.** Ideal format for low-dose (0.3-0.5mg) use.
- **Liquid drops** — Allow precise microdosing (0.1-0.5mg increments). Absorption can be sublingual if held under the tongue, or standard oral if swallowed. Excellent for dose titration and for those who have difficulty with pills.
- **Topical/transdermal patches** — Bypass first-pass metabolism entirely. Sustained delivery over 6-8 hours. Limited commercial availability. Useful for those with GI absorption issues.
- **Intranasal sprays** — Rapid absorption via nasal mucosa, near-immediate onset. Limited product availability but emerging interest for acute use.
- **Gummies and chewables** — Popular but often contain 3-10mg per serving (too high). Usually loaded with sugar. Generally inferior delivery method; most melatonin is swallowed and subject to standard first-pass metabolism.
- **Synthetic vs. animal-derived** — Always choose **synthetic melatonin** (labeled as "pharmaceutical grade" or "USP verified"). Animal-derived melatonin (from bovine pineal glands) carries contamination risk and is inconsistent in potency.

**Key bioavailability considerations:**

- **First-pass metabolism** — CYP1A2 is the primary enzyme. Smokers metabolize melatonin 50-60% faster (CYP1A2 induction). Caffeine competes for the same enzyme, potentially increasing melatonin levels if taken together (not recommended).
- **Food effects** — High-fat meals delay absorption and reduce peak concentration. Take on an empty stomach or with a light snack for most consistent absorption.
- **Product quality** — Studies show actual melatonin content in supplements varies from -83% to +478% of labeled dose. Choose USP Verified, NSF Certified, or third-party tested products.
