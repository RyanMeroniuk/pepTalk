# Melatonin
## Side Effects

**Common (usually dose-dependent, resolve with dose reduction):**

- **Morning grogginess/drowsiness** — The most frequent complaint, almost always caused by taking too high a dose (3mg+). Resolve by reducing to 0.3-0.5mg.
- **Vivid or disturbing dreams** — Melatonin increases REM sleep duration and intensity. Dose-dependent; more common at 3mg+.
- **Headache** — Reported in some clinical trials, typically mild and transient.
- **Dizziness** — Occasional, especially at higher doses or when combined with other sedating substances.
- **Nausea** — Uncommon but reported, particularly with high doses on an empty stomach.
- **Daytime sleepiness** — If melatonin is taken too late at night or at excessive doses, residual effects can carry into the morning.

**Less common:**

- **Paradoxical waking at 2-3 AM** — A telltale sign of receptor desensitization from chronically high doses. The body compensates by downregulating MT1/MT2 receptors, leading to mid-sleep awakenings.
- **Mild hypothermia** — Melatonin lowers core body temperature; sensitive individuals may feel unusually cold.
- **Mood changes** — Rare reports of irritability or mild depressive symptoms, particularly in individuals predisposed to mood disorders.
- **Gastrointestinal discomfort** — Mild stomach upset, cramping, or changes in bowel habits.
- **Blood pressure changes** — Melatonin can lower blood pressure; those already on antihypertensives should monitor.

**Theoretical/long-term concerns:**

- **Receptor desensitization** — Chronic use of high doses (5-10mg) may downregulate melatonin receptors, reducing endogenous sensitivity. Not well-documented at physiological doses (0.3-1mg).
- **Reproductive hormone effects** — High-dose melatonin can suppress GnRH and affect reproductive hormones. Unlikely at physiological doses but relevant for those taking 5mg+ chronically.
- **Autoimmune considerations** — Because melatonin is immunostimulatory, it could theoretically exacerbate autoimmune conditions. Use with caution in rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, and similar conditions.

**Safety profile:**

- **No evidence of dependence or withdrawal** — Unlike benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, or even antihistamines, melatonin does not create physical dependency.
- **No evidence of tolerance at low doses** — Physiological doses can be used long-term without loss of efficacy.
- **Very wide therapeutic index** — No documented lethal dose in humans. Doses up to 100mg have been used in research settings without serious adverse events (not recommended for routine use).
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — Insufficient safety data. Generally not recommended unless specifically directed by a physician.
