# Mucuna Pruriens
## Drug Interactions

> Mucuna contains active L-DOPA. Its interaction profile is closer to the drug levodopa than to a typical herb. The first three interactions below are serious and can be dangerous.

- **MAOIs — CONTRAINDICATED (potentially life-threatening):** Combining Mucuna with a monoamine oxidase inhibitor — phenelzine, tranylcypromine, isocarboxazid, the MAO-B inhibitors selegiline and rasagiline, the antibiotic linezolid, and high-dose St. John's Wort (MAOI-like) — can precipitate a **hypertensive crisis** and dangerous catecholamine/serotonin excess. Do not combine. This is the single most important interaction
- **Antipsychotics and dopamine antagonists — contraindicated / counterproductive:** Antipsychotics (haloperidol, risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, etc.) and the antiemetic metoclopramide block dopamine receptors. They directly oppose Mucuna's mechanism, and Mucuna can undermine the medication's effect (and theoretically worsen psychiatric illness). Avoid combining without psychiatric supervision
- **Levodopa/carbidopa and other Parkinson's medications — contraindicated without a neurologist:** Mucuna is itself a levodopa source. Adding it to prescription levodopa/carbidopa, dopamine agonists (pramipexole, ropinirole), COMT inhibitors (entacapone), or MAO-B inhibitors causes additive dopaminergic effect — risking dyskinesia, hypotension, hallucinations, and dose-stacking toxicity. Only a treating neurologist should manage any combined or substituted use
- **Antihypertensive medications — caution/monitor:** L-DOPA/dopamine can lower blood pressure (orthostatic hypotension). Additive effect with antihypertensives may cause dizziness or fainting; monitor blood pressure, especially on standing
- **Other serotonergic / catecholaminergic agents (SSRIs, SNRIs, stimulants, triptans) — caution:** Combining with drugs that raise serotonin or catecholamines warrants caution for additive CNS and cardiovascular effects. Discuss with a prescriber before stacking
- **Diabetes/blood-pressure timing note:** Aside from the additive effects above, no special absorption-timing separation from these drugs is established; the protein-timing rule (separate Mucuna from high-protein meals by a couple of hours) is about food, not medications
- **Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) — relevant nuance:** Because Mucuna's L-DOPA lacks carbidopa, high-dose supplemental B6 can increase peripheral conversion of L-DOPA to dopamine, reducing brain delivery and increasing peripheral side effects. Be mindful of stacking high-dose B6
- **Anesthesia / surgery — disclose:** Its cardiovascular and CNS effects (and L-DOPA content) are relevant to anesthesia. Disclose use and discuss discontinuation before surgery
- **Contraindicated populations:**
  - **Anyone on an MAOI, antipsychotic/dopamine antagonist, or prescription Parkinson's regimen** (without specialist supervision)
  - **Psychiatric history** of psychosis, bipolar disorder, or schizophrenia (dopamine elevation risk)
  - **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — insufficient data; dopaminergic and prolactin-lowering effects
  - **Melanoma history / active melanoma** — levodopa carries a theoretical caution regarding melanoma; discuss with a physician
- **Overall:** Treat Mucuna as a pharmacologically active dopaminergic agent. The contraindications with MAOIs, antipsychotics, and Parkinson's drugs are the headline safety issues — always disclose Mucuna use to your healthcare providers
