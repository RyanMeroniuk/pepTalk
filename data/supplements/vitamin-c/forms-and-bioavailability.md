# Vitamin C
## Forms and Bioavailability

### Standard Forms
- **L-ascorbic acid (ascorbic acid):** The natural, biologically active form. Highly bioavailable at doses up to 200 mg (~100% absorption). Absorption decreases with increasing dose: ~73% at 500 mg, ~50% at 1,250 mg. Acidic (pH 2.4), which can cause stomach upset in some people. The gold standard and most studied form. Best taken in divided doses throughout the day.
- **Sodium ascorbate:** Buffered, non-acidic form (pH ~7.4). Well-tolerated by sensitive stomachs. Contains ~111 mg sodium per 1,000 mg. Good choice for those who experience GI irritation from ascorbic acid. Similar bioavailability to ascorbic acid.
- **Calcium ascorbate (Ester-C):** Buffered form containing calcium and small amounts of dehydroascorbic acid, calcium threonate, and other metabolites. pH ~6.5-7. Marketed as Ester-C with claims of superior absorption and retention, but independent studies show bioavailability is comparable to standard ascorbic acid. More expensive without clear advantage.
- **Magnesium ascorbate:** Buffered form providing both vitamin C and magnesium. Gentle on the stomach. Contains ~6% elemental magnesium. Good option for those supplementing both nutrients.

### Advanced Forms
- **Liposomal vitamin C:** Ascorbic acid encapsulated in phospholipid liposomes (typically phosphatidylcholine from sunflower lecithin). Bypasses SVCT1 transporter saturation in the gut by using lipid-mediated absorption pathways. Studies show 1.5-2x higher peak plasma levels than standard ascorbic acid at equivalent doses. Best for achieving higher blood levels without GI distress. Can approach (but not equal) IV vitamin C plasma concentrations. Quality varies dramatically between brands — true liposomal products are viscous liquids, not dry powders.
- **PureWay-C:** A lipid-metabolite formulation of ascorbic acid bound to fatty acids (lipid metabolites from citrus) and bioflavonoids. Some studies suggest faster absorption and higher cellular retention than standard ascorbic acid. Limited independent research.
- **Ascorbyl palmitate:** Fat-soluble ester of vitamin C. Incorporated into cell membranes where it provides antioxidant protection in the lipid phase (complementing water-soluble vitamin C). Used in topical vitamin C products and as a food preservative. Less effective at raising plasma vitamin C levels than ascorbic acid.

### IV Vitamin C
- **Sodium ascorbate IV:** Administered intravenously, bypassing intestinal absorption entirely. Achieves plasma levels 30-70x higher than any oral form (reaching 15-25 mmol/L vs. 0.2-0.4 mmol/L from oral). At these concentrations, vitamin C acts as a pro-oxidant, generating hydrogen peroxide selectively toxic to cancer cells. Used in integrative oncology (25-100g IV), sepsis protocols (1.5g IV q6h), and acute infections. Requires medical supervision and G6PD testing before high-dose use.

### Best Form by Purpose
- **General daily supplementation:** L-ascorbic acid (divided doses) or sodium ascorbate if stomach-sensitive
- **Higher therapeutic doses (2-4g/day):** Liposomal vitamin C to maximize absorption and minimize GI side effects
- **Acute immune support:** Liposomal vitamin C (every 2-3 hours) or IV vitamin C for severe illness
- **Skin and anti-aging (topical):** L-ascorbic acid serum at 10-20% concentration, pH <3.5, ideally with vitamin E and ferulic acid (Skinceuticals CE Ferulic formula)
- **Budget-friendly:** Bulk L-ascorbic acid powder dissolved in water — equally effective as expensive capsules
