# Vitamin C
## Things to Avoid

### Substances That Deplete or Oppose Vitamin C
- **Smoking:** Smokers have 25-40% lower plasma vitamin C levels than non-smokers. Each cigarette depletes approximately 25 mg of vitamin C through increased oxidative stress. Smokers need at minimum 35 mg/day more than non-smokers, and most practitioners recommend 1,000-2,000 mg/day supplementation.
- **Chronic alcohol use:** Depletes vitamin C stores through increased urinary excretion, impaired absorption, and increased oxidative demand. Alcoholics are at high risk for subclinical scurvy.
- **Chronic stress:** The adrenal glands consume large amounts of vitamin C during cortisol synthesis. Prolonged psychological or physical stress significantly increases vitamin C requirements.
- **Environmental toxins (air pollution, heavy metals):** Vitamin C is consumed neutralizing free radicals generated by pollution exposure, pesticides, and heavy metal toxicity. Urban dwellers and those with occupational exposures have higher vitamin C needs.
- **NSAIDs (aspirin, ibuprofen):** Chronic NSAID use may reduce plasma vitamin C levels. Aspirin in particular competes with vitamin C for absorption and increases urinary excretion.

### Behaviors That Reduce Efficacy
- **Taking entire daily dose at once:** Vitamin C has a plasma half-life of approximately 2 hours. Taking 1,000 mg at once wastes most of it — absorption is only ~50% at 1,000 mg and drops further at higher single doses. Always divide into 2-4 doses throughout the day.
- **Very high single doses without liposomal form:** Standard ascorbic acid absorption saturates at ~200 mg per dose. For higher therapeutic dosing, use liposomal vitamin C or divide doses at 2-3 hour intervals.
- **Taking mega-doses immediately post-exercise:** Some evidence suggests that very high-dose antioxidants (>1g vitamin C) taken immediately after resistance training may blunt beneficial exercise-induced ROS signaling that drives mitochondrial biogenesis and muscle adaptation. Consider waiting 2-3 hours post-workout before taking high-dose vitamin C.
- **Storing vitamin C supplements in heat/light:** Vitamin C degrades rapidly when exposed to heat, light, and moisture. Store supplements in a cool, dark, dry place. Liquid vitamin C is particularly prone to oxidation (turns yellow/brown when degraded).
- **Using degraded supplements:** If your vitamin C powder has turned yellow or brown, or if liquid vitamin C has darkened significantly, it has oxidized to dehydroascorbic acid and further breakdown products. Replace it.

### Medical Considerations
- **Do not take high-dose vitamin C before surgery** without informing your surgeon. Stop 1-2 weeks before elective surgery as high doses may affect anesthesia and blood parameters.
- **Do not use high-dose IV vitamin C without G6PD testing.** Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency can cause hemolytic anemia with high-dose IV vitamin C.
- **Do not take vitamin C concurrently with certain chemotherapy drugs** without oncologist approval. Vitamin C's antioxidant effects may theoretically protect cancer cells from oxidative chemotherapy agents (though this is debated and some oncologists use IV C adjunctively).
