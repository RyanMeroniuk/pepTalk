# Vitamin C
## Natural Food Sources

### Highest Sources (fruits)
- **Kakadu plum:** 100g = 2,300-3,150 mg (highest known food source)
- **Acerola cherry:** 1 cup = 1,644 mg
- **Rose hips:** 100g = 426 mg
- **Guava:** 1 fruit = 228 mg
- **Black currants:** 1 cup = 203 mg
- **Kiwi fruit:** 1 medium = 71 mg
- **Papaya:** 1 cup = 88 mg
- **Strawberries:** 1 cup = 89 mg
- **Oranges:** 1 medium = 70 mg
- **Pineapple:** 1 cup = 79 mg
- **Mango:** 1 cup = 60 mg
- **Grapefruit:** 1/2 fruit = 39 mg
- **Cantaloupe:** 1 cup = 58 mg
- **Lemon juice:** 1/4 cup = 24 mg

### Highest Sources (vegetables)
- **Red bell pepper (raw):** 1 medium = 152 mg
- **Green bell pepper (raw):** 1 medium = 96 mg
- **Broccoli (cooked):** 1 cup = 101 mg
- **Brussels sprouts (cooked):** 1 cup = 97 mg
- **Cauliflower (raw):** 1 cup = 52 mg
- **Kale (raw):** 1 cup = 80 mg
- **Snow peas:** 1 cup = 77 mg
- **Cabbage (red, raw):** 1 cup = 51 mg
- **Tomatoes:** 1 medium = 17 mg
- **Sweet potato (baked):** 1 medium = 22 mg
- **Spinach (raw):** 1 cup = 8 mg

### Herbs and Spices
- **Thyme (fresh):** 1 oz = 45 mg
- **Parsley (fresh):** 1 cup = 80 mg
- **Chili peppers (red):** 1 pepper = 65 mg
- **Coriander/cilantro (fresh):** 1 cup = 16 mg

### Key Notes
- **Cooking destroys vitamin C:** Heat, water, and prolonged storage degrade vitamin C. Raw or lightly steamed foods retain the most. Boiling vegetables can destroy 50-60% of vitamin C content. Steaming retains 80-90%.
- **Freshness matters:** Vitamin C content declines after harvest. Fresh, locally grown produce has more than store-bought produce that has been shipped long distances.
- **Frozen is often better than "fresh":** Flash-frozen fruits and vegetables retain vitamin C better than produce that sits in the store for days.
- **Whole food sources include bioflavonoids** (hesperidin, rutin, quercetin) that naturally enhance vitamin C absorption and function — an advantage over isolated supplements.
