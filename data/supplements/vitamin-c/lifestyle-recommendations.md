# Vitamin C
## Lifestyle Recommendations

### Dietary Strategy
- **Eat 5-9 servings of vitamin C-rich fruits and vegetables daily.** A diverse produce intake provides 200-400 mg vitamin C along with naturally synergistic bioflavonoids. Prioritize red bell peppers, kiwi, strawberries, broccoli, citrus, and guava.
- **Eat produce raw or lightly steamed** when possible. Steam rather than boil. If you microwave vegetables briefly, vitamin C retention is actually quite good.
- **Buy fresh and local when possible.** Vitamin C degrades during transport and storage. Frozen produce is often superior to "fresh" produce that has traveled long distances.
- **Pair vitamin C foods with iron-rich plant foods** at every meal to maximize iron absorption (especially important for vegetarians, vegans, and women of reproductive age).

### Supplementation Strategy
- **Baseline daily supplement:** 500-1,000 mg/day divided into 2-3 doses
- **Acute illness protocol:** Increase to 1,000 mg every 2-3 hours (liposomal preferred) until symptoms resolve, then return to baseline
- **Stressful periods:** Increase to 1,500-2,000 mg/day during high stress (the adrenal glands are vitamin C's biggest consumer during stress)
- **Post-surgery recovery:** 500-1,000 mg/day to support wound healing and collagen repair (resume after surgeon clears supplementation)

### Skin Health
- **Topical vitamin C serum:** Use a 10-20% L-ascorbic acid serum (pH <3.5) every morning before sunscreen for UV protection, brightening, and collagen stimulation. Look for formulations that include vitamin E (0.5-1%) and ferulic acid (0.5-1%) — this combination (the "Duke antioxidant patent") increases efficacy 4-8x.
- **Internal + topical:** Combine oral supplementation with topical application for synergistic skin benefits.

### Exercise and Recovery
- **Pre-workout (optional):** 250-500 mg 30-60 minutes before exercise may reduce oxidative stress during training
- **Post-workout timing:** Wait 2-3 hours after intense resistance training before taking high doses (>1g) to avoid blunting beneficial adaptation signals. For endurance athletes, this concern is less relevant.
- **Recovery days:** Standard supplementation (500-1,000 mg/day) supports recovery and reduces muscle soreness

### Immune Resilience
- **Daily consistency matters more than mega-dosing.** Regular daily vitamin C intake builds immune cell reserves. Immune cells accumulate vitamin C to 10-100x plasma levels, but only when plasma levels are consistently maintained.
- **At first sign of illness:** Start the acute protocol immediately. Early intervention is more effective than starting after symptoms are established.
- **During cold and flu season:** Consider increasing daily intake to 1,000-1,500 mg/day as a preventive measure

### Stress Management
- **Recognize that stress rapidly depletes vitamin C.** If you are going through a high-stress period (work, personal, physical), proactively increase intake.
- **The adrenal glands contain 30-40x the plasma concentration of vitamin C.** Supporting adrenal function with adequate vitamin C is a cornerstone of stress resilience.

### Hydration
- **Drink adequate water** when supplementing vitamin C. Excess is excreted via urine, and adequate hydration supports kidney health and prevents the theoretical kidney stone risk from oxalate formation.
- **Target 2-3 liters of water daily,** more if taking >1,000 mg/day of vitamin C.
