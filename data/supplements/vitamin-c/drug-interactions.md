# Vitamin C
## Drug Interactions and Contraindications

### Medications Affected by Vitamin C

- **Warfarin (Coumadin):** High-dose vitamin C (>1,000 mg/day) may reduce warfarin efficacy by competing for metabolism or reducing absorption. Monitor INR closely if vitamin C dose changes. Low-moderate doses (200-500 mg/day) are generally safe but inform your anticoagulation provider.
- **Statins and niacin combinations:** One study suggested that antioxidant supplements (including vitamin C) reduced the HDL-raising effect of niacin-simvastatin combination therapy. The clinical significance is debated but worth noting.
- **Estrogen-containing contraceptives and HRT:** Vitamin C (>1g/day) may increase estrogen levels by inhibiting estrogen sulfation (an inactivation pathway). This could theoretically increase estrogen-related side effects. Clinical significance is unclear at moderate doses.
- **Protease inhibitors (indinavir):** Vitamin C (>1g/day) may reduce plasma levels of indinavir. Separate dosing by at least 2 hours.
- **Certain chemotherapy agents:** The interaction between vitamin C and chemotherapy is complex and drug-specific:
  - **Alkylating agents (cyclophosphamide, cisplatin):** Some concern that antioxidants may reduce efficacy. Discuss with oncologist.
  - **Doxorubicin:** Some preclinical evidence suggests vitamin C may actually enhance efficacy while protecting healthy tissues. Still requires oncologist approval.
  - **Bortezomib (Velcade):** Vitamin C may reduce bortezomib efficacy by interfering with its proteasome-inhibiting mechanism. Avoid concurrent use.
- **Fluphenazine (Prolixin):** Vitamin C may reduce blood levels of this antipsychotic. Monitor and adjust dosing.
- **Aluminum-containing antacids:** Vitamin C increases aluminum absorption, raising potential toxicity risk with chronic antacid use. Avoid combining.

### Medications That Deplete Vitamin C
- **Aspirin / NSAIDs:** Chronic use lowers plasma vitamin C. Aspirin accelerates urinary excretion of ascorbate. Supplement 500 mg/day if on daily aspirin therapy.
- **Corticosteroids:** Increase oxidative stress and vitamin C consumption. Supplement during steroid courses.
- **Tetracycline antibiotics:** May reduce vitamin C levels. Supplement during antibiotic courses.
- **Oral contraceptives:** May reduce plasma vitamin C levels by 20-30%. Supplement 250-500 mg/day.
- **Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs):** May reduce vitamin C absorption and lower plasma levels with chronic use.

### Lab Test Interference
- **Blood glucose (glucometer):** High-dose vitamin C (>500 mg) can cause falsely elevated glucose readings on some glucometers (especially those using GDH-PQQ enzyme). This is critical for diabetics — may lead to insulin overdosing. Use GDH-NAD or glucose oxidase-based meters, or temporarily stop vitamin C before testing.
- **Fecal occult blood test:** Vitamin C (>250 mg/day) can cause false-negative results. Stop vitamin C 48-72 hours before stool-based colorectal cancer screening.
- **Urine glucose tests:** May show false results in diabetics on vitamin C supplementation.
- **Serum bilirubin and creatinine:** High-dose vitamin C may artificially elevate these values on some lab assays.

### Contraindications
- **G6PD deficiency (for high-dose IV vitamin C):** Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency is an absolute contraindication for high-dose IV vitamin C (>10g). Can cause acute hemolytic anemia. G6PD testing is mandatory before IV vitamin C therapy. Oral vitamin C at standard doses (up to 1-2g/day) is generally safe in G6PD deficiency.
- **Hemochromatosis / iron overload:** Vitamin C dramatically increases iron absorption. Limit supplemental vitamin C to 200 mg/day and avoid taking with iron-rich meals or iron supplements. Monitor ferritin levels.
- **History of calcium oxalate kidney stones:** Use caution above 1,000 mg/day. Stay well hydrated, supplement magnesium, and consider monitoring urinary oxalate levels.
- **Severe renal insufficiency (GFR <30):** Impaired oxalate clearance increases kidney stone and nephrocalcinosis risk. Use only under medical supervision.

### Safe Combinations
- **Iron supplements:** Vitamin C actively enhances iron absorption — this is a desired interaction for iron-deficiency anemia treatment
- **Vitamin E:** Synergistic antioxidant recycling — vitamin C regenerates oxidized vitamin E
- **Zinc:** Safe combination; commonly used together for immune support
- **Collagen supplements:** Vitamin C is required for collagen synthesis — ideal pairing
- **Most common supplements and medications** at standard vitamin C doses (500-1,000 mg/day) have no concerning interactions
