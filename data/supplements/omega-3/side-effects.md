# Omega-3 (Fish Oil / Krill Oil)
## Side Effects

- **Fishy burps/aftertaste:** The most common complaint. Minimized by taking with meals, using enteric-coated capsules, refrigerating capsules, or switching to triglyceride form. Fishy burps from non-rancid oil are harmless but unpleasant. Persistent strong fishy taste may indicate rancid oil — discard and replace
- **GI discomfort:** Nausea, bloating, diarrhea, or loose stools — usually dose-dependent. Start with a lower dose and increase gradually. Taking with food reduces GI issues significantly
- **Blood thinning:** Omega-3s reduce platelet aggregation. At standard doses (1-3g/day), this is generally mild and not clinically significant. At high doses (4g+/day), discuss with physician if taking anticoagulants (warfarin, heparin), antiplatelet drugs (aspirin, clopidogrel), or before surgery. Most surgeons recommend stopping high-dose fish oil 7-14 days before elective surgery
- **Blood sugar:** Very high doses (4g+/day) may slightly increase fasting blood glucose in some individuals, though this is inconsistent across studies and generally not clinically meaningful
- **LDL cholesterol:** DHA can modestly raise LDL-C in some individuals (typically 5-10%), though LDL particle size may shift to less atherogenic large buoyant particles. Pure EPA (Vascepa) does not raise LDL. If LDL is a concern, consider EPA-dominant formulations
- **Vitamin A toxicity risk (cod liver oil only):** Cod liver oil contains preformed vitamin A and vitamin D. Excessive cod liver oil intake can lead to vitamin A toxicity. Standard fish oil does not contain vitamin A
- **Allergic reactions:** Rare. Shellfish allergy does not necessarily preclude fish oil use (shellfish allergy is to proteins, not oils). However, krill oil may be contraindicated with shellfish allergy — consult allergist
- **Oxidized/rancid oil risks:** Consuming rancid fish oil may increase oxidative stress, promote LDL oxidation, and worsen inflammation — the opposite of the intended benefit. This is why quality and storage matter enormously
- **Prostate cancer concern (historical):** The SELECT trial raised concerns about omega-3 and prostate cancer risk, but the study measured blood levels (not supplementation) and subsequent meta-analyses have not confirmed a causal relationship. Current consensus: no clear increased risk
