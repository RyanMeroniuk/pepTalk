# Omega-3 (Fish Oil / Krill Oil)
## Foods to Avoid

- **Excessive omega-6 vegetable/seed oils:** These directly compete with omega-3 for the same enzymatic pathways (delta-6 desaturase, COX, LOX). High omega-6 intake undermines the benefits of omega-3 supplementation by maintaining the pro-inflammatory eicosanoid balance you are trying to correct:
  - **Soybean oil** — the single largest source of omega-6 in the American diet
  - **Corn oil**
  - **Sunflower oil (high-linoleic)**
  - **Cottonseed oil**
  - **Safflower oil**
  - **Canola oil** (moderate omega-6 but often highly processed)
  - **Note:** These oils are pervasive in restaurant food, packaged snacks, salad dressings, and fried food. Reducing them is arguably as important as taking omega-3 supplements

- **Fried foods and fast food:** Cooked in high omega-6 seed oils at high temperatures, producing oxidized fats and trans fats that directly oppose omega-3 benefits

- **Processed snack foods:** Chips, crackers, cookies, and packaged baked goods almost universally contain seed oils. Check ingredient labels for soybean oil, canola oil, or "vegetable oil"

- **Margarine and shortening:** Often contain trans fats or interesterified fats that worsen the omega-6:omega-3 ratio and promote inflammation

- **Grain-fed conventional meat (in excess):** Contains significantly more omega-6 and less omega-3 than grass-fed. Not necessary to eliminate, but be aware that relying heavily on conventional meat increases omega-6 load

- **High-sugar foods:** Excess sugar promotes systemic inflammation through separate pathways (AGEs, insulin resistance), partially negating omega-3 anti-inflammatory benefits

- **Alcohol (in excess):** Increases lipid peroxidation, potentially oxidizing omega-3 fatty acids in cell membranes. Moderate intake is likely fine, but heavy drinking counteracts omega-3 cardiovascular benefits
