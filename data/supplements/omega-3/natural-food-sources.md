# Omega-3 (Fish Oil / Krill Oil)
## Natural Food Sources

- **Fatty cold-water fish (highest EPA+DHA):**
  - **Wild-caught salmon (sockeye):** ~1,800mg EPA+DHA per 3.5oz serving — the gold standard food source
  - **Atlantic mackerel:** ~2,600mg EPA+DHA per 3.5oz serving (avoid king mackerel due to mercury)
  - **Sardines:** ~1,400mg EPA+DHA per 3.5oz serving. Low mercury, sustainable, inexpensive, bones provide calcium
  - **Anchovies:** ~1,400mg EPA+DHA per 3.5oz serving. Low mercury, sustainable
  - **Herring:** ~1,700mg EPA+DHA per 3.5oz serving
  - **Rainbow trout (wild):** ~1,000mg EPA+DHA per 3.5oz serving
  - **Albacore tuna:** ~1,300mg EPA+DHA per 3.5oz serving (moderate mercury — limit to 2-3 servings/week)

- **Shellfish (moderate EPA+DHA):**
  - **Oysters:** ~600mg EPA+DHA per 3.5oz serving, plus zinc and B12
  - **Mussels:** ~500mg EPA+DHA per 3.5oz serving
  - **Shrimp:** ~300mg EPA+DHA per 3.5oz serving

- **ALA sources (plant-based, poor conversion to EPA/DHA):**
  - **Flaxseeds/flaxseed oil:** ~6,400mg ALA per tablespoon of oil. Only 5-10% converts to EPA, 2-5% to DHA
  - **Chia seeds:** ~5,000mg ALA per ounce
  - **Hemp seeds:** ~1,000mg ALA per tablespoon
  - **Walnuts:** ~2,500mg ALA per ounce

- **Eggs (omega-3 enriched):** Hens fed flaxseed or algae produce eggs with 100-500mg omega-3 (mix of ALA, EPA, DHA depending on feed)
- **Grass-fed beef:** Contains modestly more omega-3 than grain-fed (~80mg vs ~40mg per 3.5oz), but still a poor source compared to fish
- **Seaweed/nori:** Small amounts of EPA. Not a meaningful source for supplementation but contributes to baseline intake in Japanese diets

- **SMASH fish mnemonic:** Salmon, Mackerel, Anchovies, Sardines, Herring — the best fish for omega-3 with the lowest mercury risk
- **Mercury note:** Larger, longer-lived predatory fish (swordfish, shark, king mackerel, tilefish, bigeye tuna) accumulate more mercury. Stick to smaller fish or wild salmon for the best omega-3 to mercury ratio
