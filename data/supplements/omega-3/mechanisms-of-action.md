# Omega-3 (Fish Oil / Krill Oil)
## Mechanisms of Action

- **Eicosanoid pathway modulation:** EPA competes with arachidonic acid (AA, an omega-6) for cyclooxygenase (COX) and lipoxygenase (LOX) enzymes, shifting production away from pro-inflammatory prostaglandins (PGE2), thromboxanes (TXA2), and leukotrienes (LTB4) toward anti-inflammatory and less potent series-3 prostaglandins and series-5 leukotrienes
- **Specialized pro-resolving mediators (SPMs):** EPA and DHA are precursors to resolvins (E-series from EPA, D-series from DHA), protectins (including neuroprotectin D1 from DHA), and maresins. These actively resolve inflammation rather than merely suppressing it — a critical distinction from NSAIDs
- **Cell membrane incorporation:** DHA integrates into phospholipid bilayers, increasing membrane fluidity. This is particularly important in neurons, retinal cells, and cardiac cells where membrane fluidity affects receptor signaling, neurotransmitter function, and ion channel activity
- **Gene expression regulation:** Omega-3s activate PPARs (peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors), particularly PPAR-gamma and PPAR-alpha, modulating genes involved in lipid metabolism, inflammation, and insulin sensitivity. They also suppress NF-kB, the master inflammatory transcription factor
- **Triglyceride reduction:** EPA and DHA reduce hepatic VLDL production and accelerate triglyceride clearance from plasma. At therapeutic doses (2-4g EPA+DHA), triglycerides drop 15-45%
- **Endocannabinoid modulation:** DHA serves as a precursor to endocannabinoid-like compounds (synaptamide/DHEA) that promote neurogenesis and have anti-inflammatory properties in the CNS
- **Platelet function:** EPA reduces platelet aggregation by competing with AA for thromboxane synthesis, producing the less potent TXA3 instead of TXA2
- **Heart rate and rhythm:** DHA modulates sodium and calcium ion channels in cardiac myocytes, contributing to anti-arrhythmic effects and modest blood pressure reduction
