# Resveratrol
## Natural Food Sources

- **Red grapes (with skin):** ~0.2-1.5 mg per cup of red grapes. Resveratrol concentrates in the skin as a defense compound against fungal infection (phytoalexin). Muscadine grapes contain the highest levels among grape varieties
- **Red wine:** ~0.2-5.8 mg per 5 oz glass depending on grape variety, region, and fermentation time. Pinot Noir consistently contains the highest concentrations due to cool-climate growing conditions and thin grape skins that upregulate resveratrol production. The "French Paradox" myth: you would need to drink 100-300+ glasses of red wine per day to reach a therapeutic dose of 250-500 mg, making wine a negligible source for supplementation purposes
- **Peanuts (raw, with skin):** ~0.01-0.26 mg per cup. Boiled peanuts contain higher levels (~0.32-1.28 mg per cup) because boiling extracts resveratrol from the skin into the nut. Still far below therapeutic doses
- **Blueberries:** ~0.01-0.06 mg per cup. Contains both trans-resveratrol and pterostilbene (a methylated analog with better bioavailability). Wild blueberries contain higher concentrations than cultivated varieties
- **Dark chocolate / cocoa:** ~0.04-0.06 mg per ounce of dark chocolate (70%+ cacao). Trace amounts only, though cocoa provides synergistic polyphenols (epicatechin, catechins) that share overlapping antioxidant pathways
- **Japanese knotweed (Polygonum cuspidatum / Reynoutria japonica):** ~3.9 mg per gram of dried root. The primary commercial supplement source worldwide due to dramatically higher concentrations than any food. Most resveratrol supplements are extracted from the root of this invasive plant species. Contains both trans-resveratrol and emodin
- **Mulberries:** ~0.3-0.5 mg per cup. Higher than most berries but still sub-therapeutic. Black mulberries contain more than white varieties
- **Cranberries (raw):** ~0.2 mg per cup. Modest levels compared to grapes. Cranberry juice cocktails contain negligible amounts due to dilution and processing
- **Pistachios:** ~0.1-0.8 mg per ounce. One of the higher nut sources alongside peanuts
- **Itadori tea (Japanese knotweed tea):** Traditional Japanese remedy made from dried knotweed root. Contains significantly more resveratrol per serving than any Western dietary source, historically used for cardiovascular and anti-inflammatory purposes

**Key takeaway:** No dietary source provides anywhere near a therapeutic dose (150-500 mg/day). The highest food source (red wine at ~5.8 mg per glass max) would require consuming impossibly large quantities. Supplementation from Japanese knotweed extract is the only practical way to achieve clinically studied doses.
