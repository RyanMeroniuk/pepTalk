# Resveratrol
## Foods to Avoid

- **High-fiber meals:** Dietary fiber (especially insoluble fiber from whole grains, bran, and legumes) can bind to polyphenols like resveratrol in the gut lumen through hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interactions. This traps resveratrol within the fiber matrix, reducing the amount available for absorption in the small intestine. While some bound resveratrol may be released by colonic bacteria and absorbed in the large intestine, this is far less efficient. Avoid taking resveratrol supplements alongside high-fiber cereals, bran muffins, or psyllium husk
- **High-protein meals (excessive):** Very high-protein meals may reduce resveratrol absorption because resveratrol binds readily to proteins (particularly albumin and casein). In the gut, dairy proteins and meat proteins can complex with resveratrol before it reaches the intestinal wall. This is most relevant with dairy-heavy meals (whey shakes, large amounts of milk or yogurt consumed simultaneously with the supplement)
- **Dairy products consumed simultaneously:** Milk proteins (casein and whey) have a strong affinity for polyphenols. Studies on other polyphenols (tea catechins, flavonoids) show that milk protein binding significantly reduces their bioavailability. The same mechanism applies to resveratrol. Avoid taking resveratrol with a glass of milk or immediately after a dairy-heavy meal
- **Raw cruciferous vegetables in large amounts:** Large servings of raw broccoli, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts, and cabbage can upregulate phase II detoxification enzymes (particularly UGT and SULT) through their sulforaphane and indole-3-carbinol content. While this enzyme induction is generally beneficial for detoxification, it accelerates the conjugation and clearance of resveratrol. This effect is more relevant with chronic high cruciferous intake rather than a single meal
- **Excessive alcohol:** While red wine contains resveratrol, consuming large amounts of alcohol alongside resveratrol supplementation is counterproductive. Alcohol competes for CYP enzyme metabolism, creates oxidative stress that resveratrol must then counteract rather than provide net benefit, and impairs gut barrier integrity which may alter absorption kinetics
- **Highly processed foods with trans fats:** Trans fats and oxidized seed oils increase systemic inflammation and oxidative stress, working against resveratrol's anti-inflammatory and antioxidant mechanisms. While they do not directly block absorption, they create a metabolic environment that diminishes resveratrol's functional benefits

**Key takeaway:** The most impactful avoidance is high-fiber supplements and dairy taken at the same time as resveratrol. Separate resveratrol dosing from fiber supplements and dairy by at least 1-2 hours for optimal absorption.
