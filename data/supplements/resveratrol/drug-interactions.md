# Resveratrol
## Drug Interactions

### Significant Interactions (Use Caution)

- **Anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs (warfarin, heparin, aspirin, clopidogrel, rivaroxaban):** Resveratrol inhibits platelet aggregation through suppression of thromboxane A2 synthesis and COX-1 inhibition. Combined with pharmaceutical blood thinners, this creates additive anticoagulant effects and increases bleeding risk. Patients on warfarin should monitor INR closely if adding resveratrol, as it may potentiate warfarin's effects. Discontinue resveratrol at least 2 weeks before any scheduled surgery
- **CYP3A4 substrates (statins, calcium channel blockers, immunosuppressants, certain SSRIs, benzodiazepines, sildenafil):** Resveratrol inhibits CYP3A4, a major drug-metabolizing enzyme responsible for processing approximately 50% of all pharmaceutical drugs. Inhibiting CYP3A4 slows the breakdown of these medications, effectively increasing their blood levels and potency. This can lead to increased side effects or toxicity. Particularly relevant for atorvastatin, simvastatin, amlodipine, cyclosporine, tacrolimus, and midazolam
- **CYP1A2 substrates (caffeine, theophylline, clozapine, tizanidine):** Resveratrol inhibits CYP1A2, which metabolizes caffeine and several medications. This can increase circulating caffeine levels (potentially causing jitteriness, insomnia, or tachycardia) and raise blood levels of theophylline (used for asthma) to potentially toxic concentrations. Monitor caffeine sensitivity when starting resveratrol supplementation
- **Diabetes medications (metformin, sulfonylureas, insulin):** Resveratrol lowers blood glucose through AMPK activation and improved insulin sensitivity. Combined with glucose-lowering medications, this creates additive hypoglycemic effects. While resveratrol may allow eventual dose reduction of diabetes medications (a potential benefit), unsupervised combination risks hypoglycemia. Blood glucose monitoring should increase when adding resveratrol to an existing diabetes medication regimen
- **Immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus, sirolimus):** Beyond the CYP3A4 interaction (which raises blood levels of these drugs), resveratrol has immunomodulatory properties that could theoretically interfere with the intended immunosuppression in transplant patients or autoimmune conditions. This is a dual-risk interaction: pharmacokinetic (enzyme inhibition) and pharmacodynamic (immune modulation)
- **Antihypertensive medications:** Resveratrol lowers blood pressure through eNOS activation and improved endothelial function. Combined with antihypertensive drugs, this may cause additive blood pressure reduction leading to hypotension, dizziness, or syncope. Monitor blood pressure regularly when combining
- **NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac):** Both resveratrol and NSAIDs inhibit COX enzymes and platelet function. Combined use increases GI bleeding risk and amplifies antiplatelet effects. Use the combination cautiously, especially in individuals with history of GI ulcers or bleeding disorders
- **Estrogen-containing medications (oral contraceptives, HRT):** Resveratrol acts as a phytoestrogen with selective estrogen receptor modulation activity. It can compete with pharmaceutical estrogens for receptor binding, potentially altering the efficacy of oral contraceptives or hormone replacement therapy. The interaction is dose-dependent and unpredictable, particularly at high resveratrol doses (>500 mg/day)

### Contraindications

- **Hormone-sensitive cancers at high doses:** Resveratrol's estrogenic activity at high doses (>1,000 mg/day) is contraindicated in individuals with estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer, endometrial cancer, or ovarian cancer. At moderate doses (150-500 mg), resveratrol may actually have anti-estrogenic (SERM-like) properties, but the dose-response relationship is not well enough characterized to ensure safety in hormone-sensitive cancer patients. Consult an oncologist before use
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** Insufficient safety data exists for resveratrol supplementation during pregnancy and lactation. Resveratrol's estrogenic activity, antiplatelet effects, and CYP enzyme inhibition create theoretical risks to fetal development and drug metabolism during pregnancy. The phytoestrogen effects are of particular concern during critical windows of fetal hormonal development. Dietary amounts from food sources (grapes, berries) are considered safe; supplemental doses are not recommended
- **Active bleeding disorders (hemophilia, thrombocytopenia):** Resveratrol's antiplatelet activity is contraindicated in individuals with pre-existing bleeding disorders where platelet function or count is already compromised
- **Pre-surgical period (within 2 weeks):** Discontinue resveratrol supplementation at least 14 days before any scheduled surgical procedure due to increased bleeding risk from antiplatelet effects
