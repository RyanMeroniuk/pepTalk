# Resveratrol
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Trans-resveratrol (the ONLY form that matters):** Trans-resveratrol is the biologically active geometric isomer. Cis-resveratrol is largely inactive. All quality supplements specify trans-resveratrol content. If a label just says "resveratrol" without specifying "trans," it may contain a mix of active and inactive forms
- **Sources of supplemental resveratrol:**
  - **Japanese knotweed (Polygonum cuspidatum)** — the most common and cost-effective source. Contains high concentrations of trans-resveratrol. Most supplements use this source
  - **Red grape extract (Vitis vinifera)** — a food-derived source but typically lower in resveratrol content per gram. May contain additional beneficial polyphenols (OPCs, anthocyanins)
  - **Synthetic trans-resveratrol** — lab-produced, high purity. Used in some premium and clinical-grade products
- **Bioavailability challenge (critical):** Oral resveratrol has approximately 1-5% bioavailability due to rapid Phase II metabolism. In the intestinal wall and liver, resveratrol is quickly conjugated to glucuronide and sulfate metabolites. These metabolites have reduced biological activity compared to free resveratrol
- **Micronized resveratrol (recommended):** Micronization reduces particle size, increasing surface area and improving dissolution and absorption. Micronized trans-resveratrol achieves 3-5x higher plasma levels than standard powder
- **Liposomal resveratrol:** Encapsulation in phospholipid vesicles protects resveratrol from first-pass metabolism and enhances cellular delivery. Emerging evidence suggests improved bioavailability, though standardization varies between products
- **Piperine (black pepper extract) enhancement:** Piperine inhibits glucuronidation (the primary route of resveratrol inactivation). Adding 5-20mg piperine (BioPerine) increases resveratrol bioavailability by approximately 229% in one study. Many quality supplements include piperine
- **Take with fat (essential):** Resveratrol is lipophilic. Absorption improves significantly when taken with dietary fat. David Sinclair takes his resveratrol mixed into full-fat yogurt. Olive oil, avocado, nuts, and eggs are also effective
- **Sublingual delivery:** Some formulations target sublingual absorption to bypass first-pass metabolism. Limited data but theoretically sound
- **Quercetin combination:** Some products combine resveratrol with quercetin, another polyphenol that inhibits resveratrol-metabolizing enzymes (sulfotransferases), potentially increasing resveratrol's half-life
- **Light sensitivity:** Trans-resveratrol converts to inactive cis-resveratrol upon UV light exposure. Store supplements in opaque containers away from direct light. Dark glass or opaque capsules are preferred
- **Stability:** Resveratrol is sensitive to light, heat, and oxygen. Store in a cool, dark place. Refrigeration extends shelf life. Look for products with nitrogen-flushed packaging
