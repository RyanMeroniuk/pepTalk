# Resveratrol
## Things to Avoid

- **High doses above 1,000 mg/day:** At high doses, resveratrol exhibits estrogenic activity by binding to estrogen receptors (ERalpha and ERbeta). While low-to-moderate doses (150-500 mg) show selective estrogen receptor modulation (potentially beneficial), doses exceeding 1,000 mg/day may have unpredictable hormonal effects. This is particularly concerning for individuals with estrogen-sensitive conditions (breast cancer, endometriosis, uterine fibroids). Stick to clinically studied dose ranges
- **Combining with blood thinners without medical supervision:** Resveratrol has antiplatelet and anticoagulant properties (inhibits platelet aggregation, reduces thromboxane A2 synthesis). Combining with warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel, or other anticoagulants/antiplatelets increases bleeding risk. If combining, do so only with physician oversight and regular INR/coagulation monitoring. Discontinue resveratrol 2 weeks before scheduled surgery
- **Light exposure (storing supplements in light):** Trans-resveratrol undergoes photoisomerization when exposed to UV and visible light, converting to cis-resveratrol, which has reduced biological activity. Supplements should be stored in opaque, dark containers away from direct sunlight and fluorescent lighting. Do not transfer resveratrol to clear pill organizers for extended periods
- **Heat exposure:** Resveratrol degrades at sustained temperatures above 70 degrees C (158 degrees F). Do not store supplements in hot cars, near stoves, or in direct sunlight. Do not add resveratrol powder to hot beverages or cooked foods. Room temperature storage is fine, but refrigeration extends shelf life
- **Oxygen exposure:** Resveratrol is susceptible to oxidative degradation. Keep supplement bottles tightly sealed. Do not leave capsules exposed to air for extended periods. Once a bottle is opened, consume within the recommended timeframe (typically 3-6 months)
- **Taking on a completely empty stomach:** Resveratrol is fat-soluble and poorly absorbed without dietary fat. Taking it in a fasted state reduces bioavailability and may cause mild GI discomfort (nausea, stomach upset) in some individuals. Always pair with a fat-containing meal or snack
- **Excessive alcohol consumption while supplementing:** While resveratrol is cardioprotective, pairing supplementation with heavy alcohol use is counterproductive. Alcohol generates acetaldehyde and free radicals that overwhelm the antioxidant benefit. Alcohol also impairs liver function where resveratrol is metabolized, potentially altering its pharmacokinetics unpredictably
- **Combining with high-dose NSAID regimens:** Both resveratrol and NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) inhibit COX enzymes and platelet aggregation. Concurrent use may increase GI bleeding risk and amplify anticoagulant effects beyond what either substance would cause alone
- **Assuming wine provides therapeutic doses:** The most common misconception. Even the highest-resveratrol wines contain only ~5-6 mg per glass. Therapeutic doses are 150-500 mg/day. The alcohol and sugar content of 30-100+ glasses of wine would cause catastrophic health damage long before any resveratrol benefit was achieved. Use standardized supplements instead
