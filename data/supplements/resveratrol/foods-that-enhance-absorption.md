# Resveratrol
## Foods That Enhance Absorption

- **Fat-containing foods:** Resveratrol is lipophilic (fat-soluble), meaning it dissolves in and is transported by dietary fats. Taking resveratrol with a meal containing healthy fats (avocado, olive oil, nuts, fatty fish) significantly improves intestinal absorption. Fat triggers bile salt release, which emulsifies the resveratrol and increases its surface area for absorption across the intestinal lining. Studies show 2-5x improved absorption when taken with a fat-containing meal versus on an empty stomach
- **Quercetin-rich foods (onions, apples, capers, berries):** Quercetin inhibits the sulfotransferase and glucuronosyltransferase enzymes in the gut wall and liver that rapidly metabolize resveratrol into inactive conjugates (sulfates and glucuronides). By competing for the same phase II metabolism enzymes, quercetin effectively slows resveratrol breakdown and increases the amount of free (unconjugated) resveratrol that reaches systemic circulation. Some studies show quercetin co-administration doubles resveratrol bioavailability
- **Piperine-containing foods (black pepper):** Piperine inhibits hepatic and intestinal glucuronidation, one of the primary pathways that inactivates resveratrol during first-pass metabolism. Piperine also inhibits CYP3A4, slowing oxidative metabolism. Studies on piperine with other polyphenols (notably curcumin) show up to 2,000% bioavailability improvement, and a similar mechanism applies to resveratrol, though the magnitude is less dramatic (~1.5-2x improvement)
- **Olive oil:** Beyond its role as a fat source, olive oil contains oleocanthal and hydroxytyrosol, polyphenols that share similar absorption pathways and may have synergistic antioxidant effects. The monounsaturated fat (oleic acid) in olive oil is particularly effective at forming micelles that transport resveratrol across the gut lining
- **Curcumin-containing foods (turmeric):** Curcumin and resveratrol share overlapping metabolic pathways (both undergo extensive glucuronidation and sulfation). When taken together, they compete for the same conjugation enzymes, mutually slowing each other's metabolism and increasing circulating levels of both compounds. The combination also provides synergistic anti-inflammatory effects through complementary NF-kB inhibition
- **Grape seed extract:** Contains proanthocyanidins that may slow resveratrol metabolism through enzyme competition and also provide synergistic cardiovascular benefits. Historically co-present with resveratrol in whole grape consumption, suggesting evolutionary co-optimization of absorption

**Key takeaway:** Always take resveratrol with a fat-containing meal. Adding quercetin (500 mg) or piperine (5-10 mg) as co-supplements is the most practical strategy to meaningfully improve resveratrol bioavailability beyond food-based approaches.
