# L-Tyrosine
## Foods to Avoid

### High-Protein Meals (Timing Conflict, Not a Ban)
- **Don't take tyrosine WITH a high-protein meal** when the goal is the cognitive/stress effect — the competing large neutral amino acids (phenylalanine, tryptophan, BCAAs) outcompete tyrosine at the blood-brain barrier and blunt brain uptake
- **This is a timing issue, not a prohibition** — protein is healthy and supplies tyrosine for general needs; just separate supplemental tyrosine from protein-heavy meals by an hour or two

### Tyramine-Rich Foods (Only If on MAOIs)
- **If taking an MAOI**, aged cheeses, cured meats, fermented foods, and other tyramine-rich foods are dangerous (hypertensive crisis risk) — and tyrosine supplementation compounds the catecholamine concern. This is a drug-interaction issue (see drug-interactions), relevant specifically to MAOI users.
- **For people not on MAOIs**, tyramine-containing foods are not a tyrosine-specific concern

### Stimulant Overload
- **Excess caffeine or other stimulants alongside high-dose tyrosine** can compound catecholamine-driven overstimulation (jitteriness, anxiety, elevated heart rate). Not a chemical "food to avoid," but a sensible limit when stacking.

### Things That Are Irrelevant
- **There are no foods that chemically destroy or block tyrosine** outside the amino acid competition issue
- **Acidic foods, fiber, and fat** do not impair tyrosine absorption into the body (only protein competition affects BRAIN uptake)

### Timing With Other Supplements
- **Separate from other large-dose amino acids** (e.g., a big BCAA or tryptophan dose) when the goal is brain tyrosine, since they compete for the same transporter
- **Pairs fine with caffeine, L-theanine, and Alpha-GPC** in cognitive stacks (see peptide-synergies) — these don't compete at the LNAA transporter

### Bottom Line
- The practical rule is simple: **take tyrosine away from protein**, moderate stimulants if sensitive, and (for MAOI users only) heed the tyramine-food warning as a medical issue.
