# L-Tyrosine
## Benefits

### Cognition Under Acute Stress (Strongest Evidence)
- **Preserves working memory and cognitive flexibility** during demanding multitasking and high cognitive load (Colzato et al., 2013; 2014)
- **Maintains performance under environmental stress** — cold exposure, loud noise, and combined stressors (Banderet & Lieberman, 1989, a foundational military study)
- **Improves response inhibition and task-switching** under cognitively demanding conditions
- **Key nuance:** these benefits appear specifically when stress depletes catecholamines — not in calm, rested baseline states

### Sleep Deprivation
- **Counteracts cognitive decline during sleep loss** — tyrosine improved performance and alertness in sleep-deprived individuals (e.g., overnight military operations; Neri et al., 1995)
- **Useful for occasional all-nighters or shift work** as a non-stimulant aid to maintain function
- **Not a replacement for sleep** — it buffers performance, it does not restore the recovery sleep provides

### Stress Resilience & Performance
- **Maintains performance under physical and psychological stressors** — cold, demanding tasks, acute stress challenges
- **Reduces stress-related performance decrements** rather than enhancing peak performance in easy conditions

### Mood (Limited / Context-Dependent)
- **May support mood under acute stress** by maintaining catecholamine availability
- **Weak evidence for chronic depression or daily mood enhancement** — tyrosine is not a reliable antidepressant; studies in non-stressed or depressed populations have been largely disappointing
- **Honest framing:** do not expect daily mood lift in a well-rested person

### As a Non-Stimulant Cognitive Adjunct
- **Stacks with or substitutes for caffeine** — provides catecholamine substrate without the jitteriness or crash of stimulants
- **Pairs well with L-theanine and Alpha-GPC** for a broader cognitive stack (see peptide-synergies)

### Secondary Roles
- **Thyroid support** — tyrosine is a precursor to thyroid hormones, though supplementation is not a treatment for thyroid disorders
- **Phenylketonuria (PKU)** — tyrosine becomes essential in PKU (where phenylalanine cannot be metabolized); used under medical supervision

### Realistic Expectations
- **Tyrosine is a situational tool, not a daily booster** — its benefits are tightly tied to acute stress and catecholamine depletion
- **Best used before a known stressor** (exam, deadline, cold exposure, sleep-deprived shift) rather than taken continuously
- **Effects are acute** — felt within ~30-60 minutes of a dose, not cumulative over weeks
