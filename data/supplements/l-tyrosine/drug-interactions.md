# L-Tyrosine
## Drug Interactions

### Contraindicated / Avoid Without Medical Supervision
- **MAOIs (monoamine oxidase inhibitors — phenelzine, tranylcypromine, selegiline, isocarboxazid)** — MAOIs prevent the breakdown of catecholamines and tyramine. Adding tyrosine (a catecholamine precursor) can drive a buildup of norepinephrine and risk a hypertensive crisis (dangerously high blood pressure). This combination is contraindicated.
- **Levodopa (L-DOPA, for Parkinson's disease)** — tyrosine and levodopa compete for the same intestinal and blood-brain-barrier transporters. Taking tyrosine can reduce levodopa absorption and effectiveness. Separate by several hours and consult a neurologist; generally avoid concurrent dosing.

### Moderate Interactions (Use with Caution / Monitor)
- **Thyroid medications (levothyroxine, liothyronine) and thyroid conditions** — tyrosine is a precursor to thyroid hormones (T3/T4). In hyperthyroidism or with thyroid replacement, supplemental tyrosine could theoretically affect thyroid hormone levels. Monitor and consult a physician; in practice, separate dosing and avoid in uncontrolled hyperthyroidism.
- **Stimulants (amphetamines, methylphenidate, high-dose caffeine)** — additive catecholaminergic stimulation; may increase the risk of overstimulation, elevated heart rate, or blood pressure. Use caution and monitor.

### Mild Interactions (Monitor)
- **Antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs)** — no well-established dangerous interaction, but combining agents that affect monoamine systems warrants awareness; monitor for overstimulation and consult a prescriber. (The serious monoamine concern is specifically with MAOIs, above.)
- **Blood pressure medications** — tyrosine can mildly raise catecholamines; theoretically opposing antihypertensive effects in sensitive individuals. Monitor.

### Timing-Sensitive Notes
- **Take tyrosine hours apart from levodopa and from thyroid medication** — transporter competition (levodopa) and the thyroid-precursor relationship make spacing prudent
- **Separate from large doses of other amino acids** — competition at the blood-brain-barrier transporter; space them for the cognitive effect

### Population Cautions
- **Hyperthyroidism / Graves' disease** — caution; thyroid hormone precursor
- **Melanoma history** — tyrosine is a melanin precursor; theoretical caution, discuss with an oncologist
- **Bipolar disorder / psychiatric conditions** — catecholamine substrate could affect mood states; consult a prescriber
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data; consult a healthcare provider
- **Phenylketonuria (PKU)** — tyrosine becomes essential and is used therapeutically under medical supervision

### Safety Notes
- **The MAOI interaction is the most important to flag** — it can be dangerous (hypertensive crisis)
- **Always inform healthcare providers** of tyrosine use, especially if taking MAOIs, levodopa, thyroid medication, or stimulants
- **This is educational information, not medical advice** — review interactions with a pharmacist or physician
