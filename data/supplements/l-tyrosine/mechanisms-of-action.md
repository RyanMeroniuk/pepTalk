# L-Tyrosine
## Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Catecholamine Synthesis (Primary Mechanism)
- **Tyrosine is the precursor in the catecholamine pathway:** Tyrosine → L-DOPA → Dopamine → Norepinephrine → Epinephrine
- **The rate-limiting enzyme is tyrosine hydroxylase**, which converts tyrosine to L-DOPA
- Under normal conditions tyrosine hydroxylase is saturated, so extra tyrosine does little — this is why tyrosine has minimal effect in unstressed people
- **Under high demand**, when neurons fire rapidly and deplete catecholamine stores, substrate availability becomes more limiting, and supplemental tyrosine supports continued synthesis

### 2. The "Substrate Depletion" Model (Why Stress Matters)
- **Acute stressors (cold, noise, sleep loss, cognitive overload) accelerate catecholamine release and turnover**
- When release outpaces synthesis, dopamine and norepinephrine levels fall, degrading cognitive performance
- **Supplemental tyrosine replenishes the substrate pool**, helping maintain neurotransmitter output and preserving performance that would otherwise decline
- This explains the consistent finding: tyrosine helps under stress, not at baseline

### 3. Working Memory and Cognitive Flexibility
- **Dopamine in the prefrontal cortex underlies working memory and cognitive flexibility**
- By supporting dopamine availability under load, tyrosine helps preserve these executive functions during demanding multitasking (Colzato et al., 2013; 2014)
- Norepinephrine supports alertness and attention, also preserved under stress with tyrosine

### 4. Crossing the Blood-Brain Barrier
- **Tyrosine competes with other large neutral amino acids (LNAAs)** — phenylalanine, tryptophan, leucine, valine — for the LAT1 transporter into the brain
- **Taking tyrosine away from high-protein meals** (which flood the blood with competing LNAAs) improves its entry into the brain — a key practical dosing point
- An empty-stomach or carbohydrate-paired dose favors tyrosine uptake

### 5. Thyroid Hormone and Melanin Precursor (Secondary)
- **Tyrosine is a building block for thyroid hormones (T3 and T4)** — iodine is added to tyrosine residues
- **Tyrosine is also the precursor to melanin** (skin/hair pigment) via the enzyme tyrosinase
- These are background roles, not the basis for cognitive supplementation

### 6. Why Effects Are Acute, Not Cumulative
- Tyrosine works as an on-demand substrate buffer; it does not build up a reservoir or produce lasting changes with chronic dosing
- This is why it is typically dosed acutely before a stressor rather than taken as a daily maintenance nootropic
