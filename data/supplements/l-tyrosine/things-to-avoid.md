# L-Tyrosine
## Things to Avoid

### Misconceptions to Avoid
- **Don't expect a daily mood or motivation boost** — tyrosine's evidence is for preserving cognition under ACUTE stress (sleep loss, cold, heavy cognitive load), not for everyday mood enhancement in rested people. This is the most common misunderstanding.
- **Don't treat it as a stimulant** — tyrosine is a substrate, not a stimulant; it won't reliably produce energy or euphoria on its own
- **Don't assume NALT is better** — N-Acetyl-L-Tyrosine is more soluble but converts to tyrosine less efficiently and may raise plasma tyrosine less than plain L-tyrosine

### Dosing & Timing Mistakes
- **Taking it with a high-protein meal** — competing amino acids blunt brain uptake; take it away from protein
- **Taking it late in the day** — its catecholamine-substrate effect can interfere with sleep in some people
- **Daily baseline dosing in healthy people** — little benefit; reserve it for known upcoming stressors
- **Megadosing routinely** — high doses (7+ g) raise the chance of GI upset, headache, and overstimulation without added benefit for most situations

### Population-Specific Cautions
- **Hyperthyroidism / Graves' disease** — tyrosine is a thyroid hormone precursor; could theoretically worsen overactivity. Avoid or consult a physician.
- **Melanoma history** — tyrosine is a melanin precursor; theoretical caution, discuss with an oncologist
- **Bipolar disorder** — catecholamine substrate could theoretically affect mood states; consult a prescriber
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data; consult a healthcare provider

### Drug Cautions (See drug-interactions)
- **MAOIs** — avoid; serious hypertensive interaction risk via catecholamine pathways
- **Levodopa** — tyrosine and levodopa compete for the same transporter; can interfere with Parkinson's medication absorption
- **Thyroid medication (levothyroxine)** — theoretical interaction and timing considerations

### Behaviors That Undermine Benefits
- **Stacking with heavy stimulants** while sensitive — compounds overstimulation
- **Using it to replace sleep** — it buffers cognition during sleep loss but does not provide recovery; chronic reliance is counterproductive
- **Expecting cumulative effects** — tyrosine works acutely; there is no benefit reservoir to build

### Medical Note
- **This is educational information, not medical advice.** People with thyroid, psychiatric, or neurological conditions, or on the medications above, should consult a healthcare provider before use.
