# L-Tyrosine
## Natural Food Sources

### High-Protein Animal Sources
- **Cheese (especially aged, e.g., Parmesan)** — among the richest sources; "tyrosine" is named from the Greek *tyros* (cheese), where it was first isolated. ~1.5-2 g per 100 g in hard cheeses.
- **Beef, pork, lamb** — ~1.0-1.6 g per 100 g
- **Chicken and turkey** — ~1.0-1.5 g per 100 g
- **Fish (salmon, tuna, cod)** — ~0.7-1.5 g per 100 g
- **Eggs** — ~0.5 g per egg

### Plant Sources
- **Soybeans / soy products (tofu, tempeh, edamame)** — ~1.0-1.5 g per 100 g; among the best plant sources
- **Legumes (lentils, chickpeas, beans)** — moderate amounts
- **Nuts and seeds (pumpkin seeds, sesame, peanuts, almonds)** — moderate amounts
- **Whole grains** — smaller amounts

### Dairy
- **Milk and yogurt** — moderate amounts; dairy protein (casein, whey) is tyrosine-rich

### Important Context
- **Tyrosine is abundant in any adequate-protein diet** — deficiency is rare in healthy people eating enough protein
- **Tyrosine also derives from phenylalanine** — the body converts the essential amino acid phenylalanine to tyrosine, so even diets lower in direct tyrosine usually supply enough via this route
- **The catch for cognitive use:** eating tyrosine within a high-protein meal does NOT efficiently raise BRAIN tyrosine, because the meal floods the blood with competing large neutral amino acids that share tyrosine's transporter into the brain
- **This is the paradox** — food provides plenty of tyrosine for general needs, but supplemental tyrosine taken AWAY from protein is what raises brain levels for the acute cognitive/stress effect

### Supplementation vs Food
- **For general nutrition:** food easily covers tyrosine needs
- **For the acute cognitive/stress effect:** isolated supplemental L-tyrosine taken away from protein is required — you cannot replicate the brain-uptake effect by eating cheese, because the accompanying amino acids compete at the blood-brain barrier
