# L-Tyrosine
## Foods That Enhance Absorption

### The Key Principle: Reduce Competition at the Blood-Brain Barrier
- **Tyrosine competes with other large neutral amino acids (LNAAs)** — phenylalanine, tryptophan, leucine, isoleucine, valine — for the LAT1 transporter into the brain
- **The single most important "absorption" lever is taking tyrosine AWAY from protein**, so it isn't outcompeted by other amino acids entering the brain
- This is the opposite of most supplements — here, an empty stomach (or carb-only pairing) is the optimization

### Carbohydrates (Favor Tyrosine Uptake)
- **A small carbohydrate dose triggers insulin**, which drives branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs) into muscle, lowering their blood levels
- **This reduces competition** for the brain transporter, modestly favoring tyrosine's entry
- **Practical pairing:** take tyrosine with a small amount of juice or fruit (not a protein-rich meal) when the goal is brain uptake

### Cofactors for Catecholamine Synthesis (Support the Pathway)
- **Vitamin B6 (pyridoxal-5-phosphate)** — cofactor for aromatic amino acid decarboxylase (DOPA → dopamine); poultry, fish, bananas, potatoes
- **Vitamin C** — cofactor for dopamine beta-hydroxylase (dopamine → norepinephrine); citrus, peppers, berries
- **Copper** — also a cofactor for dopamine beta-hydroxylase
- **Folate and iron** — support tyrosine hydroxylase (the rate-limiting enzyme) and overall catecholamine metabolism
- Adequate status of these cofactors ensures the tyrosine you provide can actually be converted to neurotransmitters

### Practical Pairings
- **Empty stomach, ~30-60 min before the stressor** — the default for cognitive use
- **Optionally with a little juice/fruit** (carbs) to further reduce amino acid competition
- **Ensure adequate B6, vitamin C, folate, and copper** in the diet to support the conversion pathway

### What Works AGAINST Absorption
- **High-protein meals** — flood the blood with competing LNAAs and blunt tyrosine's brain uptake; this is the main thing to avoid timing-wise

### Bottom Line
- **Take tyrosine away from protein, ideally on an empty stomach or with a little carbohydrate**, and keep B6/vitamin C/copper/folate adequate so the substrate can be converted. Timing relative to protein matters more than any food you add.
