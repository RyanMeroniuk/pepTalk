# Magnesium
## Mechanisms of Action

### ATP and Energy Production
- **Every ATP molecule** in the body exists as Mg-ATP complex; magnesium is required for ATP to be biologically active
- Magnesium is a cofactor for enzymes in glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation
- Without adequate magnesium, cellular energy production is fundamentally impaired
- This is why fatigue is the most universal symptom of magnesium deficiency

### Neuromuscular Function
- **Calcium channel regulation:** Magnesium acts as a natural calcium channel blocker, preventing excessive calcium influx into cells
- **Muscle relaxation:** Calcium causes muscle contraction; magnesium enables relaxation. Inadequate magnesium leads to cramps, spasms, and tension.
- **Nerve transmission:** Magnesium modulates sodium-potassium ATPase pumps that maintain nerve cell membrane potential
- **Neuromuscular junction:** Magnesium regulates acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction

### GABA and Sleep Regulation
- **GABA receptor agonism:** Magnesium binds to GABA-A receptors, enhancing the effect of GABA (the brain's primary inhibitory/calming neurotransmitter)
- **NMDA receptor antagonism:** Magnesium blocks NMDA glutamate receptors (excitatory), reducing neuronal excitability — this is why magnesium calms anxiety, reduces pain perception, and promotes sleep
- **Melatonin synthesis:** Magnesium is a cofactor in the conversion of tryptophan to serotonin and serotonin to melatonin
- **HPA axis modulation:** Magnesium regulates the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis, attenuating cortisol release in response to stress

### Cardiovascular Function
- **Vascular smooth muscle relaxation:** Magnesium relaxes blood vessel walls by opposing calcium-mediated contraction, reducing blood pressure
- **Antiarrhythmic effects:** Magnesium stabilizes cardiac cell membranes and regulates potassium and calcium fluxes that control heart rhythm
- **Endothelial function:** Magnesium supports nitric oxide (NO) production, promoting vasodilation
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Magnesium reduces C-reactive protein (CRP) and other inflammatory markers

### Insulin and Glucose Metabolism
- **Insulin receptor function:** Magnesium is required for insulin receptor tyrosine kinase activity — the key step in insulin signaling
- **GLUT4 translocation:** Magnesium supports the translocation of glucose transporters to the cell surface, enabling glucose uptake
- **Beta-cell function:** Magnesium is required for insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells
- **Bidirectional relationship:** Low magnesium causes insulin resistance, and insulin resistance causes magnesium depletion via increased renal excretion

### Bone Metabolism
- **Hydroxyapatite formation:** Magnesium is incorporated into the bone crystal lattice, affecting bone quality and density
- **Vitamin D activation:** Magnesium is required for the conversion of vitamin D to its active form (1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D) — vitamin D supplementation without adequate magnesium is less effective
- **PTH regulation:** Magnesium modulates parathyroid hormone secretion, which controls calcium homeostasis
- **Osteoblast/osteoclast balance:** Magnesium supports bone-building osteoblast activity

### DNA and Protein Synthesis
- **DNA stability:** Magnesium stabilizes DNA and RNA structure
- **DNA repair:** Cofactor for DNA repair enzymes including topoisomerases and helicases
- **Ribosomal function:** Required for ribosome assembly and protein translation
- **Cell division:** Essential for normal mitosis
