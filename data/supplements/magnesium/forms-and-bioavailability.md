# Magnesium
## Forms and Bioavailability

This is one of the most important supplement topics because different magnesium forms have dramatically different effects, absorption rates, and target tissues.

### Comprehensive Form Guide

#### 1. Magnesium Glycinate (Bisglycinate)
- **Bioavailability:** High (~80% relative absorption)
- **Elemental magnesium:** ~14% by weight
- **What it is:** Magnesium chelated to two molecules of the amino acid glycine
- **Best for:** Sleep, anxiety, muscle relaxation, daily supplementation
- **Why:** Glycine itself is an inhibitory neurotransmitter that enhances GABA activity and promotes calm. The combination of magnesium + glycine provides a synergistic calming effect.
- **GI tolerance:** Excellent — rarely causes loose stools because it is absorbed efficiently, not osmotically active
- **Dose:** 200-400mg elemental magnesium, taken 30-60 minutes before bed
- **Who should use it:** Anyone prioritizing sleep, anxiety reduction, or general magnesium repletion without GI side effects
- **Top pick for:** Overall best daily magnesium supplement

#### 2. Magnesium L-Threonate (Magtein)
- **Bioavailability:** Moderate-high (for brain tissue specifically)
- **Elemental magnesium:** ~8% by weight (very low — need high doses for meaningful elemental magnesium)
- **What it is:** Magnesium bound to L-threonic acid, a metabolite of vitamin C. Patented form.
- **Best for:** Cognitive function, brain health, memory, focus, neurological conditions
- **Why:** The only form clinically shown to cross the blood-brain barrier efficiently and raise brain magnesium levels. A MIT study (Slutsky et al., 2010) showed it enhanced synaptic density and improved learning and memory in animal models.
- **GI tolerance:** Excellent
- **Dose:** 1,500-2,000mg magnesium L-threonate (= ~144mg elemental magnesium); typically split into morning and evening doses
- **Who should use it:** Nootropic users, anyone focused on cognitive optimization, people with brain fog or age-related cognitive decline
- **Limitation:** Low elemental magnesium content means it may not adequately address systemic magnesium deficiency. Often combined with another form.

#### 3. Magnesium Citrate
- **Bioavailability:** Good (~25-30% absorption)
- **Elemental magnesium:** ~16% by weight
- **What it is:** Magnesium bound to citric acid
- **Best for:** General supplementation, constipation relief, migraine prevention
- **Why:** Well-absorbed, affordable, and widely available. Has mild osmotic laxative effect.
- **GI tolerance:** Moderate — can cause loose stools or diarrhea at higher doses (this is a feature for constipation, a bug for other uses)
- **Dose:** 200-400mg elemental magnesium for general use; 400-600mg for migraine prevention
- **Who should use it:** People who need magnesium AND tend toward constipation; migraine sufferers (most studied form for migraines)
- **Caution:** Avoid if you have loose stools or IBS-D

#### 4. Magnesium Taurate
- **Bioavailability:** Good
- **Elemental magnesium:** ~9% by weight
- **What it is:** Magnesium bound to the amino acid taurine
- **Best for:** Cardiovascular health, blood pressure, heart rhythm, blood sugar
- **Why:** Taurine itself supports cardiac function, blood pressure regulation, and insulin sensitivity. The combination targets cardiovascular and metabolic health specifically.
- **GI tolerance:** Excellent
- **Dose:** 200-400mg elemental magnesium (requires high dose due to low elemental content)
- **Who should use it:** Anyone with cardiovascular concerns, hypertension, arrhythmias, or diabetes
- **Note:** Taurine also supports GABA activity, so this form has secondary calming effects

#### 5. Magnesium Malate
- **Bioavailability:** Good
- **Elemental magnesium:** ~15% by weight
- **What it is:** Magnesium bound to malic acid
- **Best for:** Energy production, fatigue, fibromyalgia, muscle pain
- **Why:** Malic acid is a key intermediate in the Krebs cycle (cellular energy production). The combination supports ATP synthesis and may reduce muscle pain and fatigue.
- **GI tolerance:** Good
- **Dose:** 200-400mg elemental magnesium
- **Who should use it:** People with fatigue, fibromyalgia, or those who want an energizing magnesium (better for morning/daytime use than glycinate)
- **Note:** Less sedating than glycinate — good daytime option

#### 6. Magnesium Orotate
- **Bioavailability:** Good
- **Elemental magnesium:** ~7% by weight
- **What it is:** Magnesium bound to orotic acid
- **Best for:** Heart health, athletic endurance, cellular repair
- **Why:** Orotic acid enhances the transport of magnesium into cells, particularly cardiac cells. Orotic acid is also involved in pyrimidine synthesis (DNA/RNA building blocks).
- **GI tolerance:** Good
- **Dose:** 200-400mg elemental magnesium
- **Who should use it:** Athletes, cardiac patients, those focused on cellular repair
- **Limitation:** Expensive; limited clinical data compared to other forms

#### 7. Magnesium Oxide
- **Bioavailability:** Poor (~4% absorption rate)
- **Elemental magnesium:** ~60% by weight (highest of any form)
- **What it is:** Simple inorganic magnesium salt
- **Best for:** Constipation relief, acid reflux (antacid), when bioavailability does not matter
- **Why it is popular:** Very cheap to manufacture and contains the most elemental magnesium by weight, so labels look impressive. But only ~4% is absorbed.
- **GI tolerance:** Poor — strong osmotic laxative effect; frequently causes diarrhea
- **Dose:** 400-800mg for constipation
- **Who should use it:** Only for constipation relief; NOT for correcting magnesium deficiency
- **Avoid for:** Daily supplementation, sleep, muscle cramps, or any therapeutic purpose requiring absorbed magnesium

#### 8. Magnesium Chloride
- **Bioavailability:** Good (~12-20% oral; excellent topical)
- **Elemental magnesium:** ~12% by weight
- **What it is:** Magnesium salt with chloride
- **Best for:** Topical application (magnesium oil, flakes for bath/foot soak), muscle soreness
- **Why:** Absorbs well through the skin; bypasses GI tract entirely
- **Topical protocol:** Apply magnesium oil to legs, feet, or sore muscles before bed; or dissolve magnesium chloride flakes in a warm bath (2-3 cups)
- **Who should use it:** People with GI issues who cannot tolerate oral magnesium; athletes for localized muscle recovery

#### 9. Magnesium Sulfate (Epsom Salt)
- **Bioavailability:** Poor orally; decent transdermally
- **What it is:** Magnesium + sulfate
- **Best for:** Bath soaks for muscle relaxation and soreness
- **Note:** Not recommended for oral supplementation. Primarily used in baths (1-2 cups Epsom salt) or clinically as IV magnesium for preeclampsia and cardiac emergencies.

### Quick Reference Guide

| Goal | Best Form | Dose (elemental) |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| Sleep + relaxation | Glycinate | 200-400mg before bed |
| Brain + cognition | L-Threonate | 144mg (1500-2000mg threonate) |
| General daily use | Glycinate or Citrate | 200-400mg |
| Constipation | Citrate or Oxide | 200-600mg |
| Migraines | Citrate | 400-600mg |
| Heart health | Taurate | 200-400mg |
| Energy + fatigue | Malate | 200-400mg |
| Muscle recovery | Glycinate (oral) + Chloride (topical) | 200-400mg + topical |
| Athletic performance | Malate or Orotate | 200-400mg |
| Blood sugar | Taurate or Glycinate | 200-400mg |

### Stacking Multiple Forms
Many people benefit from combining forms to target multiple goals:
- **Sleep stack:** Glycinate (300mg) + L-Threonate (1000mg) at bedtime
- **Performance stack:** Malate (200mg) in morning + Glycinate (300mg) at night
- **Cognitive stack:** L-Threonate (1000mg morning + 1000mg evening) + Glycinate (200mg evening)
- **Heart health stack:** Taurate (200mg) + Glycinate (200mg)

### Key Considerations
- **Total daily elemental magnesium** from all forms combined should generally stay within 400-600mg from supplements
- **Chelated forms** (glycinate, threonate, taurate, malate) are generally better absorbed and gentler on the stomach than salt forms (oxide, sulfate, chloride)
- **The "bowel tolerance" method:** Gradually increase magnesium dose until stools become loose, then back off slightly — that is your individual absorption ceiling
