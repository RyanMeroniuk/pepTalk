# Magnesium
## Things to Avoid

### Using Magnesium Oxide for Therapeutic Purposes
- **Why:** Magnesium oxide has only ~4% bioavailability. A 500mg magnesium oxide pill delivers only ~20mg of absorbable magnesium. The rest passes through the GI tract, causing diarrhea.
- **Exception:** Intentional use as a laxative
- **Solution:** Choose glycinate, citrate, taurate, malate, or L-threonate depending on your goal

### Taking All Magnesium at Once
- **Why:** The body absorbs magnesium more efficiently in smaller doses. Absorption decreases at higher single doses, and the osmotic load can trigger diarrhea.
- **Solution:** Split doses — take 200mg morning, 200-300mg evening. Or at minimum, do not exceed 350mg elemental in a single dose.

### Relying on Serum Magnesium Tests
- **Why:** Only 0.3% of total body magnesium is in serum. Serum levels are tightly regulated and will appear normal even when tissue stores are significantly depleted. A "normal" serum magnesium does not rule out deficiency.
- **Solution:** Request RBC (red blood cell) magnesium test instead. Optimal RBC magnesium: 5.5-6.5 mg/dL. Some practitioners also use intracellular magnesium or magnesium loading tests.

### Taking Magnesium at the Same Time as Certain Medications
- **Why:** Magnesium chelates tetracycline and quinolone antibiotics, bisphosphonates, and thyroid medications, dramatically reducing their absorption
- **Solution:** Separate magnesium from these medications by at least 2-4 hours

### Chronic Stress Without Magnesium Repletion
- **Why:** Stress hormones (cortisol, adrenaline, noradrenaline) cause renal magnesium wasting. Simultaneously, magnesium deficiency amplifies the stress response by increasing HPA axis reactivity. This creates a self-reinforcing depletion cycle.
- **Solution:** People under chronic stress should prioritize magnesium supplementation (400-600mg/day) alongside stress management practices

### Excessive Sweating Without Replacement
- **Why:** Magnesium is lost through sweat (6-25mg per liter of sweat, depending on magnesium status). Athletes, sauna users, and people in hot climates can lose significant amounts.
- **Solution:** Supplement magnesium on training days and after sauna. Consider adding magnesium to electrolyte drinks.

### Taking Magnesium With High-Dose Zinc Without Monitoring
- **Why:** While magnesium and zinc are often taken together (e.g., ZMA formula), very high doses of zinc (>50mg) can interfere with magnesium absorption
- **Solution:** At standard supplemental doses (15-30mg zinc, 200-400mg magnesium), this is not a significant concern. Only relevant at high therapeutic doses.

### Long-Term PPI Use Without Monitoring
- **Why:** The FDA issued a safety communication in 2011 warning that proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole, etc.) used for >1 year can cause hypomagnesemia. PPIs impair both passive and active magnesium absorption in the intestine.
- **Solution:** Anyone on a PPI for more than 3 months should supplement magnesium (chelated form, since stomach acid is reduced) and monitor RBC magnesium levels

### Ignoring Co-Factor Nutrients
- **Why:** Magnesium works synergistically with vitamin D, vitamin K2, vitamin B6, and calcium. Supplementing magnesium alone while deficient in these cofactors limits benefits.
- **Solution:** Ensure adequate vitamin D (3,000-5,000 IU/day), vitamin K2 (100-200mcg/day MK-7), and B6 (25-50mg/day) alongside magnesium

### Using Magnesium to Mask Underlying Conditions
- **Why:** Chronic muscle cramps, insomnia, anxiety, and heart palpitations can have causes beyond magnesium deficiency (thyroid disorders, electrolyte imbalances, cardiac conditions)
- **Solution:** If symptoms persist after 4-6 weeks of adequate magnesium supplementation, seek medical evaluation
