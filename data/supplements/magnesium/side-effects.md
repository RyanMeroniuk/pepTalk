# Magnesium
## Side Effects

### Common Side Effects
- **Loose stools or diarrhea** — The most common side effect, especially with magnesium oxide, citrate, and sulfate. Chelated forms (glycinate, threonate, taurate) rarely cause this.
- **Abdominal cramping** — Related to the osmotic effect of poorly absorbed forms drawing water into the intestines
- **Nausea** — Occasionally reported, usually with high doses on an empty stomach
- **Drowsiness** — Particularly with glycinate and threonate; this is a feature for sleep but can be unwanted during daytime

### Less Common Side Effects
- **Low blood pressure** — Magnesium relaxes blood vessels; individuals on blood pressure medication should monitor for hypotension
- **Reduced heart rate** — Magnesium slows conduction through the AV node; usually beneficial but can be excessive in those with existing bradycardia
- **Fatigue** — At very high doses, the relaxant effect can feel like excessive tiredness
- **Headache** — Rarely reported; may occur during initial supplementation

### Serious Side Effects (Rare — Generally Only with Excessive Dosing or Renal Impairment)

**Hypermagnesemia (magnesium toxicity):**
- Occurs almost exclusively in people with impaired kidney function who cannot excrete excess magnesium
- Also possible with massive oral overdose or IV administration errors
- **Early symptoms (serum Mg 4-6 mg/dL):** Nausea, vomiting, facial flushing, low blood pressure, drowsiness
- **Moderate toxicity (serum Mg 6-12 mg/dL):** Loss of deep tendon reflexes, muscle weakness, heart rhythm changes (prolonged PR interval, widened QRS)
- **Severe toxicity (serum Mg >12 mg/dL):** Respiratory depression, cardiac arrest, coma
- **Note:** This is extremely rare from oral supplementation in healthy individuals. The kidneys efficiently excrete excess magnesium.

### Who Should Be Cautious
- **Kidney disease:** Impaired renal function is the primary risk factor for magnesium toxicity. People with GFR <30 should consult their doctor before supplementing.
- **Heart block:** Magnesium can worsen certain conduction abnormalities
- **Myasthenia gravis:** Magnesium may exacerbate muscle weakness
- **People taking medications that reduce magnesium excretion:** Potassium-sparing diuretics, lithium

### GI Tolerance by Form (Best to Worst)
1. **Glycinate** — Rarely causes GI issues
2. **L-Threonate** — Rarely causes GI issues
3. **Taurate** — Rarely causes GI issues
4. **Malate** — Occasional mild GI effects
5. **Orotate** — Occasional mild GI effects
6. **Citrate** — Moderate GI effects (dose-dependent)
7. **Chloride** — Moderate GI effects
8. **Sulfate** — Significant laxative effect
9. **Oxide** — Strong laxative effect; frequent diarrhea
