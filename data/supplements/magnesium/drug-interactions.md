# Magnesium
## Drug Interactions

### Major Interactions (Avoid Concurrent Use or Separate Significantly)

**Quinolone antibiotics (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, moxifloxacin):**
- **Interaction:** Magnesium chelates quinolones, reducing antibiotic absorption by up to 90%
- **Management:** Take magnesium at least 2 hours before or 6 hours after quinolone antibiotics

**Tetracycline antibiotics (doxycycline, minocycline, tetracycline):**
- **Interaction:** Same chelation mechanism; magnesium forms insoluble complexes with tetracyclines
- **Management:** Separate by at least 2 hours before or 4-6 hours after

**Bisphosphonates (alendronate, risedronate, ibandronate):**
- **Interaction:** Magnesium significantly reduces bisphosphonate absorption
- **Management:** Take bisphosphonates at least 2 hours before magnesium (bisphosphonates are typically taken first thing in the morning on an empty stomach)

**Levothyroxine (Synthroid, Levoxyl) and thyroid medications:**
- **Interaction:** Magnesium reduces levothyroxine absorption by forming insoluble complexes
- **Management:** Take levothyroxine at least 4 hours before or after magnesium. This is a commonly overlooked interaction.

**Mycophenolate mofetil (CellCept):**
- **Interaction:** Magnesium antacids reduce mycophenolate absorption
- **Management:** Separate by at least 2 hours

### Moderate Interactions

**Diuretics (loop and thiazide):**
- **Furosemide, hydrochlorothiazide, bumetanide:** These increase renal magnesium excretion, potentially causing hypomagnesemia
- **Management:** Monitor magnesium levels; supplement 300-400mg/day to compensate. Use chelated forms.
- **Note:** Potassium-sparing diuretics (spironolactone, amiloride) may REDUCE magnesium excretion — monitor for hypermagnesemia in combination with supplements

**Blood pressure medications (calcium channel blockers):**
- **Amlodipine, nifedipine, diltiazem, verapamil:** Magnesium also acts as a calcium channel blocker. The combination can enhance blood pressure lowering effects.
- **Management:** Monitor blood pressure; may need dose adjustment of medication. This is generally a beneficial interaction but can cause hypotension.

**Digoxin:**
- **Interaction:** Magnesium deficiency increases the risk of digoxin toxicity. Conversely, magnesium supplementation can reduce digoxin's effects.
- **Management:** Maintain adequate magnesium levels; do not supplement high doses without monitoring

**Proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole, esomeprazole):**
- **Interaction:** Bidirectional — PPIs cause magnesium depletion (FDA warning), and low stomach acid impairs magnesium absorption
- **Management:** Use chelated magnesium forms (glycinate, threonate) that do not require stomach acid for absorption. Monitor RBC magnesium levels.

**Potassium supplements:**
- **Interaction:** Magnesium depletion causes refractory potassium depletion. You cannot correct potassium deficiency without first correcting magnesium.
- **Management:** Always address magnesium status when treating hypokalemia

**Muscle relaxants (baclofen, cyclobenzaprine, methocarbamol):**
- **Interaction:** Additive muscle relaxant effects; magnesium is a natural muscle relaxant
- **Management:** Monitor for excessive sedation or muscle weakness

**Benzodiazepines and sedatives:**
- **Interaction:** Magnesium enhances GABAergic activity and can potentiate the sedating effects of benzodiazepines, barbiturates, and other CNS depressants
- **Management:** Start with lower magnesium doses if taking sedating medications; monitor for excessive drowsiness

**Lithium:**
- **Interaction:** Magnesium may increase lithium levels by reducing renal excretion
- **Management:** Monitor lithium levels closely if starting or changing magnesium supplementation

### Minor Interactions

**Zinc supplements:**
- **Interaction:** Very high zinc doses may mildly reduce magnesium absorption
- **Note:** At standard supplemental doses, no significant interaction

**Calcium supplements:**
- **Interaction:** High-dose calcium and magnesium compete for absorption
- **Management:** Separate high-dose supplements by 2 hours; dietary calcium is not a concern

**Iron supplements:**
- **Interaction:** Minimal interaction between magnesium and iron
- **Note:** Separate by 1-2 hours as a precaution

### Prescription Drug Interactions That DEPLETE Magnesium
The following medications are known to deplete magnesium — supplementation is especially important for users of:
- **Proton pump inhibitors** (omeprazole, pantoprazole)
- **Loop diuretics** (furosemide, bumetanide)
- **Thiazide diuretics** (hydrochlorothiazide)
- **Corticosteroids** (prednisone, dexamethasone)
- **Oral contraceptives**
- **Estrogen replacement therapy**
- **Cisplatin and other chemotherapy agents**
- **Tacrolimus and cyclosporine** (immunosuppressants)
- **Aminoglycoside antibiotics** (gentamicin, tobramycin)
- **Amphotericin B**

### Pregnancy Considerations
- **FDA Category:** Magnesium is generally recognized as safe during pregnancy at recommended doses
- **RDA during pregnancy:** 350-400mg/day depending on age
- **Importance:** Magnesium deficiency during pregnancy is associated with preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, preterm labor, and fetal growth restriction
- **IV magnesium sulfate** is a standard treatment for eclampsia and preterm labor prevention — magnesium is widely used in obstetrics
- **Supplementation:** 300-400mg magnesium glycinate daily is considered safe during pregnancy
- **Caution:** Avoid magnesium oxide (diarrhea risk) during pregnancy. Do not exceed 400mg/day from supplements without medical guidance.
- **Note:** Many prenatal vitamins contain only magnesium oxide (poorly absorbed). Consider adding a chelated form.

### Breastfeeding
- **RDA during lactation:** 310-320mg/day
- **Magnesium is secreted in breast milk; adequate intake supports infant development
- **Chelated magnesium supplementation at recommended doses is safe during breastfeeding
