# Magnesium
## Foods to Avoid (When Taking Magnesium)

### High-Oxalate Foods
- **What:** Spinach (raw), rhubarb, beet greens, Swiss chard (raw), sweet potatoes, almonds
- **Why:** Oxalic acid binds magnesium in the GI tract, forming insoluble magnesium oxalate that cannot be absorbed. Ironically, spinach is high in magnesium but also high in oxalates, reducing its effective magnesium delivery.
- **How much impact:** Raw spinach has ~5% magnesium bioavailability due to oxalates; cooking reduces oxalate content by 30-50%
- **Mitigation:** Cook high-oxalate greens (boiling or steaming reduces oxalate content significantly). Do not take magnesium supplements at the same time as consuming raw high-oxalate foods.

### Phytate-Rich Foods
- **What:** Wheat bran, brown rice, corn, unfermented soy, raw legumes
- **Why:** Phytic acid chelates magnesium (and other divalent minerals), reducing absorption
- **How much impact:** Phytates can reduce magnesium absorption by 30-40%
- **Mitigation:** Soaking grains/legumes for 12-24 hours, sprouting, or fermenting (e.g., sourdough) breaks down phytates. Separate magnesium supplements from high-phytate meals by 2 hours.

### High-Dose Calcium Supplements
- **What:** Calcium carbonate or calcium citrate supplements above 300mg
- **Why:** Calcium and magnesium compete for the same absorption transporters in the intestine (TRPM6/7). High-dose calcium can reduce magnesium absorption.
- **How much impact:** Taking 600mg+ calcium simultaneously with magnesium can reduce magnesium absorption by 20-30%
- **Mitigation:** Separate calcium and magnesium supplements by 2 hours. Dietary calcium from food does not significantly interfere at normal levels.

### High-Phosphorus Foods and Sodas
- **What:** Processed foods, dark colas (phosphoric acid), processed meats, fast food
- **Why:** Excess phosphorus binds magnesium in the gut and increases renal magnesium excretion. The phosphoric acid in cola is a significant magnesium depleter.
- **How much impact:** Regular cola consumption is associated with lower magnesium status in population studies
- **Mitigation:** Minimize soft drink consumption, especially cola. Reduce processed food intake.

### Excessive Fiber at Supplement Time
- **What:** Very high-fiber meals (>15g fiber in a single sitting)
- **Why:** Fiber can bind minerals and accelerate intestinal transit time, reducing the window for absorption
- **How much impact:** Moderate; mainly relevant at very high fiber intakes
- **Mitigation:** Do not take magnesium supplements with a fiber supplement or extremely high-fiber meal

### Alcohol
- **What:** All alcoholic beverages
- **Why:** Alcohol increases renal magnesium excretion (by up to 260% in acute intoxication), impairs intestinal absorption, and causes magnesium wasting through GI losses. Chronic alcohol use is one of the most potent causes of magnesium depletion.
- **How much impact:** Severe — chronic alcoholics have clinically significant magnesium deficiency in up to 30-60% of cases
- **Mitigation:** Minimize alcohol; if drinking, supplement magnesium the next morning to compensate for losses

### Excessive Caffeine
- **What:** Coffee (>3 cups/day), energy drinks, high-dose caffeine supplements
- **Why:** Caffeine has a mild diuretic effect that increases urinary magnesium excretion. At moderate intakes (1-2 cups coffee), the effect is minimal. At high intakes, it becomes meaningful.
- **How much impact:** Modest; ~5mg magnesium lost per cup of coffee
- **Mitigation:** 1-2 cups of coffee per day is not a concern. If consuming more, ensure dietary/supplemental magnesium compensates. Do not take magnesium at the exact same time as coffee.

### Refined Sugar
- **What:** Added sugars, sweets, processed carbohydrates
- **Why:** The body uses magnesium to metabolize glucose. High-sugar diets increase magnesium utilization and urinary excretion. For every molecule of glucose metabolized, 28 atoms of magnesium are consumed.
- **How much impact:** Significant over time; high-sugar diets are strongly associated with magnesium depletion
- **Mitigation:** Reduce refined sugar intake; ensure adequate magnesium supplementation if diet is high in processed carbs
