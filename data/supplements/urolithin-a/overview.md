# Urolithin A
## Overview

Urolithin A is a metabolite produced when gut bacteria break down ellagitannins and ellagic acid — compounds found in pomegranate, walnuts, and certain berries. Its defining property is the ability to trigger mitophagy: the selective clearance of damaged mitochondria, which lets cells replace worn-out mitochondria with healthy ones. Because mitochondrial quality declines with age and is central to muscle function, urolithin A has emerged as one of the better-evidenced longevity-adjacent supplements, with human randomized trials showing improvements in muscle mitochondrial function, endurance, and strength. A crucial practical point: only an estimated 30-40% of people carry the gut bacteria needed to make meaningful urolithin A from food, so most people cannot rely on diet (eating pomegranate) and benefit from direct supplementation. The branded ingredient Mitopure holds FDA GRAS status and is the form used in the published human trials.

### Why It Matters

- **Mitophagy induction:** Urolithin A is a well-characterized inducer of mitophagy (via the PINK1/PRKN/Parkin pathway and related mechanisms), clearing damaged mitochondria so cells can renew their mitochondrial pool.
- **Muscle function — human evidence:** Unlike many longevity compounds, urolithin A has randomized human trials showing improved muscle endurance and strength and improved mitochondrial gene expression — a relatively strong evidence base.
- **Most people are "non-producers":** Roughly 60-70% of people lack the gut bacteria to convert dietary ellagitannins into urolithin A, making direct supplementation the reliable route.
- **Mitochondrial aging:** By improving mitochondrial quality, urolithin A targets a fundamental hallmark of aging relevant to muscle, energy, and broader healthspan.
- **Strong regulatory footing:** The Mitopure form has FDA GRAS status and a clean safety profile in trials.

### Who Might Be Interested

- **People focused on muscle health and endurance** — especially older adults and athletes, given the human muscle data
- **Longevity-focused users** — interested in mitochondrial quality and mitophagy
- **"Non-producers"** — the majority of people who cannot make urolithin A from diet
- **Those building a mitochondrial stack** — alongside NMN, CoQ10, and PQQ
