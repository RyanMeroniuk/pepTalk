# Urolithin A
## Natural Food Sources

Urolithin A is NOT found directly in food. It is produced by gut bacteria that metabolize ellagitannins and ellagic acid — so the "food sources" below are the *precursors*, not urolithin A itself.

### Foods Rich in Ellagitannins / Ellagic Acid (Precursors)
- **Pomegranate** — the most famous source; pomegranate juice, arils, and peel are rich in ellagitannins (punicalagins)
- **Walnuts** — a leading nut source of ellagitannins
- **Berries** — raspberries, strawberries, blackberries, and cloudberries
- **Pecans and other tree nuts** — contribute ellagic acid
- **Muscadine grapes** — notable ellagitannin content
- **Some teas and oak-aged wines/spirits** — minor contributors via ellagitannins

### The Critical Catch: Most People Can't Convert It
- **Only an estimated ~30-40% of people ("producers") carry the gut bacteria** needed to convert dietary ellagitannins into meaningful urolithin A
- **The other ~60-70% ("non-producers")** generate little to no urolithin A no matter how much pomegranate or walnuts they eat
- **Even among producers, the amount made varies widely** and is generally well below the 500-1000 mg used in trials
- You cannot easily know your producer status without a urine/blood test

### Important Context
- **Eating pomegranate is healthy but unreliable as a urolithin A source** — for most people it will not produce trial-relevant levels
- **This is the central reason direct urolithin A supplementation exists** — it bypasses the gut-bacteria bottleneck and delivers a consistent dose regardless of microbiome composition
- **A diet rich in ellagitannin foods is still worthwhile** for general polyphenol/antioxidant benefits, but should not be relied upon to deliver meaningful urolithin A
