# Urolithin A
## Foods to Avoid

### Foods/Substances That Work Against Urolithin A's Goals
- **Excessive alcohol** — impairs mitochondrial function and biogenesis, working directly against urolithin A's purpose of improving mitochondrial quality; also disrupts the gut microbiome relevant to precursor conversion
- **Highly processed, high-sugar foods** — promote mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and inflammation, undermining the mitochondrial benefits urolithin A provides
- **Chronic overnutrition** — metabolic overload stresses mitochondria and blunts mitophagy/autophagy signaling

### Gut-Disrupting Patterns (Relevant for Precursor Conversion)
- **Diets that harm microbiome diversity** (very low fiber, ultra-processed, frequent unnecessary antibiotics) reduce the gut bacteria that producers rely on to convert dietary ellagitannins
- **Note:** This matters only if relying on food precursors; direct urolithin A supplementation does not depend on gut bacteria

### Taking It Without Fat (Timing Mistake)
- **Fasted, fat-free dosing** reduces absorption of this lipophilic compound. Take urolithin A with a fat-containing meal.

### No Specific Food Blocks Urolithin A
- **There are no common foods that destroy or block supplemental urolithin A** in a meaningful way
- The relevant factor is the overall pattern: a diet that harms mitochondrial and gut health works against urolithin A's intended benefits

### Practical Guidance
- **Build a whole-food, mitochondria-supportive diet** — adequate protein, healthy fats, fiber, and antioxidant-rich foods (including ellagitannin foods like walnuts and pomegranate for general health)
- **Minimize alcohol and ultra-processed foods** so urolithin A's mitochondrial benefits are not offset
- **Pair urolithin A with a fatty meal** for absorption
