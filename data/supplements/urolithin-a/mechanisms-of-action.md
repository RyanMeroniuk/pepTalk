# Urolithin A
## Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Mitophagy Induction (Defining Mechanism)
- **Urolithin A triggers mitophagy** — the selective autophagic clearance of damaged or dysfunctional mitochondria
- It engages the **PINK1/Parkin (PRKN) pathway**: PINK1 accumulates on damaged mitochondria and recruits Parkin, which tags them for removal; urolithin A promotes this quality-control process
- By clearing defective mitochondria, cells can replace them through mitochondrial biogenesis, refreshing the overall mitochondrial pool ("mitochondrial recycling")
- This is the central, best-characterized mechanism and the basis for its muscle and longevity effects

### 2. Improved Mitochondrial Function & Biogenesis
- Following mitophagy, urolithin A is associated with improved mitochondrial respiratory capacity and upregulation of mitochondrial gene expression in human muscle
- Supports a healthier balance between clearing old mitochondria and building new ones

### 3. Skeletal Muscle Effects
- In human randomized trials, urolithin A improved muscle endurance (e.g., more contractions before fatigue) and measures of strength, alongside improved mitochondrial gene-expression signatures
- The muscle benefit is thought to stem directly from improved mitochondrial quality in muscle fibers

### 4. Anti-Inflammatory & Antioxidant Effects
- Reduces inflammatory signaling and oxidative stress in preclinical models, partly as a downstream consequence of healthier mitochondria producing less reactive oxygen species
- May support the gut barrier and reduce intestinal inflammation in some models

### 5. Gut-Microbiome Origin (Key Pharmacology Point)
- Urolithin A is produced by specific gut bacteria metabolizing ellagitannins/ellagic acid from food
- **Only ~30-40% of people carry the necessary bacteria** to generate meaningful amounts — the "non-producer" majority cannot reliably make it from diet
- Direct supplementation bypasses this dependence on microbiome composition, delivering urolithin A regardless of gut flora

### 6. Broader Healthspan Pathways (Preclinical)
- Animal studies report benefits in muscle, brain, joint, and immune contexts, generally attributed to improved mitochondrial quality and reduced inflammation
- These broader effects are largely preclinical; the strongest human data is in muscle/mitochondrial function

### Evidence Note
- Urolithin A is unusual among longevity compounds in having **human randomized controlled trials** supporting its muscle and mitochondrial effects. The mitophagy mechanism is well established; broader anti-aging claims beyond muscle/mitochondrial endpoints remain more preliminary.
