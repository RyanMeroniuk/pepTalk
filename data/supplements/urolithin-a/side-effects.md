# Urolithin A
## Side Effects

### Very Well Tolerated
- **Urolithin A (Mitopure) has a clean safety profile in human randomized trials** at doses of 500-1000 mg/day over several months
- **FDA GRAS status** reflects a favorable safety assessment for the branded form
- A dedicated safety study found urolithin A well tolerated with no significant adverse effects at the studied doses

### Common (Mild, Infrequent)
- **Mild GI discomfort** — occasional nausea, bloating, or loose stools; uncommon and usually mild. Taking with food helps.
- **No characteristic significant side effects** reported in trials

### Theoretical / Limited-Data Areas
- **Very long-term safety (multi-year)** — trials run up to several months; multi-year continuous-use safety is less characterized, though the profile so far is reassuring
- **High doses beyond 1000 mg/day** — not well studied; no clear benefit to exceeding trial doses

### Special Populations
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data for supplemental doses; avoid or consult a clinician (dietary ellagitannin foods like pomegranate are fine)
- **Active cancer** — given urolithin A's effects on mitophagy and mitochondrial metabolism, those with active cancer should consult their oncologist before use as a precaution
- **Children** — not studied; not recommended

### Drug-Interaction Risk
- **Low documented interaction risk** — urolithin A is not strongly characterized as a CYP-enzyme inhibitor like the flavonols (quercetin, fisetin). See drug-interactions for precautionary notes.

### Overall Safety Profile
- **One of the better-tolerated longevity-oriented supplements**, supported by dedicated human safety data and GRAS status
- **No established organ toxicity** at researched doses
- **Main considerations are the usual precautions** (pregnancy, active cancer) and the lack of very-long-term data
