# Urolithin A
## Benefits

### Muscle Endurance & Strength (Strongest Human Evidence)
- **Improved muscle endurance** — randomized human trials (e.g., Singh et al., 2022, JAMA Network Open; Liu et al., 2022) showed improvements in muscle endurance (more contractions to fatigue) in middle-aged and older adults
- **Improved measures of strength** — some trials reported gains in muscle strength over months of supplementation
- **Relevant for aging muscle** — targets the mitochondrial decline that contributes to age-related loss of muscle function
- **This human muscle/endurance data is what most distinguishes urolithin A** from less-evidenced longevity compounds

### Mitochondrial Health
- **Mitophagy** — clears damaged mitochondria so cells can renew their mitochondrial pool, addressing a core hallmark of aging
- **Improved mitochondrial gene expression** — human trials showed favorable changes in mitochondrial gene-expression signatures in muscle
- **Better mitochondrial respiratory capacity** in preclinical and human-marker data

### Cellular & Inflammatory
- **Anti-inflammatory effects** — reduces inflammatory signaling in preclinical models, partly via healthier mitochondria producing less oxidative stress
- **Gut health** — may support the intestinal barrier and reduce gut inflammation in animal models

### Recovery & Performance (Emerging)
- **Interest for athletes** — improved mitochondrial quality and endurance capacity draws interest for training and recovery; direct athletic-performance data is still emerging

### Broader Healthspan (Preclinical)
- **Brain, joint, and immune benefits** reported in animal models, attributed to improved mitochondrial quality and reduced inflammation; these remain preliminary in humans

### Reliable Delivery Regardless of Gut Flora
- **Works for "non-producers"** — because ~60-70% of people cannot make meaningful urolithin A from diet, supplementation provides a benefit they cannot get from eating pomegranate or walnuts alone

### Important Caveat
- **Urolithin A's strongest, most reliable benefits are in muscle endurance/strength and mitochondrial function, supported by human RCTs.** Broader anti-aging benefits beyond these endpoints are largely preclinical and should be framed as promising but less established.
