# Berberine
## Side Effects

- **GI distress (most common and most significant):** Diarrhea, cramping, bloating, nausea, flatulence, and abdominal pain are the primary side effects, affecting a substantial percentage of users at the 1,500mg/day dose. Caused by berberine's antimicrobial effects on gut bacteria (die-off reaction), direct GI irritation, and increased gut motility. Mitigation: start with a low dose and ramp up gradually; take with food; switch to dihydroberberine (GlucoVantage) which is much better tolerated
- **Hypoglycemia:** Berberine effectively lowers blood glucose. In individuals with normal blood sugar or those on diabetes medications, it can cause blood sugar to drop too low — resulting in dizziness, shakiness, confusion, sweating, and weakness. Always monitor blood sugar when starting berberine, especially if on diabetes medications
- **Hypotension (low blood pressure):** Berberine may lower blood pressure. Combined with antihypertensive medications, this can cause lightheadedness, dizziness, or fainting. Monitor blood pressure during initial use
- **Constipation (paradoxical):** While diarrhea is more common, some users experience constipation as the gut microbiome shifts. Usually temporary as the microbiome adjusts
- **Headache:** Occasionally reported, possibly related to blood sugar or blood pressure changes. Usually mild
- **Skin reactions:** Rare rash or itching. Berberine-containing plants (goldenseal) have been associated with occasional contact dermatitis
- **Liver effects:** Berberine is metabolized hepatically. While it generally improves liver markers (ALT, AST) in fatty liver disease, there are rare case reports of elevated liver enzymes. Individuals with existing liver disease should use caution and monitor liver function
- **Drug interactions (the major concern):** Berberine is a potent inhibitor of multiple CYP enzymes (CYP3A4, CYP2D6, CYP2C9) and P-glycoprotein. This is not technically a side effect but is the most clinically important safety consideration. See drug interactions section for details
- **Bilirubin displacement in neonates:** Berberine can displace bilirubin from albumin. Contraindicated during pregnancy and breastfeeding due to risk of neonatal jaundice/kernicterus
- **Uterine stimulation:** Berberine may stimulate uterine contractions. Contraindicated during pregnancy
- **Immune modulation:** Berberine has immunomodulatory effects. While generally beneficial, caution is warranted in transplant patients on immunosuppressants
