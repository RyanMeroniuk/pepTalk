# Berberine
## Drug Interactions

- **CYP enzyme inhibition (the most significant concern):** Berberine is a potent inhibitor of multiple cytochrome P450 enzymes that metabolize the majority of pharmaceutical drugs. This means berberine can INCREASE blood levels of drugs metabolized by these enzymes to potentially dangerous levels:
  - **CYP3A4 (inhibited):** Metabolizes approximately 50% of all prescription drugs. Affected medications include: statins (atorvastatin, simvastatin, lovastatin — NOT pravastatin or rosuvastatin), calcium channel blockers (amlodipine, nifedipine, diltiazem), immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus), benzodiazepines (alprazolam, midazolam, triazolam), HIV protease inhibitors, many chemotherapy agents, macrolide antibiotics (erythromycin, clarithromycin), and sildenafil/tadalafil
  - **CYP2D6 (inhibited):** Metabolizes many antidepressants (SSRIs: fluoxetine, paroxetine; SNRIs: venlafaxine; TCAs: amitriptyline), beta-blockers (metoprolol, propranolol), antipsychotics (haloperidol, risperidone), opioids (codeine conversion to morphine, tramadol), and tamoxifen (critical — reduced conversion to active metabolite endoxifen)
  - **CYP2C9 (inhibited):** Metabolizes warfarin (increased bleeding risk), phenytoin, NSAIDs (ibuprofen, diclofenac), sulfonylureas (glipizide, glimepiride — combined hypoglycemia risk with berberine's glucose-lowering effect)
  - **CYP1A2 (inhibited):** Metabolizes caffeine, theophylline, clozapine, and some fluoroquinolone antibiotics

- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) inhibition:** Berberine inhibits P-gp, the efflux pump that removes drugs from cells and limits intestinal absorption. This increases blood levels of P-gp substrates including: digoxin (narrow therapeutic window — dangerous), cyclosporine, certain chemotherapy drugs, dabigatran, and fexofenadine

- **Diabetes medications (additive hypoglycemia):**
  - **Metformin:** Berberine and metformin both activate AMPK and lower blood glucose. Combined use can cause hypoglycemia. If combining, reduce doses of both and monitor blood sugar closely. Some integrative physicians intentionally combine them at lower doses, but this requires medical supervision
  - **Sulfonylureas (glipizide, glimepiride, glyburide):** High risk of hypoglycemia — both berberine and sulfonylureas lower blood sugar, and berberine inhibits CYP2C9 which metabolizes sulfonylureas, increasing their blood levels further. Avoid combination or use only under close medical supervision
  - **Insulin:** Additive hypoglycemia risk. Monitor blood sugar closely and adjust insulin dose if adding berberine
  - **GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide):** Berberine stimulates endogenous GLP-1 secretion. Combination may increase GI side effects and hypoglycemia risk. Use cautiously
  - **SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin):** Additive glucose-lowering. Monitor for hypoglycemia

- **Blood thinners / anticoagulants:**
  - **Warfarin:** HIGH RISK — berberine inhibits CYP2C9 (which metabolizes warfarin) AND has its own antiplatelet effects. Can significantly increase INR and bleeding risk. Avoid combination or use only with frequent INR monitoring
  - **Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs — apixaban, rivarelbaan, dabigatran):** Berberine's P-gp and CYP3A4 inhibition can increase blood levels. Increased bleeding risk
  - **Antiplatelet drugs (aspirin, clopidogrel):** Additive antiplatelet effects. Increased bleeding risk

- **Statins:**
  - Berberine inhibits CYP3A4, which metabolizes atorvastatin, simvastatin, and lovastatin. This can increase statin blood levels, increasing risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis
  - **Safer statin options if combining:** Pravastatin and rosuvastatin are NOT metabolized by CYP3A4 and are safer with berberine
  - **The irony:** Berberine's own lipid-lowering effects overlap with statins. For some patients, berberine may reduce or replace the need for a statin (discuss with prescriber)

- **Antihypertensives:** Berberine has modest blood pressure-lowering effects. Additive with antihypertensive medications. Monitor for hypotension, particularly with calcium channel blockers (which are also CYP3A4 substrates)

- **Thyroid medications (levothyroxine):** Berberine may reduce thyroid hormone absorption if taken simultaneously. Separate by 4+ hours

- **Antibiotics:** Berberine has its own antimicrobial effects. Additive effects with antibiotics — could be beneficial (SIBO treatment) or could cause excessive disruption of gut flora. Coordinate with prescriber

- **Immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus):** Berberine's CYP3A4 and P-gp inhibition can dramatically increase blood levels of these narrow-therapeutic-window drugs. AVOID combination

- **Chemotherapy drugs:** Multiple potential interactions via CYP3A4, P-gp, and direct effects. Some preclinical evidence suggests berberine may sensitize cancer cells to chemotherapy, but DO NOT combine without oncologist guidance

- **Sedatives and CNS depressants:** Berberine may potentiate sedative effects of benzodiazepines, barbiturates, and other CNS depressants, particularly those metabolized by CYP3A4

- **Contraindicated populations:**
  - **Pregnancy:** Berberine stimulates uterine contractions and can displace bilirubin in neonates. Absolutely contraindicated
  - **Breastfeeding:** Berberine passes into breast milk. Risk of neonatal jaundice (bilirubin displacement). Contraindicated
  - **Children/neonates:** Risk of kernicterus from bilirubin displacement. Do not use
  - **Pre-surgery:** Discontinue berberine 2 weeks before surgery due to blood sugar-lowering and antiplatelet effects

- **General guidance:** If you take ANY prescription medication, consult a pharmacist or physician before adding berberine. The CYP inhibition profile is broad enough that interactions are likely rather than unlikely with polypharmacy
