# Berberine
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Standard berberine HCl (berberine hydrochloride):**
  - **Bioavailability:** Very poor — only ~5% oral bioavailability due to extensive first-pass metabolism by CYP enzymes and P-glycoprotein efflux in the gut
  - **Paradox:** Despite poor absorption, berberine is clinically effective. This is partly because it works locally in the gut (microbiome remodeling, GLP-1 stimulation, antimicrobial effects) and partly because gut metabolites of berberine are themselves bioactive
  - **Standard dose:** 500mg 2-3 times daily (1,000-1,500mg total). The need for multiple daily doses is partly to compensate for poor absorption
  - **GI side effects:** The main limitation — causes diarrhea, cramping, and nausea in many users, especially at the 1,500mg/day dose. This is due to both the antimicrobial effects on gut bacteria and direct GI irritation

- **Dihydroberberine (DHB / GlucoVantage) — improved form:**
  - **What it is:** A reduced (hydrogenated) form of berberine that is the actual metabolite berberine converts to in the gut before absorption
  - **Bioavailability:** ~5x better absorption than standard berberine HCl. Absorbed more efficiently in the intestine because it bypasses the conversion step
  - **GI tolerance:** Significantly better tolerated — less diarrhea, cramping, and GI distress because a lower dose achieves equivalent blood levels
  - **Dosing:** 100-200mg dihydroberberine equals approximately 500mg standard berberine HCl in terms of blood levels
  - **Brand:** GlucoVantage is the most recognized branded dihydroberberine ingredient
  - **Best for:** Anyone who experiences GI issues with standard berberine, or who wants a more efficient dose

- **Berberine phytosome (berberine + phospholipids):**
  - Some manufacturers use phospholipid complexation to improve berberine absorption
  - Improved bioavailability over standard berberine HCl
  - Less clinical data than standard berberine or dihydroberberine

- **Berberine + piperine combinations:**
  - Piperine (black pepper extract) inhibits P-glycoprotein and CYP enzymes that metabolize berberine, increasing its absorption
  - Caution: This same CYP inhibition stacks with berberine's own CYP inhibition, potentially increasing drug interactions. Use with extra caution if on medications

- **Berberine sulfate and berberine citrate:**
  - Alternative salt forms. Berberine HCl is the most studied and most commonly available. No strong evidence that alternative salts offer meaningful bioavailability advantages

- **Liquid/tincture forms:**
  - Berberine-containing plant tinctures (goldenseal, barberry) provide berberine in a liquid matrix. Dose is variable and generally lower than capsule forms. Difficult to achieve therapeutic doses

- **Key purchasing considerations:**
  - Choose either standard berberine HCl at 500mg 3x/day or dihydroberberine (GlucoVantage) at 100-200mg 2-3x/day
  - Third-party testing for purity and potency is important (berberine content can vary significantly in raw plant materials)
  - Enteric-coated capsules may reduce GI side effects of standard berberine
