# Cordyceps
## Side Effects

- **GI discomfort:** The most commonly reported effect — mild nausea, stomach upset, dry mouth, or diarrhea, especially at higher doses or when starting. Usually transient. Taking with food can help
- **Generally well tolerated:** In clinical trials of CS-4 and cultivated Cordyceps at 1-3g/day, side effects were uncommon and mild. It has a long history of traditional use as a food/tonic
- **Blood sugar reduction:** Cordyceps may lower blood glucose. Beneficial for most, but those on diabetes medication should monitor for hypoglycemia
- **Bleeding/antiplatelet potential:** Cordyceps may have mild antiplatelet/anticoagulant activity in preclinical data. Theoretical increased bleeding risk if combined with blood thinners; discontinue before surgery as a precaution
- **Immune activation in autoimmune conditions:** Because beta-glucans stimulate innate immune activity, individuals with autoimmune disease (e.g., lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, MS) should use caution and consult their physician — the immune-modulating effect is theoretically undesirable in some autoimmune contexts
- **Allergic reactions:** Rare. Individuals with mold/mushroom allergies should be cautious. Discontinue if hives, swelling, or breathing difficulty occur
- **Wild C. sinensis contamination:** Wild caterpillar fungus has documented concerns with heavy-metal (arsenic, lead) contamination and adulteration. Cultivated, third-party-tested products avoid this
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** Insufficient safety data. Avoid during pregnancy and breastfeeding unless directed by a physician
- **Overall safety profile:** Considered one of the safer medicinal mushrooms at standard supplement doses; main cautions are blood-sugar lowering, theoretical bleeding risk, autoimmune contexts, and contamination of wild product
