# Cordyceps
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Cordyceps militaris fruiting body / mycelium extract — the practical choice:**
  - **What it is:** Cultivated C. militaris grown on substrate, available as fruiting body or mycelial biomass. C. militaris generally contains substantially more cordycepin than wild C. sinensis, making it the preferred and most cost-effective commercial source.
  - **What to look for:** Standardization to **cordycepin** content (the signature bioactive) and/or beta-glucan content. Hot water or dual extraction to break down chitin and release polysaccharides.
  - **Best for:** General supplementation, endurance/energy protocols, immune support.

- **CS-4 (Cordyceps sinensis fermented mycelium):**
  - **What it is:** A standardized, lab-fermented mycelial strain of C. sinensis. CS-4 is the form used in most of the human exercise research, so it has the strongest clinical track record even if cordycepin content is lower than C. militaris.
  - **Best for:** Those who want a form matching the published human studies.

- **Wild Cordyceps sinensis (caterpillar fungus):**
  - **What it is:** The traditional wild-harvested fungus from the Tibetan plateau. Extremely expensive, subject to adulteration and heavy-metal contamination concerns, and not necessarily higher in active compounds than cultivated forms.
  - **Verdict:** Prestige product. Not necessary — cultivated C. militaris or CS-4 is more reliable and far cheaper.

- **Mycelium-on-grain (MOG) — be cautious:**
  - As with other medicinal mushrooms, cheap "mycelium" products grown on grain can be largely starch (alpha-glucan) rather than mushroom beta-glucan. Demand products that specify cordycepin and/or beta-glucan content rather than just "total polysaccharides."

- **Bioavailability of cordycepin (the key limitation):**
  - Cordycepin is rapidly deaminated by adenosine deaminase (ADA) in blood, giving it a short half-life and limiting systemic exposure. This is a genuine constraint on its activity.
  - Some research formulations co-administer pentostatin (an ADA inhibitor) to extend cordycepin's half-life; this is a research approach, not a consumer product.
  - Practically, choosing a high-cordycepin C. militaris extract and consistent daily dosing is the realistic way to maximize exposure.

- **Capsules vs powder vs tincture:**
  - **Capsules:** Convenient, standardized dosing.
  - **Powder:** Cost-effective, mixes into coffee/smoothies; hot liquid aids beta-glucan dissolution.
  - **Dual-extract tincture:** Good extraction of both water- and alcohol-soluble compounds; check the extract ratio.

- **Key label requirements for a quality product:**
  - Specifies the species (C. militaris or CS-4)
  - Lists **cordycepin** and/or beta-glucan percentage
  - Uses hot water or dual extraction
  - Third-party tested for heavy metals (especially relevant for any wild C. sinensis)
