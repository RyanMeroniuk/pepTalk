# Cordyceps
## Foods to Avoid

- **Excessive alcohol:** Alcohol impairs aerobic adaptation, recovery, and mitochondrial function — directly opposing the endurance and energy benefits Cordyceps is taken for. Heavy or regular drinking undermines the training adaptations Cordyceps is meant to support
- **High-sugar, high-glycemic diet:** Chronic hyperglycemia and insulin resistance work against Cordyceps' proposed metabolic and insulin-sensitizing benefits. A diet dominated by refined sugar and processed carbohydrate counteracts the metabolic-adaptogen rationale
- **Ultra-processed, pro-inflammatory foods:** Refined seed oils, trans fats, and additive-heavy processed foods promote systemic oxidative stress and inflammation — the opposite of the antioxidant/anti-inflammatory state Cordyceps is used to support. Endurance recovery suffers in a chronically inflamed system
- **No specific food blocks Cordyceps:** Unlike minerals (which compete for absorption), there is no well-documented food that directly blocks Cordyceps' active compounds. Most "avoid" guidance is about diet quality that supports vs. undermines the goals (endurance, recovery, metabolic health) rather than direct absorption interference
- **Foods you are intolerant to:** Undiagnosed food sensitivities drive low-grade inflammation that impairs recovery and blunts the benefit of any recovery-oriented supplement
- **Caffeine timing (not a true conflict):** Cordyceps pairs fine with caffeine, but taking large amounts of caffeine late in the day can harm the sleep that endurance recovery depends on — a timing consideration, not a food interaction
