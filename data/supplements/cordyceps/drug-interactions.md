# Cordyceps
## Drug Interactions

- **Anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs (warfarin, heparin, aspirin, clopidogrel) — caution:** Cordyceps may have mild antiplatelet/anticoagulant activity in preclinical data, creating a theoretical additive bleeding risk. Monitor for unusual bruising or bleeding, inform your prescriber, and discontinue before surgery. Keep dosing separate is not the issue here — the concern is additive effect, so medical monitoring is what matters
- **Diabetes medications (metformin, sulfonylureas, insulin) — caution/monitor:** Cordyceps may lower blood glucose. Additive hypoglycemic effect is possible; monitor blood sugar more closely when starting, and dose adjustments to diabetes medication may be needed
- **Immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus, mycophenolate) and immune-modulating biologics — caution:** Cordyceps beta-glucans stimulate innate immune function, which could theoretically oppose immunosuppressive therapy. Use with caution in transplant recipients and those on immunosuppressants; consult the prescriber
- **Antihypertensives / vasodilators — monitor:** Cordyceps may increase nitric oxide and promote vasodilation, potentially adding to blood-pressure-lowering medications. Monitor blood pressure if combining
- **Anticoagulant timing note:** While the main interaction concern is additive bleeding risk (not absorption), some clinicians prefer to separate herbal supplements from time-sensitive medications by a few hours as general practice; this is precautionary, not based on a documented Cordyceps absorption interaction
- **Bronchodilators / asthma medications — monitor:** Given Cordyceps' traditional respiratory use and possible bronchodilatory effects, monitor for additive effects if you use respiratory medications, though clinical significance is low
- **Caffeine and stimulants — generally compatible:** Cordyceps pairs commonly with caffeine for energy support with no documented adverse interaction; just be mindful of late-day caffeine and sleep
- **Contraindicated contexts (consult a physician before use):**
  - **Active autoimmune disease** — immune stimulation from beta-glucans is theoretically undesirable
  - **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data; avoid unless directed
  - **Scheduled surgery** — discontinue 1-2 weeks prior due to potential antiplatelet effect
- **Overall profile:** Cordyceps has a relatively mild drug-interaction profile. The most relevant cautions are with blood thinners, diabetes medications, immunosuppressants, and antihypertensives. Disclose Cordyceps use to your healthcare providers
