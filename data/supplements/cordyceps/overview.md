# Cordyceps
## Overview

Cordyceps is a genus of parasitic fungi historically prized in Traditional Chinese Medicine as a tonic for vitality, stamina, lung function, and longevity. The wild form, Cordyceps sinensis, grows on the larvae of ghost moths on the Tibetan plateau and is one of the most expensive natural products in the world; the cultivated form, Cordyceps militaris, is grown on grain or solid substrate and is now the practical source for most supplements, valued for its higher cordycepin content. Modern interest centers on Cordyceps as an ergogenic and anti-fatigue supplement — it is marketed for aerobic capacity, VO2 max support, oxygen utilization, and endurance — and as a metabolic and immune-modulating adaptogen. The human exercise evidence is genuinely mixed: some controlled trials in older or untrained adults show modest improvements in aerobic metabolic markers and ventilatory threshold, while studies in trained athletes are smaller, often used multi-ingredient products, and show inconsistent results. It belongs to the functional-mushroom family alongside its siblings lion's mane (cognitive/neurotrophic) and reishi (calming/immune), and the three are frequently combined into a single mushroom protocol. Cordyceps is best understood as a promising, traditionally-supported endurance and recovery adjunct rather than a proven performance enhancer.
