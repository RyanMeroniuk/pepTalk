# Cordyceps
## Mechanisms of Action

- **ATP and cellular energy support:** Cordyceps contains adenosine and cordycepin (3'-deoxyadenosine), nucleoside analogs structurally related to adenosine, the backbone of ATP. Proposed mechanisms include enhanced ATP synthesis and improved efficiency of cellular energy production, which is the basis for its anti-fatigue and endurance claims. Direct human evidence for increased ATP is limited and largely preclinical.
- **Improved oxygen utilization / VO2 support:** Several trials report improved oxygen uptake efficiency, ventilatory threshold, and aerobic metabolic markers, particularly in older or untrained adults. The mechanism may involve better oxygen extraction at the tissue level rather than a large change in maximal VO2 itself. Results in trained athletes are inconsistent.
- **Increased nitric oxide and vasodilation:** Animal and in vitro data suggest Cordyceps may increase nitric oxide production and promote vasodilation, improving blood flow and oxygen delivery to working muscle. This complements its proposed endurance benefits.
- **Mitochondrial and metabolic effects:** Cordyceps polysaccharides and cordycepin have shown effects on AMPK activation and mitochondrial function in preclinical models, supporting its framing as a metabolic adaptogen and a rationale for pairing with mitochondrial peptides.
- **Anti-fatigue via lactate and antioxidant pathways:** In rodent endurance models, Cordyceps extracts reduced exercise-induced lactate accumulation and oxidative stress markers and extended time to exhaustion. These animal findings are the strongest mechanistic support for the anti-fatigue claim.
- **Beta-glucan immune modulation:** Cordyceps polysaccharides (beta-glucans) activate innate immune cells — macrophages and NK cells — through dectin-1 and related pathways, producing immunomodulatory (balancing) rather than purely stimulant effects.
- **Cordycepin's broad bioactivity:** Cordycepin interferes with RNA synthesis and shows anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and (in vitro) antiproliferative activity. It is rapidly broken down by adenosine deaminase in the body, so bioavailability is a real limitation; some products are co-formulated with pentostatin or rely on high cordycepin strains to address this.
- **Blood-sugar and lipid effects (preclinical):** Animal studies suggest Cordyceps polysaccharides may improve insulin sensitivity and lipid profiles, supporting metabolic-health framing. Human data is preliminary.
