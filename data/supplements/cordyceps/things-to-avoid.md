# Cordyceps
## Things to Avoid

- **Expecting an acute stimulant effect:** Cordyceps is not caffeine. Its endurance and anti-fatigue benefits in studies appeared after weeks of daily dosing, not within hours. Do not judge it by how you feel on day one
- **Overpaying for wild Cordyceps sinensis:** Wild caterpillar fungus is among the most expensive natural products on earth, is frequently adulterated, and carries heavy-metal contamination concerns — without being clearly superior to cultivated C. militaris or CS-4. Cultivated, third-party-tested product is the smarter choice
- **Buying mycelium-on-grain without verification:** Cheap "mycelium" products can be largely grain starch (alpha-glucan). Insist on products that specify cordycepin and/or beta-glucan content, not just "total polysaccharides"
- **Overstating the performance evidence:** The human exercise data is genuinely mixed — modest aerobic benefit in untrained/older adults, inconsistent in trained athletes, and several positive trials used multi-ingredient products. Treat Cordyceps as a promising adjunct, not a proven ergogenic
- **Combining with blood thinners without medical awareness:** Due to possible mild antiplatelet effects, inform your prescriber if you take anticoagulant/antiplatelet medication, and discontinue before surgery as a precaution
- **Use in autoimmune disease without medical guidance:** Beta-glucan immune activation is theoretically undesirable in some autoimmune conditions. Consult a physician before use if you have an autoimmune disorder
- **Ignoring blood sugar if diabetic:** Cordyceps may lower blood glucose; monitor more closely if you take diabetes medication
- **Use during pregnancy/breastfeeding:** Insufficient safety data — avoid unless directed by a physician
- **Relying on Cordyceps to replace training or sleep:** It is an adjunct to endurance work and recovery, not a substitute. The benefits are realized in combination with consistent aerobic training and adequate sleep
