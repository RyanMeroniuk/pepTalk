# Shilajit
## Foods to Avoid

- **Other sources of heavy metals or excess minerals:** Because shilajit is mineral-dense (and even purified products contribute a mineral load), avoid stacking it with high-dose iron supplements, large amounts of liver/organ meats daily, or multiple mineral-heavy supplements at once without accounting for total intake. This is especially important for anyone with iron-overload risk
- **Excessive alcohol:** Alcohol stresses the liver, worsens sleep, and lowers testosterone — opposing shilajit's energy and hormonal-support goals. The liver also handles clearance, which matters more if any residual contaminants are present
- **High-purine foods (if gout-prone):** Shilajit may raise uric acid in some people. Those prone to gout should be cautious about combining it with very high-purine diets (large amounts of organ meats, certain seafood, excessive alcohol)
- **High-sugar, ultra-processed diets:** Drive inflammation and metabolic dysfunction, undermining the energy and hormonal benefits shilajit is meant to support

### Behaviors, Not Foods
- **Consuming raw/unpurified shilajit:** The most important "avoid" — not a food interaction but the central safety rule. Never consume raw, wild, or untested shilajit due to heavy-metal contamination risk
- **Megadosing to chase effects:** More is not better and increases cumulative mineral and any residual heavy-metal exposure

### Generally Fine
- There are no specific common foods that block shilajit's fulvic-acid absorption
- The traditional warm-milk or warm-water preparation is fine and may aid palatability and dissolution
