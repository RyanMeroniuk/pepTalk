# Shilajit
## Overview

Shilajit is a sticky, tar-like mineral exudate that oozes from rock crevices in high mountain ranges such as the Himalayas, formed over centuries from the compressed, slowly decomposed remains of plants and microbes. In Ayurvedic medicine it is revered as a rasayana — a rejuvenating tonic believed to enhance energy, vitality, and longevity. Its biological interest in modern supplementation centers on two components: fulvic acid (a humic substance that acts as an antioxidant, mineral carrier, and mitochondrial support agent) and dibenzo-alpha-pyrones (DBPs), which appear to support cellular energy production. Small human trials, mostly using the standardized purified extract PrimaVie, suggest shilajit may support mitochondrial energy and reduce fatigue, and a few report modest increases in total testosterone in men. The strongest framing is as a mitochondrial/bioenergetic and trace-mineral support supplement with adjunctive, modest testosterone support — not a powerful hormone booster. The single most important consideration with shilajit is sourcing: raw, unpurified, or informally harvested shilajit frequently contains dangerous levels of heavy metals (lead, mercury, arsenic) and other contaminants because it concentrates whatever is in its rock environment. For this reason, only purified, standardized, third-party-tested products should ever be consumed. It is commonly stacked within the testosterone-support cluster alongside tongkat-ali, boron, and CoQ10.
