# Shilajit
## Natural Food Sources

- **No dietary food equivalent:** Shilajit is a geological/biological exudate harvested from mountain rocks — it is not a food and has no common dietary counterpart. Its characteristic compounds (fulvic acid, dibenzo-alpha-pyrones) and dense trace-mineral profile cannot be obtained from ordinary foods in comparable form

- **Fulvic acid in the broader environment:** Fulvic acid, shilajit's key active fraction, occurs in humic-rich soils, compost, and some natural water sources. Trace amounts may be present in produce grown in fulvic-rich soil, and fulvic/humic mineral supplements exist separately — but these are not equivalent to shilajit's concentrated matrix

- **Trace minerals from food:** The minerals shilajit delivers (iron, zinc, magnesium, selenium, etc.) are abundantly available from a varied whole-food diet (meat, shellfish, legumes, nuts, leafy greens, whole grains) — without the heavy-metal contamination risk. For mineral status, food and dedicated mineral supplements are safer sources

- **Traditional preparation (resin, not a food):**
  - The purified resin is dissolved in warm water or warm milk and consumed as a tonic. This is a supplement preparation, not a culinary food
  - Honey is sometimes used as a carrier to mask the strong, bitter, tar-like taste

- **Why this matters for safety:** Because shilajit has no food equivalent and must be harvested from rock and purified, there is no way to obtain it casually or "from diet." Any shilajit you consume is a supplement whose safety depends entirely on purification and third-party heavy-metal testing — there is no safe "raw/foraged" route
