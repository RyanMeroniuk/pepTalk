# Shilajit
## Drug Interactions

### Contraindications (Avoid / Physician Oversight Required)
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** Contraindicated — heavy-metal exposure risk alone is disqualifying, compounded by hormonal activity and lack of safety data
- **Iron-overload disorders (hemochromatosis):** Contraindicated due to shilajit's iron content
- **Hormone-sensitive cancers and prostate conditions:** Use only under physician oversight given modest testosterone/DHEA-S effects; avoid in active hormone-sensitive cancer without specialist input
- **Active cancer (general):** Do not initiate without oncologist input

### Caution / Monitor (Moderate)
- **Iron supplements and iron-containing multivitamins:** Additive iron load. Monitor iron status (ferritin) if combining, and avoid in those with elevated iron stores
- **Blood pressure medications (antihypertensives):** Shilajit may modestly affect blood pressure; monitor when combining, especially at initiation
- **Diabetes medications:** Some data suggest shilajit may influence blood glucose; monitor blood sugar if combining
- **Hormone therapy / TRT:** Combining with testosterone replacement or other hormonal agents stacks androgenic effects and complicates monitoring (testosterone, hematocrit). Coordinate with the prescribing clinician
- **Gout medications / urate-affecting drugs:** Shilajit may raise uric acid; monitor in those with gout or on urate-lowering therapy
- **Blood thinners / anticoagulants:** Limited data; use caution and monitor, as mineral/antioxidant-rich botanicals can theoretically interact around clotting and procedures

### Timing Considerations
- **Separate from medications with narrow absorption windows:** Because fulvic acid is a mineral chelator/carrier, it could theoretically bind certain drugs in the gut (similar to how minerals affect some antibiotics). As a precaution, take shilajit several hours apart from medications such as tetracycline/quinolone antibiotics, thyroid hormone (levothyroxine), and bisphosphonates rather than at the same time
- **Take other mineral supplements at a different time** to better track total mineral load

### Heavy-Metal / Lab Considerations (Critical)
- **The dominant "drug interaction" concern is contamination, not pharmacology:** A contaminated product introduces lead, mercury, and arsenic, which carry their own toxicities and can confound lab interpretation. Only purified, third-party-tested products should be used
- **Inform your physician** that you take shilajit before procedures and when interpreting iron, uric acid, liver, or hormone labs

### General Safety Notes
- With a purified, tested product, classic pharmacokinetic drug interactions are not well-characterized and are likely modest at recommended doses
- The principal safety levers are product purity (heavy metals), iron/mineral load, and the hormone-sensitive-condition caution
