# Shilajit
## Side Effects

### CRITICAL: Heavy-Metal Contamination (Primary Safety Concern)
- **This is the single most important risk with shilajit.** Because shilajit is a mineral exudate that concentrates whatever is present in its rock environment, raw or unpurified shilajit frequently contains dangerous levels of **lead, mercury, and arsenic**, and may also carry mycotoxins, free radicals, and fungal contamination
- **Heavy-metal toxicity is serious and cumulative:** lead and mercury accumulate in the body and can cause neurological, renal, and developmental harm over time. There is no benefit that justifies consuming a contaminated product
- **Mitigation:** Use ONLY purified, standardized, third-party-tested products (e.g., PrimaVie) with a published certificate of analysis confirming heavy metals are within safe limits. Treat any "raw," "wild," or untested shilajit as unsafe

### Common (with purified products, generally mild)
- **GI discomfort:** Mild nausea, stomach upset, or changes in bowel habits, especially at higher doses or on an empty stomach
- **Dizziness or headache:** Occasionally reported, usually transient
- **Increased uric acid:** Shilajit may raise uric acid in some individuals, a concern for those prone to gout

### Less Common / Theoretical
- **Iron overload:** Shilajit contains iron and other minerals. People with hemochromatosis or iron-overload conditions should avoid it, and others should account for the added mineral load
- **Allergic reaction:** Rare; discontinue if rash, itching, or swelling occurs
- **Blood pressure effects:** Possible modest changes; monitor if you have cardiovascular conditions
- **Hormonal effects:** Because it can modestly raise testosterone/DHEA-S, the hormone-sensitive-condition caution applies (see contraindications)

### Contraindications
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** Avoid — heavy-metal risk alone makes this inappropriate, plus hormonal activity and insufficient safety data
- **Hemochromatosis / iron-overload disorders:** Avoid due to iron content
- **Gout / high uric acid:** Use caution; shilajit may raise uric acid
- **Hormone-sensitive conditions:** Use caution given modest testosterone/DHEA-S effects
- **Children:** Not appropriate, especially given heavy-metal concerns

### Safety Profile Summary
- With a *purified, tested* product, side effects are generally mild and uncommon
- The dominant safety issue is not the herb's intrinsic toxicity but **contamination of poor-quality raw products** — sourcing is everything
