# Lutein & Zeaxanthin
## Foods to Avoid

- **Fat-free meals at dosing time:** The single biggest absorption mistake. Lutein and zeaxanthin are fat-soluble; taking them without dietary fat sharply reduces uptake. Always pair with fat
- **Very high simultaneous doses of competing carotenoids:** Beta-carotene and other carotenoids share absorption pathways with lutein/zeaxanthin. Taking large isolated beta-carotene supplements at the same time can reduce lutein/zeaxanthin absorption. Food-based carotenoid variety is fine; megadose single carotenoids are best spaced
- **Excessive alcohol:** Increases oxidative stress and impairs nutrient absorption, working against the antioxidant purpose of these carotenoids
- **Highly processed, low-vegetable diets:** Provide little dietary carotenoid baseline and tend to be pro-oxidant. Supplements help, but a vegetable-poor diet undermines overall eye and systemic antioxidant status
- **Olestra and fat blockers:** Fat substitutes (olestra) and fat-absorption-blocking products/medications (e.g., orlistat) reduce absorption of fat-soluble carotenoids. Avoid taking lutein/zeaxanthin with these

### Foods That Are Not a Concern
- **Most foods do not interfere** beyond the fat-free-meal and fat-blocker issues
- **Fiber and protein** do not meaningfully impair absorption when fat is present

### The Real "Avoid" List (Behaviors)
- **Taking it fasted** — undermines absorption
- **Inconsistency** — macular pigment accumulates over months; sporadic use limits benefit
- **Expecting fast results** — visual benefits track the slow rise in macular pigment

### Timing With Other Supplements
- **Pairs well with omega-3, astaxanthin, and fat-soluble vitamins (D, E, K)** — all benefit from the same fatty meal
- **Space from isolated high-dose beta-carotene** to avoid absorption competition
- **Separate from fat blockers (orlistat)** by design, as they impair carotenoid uptake
