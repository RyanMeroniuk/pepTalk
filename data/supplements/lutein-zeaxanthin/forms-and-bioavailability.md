# Lutein & Zeaxanthin
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Marigold-derived lutein/zeaxanthin (most common):** Most supplements source lutein and zeaxanthin from marigold (Tagetes) flowers. This is the standard, well-studied commercial source
- **Free vs esterified lutein:** Supplemental lutein comes as free lutein or lutein esters (bound to fatty acids). Free lutein is ready to absorb; lutein esters must be cleaved by digestive enzymes first but are concentrated and stable. Both are bioavailable when taken with fat; esters generally require a fat-containing meal to absorb well
- **Meso-zeaxanthin:** A third macular carotenoid found mainly in the retina. Some supplements add meso-zeaxanthin directly; otherwise the body forms it from lutein in the retina. Products marketed as the "macular triad" include all three (lutein + zeaxanthin + meso-zeaxanthin)
- **AREDS2 ratio:** The clinically studied formula uses lutein 10 mg + zeaxanthin 2 mg — roughly a 5:1 ratio, which most reputable eye-health products follow
- **Softgel in oil (best absorbed):** As fat-soluble carotenoids, lutein and zeaxanthin absorb best in oil-based softgels and with fatty meals. Powders/tablets without fat absorb less efficiently
- **Bioavailability — fat dependence:** Absorption is markedly higher when taken with dietary fat. Studies show co-ingestion with fat (e.g., egg, oil, avocado) substantially increases carotenoid uptake
- **Egg yolk as a highly bioavailable food source:** Lutein/zeaxanthin from egg yolk is among the most bioavailable dietary forms because the yolk's fat and phospholipids enhance absorption
- **Tissue accumulation:** Macular pigment rises gradually over weeks to months of consistent intake; benefits track this slow accumulation
- **Stability:** Carotenoids are sensitive to light, heat, and oxygen; quality products protect them and should be stored away from light and heat
- **Practical choice:** A softgel providing roughly lutein 10 mg + zeaxanthin 2 mg (optionally with meso-zeaxanthin), taken with a fat-containing meal, is the evidence-aligned default
