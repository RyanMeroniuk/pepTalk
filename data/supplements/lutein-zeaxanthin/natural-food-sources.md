# Lutein & Zeaxanthin
## Natural Food Sources

Lutein and zeaxanthin are abundant in dark leafy greens and egg yolks. The body cannot make them, so dietary intake (or supplements) is the only source.

### Highest Sources (Dark Leafy Greens)
- **Kale** — one of the richest sources (roughly 18-22 mg lutein+zeaxanthin per cooked cup)
- **Spinach** — very high (roughly 20 mg per cooked cup); a classic source
- **Collard greens** — comparable to kale
- **Turnip greens, Swiss chard, mustard greens** — all rich sources
- **Romaine lettuce** — moderate amounts

### Egg Yolks (Most Bioavailable)
- **Egg yolk** — lower absolute amount than greens but the most bioavailable food source, because the yolk's fat and phospholipids dramatically enhance absorption. A highly efficient way to raise macular pigment

### Other Vegetable Sources
- **Corn / sweet corn** — a notable zeaxanthin source (one of the better dietary zeaxanthin foods)
- **Broccoli, peas, Brussels sprouts** — moderate amounts
- **Green beans, zucchini** — modest amounts
- **Orange/yellow peppers** — contain zeaxanthin
- **Pistachios** — among the higher-lutein nuts

### Other Sources
- **Marigold flowers** — not a food, but the main commercial source for supplements
- **Orange juice, oranges, kiwi, grapes** — small amounts

### Important Context
- **Cooking can help or hurt:** Light cooking (and chopping) of greens can improve carotenoid bioavailability by breaking down cell walls, but prolonged high-heat cooking degrades carotenoids. Eating greens with fat (olive oil, etc.) is the key absorption factor
- **Dietary intake vs supplement doses:** A serving of cooked spinach or kale can provide more lutein than a 10 mg supplement, but consistency is the challenge — supplements ensure a steady AREDS2-level dose
- **Always pair with fat:** Whether from food or supplement, lutein/zeaxanthin absorption depends heavily on co-ingested dietary fat (egg yolk, olive oil, avocado)
- **Pairs with omega-3 foods:** Including fatty fish alongside leafy greens supports both retinal structure (omega-3) and macular pigment (lutein/zeaxanthin)
