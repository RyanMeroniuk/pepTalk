# Lutein & Zeaxanthin
## Overview

Lutein and zeaxanthin are dietary xanthophyll carotenoids that selectively accumulate in the macula of the retina, where they form the macular pigment — a yellow filter that absorbs damaging high-energy blue light and neutralizes free radicals in the most light-stressed tissue of the eye. They are the only carotenoids deposited in the retina and lens, making them uniquely targeted eye-health nutrients. The National Eye Institute's AREDS2 trial established their role (alongside other nutrients) in slowing progression of age-related macular degeneration (AMD), and they are widely studied for blue-light/screen protection, contrast sensitivity, and glare recovery.

- **Why it matters:** The macula is bombarded by light and oxygen, making it extremely vulnerable to oxidative and photic damage. Lutein and zeaxanthin physically filter blue light and act as antioxidants exactly where this damage occurs — the body cannot make them, so they must come from diet or supplements.
- **AREDS2-backed:** The AREDS2 formula (lutein 10 mg + zeaxanthin 2 mg, with other nutrients) is one of the few evidence-based nutritional interventions shown to slow progression to advanced AMD in at-risk individuals.
- **Macular pigment optical density (MPOD):** Supplementation raises MPOD, a measurable marker of macular pigment that correlates with visual performance and protection.
- **Screen/blue-light era relevance:** As blue-light exposure from screens has risen, interest in lutein/zeaxanthin for reducing eye strain, improving contrast, and speeding glare recovery has grown, with several supportive trials.
- **Beyond the eye:** Both carotenoids also deposit in the brain and skin, supporting emerging interest in cognition and skin photoprotection.
- **Honest framing:** AMD-progression and macular-pigment data are well established; cognitive and broad skin claims are emerging. Benefits require consistent intake over months and absorption with dietary fat.
