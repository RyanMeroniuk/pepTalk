# DHEA
## Overview

DHEA (dehydroepiandrosterone) is fundamentally different from the other items in a typical supplement stack: it is a hormone, not an inert nutrient or herb. Produced mainly by the adrenal glands, DHEA and its sulfated form DHEA-S are the most abundant circulating steroids in the human body, and they serve as an upstream precursor that the body converts into androgens (testosterone, DHT) and estrogens (estradiol). DHEA levels peak in the mid-twenties and then decline steadily with age — falling by roughly 70-80% by the seventh or eighth decade — which is the central rationale for supplementation: replacing an age-related decline rather than adding a novel compound. Because it is a hormone precursor, taking DHEA can raise both testosterone and estrogen, with effects that differ substantially between men and women and between individuals depending on how their bodies route the precursor. In the United States, DHEA is sold over the counter as a "dietary supplement" due to a specific legal exemption, but this is a regulatory quirk: it is banned in sport by WADA as a prohormone/anabolic agent, is prescription-only or controlled in several other countries, and should be treated with the seriousness of a hormone — including baseline and follow-up bloodwork and physician involvement, especially for anyone with hormone-sensitive conditions. It is best approached as hormone optimization under guidance, not as a casual addition to a stack.
