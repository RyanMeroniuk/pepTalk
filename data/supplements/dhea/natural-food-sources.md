# DHEA
## Natural Food Sources

- **No meaningful food source:** DHEA is a steroid hormone produced inside the body (primarily by the adrenal glands). There is no food that contains DHEA in amounts that meaningfully affect the body's DHEA levels. You cannot "eat your way" to higher DHEA — it is synthesized endogenously, not obtained from diet

- **The "wild yam" myth (important to dispel):**
  - Some supplements are marketed as "natural DHEA from wild yam" or sell wild yam (Dioscorea) extract implying it converts to DHEA in the body. **This is false.** Wild yam contains diosgenin, a plant sterol that can be converted to DHEA *in a laboratory* through chemical synthesis — but the human body **cannot** perform this conversion. Eating wild yam or taking wild yam extract does NOT raise DHEA
  - Any DHEA in a supplement is laboratory-synthesized (often from diosgenin as a starting material), not a botanical that your body activates

- **Cholesterol is the true precursor (endogenous pathway):** The body makes DHEA from cholesterol via pregnenolone within the adrenal glands and gonads. Dietary cholesterol provides raw material for overall steroid synthesis, but eating cholesterol does not specifically or reliably raise DHEA levels

- **What actually influences DHEA levels:**
  - **Age** is the dominant factor — DHEA peaks in the mid-twenties and declines steadily thereafter
  - **Chronic stress** and elevated cortisol can suppress DHEA (the "cortisol steal" concept, debated mechanistically but reflected in altered DHEA-to-cortisol ratios)
  - **Adequate sleep, stress management, and exercise** support healthy adrenal function and may help maintain DHEA within age-appropriate ranges — but lifestyle cannot restore youthful levels

- **Bottom line:** DHEA is a hormone, not a dietary nutrient. If supplementation is pursued, it must come from a synthesized DHEA product (treated as a hormone) — there is no dietary route and no genuine "natural food source"
