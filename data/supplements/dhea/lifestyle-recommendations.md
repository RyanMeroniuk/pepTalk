# DHEA
## Lifestyle Recommendations

- **Treat it as hormone optimization, not a supplement habit:** The most important "lifestyle" decision is to involve a physician, get baseline labs (DHEA-S, total/free testosterone, estradiol, and PSA in men), and recheck after starting. DHEA is a hormone precursor and should be managed accordingly
- **Keep body fat in check:** Adipose tissue contains aromatase, which converts DHEA-derived androgens into estrogen. Lower body fat means less unwanted estrogen conversion — directly improving DHEA outcomes, especially for men. Diet and training that keep body fat reasonable are a genuine lever
- **Resistance training and exercise:** Exercise supports healthy hormonal milieu and may help maintain endogenous DHEA. It also helps direct DHEA-derived hormones toward favorable body-composition effects
- **Manage stress and prioritize sleep:** Chronic stress and elevated cortisol are associated with altered DHEA-to-cortisol ratios. Good sleep and stress management support adrenal function and the broader hormonal picture
- **Moderate alcohol:** Alcohol burdens the liver (which metabolizes oral DHEA) and tends to shift the androgen-estrogen balance unfavorably. Keep it moderate
- **Eat a whole-food, lower-sugar diet:** Reducing refined sugar and processed food lowers inflammation and insulin resistance, which reduces aromatase activity and unwanted estrogen conversion
- **Support bone health holistically:** If using DHEA partly for bone density (more relevant in older women), combine it with vitamin D3, adequate calcium and magnesium, vitamin K2, and weight-bearing exercise rather than relying on the hormone alone
- **Reassess periodically — don't use indefinitely without review:** Because DHEA is a hormone with oncologic and cardiovascular considerations, periodic re-evaluation of need, dose, and labs is appropriate
- **Avoid entirely if competing in drug-tested sport:** DHEA is banned by WADA; the lifestyle reality for athletes is simply not to use it
