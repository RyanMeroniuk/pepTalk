# DHEA
## Things to Avoid

- **Treating DHEA like a casual supplement:** This is the central message. DHEA is a hormone precursor, not a vitamin or herb. Its OTC status in the US is a regulatory exemption, not evidence of safety. Approach it with the seriousness of hormone therapy
- **Using it without bloodwork:** Never take DHEA blindly. Check DHEA-S, total/free testosterone, and estradiol at baseline and follow-up. Individual conversion to androgens vs estrogens varies widely, and you cannot manage what you don't measure
- **Using it with hormone-sensitive conditions:** Contraindicated with prostate cancer, breast cancer, ovarian/uterine conditions, and other hormone-sensitive cancers, because DHEA converts to testosterone and estrogen and could promote hormone-driven tumor growth. Do not use without specialist oversight
- **Use during pregnancy or breastfeeding:** Contraindicated — a hormone with potential developmental effects
- **High "bodybuilding" doses:** Pushing supraphysiologic doses to chase muscle/testosterone disproportionately raises estrogen (men) and androgenic side effects like voice deepening and hirsutism (women, sometimes irreversible), with weak evidence of benefit. The goal is replacement, not overshoot
- **Women dosing like men:** Women are more sensitive to androgenic effects and generally need much lower doses (often 5-25 mg). Voice deepening can be permanent — conservative dosing is essential
- **Competing in sport while using it:** DHEA is banned by WADA and most athletic organizations as a prohormone/anabolic agent. Athletes risk sanctions
- **Trusting label accuracy without third-party testing:** Independent analyses have repeatedly found DHEA products with far more or less than labeled. Use third-party-tested products and verify with labs
- **Confusing DHEA with 7-Keto DHEA:** 7-Keto DHEA does NOT convert to sex hormones and has a different effect/risk profile. They are not interchangeable
- **Stacking it with other hormonal agents without a plan:** Combining DHEA with TRT, testosterone-support herbs (tongkat-ali, fadogia-agrestis), or other hormonal interventions multiplies the androgen/estrogen load and complicates monitoring. Coordinate with a knowledgeable clinician
- **Believing "wild yam" raises DHEA:** The body cannot convert wild yam/diosgenin to DHEA. Such products do not work as marketed
