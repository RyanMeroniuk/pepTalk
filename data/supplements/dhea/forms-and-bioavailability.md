# DHEA
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Oral micronized DHEA (most common supplement form):**
  - **What it is:** DHEA powder reduced to fine particle size to improve absorption and consistency. Most OTC capsules/tablets use micronized DHEA
  - **Why micronized:** Plain DHEA is poorly and erratically absorbed; micronization improves and stabilizes bioavailability
  - **Typical doses:** Often 25-50 mg; lower doses (5-10 mg) are frequently more appropriate, especially for women (see dosing)
  - **First-pass metabolism:** Oral DHEA undergoes substantial conversion to DHEA-S and downstream hormones on first pass through the liver, contributing to variable individual responses

- **Intravaginal DHEA (prasterone) — prescription:**
  - **What it is:** An FDA-approved prescription insert (e.g., Intrarosa) delivering DHEA locally
  - **Why it matters:** Acts locally via tissue conversion to androgens/estrogens with limited systemic exposure, used for postmenopausal vulvovaginal atrophy. This is a regulated drug, not an OTC supplement

- **7-Keto DHEA (a distinct metabolite):**
  - **What it is:** 7-oxo-DHEA, a DHEA derivative that is NOT converted to testosterone or estrogen
  - **Why it differs:** Because it does not become sex hormones, it lacks DHEA's androgenic/estrogenic effects (and is treated differently in some regulatory/sport contexts). It is marketed for metabolism/weight, with weak evidence. It should not be confused with DHEA — it is a different compound with a different risk/effect profile

- **Topical/transdermal DHEA:**
  - Creams exist; absorption is variable and harder to dose precisely. Less standardized than oral micronized forms

- **Label-accuracy and quality concerns (significant for DHEA):**
  - Because DHEA is a hormone sold OTC, independent testing has repeatedly found products with substantially more or less DHEA than labeled. Dose accuracy is a real problem
  - Choose third-party-tested products and treat label doses with skepticism — verify with bloodwork (DHEA-S, testosterone, estradiol)

- **Bioavailability summary:** Micronized oral DHEA is the practical standard but produces variable serum responses due to first-pass metabolism and individual enzyme differences. This variability is a core reason DHEA should be dosed conservatively and monitored with labs
