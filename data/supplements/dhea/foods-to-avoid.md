# DHEA
## Foods to Avoid

> DHEA's effects are hormonal, so the relevant "avoid" considerations are about supporting healthy hormone balance and not compounding estrogenic or metabolic risk — not about foods blocking absorption.

- **Excessive alcohol:** Alcohol stresses the liver (which heavily metabolizes oral DHEA), disrupts hormone balance, and can raise estrogen relative to androgens. Heavy drinking compounds DHEA's estrogenic risk in men and burdens the organ that processes it
- **High-sugar, ultra-processed diets:** Promote inflammation, insulin resistance, and increased aromatase activity in adipose tissue — meaning more conversion of DHEA-derived androgens to estrogen. This is counterproductive, especially for men trying to avoid estrogenic side effects
- **Excess body-fat-promoting overeating:** Adipose tissue is the main site of aromatase. The more body fat, the more DHEA can be converted to estrogen. Dietary patterns that drive fat gain worsen DHEA's estrogenic side-effect profile
- **Grapefruit and grapefruit juice (theoretical):** Grapefruit inhibits CYP3A4, which is involved in steroid metabolism. This could theoretically alter DHEA or downstream hormone levels, and matters more if you take other CYP3A4-sensitive medications. Treat as a cautionary, not a hard rule

### Behaviors, Not Foods
- **Skipping bloodwork:** The biggest "avoid" with DHEA is using it without monitoring DHEA-S, testosterone, and estradiol
- **Chasing high doses via diet/stacking:** No food makes high-dose DHEA safer; the risk is the hormone load itself

### Generally Fine
- There are no foods that meaningfully block DHEA absorption
- A whole-food, lower-sugar diet that keeps body fat in check actually *reduces* unwanted estrogen conversion — supporting better DHEA outcomes
