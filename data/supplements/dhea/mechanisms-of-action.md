# DHEA
## Mechanisms of Action

- **Steroid precursor / prohormone (primary mechanism):** DHEA is an upstream steroid that the body converts, in peripheral tissues, into sex hormones. Through the enzymes 3β-HSD, 17β-HSD, 5α-reductase, and aromatase, DHEA can become testosterone, dihydrotestosterone (DHT), and the estrogens estradiol and estrone. How much goes to androgens versus estrogens depends on the individual's enzyme activity, sex, and tissue — which is why effects are variable and not fully predictable
- **Sex-dependent hormonal effects:**
  - **In women** (especially postmenopausal), DHEA is a major source of both androgens and estrogens, since the ovaries no longer produce much hormone. Supplementation tends to raise testosterone and, to some degree, estrogen
  - **In men**, the testes produce most testosterone, so DHEA's contribution to testosterone is smaller; a notable fraction may aromatize to estrogen, which is why men can see disproportionate estradiol increases
- **Androgen receptor and estrogen receptor signaling:** The downstream hormones DHEA produces act on androgen and estrogen receptors throughout the body — affecting libido, mood, muscle, bone, skin, and reproductive tissue
- **Neurosteroid activity:** DHEA and DHEA-S act directly in the brain as neurosteroids, modulating GABA-A (antagonist/negative modulator) and NMDA receptors, and influencing mood, cognition, and stress resilience independent of their conversion to sex hormones
- **Anti-glucocorticoid / adrenal-axis effects:** DHEA partially counterbalances cortisol's catabolic effects; the DHEA-to-cortisol ratio is studied as a marker of stress resilience and aging
- **Bone, skin, and metabolic effects:** Via its hormonal conversions, DHEA influences bone mineral density, skin thickness/hydration, and some metabolic parameters — effects most evident in those replacing an age-related deficiency
- **Vaginal/local effects (women):** Intravaginal DHEA (prasterone) is converted locally to androgens and estrogens to treat vulvovaginal atrophy, illustrating its tissue-level precursor role
