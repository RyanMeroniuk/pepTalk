# DHEA
## Side Effects

> DHEA is a hormone precursor. Its side effects are the side effects of raising androgens and estrogens, and they differ by sex and dose.

### Androgenic Effects (more common in women, dose-dependent in both)
- **Acne and oily skin**
- **Facial/body hair growth (hirsutism)** in women
- **Scalp hair thinning / male-pattern hair loss** (via conversion to DHT) in predisposed individuals
- **Voice deepening** in women (can be irreversible) — a notable reason women must dose conservatively
- **Mood changes / increased irritability or aggression**

### Estrogenic Effects (notably in men)
- **Gynecomastia (breast tissue development)** and **water retention** from aromatization of DHEA to estrogen — men can see disproportionate estradiol increases
- **Mood and libido changes** linked to estrogen shifts

### Other
- **Sleep disturbance and palpitations** reported by some users
- **Liver effects:** As an orally metabolized steroid, caution is warranted in liver disease; monitor
- **Blood lipid changes:** May lower HDL ("good" cholesterol) in some individuals, particularly women
- **Headache, fatigue, or nasal congestion** occasionally reported

### Hormone-Sensitive Cancer Risk (Critical)
- Because DHEA converts to testosterone and estrogen, it is **contraindicated in hormone-sensitive cancers and conditions** — including prostate cancer, breast cancer, ovarian cancer, and uterine/endometrial conditions. DHEA could theoretically promote growth of hormone-driven tumors
- Anyone with a personal or strong family history of these conditions should not use DHEA without specialist oversight

### Contraindications
- **Hormone-sensitive cancers** (prostate, breast, ovarian, uterine) — contraindicated
- **Prostate conditions (BPH, elevated PSA):** Caution/contraindication given androgen conversion
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** Contraindicated — a hormone with potential to affect fetal/infant development
- **PCOS / women with already-high androgens:** Caution; may worsen androgenic symptoms
- **Liver disease, history of blood clots, or significant cardiovascular disease:** Caution; physician oversight
- **Competitive athletes:** Banned by WADA — using it risks sanctions and is a hormonal manipulation

### Safety Profile Summary
- DHEA is NOT a benign supplement; it is a hormone precursor with real androgenic, estrogenic, and oncologic-risk considerations
- Side effects are dose-dependent and sex-dependent, which is why conservative dosing, bloodwork, and physician involvement are strongly advised
