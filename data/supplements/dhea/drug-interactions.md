# DHEA
## Drug Interactions

> As a hormone precursor, DHEA's interactions are driven by its conversion to androgens and estrogens and by its hepatic metabolism. These are more significant than for typical supplements.

### Contraindications (Avoid)
- **Hormone-sensitive cancers and conditions:** Contraindicated in prostate cancer, breast cancer, ovarian cancer, and uterine/endometrial conditions. DHEA converts to testosterone and estrogen and could promote hormone-driven tumor growth
- **Active cancer (general):** Do not initiate without oncologist input
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** Contraindicated — a hormone with developmental implications
- **Hormone therapy / TRT and estrogen therapy:** Combining DHEA with testosterone replacement, estrogen therapy, or other hormonal agents stacks sex-hormone load and complicates monitoring. Should only occur under specialist management

### Caution / Monitor (Moderate)
- **Estrogens and anti-estrogens (e.g., aromatase inhibitors, tamoxifen, raloxifene):** DHEA raises estrogen via aromatization and can interfere with the intent of anti-estrogen therapy or add to estrogen therapy. Monitor and coordinate with the prescriber
- **Antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, and others):** DHEA has neurosteroid activity and may affect mood; monitor for mood changes or additive/opposing effects when combined. Some interest exists in DHEA for depression, but combining with antidepressants should be monitored
- **Insulin and diabetes medications:** DHEA may influence insulin sensitivity in some individuals; monitor blood glucose if combining
- **Anticoagulants / blood thinners (warfarin, antiplatelets):** Hormonal changes can influence clotting factors; DHEA has been associated with effects on clotting in some contexts. Use caution and monitor
- **Statins and lipid-lowering therapy:** DHEA may lower HDL in some users (especially women), which is relevant when managing a lipid profile; monitor lipids
- **Drugs metabolized by CYP enzymes (e.g., CYP3A4 substrates):** DHEA and its steroid metabolism intersect with hepatic enzyme pathways; caution with narrow-therapeutic-index drugs and with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors/inducers
- **Corticosteroids:** DHEA partially counterbalances cortisol effects; the interaction with exogenous corticosteroids is complex — monitor under medical guidance

### Timing Considerations
- **Morning dosing:** Aligning DHEA with the natural morning peak of adrenal output is the conventional timing; keep it consistent
- **Separate from other hormonal agents' monitoring:** When combined with hormonal therapies, coordinate dosing and lab timing with the prescribing clinician

### Lab / Monitoring Notes (Critical)
- **Essential labs:** DHEA-S, total and free testosterone, estradiol; PSA in men; lipid panel. Baseline and follow-up (~6-8 weeks) are strongly advised
- **Inform all healthcare providers** that you take DHEA — it alters sex-hormone labs and can affect interpretation of endocrine workups

### General Safety Notes
- The dominant interaction theme is hormonal overlap and oncologic risk, not minor pharmacokinetics
- Because DHEA is a hormone sold OTC with variable label accuracy, physician oversight and bloodwork are the key safety controls
