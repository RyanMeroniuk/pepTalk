# DHEA
## Benefits

> Context: DHEA's benefits are most credible when used to *replace an age-related deficiency* under medical guidance. Evidence is mixed, and effects differ markedly between men and women. It is a hormone, so "benefit" claims should be weighed against the hormonal risks in the side-effects and drug-interactions sections.

- **Female sexual health and vulvovaginal atrophy (best-supported use):** Intravaginal DHEA (prasterone) is an FDA-approved prescription treatment for postmenopausal vulvovaginal atrophy and painful intercourse, working by local conversion to androgens/estrogens. This is the most rigorously supported DHEA indication
- **Adrenal insufficiency (replacement context):** In people with adrenal insufficiency (e.g., Addison's disease) who have genuinely low DHEA, replacement may modestly improve well-being, mood, and sexual function. This is a true deficiency-replacement scenario
- **Mood and well-being (mixed):** Some trials, particularly in older adults or those with low baseline DHEA, report improvements in mood and depressive symptoms, likely via neurosteroid and hormonal effects. Results are inconsistent
- **Bone mineral density (modest, mainly women):** In older adults, especially women, DHEA combined with vitamin D and exercise has shown modest bone density benefits, consistent with its conversion to sex hormones that support bone
- **Sexual function and libido (variable):** Some improvement reported, more consistently in women and in those with low baseline levels; effects in men are less clear
- **Skin (women):** Topical/oral DHEA has shown improvements in skin hydration, thickness, and sebum production in older women
- **Fertility (specific use under specialists):** DHEA is sometimes used by reproductive endocrinologists for women with diminished ovarian reserve undergoing IVF; this is a specialist-directed off-label use, not a general recommendation
- **Body composition and metabolic effects (weak/inconsistent):** Despite popular claims, robust evidence that DHEA meaningfully improves body composition, strength, or metabolic health in healthy younger adults is lacking

### Realistic Expectations
- DHEA is not a reliable "testosterone booster" for healthy young men — much can aromatize to estrogen, and the testes already supply most testosterone
- The strongest evidence is in deficiency states (aging women, adrenal insufficiency) and for the FDA-approved intravaginal form
- Because it is a hormone, benefits should never be pursued without bloodwork and ideally physician oversight
