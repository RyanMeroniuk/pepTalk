# Hyaluronic Acid (oral)
## Classification

- **Category:** Joint Lubrication / Skin Hydration / Connective Tissue Support
- **Type:** Naturally occurring glycosaminoglycan (GAG); high-molecular-weight polysaccharide
- **Chemical class:** Linear polysaccharide of repeating disaccharide units (D-glucuronic acid + N-acetyl-D-glucosamine); supplemental forms are often the sodium salt (sodium hyaluronate)
- **Source:** Endogenously produced throughout the body, with high concentrations in synovial fluid, skin, eyes, and connective tissue. Supplemental HA is produced by bacterial fermentation (e.g., Streptococcus or Bacillus strains) or extracted from rooster comb.
- **Regulatory status:** Dietary supplement (oral, OTC); also used as an injectable medical device (intra-articular joint injections, dermal fillers) and topical cosmetic ingredient
- **Primary function:** Joint lubrication and shock absorption (as a key component of synovial fluid), and skin hydration (water-binding capacity in the dermis)
- **Primary systems:** Musculoskeletal (synovial fluid, cartilage), integumentary (skin), ocular (vitreous humor, tear film)
- **Key distinction:** This entry covers ORAL hyaluronic acid (sodium hyaluronate). It is distinct from intra-articular HA injections (viscosupplementation) and dermal fillers, which are medical procedures. Molecular weight (low vs high) strongly influences oral absorption and effect.
