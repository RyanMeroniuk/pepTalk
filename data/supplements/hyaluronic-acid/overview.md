# Hyaluronic Acid (oral)
## Overview

Hyaluronic acid (HA) is a glycosaminoglycan that the body produces naturally and concentrates in synovial fluid, skin, and the eyes. Its defining property is an extraordinary capacity to bind water — a single gram can hold up to six liters. As an oral supplement (usually as sodium hyaluronate), it is used to support joint lubrication and skin hydration. The evidence shows modest but real benefits for knee osteoarthritis symptoms and skin moisture, making it a useful member of the joint-and-skin support stack rather than a dramatic standalone agent.

**Why it matters:**

- **Joint lubrication and cushioning** — HA is the principal molecule responsible for the viscosity and shock-absorbing properties of synovial fluid. In osteoarthritis, synovial HA decreases in concentration and molecular weight, reducing lubrication. Oral HA aims to support synovial fluid quality.
- **Modest knee OA benefit** — Multiple randomized controlled trials and meta-analyses report that oral HA (80-200mg/day) produces small-to-moderate improvements in knee osteoarthritis pain and function over 8-12 weeks. The effect is real but modest; HA is best used alongside glucosamine-chondroitin, MSM, and collagen peptides.
- **Skin hydration** — Oral HA increases skin moisture and may improve elasticity and reduce fine wrinkles in placebo-controlled studies, likely by supporting dermal HA content and the skin's water-binding capacity.
- **The absorption question** — High-molecular-weight HA is too large to be absorbed intact and is broken down by gut bacteria into smaller fragments and building blocks. Lower-molecular-weight HA is absorbed more readily. Studies show orally consumed HA does reach skin and joint tissues, partly intact and partly as building blocks that stimulate the body's own HA synthesis.
- **Complements cartilage-building supplements** — HA addresses lubrication, while glucosamine-chondroitin and collagen peptides address the cartilage matrix and structural protein. Together they cover different aspects of joint health.
- **Excellent safety** — Oral HA is very well tolerated with an excellent safety profile and minimal side effects.
- **Distinct from injections** — Oral HA is not the same as intra-articular HA injections (viscosupplementation) or dermal fillers, which are medical procedures with their own evidence base and stronger localized effects.
