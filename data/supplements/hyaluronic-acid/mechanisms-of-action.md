# Hyaluronic Acid (oral)
## Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Water Binding & Tissue Hydration
- HA is exceptionally hydrophilic, binding up to ~1,000 times its weight in water
- In the skin's dermis, this maintains hydration, volume, and elasticity
- In synovial fluid and cartilage, water binding maintains the viscoelastic, shock-absorbing properties of the joint

### 2. Synovial Fluid Viscosity & Lubrication
- HA is the primary determinant of synovial fluid viscosity, providing boundary lubrication between cartilage surfaces
- In osteoarthritis, synovial HA declines in both concentration and molecular weight, reducing lubrication and cushioning
- Supporting HA availability aims to improve synovial fluid quality and reduce friction during joint movement

### 3. Building-Block Supply for Endogenous HA Synthesis
- High-molecular-weight oral HA is broken down by gut microbiota into oligosaccharides and the constituent sugars (glucuronic acid, N-acetylglucosamine)
- These fragments are absorbed and can serve as substrate for the body's own HA synthase enzymes, supporting endogenous HA production in skin and joints
- Some lower-molecular-weight HA fragments are absorbed more intact

### 4. Signaling via HA Receptors (CD44, RHAMM)
- HA fragments interact with cellular receptors such as CD44, influencing fibroblast and chondrocyte activity
- Low-molecular-weight HA fragments can signal cells to increase their own HA and extracellular matrix production
- This receptor signaling may explain effects that exceed what simple substrate supply would predict

### 5. Anti-Inflammatory Effects in the Joint
- HA modulates the joint inflammatory environment, reducing certain pro-inflammatory mediators and prostaglandin production in synovial tissue
- High-molecular-weight HA tends to be anti-inflammatory, while very low-molecular-weight fragments can be pro-inflammatory — molecular weight matters for the net effect

### 6. Chondrocyte and Cartilage Support
- By improving the joint environment (hydration, lubrication, reduced inflammation), HA indirectly supports chondrocyte health
- HA is itself a component of the cartilage matrix, forming the backbone to which aggrecan (a chondroitin-rich proteoglycan) attaches

### 7. Skin Fibroblast Stimulation
- Oral HA-derived fragments reaching the skin may stimulate dermal fibroblasts to produce HA and collagen
- This is the proposed mechanism behind improvements in skin hydration, elasticity, and reduced fine wrinkles seen in clinical studies
