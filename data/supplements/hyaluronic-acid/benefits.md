# Hyaluronic Acid (oral)
## Benefits

### Knee Osteoarthritis (Modest but Documented)
- **Reduced knee OA pain and stiffness** — Multiple randomized controlled trials and meta-analyses report small-to-moderate improvements in knee osteoarthritis pain and WOMAC function scores with oral HA (80-200mg/day) over 8-12 weeks.
- **Improved joint comfort during activity** — Users report reduced friction sensation and improved comfort with daily movement.
- **Additive in joint stacks** — HA complements glucosamine-chondroitin, MSM, and collagen peptides; combination products are common and may produce greater benefit than any single ingredient.
- **Realistic framing** — The effect is modest. HA is a supportive, slow-acting joint aid, not a dramatic pain reliever, and oral HA is less potent than intra-articular HA injection.

### Skin Hydration & Quality
- **Increased skin moisture** — Placebo-controlled studies show oral HA improves skin hydration, particularly in individuals with dry skin.
- **Improved elasticity and reduced fine wrinkles** — Several trials report improvements in skin elasticity, suppleness, and reduction of fine lines over 8-12 weeks.
- **Synergy with collagen** — Often combined with collagen peptides and vitamin C for comprehensive skin support, addressing both hydration (HA) and structural protein (collagen).

### Joint Lubrication & Mobility
- **Supports synovial fluid quality** — By supporting HA availability, oral HA aims to maintain the viscosity and cushioning of synovial fluid that declines with age and osteoarthritis.
- **Useful for active individuals** — Used to support joint comfort under repetitive loading, often alongside MSM and glucosamine-chondroitin.

### Eye & Mucosal Hydration (Preliminary)
- **Dry eye support** — Some evidence (often topical, but with growing oral interest) that HA supports tear film and ocular surface hydration. Oral evidence here is preliminary — flagged as low-confidence.

### Wound Healing & Tissue Repair (Supportive)
- **Tissue repair role** — HA is heavily involved in wound healing and tissue remodeling. While most clinical wound-healing use is topical/injectable, HA's role in connective-tissue repair underpins its inclusion in recovery-oriented stacks.

### Realistic Expectations
- Oral HA is a modest, well-tolerated, slow-acting supplement. Benefits for joints and skin emerge over 8-12 weeks of consistent use.
- It is best deployed as part of a stack (glucosamine-chondroitin, collagen-peptides, MSM) rather than as a standalone fix, and oral effects are gentler than HA injections or fillers.
