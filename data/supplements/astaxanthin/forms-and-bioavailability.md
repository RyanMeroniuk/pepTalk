# Astaxanthin
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Natural astaxanthin (Haematococcus pluvialis — preferred):** Extracted from the microalga that naturally produces it. Natural astaxanthin is esterified (bound to fatty acids) and exists predominantly as the 3S,3'S stereoisomer. It is the form used in most positive human research and is considered superior to synthetic. Branded examples include AstaReal and BioAstin
- **Synthetic astaxanthin:** Produced petrochemically for aquaculture feed; contains a mix of stereoisomers (including forms not found in nature) and is largely free (non-esterified). Not recommended for human supplementation — choose natural sources
- **Krill oil astaxanthin:** Krill oil naturally contains some astaxanthin alongside omega-3 phospholipids. The astaxanthin content is relatively low but contributes antioxidant stability to the oil. A convenient way to get astaxanthin plus omega-3 together
- **Softgel in oil (most common consumer form):** Because astaxanthin is fat-soluble, oil-based softgels are the standard and best-absorbed format. Look for products with a lipid carrier
- **Bioavailability — fat dependence:** Astaxanthin absorption is significantly enhanced when taken with dietary fat. Taking it with a meal containing fat can increase absorption several-fold compared with a fasted state
- **Esterified vs free form:** Natural esterified astaxanthin is cleaved by digestive lipases and absorbed well when consumed with fat. The esterified natural form is generally well bioavailable
- **Dose-response and saturation:** Plasma astaxanthin rises with dose and accumulates in tissues (skin, eye, muscle) over days to weeks. Benefits typically require consistent daily use for 2-8 weeks
- **Stability:** Astaxanthin is sensitive to light, heat, and oxygen. Quality products use protective encapsulation; store away from light and heat
- **Practical choice:** A natural astaxanthin softgel (Haematococcus-derived) at 4-12 mg, taken with a fat-containing meal, is the sensible default. Combining with omega-3 (or using a krill-oil product) pairs two complementary lipid-phase nutrients
