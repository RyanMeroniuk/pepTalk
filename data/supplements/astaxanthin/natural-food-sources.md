# Astaxanthin
## Natural Food Sources

Astaxanthin originates in microalgae and accumulates up the marine food chain, giving salmon, shrimp, and flamingos their pink-red color. Dietary amounts are far lower than supplemental doses.

### Marine Sources (Highest)
- **Wild sockeye salmon** — among the richest dietary sources, roughly 25-40 mg per kg of flesh (wild salmon has much more than farmed). A typical serving provides a few milligrams
- **Wild salmon (other species)** — varies by species; generally lower than sockeye
- **Krill** — the basis of krill oil; contains astaxanthin bound with omega-3 phospholipids
- **Shrimp and prawns** — modest amounts; concentrated in the shell and the pink flesh
- **Crab and lobster** — astaxanthin gives the cooked shell its red color
- **Rainbow trout and red sea bream** — contain astaxanthin from their diet
- **Salmon roe (ikura)** — rich in astaxanthin, contributing to the deep orange-red color

### Algal Source (the Original)
- **Haematococcus pluvialis** — the freshwater microalga that produces astaxanthin as a stress pigment; the source of most natural astaxanthin supplements. Not typically eaten directly, but it is the foundation of the marine food chain's astaxanthin
- **Phaffia rhodozyma (yeast)** — a yeast source used industrially

### Important Context
- **Wild vs farmed salmon:** Wild salmon obtains astaxanthin from its krill/algae diet and contains substantially more than farmed salmon. Farmed salmon is often given astaxanthin (sometimes synthetic) in feed to achieve pink color
- **You cannot easily reach supplement doses from food:** A 6-12 mg supplemental dose would require eating a large amount of salmon daily. Diet supports a baseline; supplementation provides the studied doses
- **Cooking:** Astaxanthin is relatively heat-stable compared with some antioxidants, but prolonged high-heat cooking and light exposure can degrade it
- **Pairs naturally with omega-3:** Astaxanthin-rich foods (salmon, krill) are also omega-3 rich, a naturally complementary combination for membrane and cardiovascular health
