# Astaxanthin
## Overview

Astaxanthin is a deep red-pink xanthophyll carotenoid produced by the microalga Haematococcus pluvialis as a defense against environmental stress, and it is one of the most potent lipid-phase antioxidants identified. Unlike many antioxidants that work in only one part of the cell, astaxanthin's elongated molecular structure spans the full width of the cell membrane, letting it neutralize free radicals at both the inner and outer membrane surfaces. It is studied primarily for eye fatigue, skin photoprotection, endurance, and cardiovascular and oxidative-stress markers. Most quality supplements use natural astaxanthin from Haematococcus pluvialis (the AstaReal brand is a widely studied example) rather than synthetic forms.

- **Why it matters:** As a fat-soluble antioxidant that embeds in cell membranes, astaxanthin protects the lipid structures most vulnerable to oxidation — membranes, LDL particles, and the lipid-rich retina and brain. It also reportedly does not flip to a pro-oxidant state at high concentrations, a concern with some other carotenoids.
- **Eye fatigue:** Some of the strongest human data are for reducing eye strain and accommodation fatigue in screen users, with several Japanese trials at 6-12 mg/day.
- **Skin:** Oral astaxanthin is studied for reducing UV-induced skin damage, improving elasticity and hydration, and supporting skin appearance, often combined with topical use.
- **Endurance and recovery:** Investigated for reducing exercise-induced oxidative stress and supporting endurance, though athletic-performance results are mixed.
- **Crosses biological barriers:** Astaxanthin crosses the blood-brain and blood-retinal barriers, supporting interest in neural and retinal applications.
- **Honest framing:** Eye-fatigue and skin data are the most developed; broader claims (longevity, major performance gains) are promising but not definitive. Effects depend on using genuine natural astaxanthin and consistent dosing.
