# Astaxanthin
## Mechanisms of Action

- **Membrane-spanning antioxidant (primary mechanism):** Astaxanthin's long polyene chain with polar end groups orients across the entire lipid bilayer, positioning it to quench free radicals at both the inner and outer membrane surfaces simultaneously. This makes it especially effective at protecting cell membranes from lipid peroxidation
- **Singlet oxygen quenching:** Astaxanthin is an exceptionally efficient quencher of singlet oxygen, the reactive species generated by UV light and certain metabolic processes — a key part of its photoprotective and anti-fatigue effects
- **Does not become pro-oxidant:** Unlike beta-carotene and some other carotenoids, astaxanthin does not readily auto-oxidize or act as a pro-oxidant at higher concentrations under physiological conditions, allowing sustained antioxidant action
- **Nrf2 pathway activation:** Astaxanthin activates the Nrf2/ARE pathway, upregulating endogenous antioxidant enzymes (superoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione peroxidase) for a sustained, amplifying antioxidant defense beyond its direct scavenging
- **Anti-inflammatory signaling:** Suppresses NF-kB activation, reducing pro-inflammatory mediators (TNF-alpha, IL-6, IL-1beta, COX-2/PGE2). This underlies its effects on exercise-induced inflammation and tissue stress
- **Mitochondrial protection:** By embedding in mitochondrial membranes and quenching ROS, astaxanthin helps preserve mitochondrial membrane integrity and function, supporting cellular energy production
- **Crosses biological barriers:** Astaxanthin crosses the blood-brain barrier and blood-retinal barrier, allowing it to deliver antioxidant protection directly to neural and retinal tissue — most other dietary carotenoids do not reach these compartments effectively
- **Ocular blood flow and accommodation:** Proposed to improve ciliary muscle function and microcirculation in the eye, contributing to reduced accommodation fatigue and eye strain
- **LDL protection:** As a lipid-phase antioxidant, astaxanthin protects LDL particles from oxidation, a relevant step in the development of atherosclerosis
