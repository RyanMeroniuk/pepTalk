# Astaxanthin
## Foods to Avoid

- **Fat-free meals at dosing time:** The biggest mistake is taking astaxanthin without fat. A fat-free or very-low-fat meal sharply reduces absorption of this fat-soluble carotenoid. Always pair with dietary fat
- **Excessive alcohol:** Alcohol increases oxidative stress and burdens the liver, working against astaxanthin's antioxidant purpose. Heavy drinking also impairs nutrient absorption broadly
- **Highly pro-oxidant, processed diets:** Diets high in refined sugar, repeatedly heated/oxidized oils, and ultra-processed foods raise oxidative load. Astaxanthin helps, but it cannot offset a continuously pro-oxidant diet
- **Very high doses of competing carotenoids at the same time:** Carotenoids (beta-carotene, lutein, lycopene, astaxanthin) share absorption pathways. Megadosing one carotenoid simultaneously with astaxanthin can modestly reduce its uptake. Moderate, food-based carotenoid variety is fine; extreme single-carotenoid megadoses at the same time are best spaced

### Foods That Are Not a Concern
- **Most foods do not interfere** — there is no common food that meaningfully blocks astaxanthin beyond the fat-free-meal issue
- **Fiber and protein** do not impair astaxanthin absorption in any meaningful way when fat is present

### The Real "Avoid" List (Behaviors)
- **Taking it fasted** — undermines absorption
- **Inconsistency** — astaxanthin accumulates in tissues over weeks; sporadic use limits benefit
- **Excessive UV exposure without other protection** — astaxanthin supports skin photoprotection but does not replace sunscreen and sensible sun habits

### Timing With Other Supplements
- **Pairs well with omega-3 and fat-soluble vitamins (D, E, K)** — all benefit from the same fatty meal
- **Space from megadose single carotenoids** if using very high isolated doses
- **Generally compatible** with most supplements when taken with food
