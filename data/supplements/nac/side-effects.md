# NAC (N-Acetylcysteine)
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild)
- **Gastrointestinal upset** — nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal discomfort are the most frequently reported effects, especially at higher doses or on an empty stomach. Taking with food and splitting doses helps
- **Sulfur smell / taste** — NAC has a characteristic "rotten egg" odor and may cause a temporary sulfur-like taste. This is normal for a thiol compound

### Uncommon
- **Headache** — usually mild and transient
- **Fatigue or drowsiness** — reported occasionally
- **Skin rash** — uncommon; discontinue if a rash develops
- **Low blood pressure / lightheadedness** — possible in sensitive individuals, particularly relevant to those on blood pressure medication

### Rare
- **Allergic / hypersensitivity reactions** — rare with oral use; more associated with IV administration in clinical settings (flushing, itching, rarely bronchospasm). People with a history of severe reactions should use medical supervision
- **Bronchospasm** — primarily a concern with inhaled/nebulized acetylcysteine in asthma, not typical with oral supplements

### Theoretical / Context-Dependent
- **Blunted exercise adaptation** — high antioxidant doses taken immediately around training may dampen the beneficial oxidative signaling that drives some training adaptations. Timing NAC away from workouts mitigates this
- **Bleeding tendency** — NAC has mild effects on platelet aggregation; relevant mainly when combined with anticoagulants or before surgery
- **Pulmonary concern (preclinical):** Some animal studies raised questions about high-dose antioxidants and pulmonary effects in disease models. Human relevance at supplement doses is unclear; this is mentioned for completeness, not as an established risk

### Safety Profile Summary
- **Long clinical track record** — decades of use as a prescription mucolytic and overdose antidote support a strong safety profile at typical doses
- **Most issues are GI and dose-related** — and resolve with lower doses or food
- **Discontinue before surgery** — due to mild antiplatelet activity, stop NAC ahead of procedures per your surgeon's guidance
- **None of this is medical advice** — discuss with a healthcare provider, especially if pregnant, breastfeeding, or on medication
