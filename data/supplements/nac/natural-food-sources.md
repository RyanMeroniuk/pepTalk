# NAC (N-Acetylcysteine)
## Natural Food Sources

NAC itself is not found in food — it is a manufactured, acetylated form of cysteine. However, the body builds glutathione from dietary cysteine, so cysteine-rich and glutathione-supporting foods provide the natural dietary route to the same end goal.

### Cysteine-Rich Foods (the substrate NAC provides)
- **Eggs** — one of the richest sources of cysteine; egg whites and yolks both contribute
- **Poultry** — chicken and turkey
- **Pork and beef** — good sources of sulfur amino acids
- **Fish and seafood** — salmon, tuna, shellfish
- **Dairy** — whey protein is particularly cysteine-dense (a reason whey supports glutathione)
- **Legumes** — lentils, beans, and soybeans provide plant-based cysteine
- **Sunflower seeds, oats, and wheat germ** — plant sources of cysteine

### Glutathione-Supporting Foods
- **Sulfur-rich vegetables** — garlic, onions, leeks (organosulfur compounds support glutathione pathways)
- **Cruciferous vegetables** — broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cauliflower, kale (also activate the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway via sulforaphane)
- **Whey protein** — provides cysteine in a highly bioavailable form and reliably supports glutathione synthesis
- **Asparagus, avocado, and spinach** — contain modest amounts of glutathione directly, though dietary glutathione is largely broken down in digestion

### Important Context
- **You cannot reach therapeutic NAC doses from food** — a single 600 mg NAC capsule supplies far more cysteine substrate than a typical meal. Food supports baseline glutathione; supplementation is what raises it acutely
- **Dietary glutathione is poorly absorbed intact** — which is exactly why supplying cysteine (via NAC or cysteine-rich protein) is the more effective strategy than eating glutathione directly
- **Glycine matters too** — glutathione also requires glycine; a diet adequate in protein (and glycine-rich foods like collagen, bone broth, and skin-on meats) supports the full synthesis pathway
