# NAC (N-Acetylcysteine)
## Drug Interactions

### Significant Interactions

- **Nitroglycerin and nitrates (CRITICAL):**
  - **Drugs:** Nitroglycerin, isosorbide mononitrate/dinitrate, and other organic nitrates
  - **Interaction:** NAC potentiates the vasodilatory and blood-pressure-lowering effect of nitrates. This can cause significant hypotension, dizziness, and severe headaches
  - **Recommendation:** Do not combine without physician guidance. This pairing is contraindicated for casual use; medical supervision is required if both are needed

- **Anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs:**
  - **Drugs:** Warfarin, heparin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, clopidogrel, high-dose aspirin
  - **Interaction:** NAC has mild antiplatelet activity that may add to bleeding risk
  - **Recommendation:** Use caution; inform your physician and monitor for bleeding. Discontinue NAC before surgery

- **Blood pressure medications:**
  - **Drugs:** ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers, beta-blockers, diuretics
  - **Interaction:** NAC may modestly lower blood pressure, an additive hypotensive effect
  - **Recommendation:** Monitor blood pressure, especially when starting NAC

### Moderate Interactions (Use With Caution / Monitor)

- **Chemotherapy agents:**
  - **Interaction:** As an antioxidant, NAC could theoretically blunt chemotherapies that rely on oxidative mechanisms to kill cancer cells. In some contexts NAC may reduce treatment-related toxicity, making the net effect complex
  - **Recommendation:** Do not use during active cancer treatment without oncologist approval

- **Activated charcoal:**
  - **Interaction:** Charcoal can adsorb NAC, reducing its absorption — relevant historically in overdose treatment
  - **Recommendation:** Separate oral NAC and activated charcoal by several hours

- **Immunosuppressants and certain antibiotics:**
  - **Interaction:** Theoretical alterations in drug levels or oxidative balance
  - **Recommendation:** Monitor and inform prescriber

### Timing-Sensitive Notes

- **Separate from binders:** Take NAC several hours apart from activated charcoal or other adsorbent binders
- **Space from nitrate dosing if co-prescribed:** Only under medical supervision

### Population & Condition Cautions

- **Pregnancy:** NAC has been used in pregnancy under medical supervision (e.g., acetaminophen overdose), but routine supplemental use should be discussed with a physician
- **Breastfeeding:** Limited data; discuss with a healthcare provider before routine use
- **Active cancer:** Coordinate any antioxidant use with the oncology team
- **Asthma:** Oral NAC is generally tolerated, but be aware that inhaled acetylcysteine can provoke bronchospasm
- **Bleeding disorders / upcoming surgery:** Caution due to mild antiplatelet effects; stop before procedures

### Beneficial / Neutral Combinations
- **Vitamin C:** Helps keep NAC and glutathione in their active reduced state — a positive pairing
- **Glycine, selenium, ALA:** Support the glutathione system; no adverse interaction
- **Acetaminophen (in overdose, clinical setting):** NAC is the antidote — but this is hospital-administered, not a self-care interaction

*None of this is medical advice. Always inform your healthcare provider of all supplements before starting medications or procedures.*
