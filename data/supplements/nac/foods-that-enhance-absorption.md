# NAC (N-Acetylcysteine)
## Foods That Enhance Absorption

NAC is reasonably absorbed regardless of food, but certain nutrients support its conversion to glutathione and help keep it in its active reduced form.

- **Vitamin C-rich foods:** Citrus, bell peppers, kiwi, strawberries, and broccoli. Vitamin C helps maintain NAC and glutathione in their active reduced (thiol) state and works alongside glutathione in the antioxidant recycling network. Taking NAC with vitamin C is a common, sensible pairing
- **Glycine sources (for glutathione synthesis):** Collagen, bone broth, gelatin, and skin-on/connective-tissue cuts of meat. Glutathione is built from cysteine + glutamate + glycine — supplying glycine alongside NAC's cysteine supports the full synthesis pathway. This is the dietary version of the GlyNAC concept
- **Selenium-rich foods:** Brazil nuts (1-2 per day), sardines, eggs. Selenium is required for glutathione peroxidase, the enzyme that uses glutathione to neutralize peroxides. Adequate selenium maximizes the value of the glutathione NAC helps produce
- **Adequate protein intake:** A protein-sufficient diet provides the glutamate and glycine needed (alongside NAC's cysteine) to actually synthesize glutathione. NAC works best in a well-nourished body
- **Magnesium and B-vitamins:** Support the broader enzymatic machinery of antioxidant defense and one-carbon metabolism that intersects with glutathione production
- **Take with a light meal if GI-sensitive:** While NAC absorbs with or without food, taking it with a small meal reduces the nausea that some people experience on an empty stomach, improving real-world consistency

### Practical Pairing Strategy
- **Simple approach:** 600 mg NAC with a meal that includes some protein and a vitamin-C-rich vegetable or fruit
- **Glutathione-maximizing approach:** NAC + glycine (or collagen) + vitamin C + adequate selenium — this covers all the rate-limiting inputs for glutathione
- **What doesn't matter much:** Exact timing relative to food has only a small effect on outcomes; consistency of daily intake matters more
