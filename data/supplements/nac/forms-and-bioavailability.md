# NAC (N-Acetylcysteine)
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Standard NAC (N-acetyl-L-cysteine):** The common oral supplement and the form used in most research. Oral bioavailability is modest (roughly 4-10% as intact NAC) because much is metabolized on first pass — but this is by design, since the cysteine it releases is what fuels glutathione synthesis. Capsules and tablets are widely available, typically 600mg.
- **Effervescent / sustained-release NAC:** Effervescent tablets dissolve in water and may be gentler on the stomach. Sustained-release formulations aim to provide steadier cysteine availability over the day.
- **NAC ethyl ester (NACET):** A lipophilic ester designed for better cell penetration and higher intracellular cysteine delivery. Promising in early research but far less studied and less available than standard NAC.
- **Liposomal glutathione (alternative, not NAC):** Some prefer liposomal or S-acetyl glutathione to raise glutathione directly. These bypass the synthesis step but are more expensive; NAC remains the more cost-effective and better-studied route to raising glutathione.
- **Inhaled / nebulized acetylcysteine:** The pharmaceutical mucolytic form delivered directly to the airways under medical supervision — not a consumer supplement format.
- **IV acetylcysteine:** The hospital form used for acetaminophen overdose; achieves full bioavailability and rapid glutathione restoration. Medical use only.
- **Absorption notes:** NAC can be taken with or without food; some take it on an empty stomach for slightly faster absorption, while taking it with food reduces the chance of nausea. Vitamin C taken alongside may help maintain NAC in its active reduced (thiol) state.
- **Stability and smell:** NAC is a sulfur compound and has a characteristic "rotten egg" odor, which is normal and not a sign of spoilage. Store in a cool, dry place away from heat and moisture.
- **Practical choice:** For most people, standard 600mg NAC capsules from a reputable, third-party-tested brand are the sensible default. Exotic esters are not necessary for typical goals.
