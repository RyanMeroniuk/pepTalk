# NAC (N-Acetylcysteine)
## Foods to Avoid

- **Excessive alcohol:** Alcohol depletes glutathione and burdens the liver — the very systems NAC supports. While NAC is studied for protecting the liver against alcohol-related oxidative stress, heavy drinking overwhelms this support and works directly against NAC's purpose. Minimize alcohol to get the most from NAC
- **Chronic high-acetaminophen use with alcohol:** Combining regular acetaminophen (paracetamol) with alcohol depletes liver glutathione and raises toxicity risk. NAC is the antidote in overdose, but this combination should not be treated casually — it is a genuine liver hazard
- **Heavily processed, pro-oxidant diets:** Diets high in refined sugar, oxidized/repeatedly-heated oils, and ultra-processed foods increase oxidative load. NAC raises glutathione, but a pro-oxidant diet keeps depleting it. The supplement amplifies a good diet; it cannot offset a poor one
- **Foods Are Mostly Not a Concern for Absorption:** There are no specific foods that block NAC absorption in a meaningful way. NAC absorbs fine with mixed meals

### The Real "Avoid" List (Behaviors More Than Foods)
- **Inconsistency:** Glutathione support benefits from steady daily intake; sporadic use limits the benefit
- **Taking high antioxidant doses right around hard training:** If your goal is fitness adaptation, large NAC doses immediately pre/post workout may blunt beneficial exercise signaling. Separate them
- **Assuming NAC offsets harmful exposures:** NAC supports detoxification but does not license heavy smoking, drinking, or toxin exposure

### Timing With Other Supplements
- **Generally compatible** — NAC can be taken with most supplements
- **Pairs well with vitamin C and glycine** (supports the active thiol state and full glutathione synthesis)
- **Activated charcoal / binders:** If you use binders, separate them from NAC by a couple of hours, as binders can adsorb many compounds
