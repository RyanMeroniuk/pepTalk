# NAC (N-Acetylcysteine)
## Things to Avoid

- **Never self-treat an acetaminophen overdose:** NAC is the hospital antidote, but overdose is a medical emergency requiring precise IV/oral dosing and monitoring. If overdose is suspected, call emergency services or poison control immediately — do not rely on supplement-dose NAC
- **Combining with nitroglycerin/nitrates without medical guidance:** NAC potentiates the vasodilation and blood-pressure-lowering effect of nitrates and can worsen nitrate headaches and cause significant hypotension. This is a meaningful interaction — see drug-interactions
- **Stacking with anticoagulants/antiplatelets unsupervised:** NAC has mild antiplatelet activity; combining with blood thinners (warfarin, apixaban, clopidogrel, high-dose aspirin) can theoretically increase bleeding risk. Inform your physician
- **High antioxidant dosing immediately around training (if fitness is the goal):** Large NAC doses peri-workout may blunt the oxidative signaling that drives some adaptations. Take NAC away from workouts
- **Using during active chemotherapy without oncologist approval:** As an antioxidant, NAC could theoretically interfere with treatments that rely on oxidative mechanisms to kill cancer cells. Coordinate with the oncology team
- **Assuming the supplement equals the drug in emergencies:** Supplement NAC is for general support; it is not a substitute for prescription acetylcysteine in clinical situations
- **Ignoring the sulfur smell as "spoilage":** The rotten-egg odor is normal for NAC and not a reason to discard a product
- **Overdoing the dose:** More is not better. Most benefits occur at 600-1800 mg/day; very high doses increase GI side effects and offer little added value for general use

### Population Cautions
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** NAC has been used clinically in pregnancy (e.g., acetaminophen overdose) under supervision, but routine supplemental use during pregnancy or breastfeeding should be discussed with a physician
- **Asthma:** Inhaled acetylcysteine can trigger bronchospasm; oral NAC is generally better tolerated but asthmatics should be aware
- **Before surgery:** Stop NAC ahead of procedures due to mild antiplatelet effects, per your surgeon's instructions
- **Bleeding disorders:** Use caution given the mild platelet effects
