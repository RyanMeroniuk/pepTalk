# NAC (N-Acetylcysteine)
## Mechanisms of Action

- **Glutathione precursor (primary mechanism):** Glutathione (GSH) is synthesized from cysteine, glutamate, and glycine. Cysteine is the rate-limiting substrate. NAC is rapidly deacetylated to cysteine after absorption, feeding the synthesis pathway and raising intracellular glutathione, especially in the liver, lungs, and immune cells.
- **Direct antioxidant / free radical scavenging:** The free thiol (-SH) group on NAC directly neutralizes reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, including hydroxyl radicals and hypochlorous acid. This provides immediate antioxidant action independent of glutathione synthesis.
- **Acetaminophen detoxification:** Toxic doses of acetaminophen generate the reactive metabolite NAPQI, which depletes hepatic glutathione and damages liver cells. NAC restores glutathione stores and provides thiol groups that bind NAPQI directly — the basis of its use as an overdose antidote.
- **Mucolytic action:** NAC's thiol group breaks the disulfide bonds that cross-link mucin glycoproteins in mucus, reducing its viscosity and making it easier to clear from the airways.
- **Glutamate modulation (CNS):** NAC drives the cystine-glutamate antiporter (system xc-), increasing extracellular glutamate in a way that restores tone to glutamatergic signaling. This is the proposed mechanism behind its study in OCD-spectrum behaviors, addiction, and compulsivity.
- **Nrf2 pathway support:** By influencing cellular redox status, NAC contributes to activation of the Nrf2/ARE pathway, upregulating endogenous antioxidant enzymes (glutathione peroxidase, glutathione reductase, superoxide dismutase) for a sustained, amplifying antioxidant effect.
- **Anti-inflammatory signaling:** Through raised glutathione and lowered oxidative stress, NAC suppresses NF-kB activation and reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-6), contributing to its effects in inflammatory respiratory conditions.
- **Heavy metal binding (minor):** The thiol group can bind certain divalent metals, contributing modestly to detoxification, though NAC is not a primary chelator.
