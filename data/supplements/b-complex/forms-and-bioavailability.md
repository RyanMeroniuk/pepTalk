# B-Complex
## Forms and Bioavailability

### The Methylation Issue: Methylated vs. Non-Methylated Forms

This is the single most important consideration when choosing a B-complex supplement. Approximately 40-60% of the population carries MTHFR gene mutations that impair conversion of synthetic B vitamins to their active forms. Using pre-methylated (active) forms bypasses this bottleneck.

### B9 — Folate (CRITICAL DISTINCTION)

- **Folic acid (synthetic, NON-methylated):** The synthetic form found in fortified foods and most cheap supplements. Must be converted through a 4-step enzymatic process: folic acid → dihydrofolate → tetrahydrofolate → 5,10-methyleneTHF → 5-methylTHF (requires MTHFR enzyme). In MTHFR C677T homozygous individuals, this final conversion is 70% impaired. Unmetabolized folic acid (UMFA) accumulates in the blood, potentially blocking folate receptors and paradoxically creating a functional folate deficiency despite high intake. UMFA has been associated with impaired NK cell function and potential cancer risk.
- **L-methylfolate (5-MTHF, L-5-methyltetrahydrofolate):** The biologically active form. Bypasses MTHFR entirely. Directly enters the methylation cycle. Also known as Metafolin, Quatrefolic, or L-5-MTHF-Ca. **This is the recommended form for everyone, and it is essential for MTHFR carriers.** Brand names: Methylpro, Thorne Methyl-Guard, Seeking Health.
- **Folinic acid (5-formyltetrahydrofolate, leucovorin):** Active reduced folate that bypasses some (but not all) conversion steps. Does not require MTHFR for its initial metabolism. Used medically in methotrexate rescue. Some practitioners use it as an alternative to methylfolate in individuals who are "overmethylators" and feel overstimulated by methylfolate.

### B12 — Cobalamin (IMPORTANT DISTINCTION)

- **Cyanocobalamin (synthetic, NON-active):** Bound to a cyanide molecule (trace amount, not toxic). Must be converted to methylcobalamin or adenosylcobalamin in the body. Conversion requires adequate glutathione and MTHFR-dependent methylation. Most common form in cheap supplements due to stability and low cost. Acceptable for most people but suboptimal for MTHFR carriers, elderly, or those with detoxification issues.
- **Methylcobalamin (active, methylated):** The bioactive form used in the cytoplasmic methylation cycle (methionine synthase reaction). Directly supports homocysteine remethylation and methylation. **Recommended for MTHFR carriers and general optimization.** Less stable than cyanocobalamin (light-sensitive), so store properly.
- **Adenosylcobalamin (dibencozide, active):** The bioactive mitochondrial form. Cofactor for methylmalonyl-CoA mutase — essential for energy production from odd-chain fatty acids and branched-chain amino acids. Best form for addressing elevated methylmalonic acid (MMA). Some practitioners recommend combining methylcobalamin + adenosylcobalamin for comprehensive B12 support.
- **Hydroxocobalamin:** Precursor form that the body converts to both methylcobalamin and adenosylcobalamin. Used in B12 injections. Longer retention time in the body than cyanocobalamin. Preferred form for B12 injections.

### B6 — Pyridoxine

- **Pyridoxine HCl (standard):** Must be converted to pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (P-5-P) by the liver using riboflavin (B2). Conversion can be impaired in liver disease, certain genetic variations, and with medications.
- **Pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (P-5-P, active):** The bioactive coenzyme form. Directly usable without conversion. **Recommended, especially for those with liver issues, MTHFR mutations, or on medications that impair conversion.** Important note: very high doses of pyridoxine HCl (>200 mg/day) can paradoxically cause peripheral neuropathy; P-5-P does not carry this risk.

### B1 — Thiamine

- **Thiamine HCl (standard):** Water-soluble, adequate for general supplementation
- **Benfotiamine (fat-soluble, active):** Lipid-soluble thiamine prodrug with 5x greater bioavailability than thiamine HCl. Crosses cell membranes more readily. Preferred for diabetic neuropathy, nervous system support, and therapeutic dosing. Activates transketolase in the pentose phosphate pathway, reducing AGEs (advanced glycation end products).
- **Thiamine TTFD (Allithiamine):** Fat-soluble form derived from garlic. Excellent CNS penetration. Used in high-dose protocols for chronic fatigue, neurological conditions, and autonomic dysfunction.

### B2 — Riboflavin

- **Riboflavin (standard):** Adequate for most people. Bright yellow color; turns urine fluorescent yellow (harmless).
- **Riboflavin-5'-phosphate (R-5-P, active):** Pre-phosphorylated active form (FMN). Bypasses hepatic activation. Preferred for those with liver issues. Important: B2 is required to activate B6 (P-5-P) and folate — adequate B2 status is essential for the methylation cycle.

### B3 — Niacin

- **Niacin (nicotinic acid):** Causes flushing (vasodilation) at therapeutic doses. Best form for raising HDL and lowering triglycerides. The flushing effect decreases with regular use.
- **Niacinamide (nicotinamide):** No flushing. Good for NAD+ support, skin health, and general supplementation. Does not have the lipid-modifying effects of nicotinic acid.
- **Nicotinamide riboside (NR) / Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN):** NAD+ precursors. More direct pathway to NAD+ synthesis. Used in longevity and anti-aging protocols. More expensive.

### B5 — Pantothenic Acid

- **Pantothenic acid (standard):** Well-absorbed calcium or sodium salt form
- **Pantethine:** Active form closer to coenzyme A. May have additional lipid-lowering properties. More expensive.

### B7 — Biotin

- **D-biotin (standard):** The natural, active form. Well-absorbed. Most supplements use this form. Standard dosing is 300-5,000 mcg/day.

### Recommended B-Complex Profile (Methylated/Active)
For optimal supplementation, look for a B-complex containing:
- **B1:** Benfotiamine or thiamine HCl (25-100 mg)
- **B2:** Riboflavin-5'-phosphate (25-50 mg)
- **B3:** Niacinamide (50-100 mg)
- **B5:** Pantothenic acid (50-100 mg)
- **B6:** Pyridoxal-5'-phosphate / P-5-P (25-50 mg)
- **B7:** D-biotin (300-1,000 mcg)
- **B9:** L-methylfolate / 5-MTHF (400-800 mcg)
- **B12:** Methylcobalamin + adenosylcobalamin (500-1,000 mcg)

Trusted brands with fully active/methylated formulas: Thorne Basic B Complex, Seeking Health B-Minus or B-Complex Plus, Pure Encapsulations B-Complex Plus, Designs for Health B-Supreme.
