# B-Complex
## Things to Avoid

### Substances That Deplete B Vitamins
- **Alcohol:** Depletes B1, B6, B9, and B12 simultaneously. Impairs absorption, storage, activation, and increases excretion. Even 2 drinks/day significantly impacts B vitamin status. Heavy drinkers should take a high-potency B-complex daily.
- **Smoking:** Depletes folate (B9) and B12. Smokers have 15-30% lower serum folate levels than non-smokers. Smoking also increases homocysteine (the toxic amino acid that B6/B9/B12 clear).
- **Chronic stress:** Adrenal cortisol production consumes B5 (pantothenic acid) and B6. Prolonged psychological or physical stress increases requirements for the entire B-complex, particularly B5, B6, and B12.
- **Oral contraceptives:** Deplete B6, B9 (folate), B12, and B2. Women on hormonal birth control should supplement with a methylated B-complex. This is especially important for those planning future pregnancy — folate stores should be replenished 3+ months before conception.
- **Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs — omeprazole, pantoprazole, lansoprazole):** Reduce stomach acid, which is required for B12 liberation from food proteins and intrinsic factor activation. Long-term PPI use (>1 year) significantly increases B12 deficiency risk. Use sublingual methylcobalamin to bypass gastric absorption.
- **Metformin:** Reduces B12 absorption by 30% through interference with calcium-dependent B12-intrinsic factor uptake in the ileum. All metformin users should supplement B12 (1,000 mcg/day methylcobalamin) and have levels monitored annually.
- **Antibiotics:** Destroy gut bacteria that produce B vitamins (B12, biotin, folate, B2). Supplement during and for 1-2 months after antibiotic courses. Replenish with probiotics and fermented foods.

### Behaviors That Reduce Efficacy
- **Taking B-complex at night:** B vitamins (especially B6, B12, and methylfolate) can be stimulating and disrupt sleep in sensitive individuals. Always take in the morning or with lunch.
- **Using cheap/synthetic forms:** Non-methylated B-complexes containing folic acid and cyanocobalamin are suboptimal for the 40-60% of the population with MTHFR mutations. Folic acid can accumulate as UMFA and potentially worsen functional folate deficiency. Always choose methylated/active forms.
- **Starting methylated B vitamins at full dose:** Methylfolate and methylcobalamin can cause overmethylation symptoms (anxiety, irritability, insomnia) if started at high doses, especially in undermethylated individuals whose systems are not accustomed to adequate methyl groups. Start at half dose for 1-2 weeks and titrate up.
- **Taking B6 (pyridoxine HCl) at high doses long-term:** Doses >200 mg/day of pyridoxine HCl can cause peripheral neuropathy. Use P-5-P (the active form) instead, which has a much better safety profile.
- **Ignoring cofactors:** B vitamins require magnesium, zinc, and iron as cofactors for activation and enzyme function. Supplementing B vitamins without adequate mineral status reduces their effectiveness.
- **Taking B-complex with tannin-rich beverages:** Coffee, tea, and red wine reduce B vitamin absorption. Take with water and food.

### Medications That Deplete B Vitamins
- **Metformin:** Depletes B12 (30% reduced absorption)
- **Proton pump inhibitors:** Deplete B12, may reduce B1 and folate absorption
- **H2 blockers (ranitidine, famotidine):** Reduce stomach acid and B12 absorption (milder than PPIs)
- **Oral contraceptives:** Deplete B2, B6, B9, B12
- **Anticonvulsants (phenytoin, carbamazepine, valproate):** Deplete folate significantly. Valproate depletes carnitine (B5-dependent synthesis).
- **Methotrexate:** Folate antagonist — requires folinic acid (leucovorin) rescue. Do NOT use methylfolate without oncologist approval during methotrexate therapy.
- **Trimethoprim and pyrimethamine:** Folate antagonists used as antibiotics. Supplement folate during use.
- **Isoniazid (INH):** Depletes B6 by binding P-5-P directly. All patients on isoniazid should supplement 25-50 mg B6/day.
- **Levodopa (L-DOPA):** B6 can reduce levodopa efficacy if not combined with carbidopa. However, if taking carbidopa/levodopa (Sinemet), B6 supplementation is safe and recommended.
- **Cholestyramine:** Reduces absorption of fat-soluble nutrients including benfotiamine.
- **Loop diuretics (furosemide):** Deplete B1 (thiamine). Heart failure patients on loop diuretics are at high risk for B1 deficiency.

### Medical Considerations
- **Do not supplement folic acid at high doses (>1 mg) without confirming B12 status.** Folic acid can mask B12 deficiency anemia while irreversible neurological damage continues.
- **Discontinue high-dose biotin (>5,000 mcg) 48-72 hours before blood work** to avoid falsified troponin and thyroid lab results.
- **Inform your doctor about B-complex supplementation** before any bloodwork, as B vitamins can affect multiple lab assays.
