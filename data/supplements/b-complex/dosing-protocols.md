# B-Complex
## Dosing Protocols

### General Maintenance (B-Complex)
- **Standard adult dose:** A comprehensive B-complex providing 25-100 mg of B1, B2, B3, B5, B6; 400-800 mcg methylfolate; 500-1,000 mcg methylcobalamin; and 300-1,000 mcg biotin. Take once daily with food.
- **Higher stress / high demand:** Up to 2x standard dose. B vitamins are water-soluble and excess is excreted, making toxicity rare (except B6 at very high doses).

### Individual B Vitamin Dosing

#### B1 (Thiamine)
- **RDA:** 1.1-1.2 mg/day
- **Optimal supplement dose:** 25-100 mg/day
- **Therapeutic (diabetic neuropathy, chronic fatigue):** Benfotiamine 150-600 mg/day
- **High-dose protocol (autonomic dysfunction, POTS):** Thiamine TTFD 50-100 mg 2-3x/day

#### B2 (Riboflavin)
- **RDA:** 1.1-1.3 mg/day
- **Optimal supplement dose:** 25-50 mg/day
- **Migraine prevention:** 400 mg/day (evidence-based, endorsed by American Academy of Neurology)
- **Note:** Will turn urine bright yellow — this is harmless

#### B3 (Niacin/Niacinamide)
- **RDA:** 14-16 mg NE/day
- **Optimal supplement dose:** 50-100 mg niacinamide/day
- **Lipid therapy (nicotinic acid):** 500-2,000 mg/day (start low, titrate up to manage flushing)
- **NAD+ support (NMN or NR):** 250-500 mg/day
- **Upper limit:** 35 mg/day (niacin for flushing); niacinamide has a higher tolerance but very high doses (>3g/day) can cause liver toxicity

#### B5 (Pantothenic Acid)
- **RDA:** 5 mg/day
- **Optimal supplement dose:** 50-100 mg/day
- **Acne/skin support:** 500-1,000 mg/day (some protocols use up to 2-5g/day, though evidence is limited)
- **Adrenal support during stress:** 250-500 mg/day

#### B6 (Pyridoxine / P-5-P)
- **RDA:** 1.3-2.0 mg/day
- **Optimal supplement dose:** 25-50 mg P-5-P/day
- **Neurotransmitter support / PMS:** 50-100 mg P-5-P/day
- **Morning sickness (pregnancy):** 10-25 mg three times daily (first-line treatment per ACOG)
- **Upper limit caution:** Do not exceed 200 mg/day of pyridoxine HCl long-term — doses above this can cause reversible peripheral neuropathy. P-5-P (the active form) does not carry the same neuropathy risk but prudent to keep below 100 mg/day.

#### B7 (Biotin)
- **RDA (AI):** 30 mcg/day
- **Optimal supplement dose:** 300-1,000 mcg/day
- **Hair/skin/nail support:** 2,500-5,000 mcg/day
- **Important warning:** High-dose biotin (>5,000 mcg) can interfere with troponin and thyroid lab assays, causing false results. Discontinue for 48-72 hours before blood work.

#### B9 (Folate / Methylfolate)
- **RDA:** 400 mcg DFE/day
- **Optimal supplement dose:** 400-800 mcg methylfolate/day
- **Pregnancy:** 800-1,000 mcg methylfolate/day (start 3 months before conception)
- **MTHFR C677T homozygous:** 800-1,000 mcg methylfolate/day minimum; some practitioners prescribe 1-5 mg for elevated homocysteine
- **Depression adjunct:** L-methylfolate 7.5-15 mg/day (Deplin is the prescription form at 7.5-15 mg)
- **Caution:** Some individuals ("overmethylators") may experience anxiety, irritability, or insomnia from methylfolate. Start low and increase gradually. Niacin (B3) or hydroxocobalamin can help buffer overmethylation symptoms.

#### B12 (Cobalamin)
- **RDA:** 2.4 mcg/day (widely considered too low)
- **Optimal supplement dose:** 500-1,000 mcg methylcobalamin/day (sublingual or capsule)
- **Deficiency repletion:** 1,000-5,000 mcg/day sublingual for 1-3 months, or B12 injections (hydroxocobalamin 1,000 mcg IM weekly for 4-8 weeks, then monthly)
- **Elderly (>60):** 1,000 mcg/day minimum (absorption declines with age due to reduced intrinsic factor and stomach acid)
- **Vegans/vegetarians:** 1,000-2,500 mcg/day (non-negotiable — plant foods contain no bioavailable B12)
- **Pernicious anemia:** B12 injections required (oral absorption is insufficient without intrinsic factor)

### Timing and Administration
- **Take with food:** B vitamins are better absorbed with food and reduce the nausea that some people experience
- **Morning or midday:** B vitamins can be mildly stimulating (especially B12 and B6). Avoid taking late in the evening to prevent sleep disruption.
- **Sublingual B12:** Place under the tongue and allow to dissolve for 1-2 minutes for direct mucosal absorption, bypassing GI tract. Useful for those with low stomach acid or intrinsic factor deficiency.
- **Do not split B-complex doses** unless directed — the synergistic effect is strongest when all B vitamins are present together.

### Monitoring
- **Homocysteine:** Target <8 umol/L (optimal), <10 umol/L (acceptable). Test every 6-12 months.
- **Methylmalonic acid (MMA):** Elevated MMA indicates functional B12 deficiency even when serum B12 appears normal. More sensitive marker than serum B12 alone.
- **Serum B12:** >500 pg/mL (optimal). Note: serum B12 can be falsely normal; MMA is more reliable.
- **RBC folate:** More reliable than serum folate for assessing long-term folate status.
