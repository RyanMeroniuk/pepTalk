# B-Complex
## Natural Food Sources

### Best Whole-Food Sources (contain multiple B vitamins)
- **Beef liver:** 3 oz = B12 (70 mcg, 2,917% DV), B2 (2.9 mg), B3 (15 mg), B5 (5.6 mg), B6 (0.9 mg), folate (215 mcg), B7 (30 mcg). The single most B-vitamin-dense food.
- **Chicken liver:** 3 oz = B12 (14 mcg), B9 (254 mcg), B2 (1.7 mg), B3 (9 mg), B5 (5.3 mg). Milder flavor than beef liver.
- **Nutritional yeast (fortified):** 2 tablespoons = B1 (9.6 mg), B2 (9.7 mg), B3 (56 mg), B6 (9.7 mg), B12 (7.8 mcg), folate (240 mcg). Best vegan source. Check label — only fortified varieties contain B12.
- **Eggs (whole, pasture-raised):** 2 large = B12 (1.1 mcg), B2 (0.5 mg), B5 (1.4 mg), B7 (20 mcg), folate (48 mcg). Biotin is in the yolk.

### Key Notes
- **B12 is ONLY found in animal foods** (and fortified products). There are no plant foods that naturally contain bioavailable B12. Vegans and vegetarians must supplement.
- **Liver is the undisputed king of B vitamins.** Even 1-2 servings per week provides massive B vitamin coverage. Desiccated liver capsules are an alternative for those who dislike the taste.
- **Folate in food is natural folate (not folic acid)** and is better utilized than synthetic folic acid, though absorption from food is approximately 50% of supplement forms.
- **Cooking reduces B vitamin content** by 10-50% depending on the vitamin, method, and duration. Steaming retains more than boiling. B12 and B2 are relatively heat-stable; B1 and folate are heat-sensitive.
