# B-Complex
## Lifestyle Recommendations

### Dietary Strategy
- **Eat liver 1-2 times per week.** Beef or chicken liver is the single most B-vitamin-dense food on the planet. A 3 oz serving of beef liver covers 2,917% of B12, 223% of B2, and significant amounts of B3, B5, B6, B9, and B7. If you dislike the taste, use desiccated liver capsules (4-6 capsules = ~1 oz liver).
- **Prioritize animal proteins.** Meat, fish, poultry, eggs, and dairy provide the most bioavailable forms of B vitamins, particularly B12 (exclusively animal-sourced).
- **Eat fermented foods daily.** Sauerkraut, kimchi, yogurt, kefir, miso, and tempeh provide B vitamins from bacterial fermentation and probiotics that support gut-based B vitamin production.
- **Include dark leafy greens at every meal.** Spinach, kale, romaine, and collard greens provide natural folate. Eat raw or lightly steamed to preserve folate content.
- **Eat whole grains, not refined.** Brown rice, oats, quinoa, and whole wheat contain 3-10x more B vitamins than their refined counterparts. Soaking and sprouting further increases bioavailability.

### Supplementation Strategy
- **Choose a methylated B-complex.** Look for methylfolate (5-MTHF), methylcobalamin, P-5-P (active B6), riboflavin-5'-phosphate (active B2), and benfotiamine (active B1). Trusted brands: Thorne Basic B Complex, Seeking Health B-Complex Plus, Pure Encapsulations B-Complex Plus.
- **Take in the morning with breakfast.** B vitamins can be stimulating. Taking with food reduces nausea and improves absorption.
- **Start low with methylated forms.** Begin at half dose for 1-2 weeks to assess tolerance, especially for methylfolate. If overmethylation symptoms occur (anxiety, irritability, insomnia), reduce dose and add niacin (50-100 mg niacinamide) as a methyl buffer.
- **Vegans: supplement B12 non-negotiably.** 1,000-2,500 mcg methylcobalamin daily (sublingual preferred). No plant food provides adequate bioavailable B12. This is the single most critical supplement for vegans.
- **Elderly (60+): use sublingual B12.** Stomach acid and intrinsic factor decline with age, impairing oral B12 absorption. Sublingual methylcobalamin bypasses the GI tract.

### MTHFR and Methylation Optimization
- **Get tested for MTHFR mutations** via 23andMe, genetic panels, or specific blood tests. Knowing your genotype informs your entire supplementation strategy.
- **Check homocysteine levels.** Target <8 umol/L (optimal). Levels >10 umol/L indicate inadequate methylation support. Recheck every 6-12 months after starting methylated B vitamins.
- **If MTHFR C677T homozygous:** Use 800-1,000 mcg methylfolate daily (some need 1-5 mg). Never use folic acid. Combine with methylcobalamin (1,000 mcg) and P-5-P (25-50 mg). Monitor homocysteine.
- **If MTHFR heterozygous:** Methylated forms are still preferred. 400-800 mcg methylfolate daily is usually sufficient.
- **Support methylation cofactors:** Magnesium (300-400 mg glycinate), zinc (15-30 mg), and adequate protein intake all support the methylation cycle.
- **Consider additional methylation support if needed:** SAMe (200-400 mg), TMG/betaine (500-1,000 mg), and riboflavin (B2, 25-50 mg) all support healthy methylation.

### Stress and Adrenal Support
- **Increase B-complex during stressful periods.** Stress rapidly depletes B5, B6, and B12. Consider doubling your B-complex dose during acute stress or adding standalone B5 (250-500 mg pantothenic acid).
- **Manage chronic stress with lifestyle interventions:** Meditation, adequate sleep, regular exercise, and social connection reduce the metabolic demand for B vitamins by lowering cortisol output.

### Alcohol Management
- **If you drink regularly,** supplement with a B-complex before or after alcohol consumption. Prioritize B1 (thiamine), folate, and B12. Alcohol is the single most destructive substance for B vitamin status.
- **Heavy drinkers or recovering alcoholics** may benefit from high-dose B1 (benfotiamine 150-300 mg/day) to prevent or reverse thiamine deficiency and associated neurological damage.

### Exercise and Athletic Performance
- **Athletes have increased B vitamin requirements** due to higher energy metabolism, increased oxidative stress, and greater turnover. Endurance athletes in particular may need higher B1, B2, B3, and B6.
- **B vitamins support energy conversion from macronutrients.** Adequate status means more efficient ATP production and better exercise capacity.
- **B12 supports red blood cell production** — critical for oxygen-carrying capacity and endurance performance.

### Sleep Optimization
- **Take B-complex in the morning,** not at night. B6, B12, and methylfolate can be stimulating.
- **B6 supports melatonin synthesis** (serotonin → melatonin pathway) when taken earlier in the day, indirectly supporting sleep quality even though the supplement itself should be taken in the morning.
- **B3 (niacinamide) at bedtime (500 mg)** is sometimes used as a sleep aid — it is the only B vitamin that may be beneficial to take at night, as it supports tryptophan preservation for serotonin/melatonin production.

### Testing and Monitoring
- **Baseline tests before starting supplementation:** Homocysteine, methylmalonic acid (MMA), serum B12, RBC folate, and MTHFR genotype.
- **Follow-up:** Recheck homocysteine and MMA 8-12 weeks after starting supplementation, then every 6-12 months.
- **Serum B12 can be misleading.** A "normal" serum B12 (200-900 pg/mL) does not rule out functional deficiency. MMA is a more sensitive marker — elevated MMA indicates B12 deficiency at the tissue level.
- **Target serum B12 >500 pg/mL** for optimal neurological and methylation function (not just the lab "normal" range of 200-900).
