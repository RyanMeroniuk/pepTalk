# B-Complex
## Mechanisms of Action

### Energy Metabolism (B1, B2, B3, B5)
- **B1 (Thiamine) — Thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP):** Cofactor for pyruvate dehydrogenase (links glycolysis to citric acid cycle), alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase (citric acid cycle), and transketolase (pentose phosphate pathway). Essential for converting carbohydrates into ATP. Deficiency causes beriberi and Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome.
- **B2 (Riboflavin) — FAD and FMN:** Cofactor for flavoproteins in the electron transport chain (Complex I and II), fatty acid oxidation (acyl-CoA dehydrogenase), and glutathione recycling (glutathione reductase). Also required to convert B6 to its active form (P-5-P) and folate to its active form.
- **B3 (Niacin) — NAD+ and NADP+:** NAD+ is required for over 500 enzymatic reactions. Central to glycolysis, citric acid cycle, fatty acid oxidation, and the electron transport chain. NADP+ is critical for anabolic reactions and glutathione recycling. NAD+ also serves as a substrate for sirtuins (SIRT1-7) involved in DNA repair, gene silencing, and longevity. NAD+ also serves as substrate for PARPs (DNA repair enzymes) and CD38 (immune signaling).
- **B5 (Pantothenic acid) — Coenzyme A (CoA):** CoA is required for the citric acid cycle (acetyl-CoA entry), fatty acid synthesis, fatty acid oxidation, steroid hormone synthesis, and acetylcholine production. The acetyl group of acetyl-CoA is central to virtually all metabolic pathways.

### Methylation and One-Carbon Metabolism (B9, B12, B6)
- **B9 (Folate) — Tetrahydrofolate (THF):** Carries one-carbon units for DNA synthesis (thymidylate and purine synthesis), amino acid metabolism (serine/glycine interconversion), and the methylation cycle. 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF) donates a methyl group to homocysteine (via B12-dependent methionine synthase) to regenerate methionine and subsequently S-adenosylmethionine (SAMe) — the universal methyl donor.
- **B12 (Cobalamin) — Methylcobalamin and Adenosylcobalamin:** Methylcobalamin is a cofactor for methionine synthase, which remethylates homocysteine to methionine (the methionine-homocysteine cycle). This is the critical link between folate metabolism and methylation. Adenosylcobalamin is a cofactor for methylmalonyl-CoA mutase in mitochondria, required for odd-chain fatty acid and branched-chain amino acid metabolism. Deficiency causes methylmalonic acid accumulation and impaired energy metabolism.
- **B6 (Pyridoxal-5'-phosphate) — P-5-P:** Cofactor for cystathionine beta-synthase (transsulfuration pathway — converts homocysteine to cysteine for glutathione synthesis) and numerous transaminases and decarboxylases. Required for synthesis of serotonin (from tryptophan via TPH), dopamine (from L-DOPA via AADC), GABA (from glutamate via GAD), norepinephrine, and histamine.

### Neurotransmitter Synthesis (B6, B9, B12)
- **Serotonin pathway:** Tryptophan → 5-HTP (requires folate/BH4) → Serotonin (requires B6/P-5-P)
- **Dopamine pathway:** Tyrosine → L-DOPA (requires folate/BH4) → Dopamine (requires B6/P-5-P) → Norepinephrine (requires vitamin C)
- **GABA pathway:** Glutamate → GABA (requires B6/P-5-P via glutamic acid decarboxylase)
- **Melatonin pathway:** Serotonin → N-acetylserotonin → Melatonin (requires B6/P-5-P and SAMe from methylation cycle)
- **Acetylcholine:** Requires B5 (pantothenic acid) via acetyl-CoA for acetylcholine synthesis

### Homocysteine Regulation (B6, B9, B12)
- **Homocysteine is a toxic amino acid** produced from methionine metabolism. Elevated levels (>10 umol/L) are an independent risk factor for cardiovascular disease, stroke, dementia, depression, and osteoporosis.
- **Three B vitamins regulate homocysteine:** B9 and B12 remethylate it to methionine; B6 converts it to cysteine via transsulfuration. Deficiency in any of these raises homocysteine.
- **Methylated B vitamins are critical** for individuals with MTHFR mutations, who cannot efficiently convert folic acid to the active 5-MTHF needed for homocysteine remethylation.

### DNA Synthesis and Cell Division (B9, B12)
- **Folate and B12 are required for thymidylate synthesis** (DNA base thymine production) and purine synthesis. Deficiency causes megaloblastic anemia (large, immature red blood cells) and impairs cell division in rapidly dividing tissues (bone marrow, gut lining, immune cells).
- **Folate is critical during pregnancy** for neural tube closure (first 28 days). Adequate methylfolate (not folic acid, especially in MTHFR carriers) dramatically reduces neural tube defect risk.

### Myelin Maintenance (B12, B9, B1)
- **B12 is essential for myelin sheath synthesis and maintenance.** Deficiency causes demyelination of peripheral and central nerves, leading to peripheral neuropathy, cognitive decline, and eventually subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord. Neurological damage from B12 deficiency can be irreversible if prolonged.
