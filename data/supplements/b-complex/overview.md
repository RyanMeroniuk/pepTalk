# B-Complex
## Overview

- **What it is:** B-complex refers to the group of 8 essential water-soluble B vitamins that function as coenzymes in hundreds of metabolic reactions. They are grouped together because they are commonly found in the same foods, share water-solubility, and work synergistically in energy metabolism, methylation, neurotransmitter production, and cellular function.
- **Why it matters:** B vitamins are the metabolic engine of the body. They are required for converting food into cellular energy (ATP), synthesizing DNA and RNA, producing neurotransmitters (serotonin, dopamine, GABA, norepinephrine), maintaining myelin sheaths on nerves, producing red blood cells, regulating homocysteine (a cardiovascular risk factor), and supporting methylation — the body's master regulatory process affecting gene expression, detoxification, and neurotransmitter metabolism.
- **Deficiency prevalence:** B vitamin deficiencies are extremely common, particularly:
  - **B12 deficiency:** Affects 6-20% of adults over 60, up to 40% of elderly populations. Vegans/vegetarians are at very high risk (50-80% deficient without supplementation). Metformin users have 30% higher deficiency rates.
  - **Folate deficiency:** 20-30% of the population in countries without mandatory fortification. Those with MTHFR mutations (40-60% of the population) have impaired folate metabolism even with adequate intake.
  - **B6 deficiency:** 10-25% of adults, higher in elderly and oral contraceptive users
  - **B1 (thiamine) deficiency:** Common in alcoholics (up to 80%), diabetics, and bariatric surgery patients
- **MTHFR mutations and methylated B vitamins:** This is one of the most important considerations in B-vitamin supplementation. MTHFR (methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase) gene mutations affect 40-60% of the population to varying degrees:
  - **C677T heterozygous:** ~40% of population, 35% reduced enzyme activity
  - **C677T homozygous:** ~10-15% of population, 70% reduced enzyme activity
  - **A1298C:** Additional common variant with milder effects
  - **Impact:** MTHFR mutations impair conversion of folic acid (synthetic) to methylfolate (active form). This leads to undermethylation, elevated homocysteine, impaired detoxification, neurotransmitter imbalances, and increased risk of cardiovascular disease, depression, anxiety, neural tube defects, and certain cancers.
  - **Solution:** Individuals with MTHFR mutations should use **methylfolate (5-MTHF/L-methylfolate)** instead of folic acid, and **methylcobalamin** instead of cyanocobalamin. Folic acid can actually accumulate as unmetabolized folic acid (UMFA) in the blood of MTHFR carriers, potentially blocking folate receptors and worsening the problem.
  - **Testing:** MTHFR genotyping is available through 23andMe, genetic testing panels, or direct blood tests. Homocysteine levels and methylmalonic acid (MMA) are functional markers of B vitamin status.
