# Vitamin D3
## Drug Interactions and Contraindications

### Medications That Reduce Vitamin D Levels (may need higher D3 doses)
- **Corticosteroids (prednisone, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone):** Accelerate vitamin D catabolism via CYP24A1 induction, impair intestinal calcium absorption, and increase renal calcium excretion. Long-term users should supplement 5,000-10,000 IU/day and monitor levels closely.
- **Anticonvulsants (phenytoin/Dilantin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine/Tegretol, valproate):** Induce hepatic CYP enzymes that degrade vitamin D metabolites. Epilepsy patients are at high risk for deficiency and osteomalacia. Higher supplementation doses and monitoring are essential.
- **Rifampin (rifampicin):** Potent CYP3A4 and CYP24A1 inducer. Dramatically increases vitamin D catabolism. Tuberculosis patients on rifampin often need 4,000-10,000 IU/day.
- **Antiretrovirals (efavirenz/Sustiva):** Induces CYP enzymes that accelerate vitamin D breakdown. HIV patients on efavirenz have significantly lower 25(OH)D levels.
- **Cholestyramine (Questran):** Bile acid sequestrant that prevents absorption of fat-soluble vitamins. Take D3 at least 4-6 hours apart from cholestyramine.
- **Orlistat (Xenical/Alli):** Lipase inhibitor that blocks fat absorption, reducing D3 uptake by 30%. Take D3 at least 2 hours before or after orlistat.

### Medications That May Be Affected by Vitamin D
- **Thiazide diuretics (hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone):** Reduce urinary calcium excretion. Combined with vitamin D (which increases calcium absorption), this can lead to hypercalcemia. Monitor serum calcium levels.
- **Digoxin (Lanoxin):** Hypercalcemia from excess vitamin D potentiates digoxin toxicity, which can cause life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias. Careful monitoring of calcium and digoxin levels is essential.
- **Calcium channel blockers (verapamil, diltiazem, amlodipine):** Vitamin D-induced hypercalcemia could theoretically oppose the effects of calcium channel blockers. Clinical significance is low at standard D3 doses but relevant at very high doses.
- **Aluminum-containing antacids:** Vitamin D increases aluminum absorption, which can be toxic. Avoid long-term use of aluminum-containing antacids with high-dose vitamin D.
- **Calcipotriene (Dovonex):** Topical vitamin D analog for psoriasis. Combining with oral vitamin D supplementation increases hypercalcemia risk. Monitor calcium levels.

### Contraindications and Cautions
- **Hypercalcemia (elevated serum calcium):** Do not supplement vitamin D without treating the underlying cause of hypercalcemia.
- **Primary hyperparathyroidism:** Vitamin D may further increase calcium levels. Supplement cautiously under medical supervision with calcium monitoring.
- **Granulomatous diseases (sarcoidosis, tuberculosis, histoplasmosis, Crohn's disease):** Activated macrophages in granulomas convert 25(OH)D to calcitriol in an unregulated manner, increasing hypercalcemia risk even at standard supplement doses. Use lower doses and monitor calcium closely.
- **Lymphoma:** Certain lymphomas produce calcitriol ectopically, creating similar hypercalcemia risk as granulomatous diseases.
- **Williams syndrome:** Genetic condition with heightened vitamin D sensitivity and hypercalcemia risk.
- **Severe kidney disease (CKD stage 4-5):** Impaired renal conversion of 25(OH)D to calcitriol. Active vitamin D analogs (calcitriol or paricalcitol) are typically used instead of standard D3. Supplementation should be guided by a nephrologist.
- **Kidney stones (calcium oxalate history):** While moderate D3 supplementation (2,000-4,000 IU/day) does not appear to increase stone risk, very high doses combined with high calcium intake may increase risk in susceptible individuals. Maintain adequate hydration and consider monitoring urinary calcium.

### Safe Combinations
- **Vitamin K2 (MK-7):** Not only safe but strongly recommended as a co-supplement
- **Magnesium:** Safe and recommended cofactor
- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** Safe; may enhance D3 absorption
- **Zinc:** Safe and supportive of VDR function
- **Most common medications:** At standard doses (2,000-5,000 IU/day), vitamin D3 is safe with the vast majority of medications
