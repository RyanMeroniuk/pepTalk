# Vitamin D3
## Mechanisms of Action

- **Hepatic hydroxylation:** After ingestion or skin synthesis, vitamin D3 travels to the liver where it is hydroxylated by CYP2R1 enzyme into 25-hydroxyvitamin D (calcidiol/25(OH)D), the primary circulating and storage form.
- **Renal activation:** 25(OH)D is further hydroxylated in the kidneys by CYP27B1 (1-alpha-hydroxylase) into 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol), the biologically active hormone form.
- **Vitamin D receptor (VDR) activation:** Calcitriol binds to VDR, a nuclear receptor present in nearly every tissue. The VDR-calcitriol complex heterodimerizes with retinoid X receptor (RXR) and binds to vitamin D response elements (VDREs) on DNA, modulating transcription of over 1,000 genes.
- **Calcium and phosphorus homeostasis:** Calcitriol upregulates intestinal calcium-binding proteins (calbindin-D9k and TRPV6) to increase calcium absorption from 10-15% (without vitamin D) to 30-40% (with adequate vitamin D). It also enhances phosphorus absorption and regulates renal calcium reabsorption.
- **Parathyroid hormone (PTH) regulation:** Adequate vitamin D suppresses PTH secretion. When vitamin D is low, PTH rises to maintain blood calcium by pulling it from bones, leading to bone loss over time.
- **Immune modulation (innate immunity):** Calcitriol upregulates cathelicidin (LL-37) and beta-defensin 2, antimicrobial peptides that destroy bacteria, viruses, and fungi. Macrophages and monocytes express CYP27B1, allowing local conversion of 25(OH)D to calcitriol at sites of infection.
- **Immune modulation (adaptive immunity):** Calcitriol promotes regulatory T-cell (Treg) differentiation, suppresses Th1 and Th17 inflammatory responses, and shifts immune balance toward tolerance — relevant for autoimmune disease prevention.
- **Gene expression regulation:** Vitamin D influences genes involved in cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. It upregulates tumor suppressor genes (p21, p27) and downregulates oncogenes, contributing to its anti-cancer properties.
- **Neurotransmitter synthesis:** Calcitriol upregulates tryptophan hydroxylase 2 (TPH2) in the brain, increasing serotonin synthesis. It also influences dopamine and norepinephrine pathways, linking vitamin D status to mood regulation.
- **Muscle function:** VDR is expressed in skeletal muscle. Calcitriol enhances muscle protein synthesis, mitochondrial function, and calcium handling in muscle fibers, improving strength and reducing fall risk.
