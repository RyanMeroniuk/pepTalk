# Vitamin D3
## Dosing Protocols

### General Maintenance Dosing
- **Adults (normal weight):** 2,000-5,000 IU/day to maintain levels of 40-60 ng/mL
- **Obese adults (BMI >30):** 5,000-10,000 IU/day (adipose tissue sequesters D3, requiring higher doses)
- **Elderly (65+):** 4,000-5,000 IU/day (reduced skin synthesis capacity and absorption)
- **Children (1-18):** 1,000-2,000 IU/day depending on age and weight
- **Infants (0-12 months):** 400-1,000 IU/day

### Repletion Protocol (for deficiency <20 ng/mL)
- **Loading dose:** 50,000 IU/week for 8-12 weeks, then transition to maintenance
- **Alternative loading:** 10,000 IU/day for 8-12 weeks, then reduce to maintenance
- **Recheck levels:** 8-12 weeks after starting repletion to assess response

### Optimal Target Ranges
- **Minimum sufficient:** 30 ng/mL (75 nmol/L)
- **Optimal range:** 40-60 ng/mL (100-150 nmol/L)
- **Upper safe limit:** 80-100 ng/mL (200-250 nmol/L)
- **Toxicity concern:** >100 ng/mL (>250 nmol/L)

### Upper Limits
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 4,000 IU/day (Institute of Medicine) — this is considered conservative by many experts
- **Endocrine Society safe upper limit:** 10,000 IU/day for adults without monitoring
- **With monitoring:** Higher doses can be used under medical supervision with periodic 25(OH)D and calcium testing

### Timing and Administration
- **Best time:** With the largest fat-containing meal of the day (typically lunch or dinner). Taking with breakfast is also effective if the meal contains fat.
- **Avoid at night:** Some individuals report sleep disruption when taking D3 in the evening, possibly due to melatonin suppression
- **Frequency:** Daily dosing is preferred over weekly or monthly boluses. Daily dosing produces more stable serum levels and better clinical outcomes.
- **Fat co-ingestion:** Take with at least 11-15g of dietary fat to maximize absorption (e.g., avocado, eggs, nuts, olive oil, butter)

### Co-supplementation Requirements
- **Vitamin K2 (MK-7):** 100-200 mcg per 5,000 IU of D3. Essential for directing calcium to bones and preventing arterial calcification.
- **Magnesium:** 200-400 mg/day. Magnesium is a cofactor for vitamin D metabolism (required for both hydroxylation steps). Deficiency in magnesium can impair vitamin D activation.
- **Zinc:** Supports VDR function. Ensure adequate intake (15-30 mg/day).

### Monitoring Schedule
- **Baseline test:** Before starting supplementation
- **Follow-up:** 8-12 weeks after starting or changing dose
- **Maintenance testing:** Every 6-12 months once stable
- **Additional markers:** PTH (should decrease as D3 normalizes), serum calcium, serum phosphorus
