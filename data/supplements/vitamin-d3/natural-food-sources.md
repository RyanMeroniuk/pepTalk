# Vitamin D3
## Natural Food Sources

### Highest Sources (animal-derived D3)
- **Cod liver oil:** 1 tablespoon = 1,360 IU
- **Wild-caught salmon (sockeye):** 3 oz = 570-800 IU
- **Swordfish:** 3 oz = 566 IU
- **Rainbow trout (farmed):** 3 oz = 645 IU
- **Sardines (canned in oil):** 3 oz = 164 IU
- **Mackerel (Atlantic):** 3 oz = 360 IU
- **Herring (Atlantic, pickled):** 3 oz = 200 IU
- **Tuna (canned, light, in water):** 3 oz = 154 IU

### Moderate Sources
- **Egg yolks (pasture-raised):** 1 large egg = 40-80 IU (pasture-raised contain 3-6x more than conventional)
- **Beef liver:** 3 oz = 42 IU
- **Pork (with fat):** 3 oz = 25-50 IU
- **Cheese (Swiss):** 1 oz = 6-12 IU
- **Butter (grass-fed):** 1 tablespoon = 9 IU

### Plant-Based Sources (D2 — ergocalciferol, less effective)
- **UV-exposed maitake mushrooms:** 1 cup = 786 IU (D2)
- **UV-exposed shiitake mushrooms:** 1 cup = 400-1,000 IU (D2) — must be sun-dried or UV-treated
- **UV-exposed white button mushrooms:** 1 cup = 366 IU (D2)
- **Portobello mushrooms (UV-exposed):** 1 cup = 316 IU (D2)

### Fortified Foods
- **Fortified milk (dairy):** 1 cup = 100-120 IU
- **Fortified orange juice:** 1 cup = 100 IU
- **Fortified cereals:** 1 serving = 40-100 IU
- **Fortified plant milks (soy, almond, oat):** 1 cup = 100-144 IU
- **Fortified yogurt:** 6 oz = 80 IU

### Key Notes
- **Wild vs. farmed fish:** Wild-caught salmon contains 3-4x more vitamin D3 than farmed salmon
- **Food alone is insufficient:** Even the richest dietary sources provide a small fraction of what most people need. A person would need to eat 5+ servings of wild salmon daily to achieve optimal levels from food alone.
- **Supplementation is typically necessary** to maintain levels in the 40-60 ng/mL range, especially during winter months or for those with limited sun exposure.
