# Vitamin D3
## Side Effects and Toxicity

### Common Side Effects (usually at higher doses)
- **Hypercalcemia:** The primary toxicity concern. Symptoms include nausea, vomiting, constipation, excessive thirst, frequent urination, confusion, and fatigue. Typically occurs only at serum 25(OH)D levels >150 ng/mL or with chronic intake >50,000 IU/day.
- **Gastrointestinal discomfort:** Nausea, bloating, or constipation — more common at higher doses
- **Headache:** Occasionally reported, may be related to calcium level changes
- **Metallic taste:** Rare, associated with hypercalcemia

### Serious Toxicity (chronic excessive intake)
- **Vascular calcification:** Excess calcium deposition in arteries and soft tissues, especially when K2 is not co-supplemented. This is the primary reason for the D3+K2 co-requirement.
- **Kidney stones:** Hypercalciuria (excess calcium in urine) can promote calcium oxalate stone formation. Risk is highest in those with prior history of kidney stones, dehydration, or very high doses without adequate hydration.
- **Kidney damage:** Chronic hypercalcemia can cause nephrocalcinosis (calcium deposits in kidney tissue) and renal insufficiency
- **Cardiac arrhythmias:** Severe hypercalcemia can cause QT shortening and cardiac rhythm disturbances

### Important Safety Notes
- **Toxicity is rare at standard doses:** At 5,000 IU/day or less, toxicity is extremely rare in adults. The Endocrine Society considers up to 10,000 IU/day safe for most adults.
- **Granulomatous diseases:** Sarcoidosis, tuberculosis, and some lymphomas can cause unregulated conversion of 25(OH)D to calcitriol, increasing hypercalcemia risk even at standard doses.
- **Hyperparathyroidism:** Primary hyperparathyroidism patients require careful monitoring when supplementing D3 due to already elevated calcium levels.
- **K2 mitigates calcification risk:** Co-supplementing vitamin K2 (MK-7, 100-200 mcg) activates osteocalcin and matrix Gla protein, directing calcium to bones and away from arteries.
- **Magnesium depletion:** High-dose vitamin D can deplete magnesium stores, as magnesium is consumed during D3 hydroxylation. Always co-supplement magnesium.
- **Individual variation:** Genetic polymorphisms in VDR, CYP2R1, and CYP27B1 genes can affect sensitivity to vitamin D. Some individuals require much higher or lower doses.
