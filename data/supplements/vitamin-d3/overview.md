# Vitamin D3
## Overview

- **What it is:** Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) is a fat-soluble secosteroid prohormone that the body converts into the active hormone calcitriol. It is synthesized endogenously in the skin upon exposure to UV-B radiation and can also be obtained through diet and supplementation.
- **Why it matters:** Vitamin D receptors (VDR) are found in virtually every cell in the body, influencing over 1,000 genes. It is essential for calcium and phosphorus homeostasis, bone mineralization, immune regulation, mood, cardiovascular health, and cancer prevention.
- **Deficiency prevalence:** An estimated 1 billion people worldwide are vitamin D deficient or insufficient. In the United States, approximately 42% of adults have serum 25(OH)D levels below 20 ng/mL (deficient), and up to 75% have levels below 30 ng/mL (insufficient). Rates are higher in darker-skinned individuals, the elderly, obese individuals, and those living at higher latitudes.
- **Testing:** The standard blood test is **serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)D)**. This measures the storage form and reflects total vitamin D status from sun, food, and supplements combined.
- **Optimal blood levels:** While the Institute of Medicine defines sufficiency as >20 ng/mL, most functional and integrative practitioners target **40-60 ng/mL** for optimal health outcomes. The Endocrine Society recommends >30 ng/mL as sufficient with a preferred range of 40-60 ng/mL.
- **Toxicity threshold:** Serum levels above 100 ng/mL are generally considered excessive and potentially toxic, though clinical toxicity typically presents above 150 ng/mL.
- **Sun exposure vs. supplementation:** The body can produce 10,000-20,000 IU of vitamin D3 from 15-30 minutes of full-body midday sun exposure in fair-skinned individuals. However, factors like latitude (above 35th parallel), season (October-March in northern hemisphere), sunscreen use (SPF 30 blocks ~97% of UV-B), skin pigmentation, age, and cloud cover significantly reduce synthesis. Most people require supplementation to maintain optimal levels year-round.
- **The K2 co-requirement:** Vitamin D3 increases calcium absorption from the gut by up to 40%. Without adequate vitamin K2, this calcium can deposit in arteries and soft tissues rather than bones and teeth. **Vitamin K2 (especially MK-7) should always be co-supplemented with D3** to direct calcium to the skeleton and prevent vascular calcification.
