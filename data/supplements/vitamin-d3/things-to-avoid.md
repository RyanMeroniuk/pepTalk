# Vitamin D3
## Things to Avoid

### Substances That Reduce Efficacy
- **Sunscreen overuse without supplementation:** SPF 15 blocks ~93% of UV-B; SPF 30 blocks ~97%. While sunscreen is important for skin cancer prevention, those who always wear sunscreen should supplement D3 since skin synthesis is essentially eliminated.
- **Smoking:** Smokers have significantly lower 25(OH)D levels. Smoking impairs hepatic hydroxylation and increases catabolism of vitamin D metabolites.
- **Excessive alcohol consumption:** More than 2 drinks/day impairs liver conversion of D3 to 25(OH)D and damages intestinal absorption.
- **Recreational drugs:** Cannabis and certain recreational substances may alter vitamin D metabolism through CYP enzyme interactions.

### Medications That Deplete or Block Vitamin D
- **Corticosteroids (prednisone, dexamethasone):** Accelerate vitamin D catabolism and impair calcium absorption. Long-term users often need higher D3 doses (5,000-10,000 IU/day).
- **Anticonvulsants (phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine):** Induce CYP enzymes that accelerate vitamin D breakdown. Epilepsy patients commonly develop vitamin D deficiency.
- **Cholestyramine and orlistat:** Fat-binding medications that reduce absorption of all fat-soluble vitamins including D3.
- **Rifampin:** Potent CYP3A4 inducer that accelerates vitamin D catabolism.
- **Certain antifungals (ketoconazole):** Inhibit CYP enzymes involved in vitamin D activation.
- **Statins:** Some evidence that statins may modestly reduce 25(OH)D levels, though the relationship is complex.
- **Proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole):** May reduce calcium absorption and potentially impair vitamin D-calcium synergy with long-term use.

### Behaviors That Reduce Vitamin D Status
- **Chronic indoor lifestyle:** Working indoors during peak UV-B hours (10am-2pm) without compensatory supplementation is the single most common cause of deficiency.
- **Covering all skin outdoors:** Full clothing coverage prevents UV-B skin exposure. Cultural or religious dress requiring full coverage significantly increases deficiency risk.
- **Night shift work:** Disrupted circadian rhythm and minimal sun exposure leads to chronically low levels.
- **Extreme dieting / very low-fat diets:** Fat-free or extremely low-fat diets impair absorption of D3 supplements and reduce dietary intake of D3-containing foods (fatty fish, eggs, dairy).
- **Chronic stress:** Elevated cortisol impairs vitamin D metabolism and increases magnesium depletion, which is a cofactor for D3 activation.
- **Taking D3 without K2:** Not a direct reduction in D3 efficacy, but allows calcium to deposit in arteries rather than bones, negating one of D3's key benefits and creating potential harm.
- **Taking D3 without magnesium:** Magnesium is required for both hydroxylation steps. Without adequate magnesium, D3 cannot be converted to its active form, rendering supplementation partially ineffective.
