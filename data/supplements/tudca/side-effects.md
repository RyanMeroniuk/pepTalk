# TUDCA (Tauroursodeoxycholic Acid)
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild, Dose-Dependent)
- **GI upset** — Diarrhea, loose stools, nausea, and abdominal discomfort are the most common effects, usually dose-related. As a bile acid, TUDCA can have a mild laxative/choleretic effect. Taking with food and starting at a lower dose minimizes this.
- **Loose stools** — Particularly at higher doses; reflects increased bile flow.

### Uncommon
- **Headache** — Occasionally reported.
- **Mild dizziness or fatigue** — Uncommon.
- **Belching or a bitter taste** — Related to bile-acid nature.

### Generally Well Tolerated
- **Good safety profile at typical doses** — TUDCA and its parent UDCA have a long history of clinical use (UDCA as a prescription drug) with good tolerability. Most adverse effects are mild and GI in nature.
- **No established liver or kidney toxicity** at supplemental doses in healthy individuals — indeed, TUDCA is used to support the liver.

### Important Context for the Optimization Community
- **TUDCA is liver-supportive, not a free pass** — Using TUDCA does not make hepatotoxic compounds (oral anabolics, prohormones) safe. It mitigates stress but does not eliminate risk. Liver enzymes should still be monitored with blood work during any hepatically stressful protocol.

### Special Populations
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — Interestingly, UDCA is used clinically for intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy under medical supervision; however, OTC TUDCA supplementation should only be used in pregnancy under a physician's guidance. Do not self-prescribe.
- **Complete biliary obstruction** — Bile-acid therapy is contraindicated in complete biliary obstruction; choleretic agents could worsen the situation. Requires medical evaluation.
- **Active gallbladder disease** — Should be used only under medical guidance, as effects on bile and gallstones depend on the specific condition.

### Drug Interaction Cautions (See Drug Interactions)
- **Bile-acid sequestrants** can bind TUDCA and reduce its absorption — separate dosing by several hours.

### When to Stop or Seek Care
- Persistent or severe diarrhea not resolving with dose reduction
- Worsening jaundice, severe abdominal pain, or signs of biliary obstruction (seek medical care)

### Safety Summary
- Well tolerated overall; mild GI effects (especially loose stools) are the main practical issue and are dose-dependent
- Key cautions are biliary obstruction, active gallbladder disease, pregnancy (medical supervision), and the broader point that it does not neutralize hepatotoxin risk
