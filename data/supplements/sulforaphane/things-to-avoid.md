# Sulforaphane
## Things to Avoid

- **Buying glucoraphanin-only products without active myrosinase:** This is the number-one mistake. Many "broccoli extract" supplements standardize to glucoraphanin but lack the enzyme to convert it, so actual sulforaphane delivery is poor and unpredictable. Choose stabilized pre-formed sulforaphane, or glucoraphanin explicitly paired with active myrosinase
- **Boiling your broccoli/sprouts:** Heat destroys myrosinase. Boiled crucifers yield almost no sulforaphane. Eat raw or lightly steamed, or add a raw myrosinase source (mustard seed powder, raw sprouts) to cooked dishes
- **Judging dose by glucoraphanin content alone:** Label glucoraphanin overstates delivered sulforaphane when conversion is poor. Look for products standardized to actual sulforaphane yield
- **Overcooking and then expecting benefits:** A common disconnect — people eat plenty of cooked broccoli and assume they're getting sulforaphane when they may be getting very little
- **Megadosing raw crucifers with low iodine status:** Very high chronic raw-cruciferous intake combined with iodine deficiency could theoretically stress the thyroid. Ensure adequate iodine; keep intake reasonable
- **High antioxidant/Nrf2 dosing right around hard training (if adaptation is the goal):** As with other antioxidant-pathway activators, very high doses peri-workout could theoretically blunt some exercise adaptations; the evidence is limited but separating from workouts is a cautious option
- **Treating it as a cancer cure:** Despite strong preclinical chemoprevention research, sulforaphane is not a cancer treatment. Don't substitute it for medical care
- **Ignoring GI tolerance:** Start moderate; concentrated cruciferous compounds can cause gas and bloating

### Population Cautions
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** Eating broccoli/sprouts is fine; concentrated supplemental safety data are limited — discuss supplements with a physician
- **Thyroid conditions:** Ensure adequate iodine; be mindful of very high chronic raw-cruciferous intake
- **On medication / chemotherapy:** As an enzyme-inducing, antioxidant-pathway compound, coordinate with your physician — see drug-interactions
- **Cruciferous allergy:** Rare, but use caution if present
