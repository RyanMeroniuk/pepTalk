# Sulforaphane
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild)
- **Digestive discomfort** — gas, bloating, and mild stomach upset are the most common effects, typical of concentrated cruciferous compounds. Starting at a lower dose and taking with food helps
- **Sulfurous taste / smell** — as an organosulfur compound, sulforaphane and broccoli-sprout products can have a characteristic sulfur taste or odor

### Uncommon
- **Nausea** — usually at higher doses or on an empty stomach
- **Loose stools** — occasionally at higher intakes
- **Headache** — infrequently reported

### Theoretical / Context-Dependent
- **Thyroid considerations:** Cruciferous vegetables contain goitrogenic compounds, and very high chronic intake combined with iodine deficiency could theoretically affect thyroid function. At typical sulforaphane doses with adequate iodine, this is not a practical concern, but those with thyroid conditions should be mindful
- **Antioxidant timing around training:** As an Nrf2 activator that boosts antioxidant defenses, very high doses immediately around exercise could theoretically blunt some adaptive oxidative signaling; the evidence is limited and the effect is likely smaller than with megadose direct antioxidants
- **Hormetic nature:** Sulforaphane is mildly stress-inducing (hormetic) by design — this is how it activates Nrf2. Extremely high doses move from beneficial hormesis toward unnecessary stress

### Rare
- **Allergic reaction** — rare; those allergic to cruciferous vegetables should use caution

### Safety Profile Summary
- **Good safety profile** — derived from common food (broccoli) with a long dietary history; human trials report it is generally well tolerated
- **Most issues are GI and dose-related** — and improve with lower doses and taking with food
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — dietary amounts (eating broccoli/sprouts) are fine; concentrated supplemental safety data are limited, so discuss supplements with a physician
- **Thyroid conditions:** Ensure adequate iodine and be mindful of very high chronic cruciferous intake
- **None of this is medical advice** — consult a healthcare provider, especially if on medication or managing a condition
