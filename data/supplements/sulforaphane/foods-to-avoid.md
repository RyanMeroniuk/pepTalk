# Sulforaphane
## Foods to Avoid

- **Boiling/overcooking cruciferous vegetables (the biggest mistake):** High heat destroys myrosinase, the enzyme required to convert glucoraphanin into sulforaphane. Boiled broccoli yields little to no sulforaphane. If you want sulforaphane from food, eat crucifers raw or lightly steamed, or add a raw myrosinase source to cooked ones
- **Relying on glucoraphanin-only supplements without a myrosinase source:** Not a food per se, but the analogous error — without active myrosinase (or pre-formed sulforaphane), conversion is poor and inconsistent
- **Excessive alcohol:** Increases oxidative and hepatic burden, working against sulforaphane's detoxification and antioxidant support
- **Heavily processed, pro-oxidant diets:** High refined sugar and oxidized oils raise oxidative load. Sulforaphane upregulates defenses, but a continuously pro-oxidant diet keeps depleting them

### Foods That Are Not a Concern
- **Most foods do not block sulforaphane** — the key issues are heat (destroys myrosinase) and lack of a conversion enzyme, not other foods interfering
- **Fat and fiber** do not impair sulforaphane in a meaningful way

### Thyroid Note (Context, Not a Strict Avoid)
- **Goitrogens in crucifers:** Raw cruciferous vegetables contain goitrogenic compounds. In the context of adequate iodine and typical intake, this is not a practical problem. Those with thyroid conditions should ensure adequate iodine and avoid extreme chronic raw-cruciferous megadosing

### The Real "Avoid" List (Behaviors)
- **Destroying myrosinase with heat** — the central pitfall
- **Inconsistency** — the Nrf2 enzyme-induction benefit accumulates with regular intake
- **Assuming label glucoraphanin equals delivered sulforaphane** — it often does not

### Timing With Other Supplements
- **Pairs well with NAC, glycine, and alpha-lipoic-acid** — these support the glutathione system that sulforaphane's Nrf2 activation upregulates
- **Generally compatible** with most supplements; take with some food if GI-sensitive
