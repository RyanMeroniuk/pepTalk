# Sulforaphane
## Lifestyle Recommendations

- **Get bioavailable sulforaphane (the foundation):** Either eat raw or lightly steamed broccoli sprouts (chewing thoroughly), or choose a supplement that delivers stabilized pre-formed sulforaphane or glucoraphanin with active myrosinase. Without active conversion, the rest is moot
- **Restore conversion in cooked crucifers:** If you cook your broccoli, add a raw myrosinase source — a sprinkle of raw mustard seed powder, daikon, or a few raw sprouts — to regenerate sulforaphane formation
- **Pair with glutathione support:** Sulforaphane's Nrf2 activation upregulates glutathione synthesis enzymes. Pairing it with NAC (cysteine donor), glycine, and adequate protein gives the body the substrate to actually build the glutathione the pathway calls for — a complementary "induce + supply" antioxidant strategy
- **Stack with complementary antioxidant pathways:** Sulforaphane (Nrf2 induction) and alpha-lipoic-acid (also activates Nrf2 and recycles antioxidants) provide layered, sustained antioxidant defense
- **Support detoxification:** Sulforaphane induces Phase II detox enzymes. Help your body process and excrete mobilized compounds by staying well hydrated, eating adequate fiber (25-35 g/day), and sweating regularly via exercise or sauna
- **Eat a cruciferous-rich diet:** Regular broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, and especially sprouts provide ongoing glucoraphanin. Variety of crucifers also supplies myrosinase-rich raw options like arugula and watercress
- **Minimize alcohol and pro-oxidant foods:** These increase the oxidative and hepatic load that sulforaphane's defenses must counter
- **Be consistent:** The Nrf2 enzyme-induction benefit accumulates with regular daily intake rather than occasional large doses
- **Ensure adequate iodine if eating lots of raw crucifers:** This offsets the theoretical goitrogen concern
- **Consider growing your own sprouts:** Home-grown 3-4 day broccoli sprouts are inexpensive and the richest, freshest source of glucoraphanin with intact myrosinase
