# L-Theanine
## Mechanisms of Action

- **Alpha brain wave induction** — L-Theanine increases alpha wave (8-13 Hz) power in the occipital and parietal regions of the brain within 30-40 minutes of ingestion. Alpha waves are the signature of relaxed, meditative focus. EEG studies consistently demonstrate this effect at doses of 50-200mg, with dose-dependent increases in alpha power. This is the primary mechanism underlying L-Theanine's unique "calm alertness."
- **GABA modulation** — L-Theanine increases brain GABA levels, the primary inhibitory neurotransmitter. Unlike benzodiazepines (which directly agonize GABA-A receptors), L-Theanine's GABA enhancement is modulatory and indirect, which is why it does not cause sedation, motor impairment, or dependency.
- **Glutamate receptor antagonism** — L-Theanine is structurally similar to glutamate and binds to glutamate receptors (AMPA, kainate, and to a lesser extent NMDA) as a weak antagonist. This reduces glutamatergic excitatory signaling without fully blocking it, preventing excitotoxicity while preserving normal neurotransmission. This is a key neuroprotective mechanism.
- **Serotonin and dopamine modulation** — L-Theanine increases serotonin and dopamine concentrations in specific brain regions (hippocampus, striatum). The serotonin increase contributes to mood stabilization and anxiety reduction. The dopamine increase supports focus, motivation, and reward processing without the overstimulation caused by dopaminergic drugs.
- **Cortisol and stress hormone reduction** — L-Theanine reduces salivary cortisol and salivary alpha-amylase (a marker of sympathetic nervous system activation) in response to acute stressors. This is a direct anti-stress effect operating through the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis.
- **Glycine potentiation** — L-Theanine may enhance glycine receptor activity, contributing to its calming and sleep-supportive effects. Glycine is an inhibitory neurotransmitter that lowers core body temperature and promotes sleep.
- **BDNF support** — Preclinical evidence indicates L-Theanine upregulates Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF), which supports neuroplasticity, learning, memory formation, and neuronal survival.
- **Nitric oxide modulation** — L-Theanine influences endothelial nitric oxide production, which may contribute to its mild blood pressure-lowering effects during stress.
- **Blood-brain barrier transit** — L-Theanine crosses the blood-brain barrier via the leucine-preferring transport system within 30-45 minutes of oral ingestion, reaching peak brain concentrations at approximately 1-2 hours. This rapid CNS access underlies its quick onset of subjective effects.
