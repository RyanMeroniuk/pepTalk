# L-Theanine
## Natural Food Sources

- **Green tea (Camellia sinensis)** — The primary natural source. A typical cup of green tea contains 20-30mg of L-Theanine. Shade-grown varieties contain significantly more because shading increases theanine content while reducing catechin conversion. Brewing time and temperature affect extraction — longer steeping (3-5 minutes) extracts more theanine.
- **Gyokuro (shade-grown Japanese green tea)** — Among the highest L-Theanine content of any tea, approximately 40-60mg per cup. Shaded for 20+ days before harvest, dramatically increasing theanine levels. Premium quality but expensive.
- **Matcha** — Stone-ground shade-grown green tea. Because you consume the entire leaf (not just an infusion), matcha delivers more L-Theanine per serving than regular brewed green tea — approximately 30-50mg per gram of powder. A typical serving (1-2g) provides 30-100mg L-Theanine.
- **Tencha** — The base tea leaf used to make matcha, before grinding. When brewed as a tea, it delivers high L-Theanine similar to gyokuro.
- **White tea** — Made from young, minimally processed tea buds and leaves. Contains approximately 15-25mg L-Theanine per cup. Lower caffeine than green tea, making it a gentler option.
- **Black tea** — Contains L-Theanine but at lower levels than green tea (approximately 10-20mg per cup) due to oxidation during processing, which converts some theanine.
- **Oolong tea** — Semi-oxidized, falling between green and black tea in L-Theanine content (approximately 15-25mg per cup).
- **Pu-erh tea** — Fermented tea with lower L-Theanine content due to microbial processing, approximately 5-15mg per cup.
- **Bay bolete mushroom (Boletus badius / Imleria badia)** — The only non-tea natural source of L-Theanine identified to date. Contains small amounts. Not a practical dietary source but scientifically interesting.

**Maximizing L-Theanine from tea:**

- **Choose shade-grown varieties** (gyokuro, matcha, kabusecha)
- **Use lower water temperature** (160-175 degrees Fahrenheit / 70-80 degrees Celsius) — theanine extracts well at lower temperatures while tannins and bitter compounds require higher heat
- **Steep for 3-5 minutes** for maximum extraction
- **First steeping has the highest L-Theanine content** — subsequent steepings progressively decrease
- **Cold brewing** (overnight in refrigerator) extracts high theanine with minimal bitterness

**Practical note:** To achieve clinically studied doses (100-200mg), you would need to drink 4-8 cups of regular green tea or 2-4 servings of matcha. Supplementation is the most practical way to reach effective doses consistently.
