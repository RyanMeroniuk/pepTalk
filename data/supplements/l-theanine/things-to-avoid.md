# L-Theanine
## Things to Avoid

- **Relying on L-Theanine alone for clinical anxiety disorders** — While L-Theanine is effective for mild-to-moderate situational anxiety and daily stress, it is not a replacement for professional treatment of generalized anxiety disorder (GAD), panic disorder, PTSD, or OCD. Use it as a complement to evidence-based treatment, not a substitute.
- **Combining with sedative medications without awareness** — L-Theanine has mild GABAergic activity. While not dangerous on its own, combining with benzodiazepines, barbiturates, or sedative antihistamines may increase drowsiness. This is generally manageable, but be aware of additive sedation, particularly when driving.
- **Taking high doses for the first time before an important event** — While L-Theanine is very well-tolerated, individual responses vary. Test your response at home before relying on it for a big presentation, exam, or performance. Most people feel calm and focused; a small minority feel slightly more drowsy than expected.
- **Expecting immediate results from a single low dose** — While acute effects are noticeable at 200mg, some individuals need a few days of consistent use (200mg twice daily) before they notice a clear difference in baseline anxiety levels. Give it at least a week of consistent use before concluding it does not work.
- **Using L-Theanine to mask chronic stress without addressing root causes** — L-Theanine can reduce the subjective experience of stress, but it does not resolve the underlying stressors. If anxiety is driven by overwork, toxic relationships, sleep deprivation, or untreated medical conditions, address those root causes alongside supplementation.
- **Stopping blood pressure medications in favor of L-Theanine** — L-Theanine has modest blood pressure-lowering effects during acute stress, but it is not a substitute for antihypertensive medication. Never adjust prescription medications based on supplement use without consulting your physician.
- **Combining excessive amounts of multiple GABAergic supplements** — Stacking very high doses of L-Theanine (400mg+) with other GABA modulators (phenibut, valerian, kava, high-dose magnesium) simultaneously may produce unexpected sedation. Each is safe individually at standard doses, but be mindful of cumulative GABAergic load.
