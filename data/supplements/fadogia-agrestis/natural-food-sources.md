# Fadogia Agrestis
## Natural Food Sources

- **No dietary food source:** Fadogia agrestis is a West African shrub whose stem is used medicinally, not a food. Its constituents are not present in any common food, and there is no way to obtain it through diet. It is entirely a supplement-derived botanical

- **Traditional preparation (medicinal, not food):**
  - **Stem decoction:** Traditionally, the stem of Fadogia agrestis is boiled in water to make a decoction used as an aphrodisiac in parts of West Africa. This is a traditional remedy preparation, not a culinary food
  - Dosing in traditional use is uncontrolled and not comparable to modern extract capsules

- **Why there is no "natural" dietary route:**
  - The plant is not cultivated or consumed as food
  - The active compounds (poorly characterized saponins/alkaloids) require extraction from the stem
  - Any fadogia intake comes from a supplement product whose identity, potency, and purity are not standardized

- **Safety implication of the lack of a food/traditional-dose baseline:** Because there is no everyday dietary exposure and no validated traditional dose translated to modern use, there is no "background safe intake" to anchor to. Combined with the rodent testicular-toxicity findings and absent human data, this is another reason fadogia should be treated as experimental rather than as a familiar dietary compound
