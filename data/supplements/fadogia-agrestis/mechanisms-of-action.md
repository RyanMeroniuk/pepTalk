# Fadogia Agrestis
## Mechanisms of Action

> All proposed mechanisms below are derived almost entirely from rodent studies. None are confirmed in humans, and the specific active compounds responsible are not well characterized.

- **Proposed LH stimulation / "luteinizing hormone mimetic" effect (primary hypothesis):** Fadogia agrestis is theorized to increase luteinizing hormone (LH) signaling or to act in an LH-like manner on the testes. LH is the pituitary hormone that stimulates Leydig cells to produce testosterone. By increasing this signal, fadogia is proposed to raise endogenous testosterone. The exact molecular basis is not established
- **Increased testosterone (rodent data):** In rat studies, fadogia stem extract raised serum testosterone in a dose-dependent manner over the dosing period. Whether this translates to humans is unknown
- **Aphrodisiac / pro-sexual effects (rodent data):** Rat studies report increased mounting frequency and sexual behavior, consistent with the traditional aphrodisiac use. Again, this is animal behavior, not human clinical evidence
- **Saponin and alkaloid content:** The plant contains saponins and alkaloids thought to underlie its bioactivity, but the specific responsible compounds and their human pharmacology are poorly defined
- **The toxicity counterpart of the mechanism (critical):** The same rodent research that reports testosterone increases also documents dose-dependent **testicular tissue damage/toxicity** at higher doses, and changes in organ tissue. This suggests the hormonal effect and the toxicity may not be cleanly separable, and that "more" is likely more dangerous, not more beneficial
- **No human pharmacokinetics:** There is essentially no data on how fadogia is absorbed, metabolized, or cleared in humans, what a safe dose is, or how long it can be used safely. Any human dosing is extrapolated from rodent studies and anecdote, not established science
