# Fadogia Agrestis
## Side Effects

> CRITICAL: Fadogia agrestis has essentially NO human safety data. The concerns below are based on rodent studies and the inherent uncertainty of an unstudied hormonal agent. The absence of reported human side effects reflects a lack of study, NOT evidence of safety.

### The Central Concern: Testicular Toxicity (Rodent Data)
- **Dose-dependent testicular damage in rats:** The most concrete finding in the fadogia literature is that, in rodent studies, higher and/or prolonged doses caused **testicular tissue toxicity** — alterations to testicular architecture and markers of damage. This is deeply concerning for an agent marketed to *support* male reproductive/hormonal health, and it is the single most important reason for caution
- **The toxicity and the testosterone effect may not be separable:** The same studies showing testosterone increases also show the testicular damage, suggesting the "benefit" and the harm may be intertwined and dose-related

### Other Organ Concerns (Rodent Data)
- Rodent studies have noted potential effects on other organ tissues (e.g., signs of stress on the liver/kidneys in some reports), raising broader toxicity questions that have never been resolved in humans

### Unknown Human Side-Effect Profile
- **We genuinely do not know fadogia's human side-effect profile.** There are no controlled human trials cataloguing adverse events, no established safe dose, and no long-term safety data
- **Anecdotal human reports** (online) describe a mix of perceived benefits and adverse effects (e.g., changes in libido, mood, testicular discomfort), but these are unverified and uncontrolled

### Theoretical / Plausible Human Risks
- **Reproductive harm:** Given the rodent testicular findings, potential impairment of testicular function or fertility is the foremost theoretical risk
- **Hormonal disruption:** As a purported LH/testosterone-pathway agent, it could disrupt the HPG axis in unpredictable ways
- **Contamination:** Poorly sourced, unstandardized botanicals carry heavy-metal and adulteration risks

### Contraindications (Strongly Advised)
- **Anyone trying to conceive / concerned about fertility:** Avoid — the rodent testicular-toxicity signal is directly relevant
- **Hormone-sensitive conditions (prostate cancer, prostate conditions, hormone-sensitive cancers):** Avoid given the testosterone-pathway activity
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** Avoid entirely
- **Anyone with liver or kidney concerns:** Avoid given rodent organ-toxicity signals
- **Competitive athletes:** Caution — purported testosterone-raising agents may be problematic for drug-tested sport

### Safety Profile Summary
- Fadogia agrestis is best regarded as **experimental and high-risk** due to rodent testicular-toxicity findings combined with an almost complete absence of human safety data
- The prudent default is non-use, especially for anyone who values fertility or has hormonal concerns
