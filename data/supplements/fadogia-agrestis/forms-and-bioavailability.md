# Fadogia Agrestis
## Forms and Bioavailability

> There is no clinically validated, standardized form of fadogia agrestis, and essentially no human bioavailability data. Everything below reflects what is on the market, not what is established as safe or effective.

- **Stem extract powder / capsules (most common):**
  - **What it is:** Dried, extracted Fadogia agrestis stem, sold as loose powder or in capsules
  - **Standardization:** Often marketed as "10:1 extract" or by a saponin percentage, but there is **no validated standard** tied to the specific active compounds, because those compounds are not well characterized. Different products are not comparable, and label claims cannot be verified against clinical benchmarks
  - **Quality concern:** With little oversight and no standard, potency, purity, and even botanical identity vary widely

- **Bulk powder:**
  - Even less standardized; dosing is imprecise. Adulteration and contamination (including heavy metals, common in poorly sourced botanicals) are real risks

- **Bioavailability:**
  - **Essentially unknown in humans.** There is no reliable data on how fadogia's constituents are absorbed, metabolized, or cleared in people, nor what blood levels any oral dose produces
  - Human dosing (commonly cited as ~600 mg/day, cycled) is extrapolated from rodent studies and popular anecdote — NOT from human pharmacokinetic research

- **No quality benchmark exists:** Unlike LJ100 for tongkat-ali or PrimaVie for shilajit, there is no gold-standard, clinically studied fadogia extract. This absence of a validated form is itself a reason for caution
- **Practical implication:** Because there is no standardized, safety-tested form and no human bioavailability data, you cannot reliably know what you are taking or how much active compound it delivers. This compounds the toxicity concerns from the rodent literature
