# Fadogia Agrestis
## Things to Avoid

> The most important "thing to avoid" with fadogia agrestis may be the supplement itself, given the imbalance between its popularity and its evidence/safety profile. The points below assume a reader who is nonetheless evaluating or using it.

- **Underestimating the toxicity signal:** The most concrete finding in the fadogia literature is **rodent testicular toxicity** at higher doses. Do not dismiss this. An agent marketed to support male hormonal health that damages testicular tissue in animals deserves serious caution
- **Assuming OTC = safe:** Fadogia is sold as a supplement, but that status reflects loose regulation, not safety testing. There is essentially no human safety data. "Available to buy" is not "shown to be safe"
- **Continuous, uncycled use:** Continuous and higher-dose use most closely mirrors the conditions linked to toxicity in rodents. If used at all, proponents emphasize cycling — but cycling is a precaution against uncertainty, not a proven safe pattern
- **Higher-than-typical doses:** Going beyond commonly cited doses (~600 mg/day) pushes toward the toxicity end of the rodent dose-response. More is more dangerous, not more beneficial
- **Using it while trying to conceive or if fertility matters:** The rodent testicular-toxicity findings make this a clear avoid for anyone concerned about reproductive health
- **Using with hormone-sensitive conditions:** Avoid with prostate cancer, prostate conditions, and hormone-sensitive cancers given the testosterone-pathway activity
- **Use during pregnancy or breastfeeding:** Avoid entirely
- **Trusting product labels:** There is no standardized, validated fadogia extract. Potency, purity, and even correct botanical identity vary, and contamination (including heavy metals) is a real risk with poorly sourced botanicals
- **Stacking it blindly with other hormonal agents:** Combining fadogia with DHEA, TRT, or several other testosterone herbs (tongkat-ali, boron, shilajit) at once multiplies hormonal load and makes it impossible to attribute any benefit or harm. If experimenting, introduce one variable at a time and monitor labs
- **Skipping medical oversight and labs:** If used at all, baseline and follow-up labs (testosterone, LH, reproductive/organ-health markers) and physician involvement are strongly advised. Discontinue immediately at any sign of testicular pain or swelling
- **Choosing fadogia over better-characterized options:** For testosterone support, tongkat-ali, boron, and lifestyle fundamentals (sleep, training, body-fat control) have far better evidence-to-risk profiles. Fadogia should not be a first choice
