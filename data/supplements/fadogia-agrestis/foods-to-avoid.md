# Fadogia Agrestis
## Foods to Avoid

> Because fadogia agrestis is essentially unstudied in humans, there is no evidence-based food-interaction list. The guidance below is precautionary, oriented around its purported hormonal activity and the rodent toxicity concerns.

- **Excessive alcohol:** Heavy alcohol stresses the liver and kidneys — the very organs that showed stress signals in some rodent fadogia studies — and independently lowers testosterone. Combining heavy drinking with an experimental hepatically-processed botanical compounds the uncertainty and risk
- **High-sugar, ultra-processed diets:** Drive inflammation and metabolic dysfunction and increase estrogen conversion in body fat, working against any testosterone goal fadogia is meant to support
- **Absorption-enhancing additions (piperine/black pepper in large amounts):** Unlike most supplements, you do NOT want to deliberately increase fadogia's systemic exposure given the toxicity signal. Avoid pairing it with compounds intended to boost botanical bioavailability

### Behaviors, Not Foods (More Important Here)
- **Combining with other hepatotoxic or hormonally active substances:** Avoid stacking fadogia with alcohol, other experimental hormonal agents, or anabolic compounds — the additive risk to the liver and reproductive system is unquantified
- **Continuous high-dose use:** The conditions most associated with rodent toxicity. Avoid

### Generally Fine
- There are no specific common foods documented to block fadogia's effect (because its pharmacology is unstudied)
- The genuinely useful "diet" advice for the underlying testosterone goal — adequate protein, healthy fat, controlling body fat, limiting alcohol — applies regardless of fadogia and is the safer focus
