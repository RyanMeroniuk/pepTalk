# Fadogia Agrestis
## Foods That Enhance Absorption

> There is essentially no human pharmacokinetic or absorption data for fadogia agrestis. Any "absorption" guidance is speculative. Given the testicular-toxicity concern in rodent studies, *enhancing* absorption is not necessarily desirable — it could increase exposure to potentially toxic constituents.

- **No established absorption enhancers:** Because fadogia's active compounds are poorly characterized and unstudied in humans, there are no evidence-based foods or nutrients known to enhance its absorption or effect

- **Taken with food for tolerability:** As with many botanicals, taking it with a meal may reduce GI discomfort. This is a general comfort measure, not a validated optimization

- **Speculative, NOT recommended:**
  - **Black pepper (piperine)** is sometimes added to botanical stacks to slow metabolism of plant compounds and raise their blood levels. For fadogia specifically, deliberately increasing systemic exposure is **ill-advised** given the rodent toxicity signal — you would potentially amplify exposure to constituents linked to testicular damage. Do not pair fadogia with absorption boosters to "get more out of it"

- **The responsible framing:** With most supplements, enhancing absorption is a goal. With fadogia agrestis, the more relevant priority is *limiting* unnecessary exposure given the unfavorable evidence base. The better-characterized testosterone-cluster options (tongkat-ali, boron) are where absorption optimization actually makes sense

- **Supportive nutrition for the underlying goal:** If the goal is testosterone support, the foundational nutrition that genuinely helps (adequate protein, healthy fat, zinc, magnesium, vitamin D) does so independent of fadogia and is the safer place to focus
