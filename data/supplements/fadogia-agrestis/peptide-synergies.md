# Fadogia Agrestis
## Peptide Synergies

> CRITICAL WARNING: Fadogia agrestis has rodent testicular-toxicity findings and essentially no human safety data. "Synergy" here describes only theoretical mechanistic overlap. Combining fadogia with hormonally active peptides or other testosterone agents INCREASES uncertainty and risk and makes it impossible to attribute any benefit or harm. These pairings are documented for completeness, not recommended. The prudent default is non-use.

### Theoretically Related (High Caution)

#### Kisspeptin + Fadogia Agrestis (Overlapping HPG-Axis Targeting)
- **Rationale (theoretical):** Kisspeptin acts at the top of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis to stimulate GnRH and downstream LH/FSH, driving endogenous testosterone. Fadogia is *proposed* to raise LH/testosterone via the testes. On paper these target the same axis from different points — but stacking two LH/testosterone-pathway agents (one of which damaged testicular tissue in rodents) is precisely the kind of overlap that could amplify both effect and harm. NOT recommended without specialist oversight.
- **If considered at all:** Physician-guided, isolated variables, full lab monitoring including reproductive markers
- **Best for:** No clear safe use case given fadogia's risk profile

#### PT-141 (Bremelanotide) + Fadogia Agrestis (Libido)
- **Rationale (theoretical):** PT-141 acts centrally on melanocortin receptors for acute sexual arousal; fadogia is traditionally an aphrodisiac and *purportedly* supports testosterone. The two could in theory address acute and hormonal aspects of libido — but PT-141 already addresses libido without fadogia's toxicity baggage, so adding fadogia introduces risk for little marginal benefit.
- **Best for:** Better served by PT-141 alone or with safer hormonal support

### Theoretically Complementary (Not Advised)

#### CJC-1295/Ipamorelin or MK-677 + Fadogia Agrestis
- **Rationale (theoretical):** GH secretagogues (CJC-1295/Ipamorelin, MK-677) raise GH/IGF-1 for recovery and body composition, addressing a different axis than fadogia's purported testosterone effect. Mechanistically separate, but this does not make fadogia safe — it simply means the GH peptide is doing the well-characterized work while fadogia adds unquantified reproductive risk.
- **Best for:** Use the GH peptide with a safer testosterone-support option instead

#### IGF-1 LR3 / Tesamorelin + Fadogia Agrestis
- **Rationale (theoretical):** These peptides drive hypertrophy or visceral-fat loss via GH/IGF-1 pathways, independent of fadogia. Again, the peptide provides the characterized benefit; fadogia adds risk without proven benefit.
- **Best for:** No compelling reason to include fadogia

### Supplement Stacking Notes (Testosterone Cluster — Caution)
- **Fadogia Agrestis + Tongkat Ali / Boron / Shilajit / DHEA:** Fadogia is frequently marketed alongside the rest of the testosterone cluster (tongkat-ali for SHBG/free-T, boron for SHBG/estradiol, shilajit for modest T support, DHEA as a hormone precursor). Stacking multiple testosterone agents at once obscures cause and effect AND compounds risk. Fadogia is the riskiest member by far due to rodent testicular toxicity and absent human data.
- **Safer alternative:** If building a testosterone-support stack, favor tongkat-ali + boron (+ vitamin D3, zinc, magnesium) and lifestyle fundamentals, and leave fadogia out unless experimenting deliberately, conservatively, cycled, and monitored.
