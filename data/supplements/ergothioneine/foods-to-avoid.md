# Ergothioneine
## Foods to Avoid

Ergothioneine has no notable food-based absorption antagonists — its dedicated transporter handles uptake reliably. The considerations here are mostly about not undermining its antioxidant purpose.

- **Excessive alcohol:** Increases oxidative stress and burdens the liver — a tissue where ergothioneine concentrates and acts. Heavy drinking works against the antioxidant role ergothioneine plays
- **Highly processed, pro-oxidant diets:** Diets high in refined sugar, oxidized/repeatedly-heated oils, and ultra-processed foods raise the oxidative load. Ergothioneine buffers stress, but a continuously pro-oxidant diet keeps depleting the body's defenses
- **A mushroom-free diet (the indirect "avoid"):** Avoiding mushrooms entirely is the main way people end up with low dietary ergothioneine. Low blood ergothioneine is associated in observational studies with higher disease and frailty risk. Including mushrooms is the simplest fix

### Foods That Are Not a Concern
- **No specific foods block ergothioneine absorption** in any meaningful way — OCTN1-mediated uptake is efficient and not notably interfered with by other foods
- **Fat, fiber, and protein** do not impair its uptake (it is water-soluble and transporter-absorbed)
- **Cooking does not destroy it** — ergothioneine is heat-stable, so cooked mushrooms remain a good source

### The Real "Avoid" List (Behaviors)
- **Inconsistency** — ergothioneine accumulates with steady intake; sporadic use limits the tissue reserve it builds
- **Relying on it to offset a poor diet** — it is a complement to, not a substitute for, an antioxidant-rich diet and healthy lifestyle

### Timing With Other Supplements
- **Generally compatible** with most supplements; no fat or special timing needed
- **Complements glutathione support (NAC, glycine) and mitochondrial antioxidants (CoQ10)** — works within the broader antioxidant network
