# Ergothioneine
## Benefits

### Antioxidant & Cytoprotection (Core, Well-Supported Mechanistically)
- **Targeted antioxidant defense** — actively concentrated by the OCTN1 transporter in high-stress tissues (liver, blood cells, eyes, skin, brain), placing protection where it is most needed
- **Stable, persistent protection** — resists auto-oxidation, acting as a long-lasting antioxidant reserve rather than being quickly consumed
- **Metal chelation** — binds redox-active metals (copper, iron) that drive damaging free-radical reactions

### Sleep & Cognition (2025 RCT — Early Human Evidence)
- **Improved sleep measures** — a 2025 randomized controlled trial reported improvements in sleep quality with ergothioneine supplementation
- **Improved memory measures** — the same RCT reported benefits on memory/cognitive measures; this is encouraging but single-trial, early evidence
- **Neuroprotection (preclinical/observational)** — lower blood ergothioneine is associated with cognitive decline and dementia risk in observational studies; protective in neural models

### Longevity & Cardiometabolic Associations (Observational)
- **Associated with lower disease risk** — higher blood ergothioneine has been associated in population studies with lower cardiovascular disease, lower mortality, and reduced frailty; lower levels track with aging and disease. These are associations, not proof of causation
- **"Longevity vitamin" candidate** — the dedicated transporter and selective retention support a hypothesized role in healthy aging

### Eye Health (Emerging)
- **Concentrates in the lens and cornea** — provides antioxidant protection to light- and oxygen-exposed ocular tissue; studied for cataract and age-related eye protection

### Skin Health (Emerging)
- **Photoprotection** — accumulates in skin and protects against UV-induced oxidative damage; used in some topical formulations

### Mitochondrial & Inflammatory
- **Protects mitochondria** — preserves mitochondrial DNA and function under oxidative stress
- **Lowers inflammatory signaling** — reduces oxidative-stress-driven inflammation in preclinical work

### Honest Framing
- The mechanistic case (dedicated transporter, tissue targeting, stable antioxidant action) is strong, and observational associations with healthy aging are intriguing. However, human interventional evidence is still early — the 2025 RCT is a notable but single data point, and most disease-relevant data are observational or preclinical. None of this is medical advice.
