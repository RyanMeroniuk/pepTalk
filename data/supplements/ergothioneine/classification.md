# Ergothioneine
## Classification

- **Category:** Cytoprotective antioxidant / "longevity vitamin" candidate / cellular stress protectant
- **Supplement class:** Naturally occurring thiourea derivative of the amino acid histidine (a betaine)
- **Also known as:** L-ergothioneine, ERGO, EGT, ercmt
- **Molecular formula:** C9H15N3O2S
- **Natural source:** Synthesized only by certain fungi and bacteria; humans obtain it entirely from diet (mushrooms are the richest source) and accumulate it via a dedicated transporter
- **Regulatory status:** Dietary supplement (OTC); synthetic L-ergothioneine has been granted novel-food and GRAS-type approvals in several jurisdictions
- **Primary systems:** Concentrates in tissues exposed to high oxidative stress — liver, kidneys, red and white blood cells, bone marrow, eyes (lens/cornea), skin, and brain
- **Key distinction:** Humans possess a dedicated, highly specific transporter for ergothioneine (OCTN1 / SLC22A4), which actively concentrates it in cells and protects it from rapid breakdown. The existence of a specialized transporter strongly suggests a physiological role — the basis for calling it a possible "longevity vitamin"
- **Uniqueness:** Unusually stable for a thiol-containing antioxidant; it does not auto-oxidize readily, allowing it to persist in tissues for a long time
