# Ergothioneine
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **L-ergothioneine (the natural, active form):** The biologically active form found in food and recognized by the OCTN1 transporter. Quality supplements use synthetic or fermentation-derived L-ergothioneine identical to the dietary form. Branded ingredients (e.g., ErgoActive and other purified L-ergothioneine ingredients) are used in supplements
- **Synthetic vs natural — chemically identical:** Synthetic and fermentation-produced L-ergothioneine are structurally identical to the form in mushrooms and behave the same in the body. Both are recognized by OCTN1
- **Transporter-mediated absorption (the key feature):** Ergothioneine absorption and tissue uptake depend on the OCTN1 (SLC22A4) transporter, expressed in the intestine and throughout the body. This active, saturable transport efficiently absorbs ergothioneine and concentrates it in cells — bioavailability is good and it is well retained
- **Long half-life / tissue retention:** Because of active uptake and resistance to breakdown, ergothioneine has an unusually long retention time in the body (a substantial portion of a dose is retained and slowly released, with low urinary excretion). This means it accumulates with consistent intake and does not need precise timing
- **Mushroom extracts as a source:** Some products provide ergothioneine via concentrated mushroom extracts; others use purified L-ergothioneine. Purified forms allow precise dosing, while mushroom-based products deliver it alongside other fungal compounds
- **Stability:** Ergothioneine is notably stable (heat- and oxidation-resistant compared with many antioxidants), so it survives cooking and storage relatively well — one reason cooked mushrooms remain a good source
- **Absorption notes:** Can be taken with or without food; the active transporter handles uptake. Consistency matters more than timing because of tissue accumulation
- **Practical choice:** A purified L-ergothioneine supplement from a reputable, third-party-tested brand provides reliable dosing. For a whole-food approach, regularly eating mushrooms (especially higher-ergothioneine species) builds tissue levels over time
