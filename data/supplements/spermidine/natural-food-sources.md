# Spermidine
## Natural Food Sources

Spermidine is widely present in foods, and unlike many longevity compounds, meaningful amounts can be obtained through diet. (Food polyamine values vary by source, ripeness, and processing.)

### Highest Concentrations
- **Wheat germ** — the richest common source and the basis for most supplements; roughly 200-400 mg per kg (the standout dietary source)
- **Aged/mature cheeses** — cheddar, parmesan, and other aged cheeses are high (polyamines increase with aging/fermentation)
- **Soybeans and soy products (especially natto)** — natto (fermented soybeans) is particularly rich
- **Mushrooms** — among the better vegetable sources

### Good Sources
- **Legumes** — lentils, peas, chickpeas, beans
- **Whole grains** — alongside wheat germ, whole-grain products contribute
- **Peas and green vegetables** — broccoli, cauliflower
- **Corn**

### Moderate Sources
- **Nuts and seeds**
- **Apples and pears**
- **Fermented foods** — fermentation tends to raise polyamine content

### Important Context
- **Diet can provide meaningful spermidine** — unlike fisetin or NMN, where food amounts fall far short of supplement doses, a polyamine-rich diet (wheat germ, aged cheese, natto, mushrooms, legumes) can contribute a substantial share of the spermidine used in studies
- **Observational data is dietary** — the population studies linking higher spermidine to lower mortality measured *food* intake, underscoring that diet is a legitimate primary source
- **Gut bacteria also produce polyamines** — a healthy microbiome is an additional endogenous source, so gut health supports overall spermidine status
- **Supplementation** (typically wheat-germ extract) is used to ensure a consistent, standardized daily amount on top of dietary intake
