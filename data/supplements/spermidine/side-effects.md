# Spermidine
## Side Effects

### Generally Very Well Tolerated
- **Spermidine is a normal dietary component consumed daily worldwide** — at typical supplemental and dietary doses it has an excellent safety and tolerability record
- Human pilot trials using wheat-germ-derived spermidine (up to ~6 mg/day) reported good tolerability with few adverse events

### Common (Mild, Infrequent)
- **Mild GI discomfort** — occasional bloating, mild nausea, or changes in bowel habits, usually transient and reduced by taking with food
- **No significant adverse effects** are characteristic at researched doses

### Wheat-Germ / Gluten Consideration
- **Wheat-germ-derived products may contain trace gluten** — people with celiac disease or gluten sensitivity should choose certified gluten-free or fermentation-derived/synthetic spermidine to avoid a reaction

### Theoretical / Context-Dependent Concerns
- **Polyamines and cell proliferation** — polyamines support cell growth, and there is a long-standing theoretical discussion about polyamines and cancer-cell proliferation. However, dietary spermidine is associated with *lower* mortality in observational studies, and autophagy induction is generally considered protective. Evidence does not show that dietary/supplemental spermidine promotes cancer, but individuals with **active cancer** should consult their oncologist given the unresolved theoretical question.
- **High-dose unknowns** — long-term safety of doses well above the researched range (>6-10 mg/day) is not characterized

### Special Populations
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — polyamines are important in development, but concentrated supplemental doses lack safety data; dietary amounts are fine, supplementation should be discussed with a clinician
- **Active cancer** — consult an oncologist (theoretical proliferation question)
- **Celiac/gluten sensitivity** — avoid wheat-germ-derived forms

### Overall Safety Profile
- **One of the more benign longevity supplements** given its status as a normal dietary molecule
- **Main practical considerations are gluten content (for the wheat-germ form) and the theoretical cancer-proliferation question** in those with active malignancy
- No established drug toxicity or organ toxicity at dietary/supplemental levels
