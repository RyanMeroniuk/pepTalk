# Saffron Extract
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild, Uncommon at Supplemental Doses)
- **GI upset** — Mild nausea, dry mouth, or stomach discomfort, occasionally reported and usually transient.
- **Headache** — Occasionally reported.
- **Drowsiness or sedation** — Some users feel mildly relaxed/sleepy, which is often desirable for evening/sleep dosing but worth noting if taken during the day.
- **Changes in appetite** — Some report reduced appetite (consistent with the Satiereal snacking research).

### Uncommon
- **Dizziness** — Uncommon.
- **Anxiety or restlessness (paradoxical)** — Rarely reported.
- **Altered mood** — As with any serotonergic agent, monitor mood, especially when combining with other serotonergic supplements or medications.

### Dose Matters — High Doses Can Be Toxic
- **Supplemental doses (28-30mg/day) are well tolerated** — The clinical safety record at standardized supplemental doses is good.
- **Very high doses are toxic** — Doses of saffron well above supplemental ranges (e.g., 5g+ of the raw spice) can cause toxicity, and very high doses (reportedly ~20g) can be dangerous. This is not a concern at the ~28-30mg standardized-extract dose, but it underscores the importance of not megadosing. Respect the studied dose.

### Special Populations
- **Pregnancy** — Avoid supplemental saffron in pregnancy. High doses of saffron have traditionally been associated with uterine stimulation and abortifacient effects. While culinary amounts in food are considered safe, supplemental/standardized doses should be avoided during pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding** — Insufficient safety data; avoid supplemental saffron while breastfeeding (precautionary).
- **Bipolar disorder** — As a serotonergic mood-active agent, use cautiously and under professional guidance, given theoretical concerns about mood activation.

### Serotonergic Consideration (See Drug Interactions)
- Saffron enhances serotonin signaling. Combined with serotonergic medications (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs) or other serotonergic supplements (5-HTP, l-tryptophan, St. John's Wort), there is a theoretical additive serotonergic risk. Coordinate with a prescriber.

### When to Stop or Seek Care
- Significant mood changes, agitation, or symptoms suggestive of excessive serotonergic activity (especially if combined with serotonergic drugs)
- Persistent GI distress or allergic-type reaction

### Safety Summary
- Well tolerated at the standardized supplemental dose; side effects are mild and uncommon
- Key cautions: do not megadose (high doses are toxic), avoid in pregnancy/breastfeeding, and be careful combining with serotonergic medications
