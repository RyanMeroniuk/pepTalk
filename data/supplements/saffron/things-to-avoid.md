# Saffron Extract
## Things to Avoid

### Megadosing (High Doses Are Toxic)
- The most important caution. The effective standardized dose is small (~28-30mg/day). Very high doses of saffron (e.g., 5g+ of the raw spice) can cause toxicity, and very high amounts are dangerous. More is NOT better with saffron. Respect the studied dose and never attempt to megadose for stronger effects.

### Using Non-Standardized or Adulterated Products
- The clinical benefits come from standardized extracts (e.g., affron) at defined doses. Non-standardized products have unpredictable active content. Saffron is also one of the most counterfeited/adulterated commodities. Choose a clinically studied branded extract with third-party testing.

### Relying on Culinary Saffron for Therapeutic Effects
- Cooking with saffron does not deliver a consistent, measured dose of actives and is impractical and expensive for therapeutic purposes. Use a standardized extract for the mood/sleep benefits.

### Combining with Serotonergic Medications Without Guidance
- Saffron enhances serotonin signaling. Combining it with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, or other serotonergic drugs raises a theoretical risk of excessive serotonergic activity. Do not combine with prescribed antidepressants without consulting the prescriber (see drug interactions).

### Stacking Multiple Strong Serotonergic Supplements
- Combining saffron with 5-HTP, l-tryptophan, or St. John's Wort layers serotonergic effects. Avoid stacking several strong serotonergic agents without professional guidance.

### Using It in Pregnancy
- Avoid supplemental saffron during pregnancy. High doses of saffron have traditionally been linked to uterine stimulation and abortifacient effects. Culinary amounts in food are considered safe, but supplemental doses should be avoided in pregnancy and breastfeeding.

### Expecting It to Replace Treatment for Clinical Depression or Insomnia
- Saffron is a supportive supplement with good evidence for mild-to-moderate low mood and sleep quality, but it is not a substitute for professional care in clinical depression or chronic insomnia. Seek appropriate treatment for significant conditions.

### Under-Dosing in Combination Products
- Some mood/sleep blends include a token amount of saffron below the ~28-30mg standardized dose. Verify it's a standardized extract at an effective dose.

### Behaviors That Undermine Saffron's Benefits
- **Alcohol and late caffeine** — Both worsen mood and sleep, working against saffron
- **Poor sleep hygiene** — Erratic schedule, screens at night, and bright evening light undermine the sleep benefit
- **Chronic high stress without management** — Limits saffron's mood and calming effects
- **Inconsistent dosing** — Mood and sleep benefits require consistent daily use

### Medical Cautions
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — Avoid supplemental saffron
- **Bipolar disorder** — Use cautiously and under professional guidance (serotonergic/mood-active)
- **On antidepressants** — Consult your prescriber before use
- **Inform healthcare providers** of all supplements
