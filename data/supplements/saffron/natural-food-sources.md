# Saffron Extract
## Natural Food Sources

Saffron's only true source is the spice itself — the dried stigmas of the Crocus sativus flower. There is no other meaningful dietary source of saffron's specific bioactives (crocins, crocetin, safranal). However, getting a consistent, clinically effective standardized dose from culinary saffron is impractical, which is why standardized extract supplements are used.

### Saffron the Spice (The Only Real Source)
- **Saffron threads (Crocus sativus stigmas)** — The culinary spice, used in dishes like paella, risotto, biryani, and Persian rice. Contains the same crocins, crocetin, and safranal as the extract.
- **One of the most expensive spices by weight** — Each flower yields only three stigmas, hand-harvested, making it costly. This is part of why supplementation with a standardized extract is more practical for therapeutic doses.
- **Culinary amounts are small** — Typical cooking uses a pinch (tens of milligrams of threads) for flavor and color, but the active content and bioavailability are not standardized, so the dose of actives is inconsistent.

### Why Diet/Spice Use Is Not a Reliable Substitute
- **Inconsistent active content** — Saffron quality, crocin/safranal content, and freshness vary enormously; culinary use does not deliver a reliable, measured dose
- **Adulteration is common** — Saffron is one of the most frequently adulterated/counterfeited foods, so culinary saffron may contain little actual saffron
- **Impractical for therapeutic dosing** — Reaching a consistent ~28-30mg standardized-extract-equivalent through cooking is unrealistic and expensive
- **Standardized extracts solve this** — Branded extracts (e.g., affron) provide a defined, consistent dose of actives, which is what the clinical trials used

### Foods That Support the Same Goals (Mood & Sleep)
While not sources of saffron, these support the serotonergic and sleep pathways saffron acts on:
- **Tryptophan-rich foods** — Turkey, eggs, dairy, soy, seeds; tryptophan is the precursor to serotonin and melatonin
- **Complex carbohydrates** — Support tryptophan uptake into the brain
- **Magnesium-rich foods** — Leafy greens, nuts, seeds; support relaxation and sleep
- **Tart cherries** — A natural source of melatonin, supporting sleep
- **Omega-3-rich fish** — Support mood and brain health

### Practical Takeaway
- For saffron's studied mood and sleep effects, use a standardized extract supplement, not culinary saffron
- Enjoy saffron as a spice for flavor, but do not rely on it for therapeutic dosing
- Support mood and sleep more broadly with tryptophan-rich foods, magnesium, omega-3, and good sleep habits, and stack saffron with melatonin and l-theanine for sleep
