# Saffron Extract
## Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Serotonin Reuptake Modulation (Primary Mood Mechanism)
- Saffron's bioactives (notably crocins and safranal) appear to inhibit the reuptake of serotonin, increasing its availability in the synapse — a mechanism conceptually similar to SSRIs but milder
- Increased serotonergic signaling underlies saffron's mood-supporting and emotional-well-being effects
- This is also the basis for the theoretical additive interaction with serotonergic medications (see drug interactions)

### 2. Dopamine & Norepinephrine Modulation
- Saffron also modulates dopaminergic and noradrenergic signaling, contributing to its effects on mood, motivation, and emotional regulation
- This broader monoamine activity may explain benefits beyond pure serotonergic action

### 3. Sleep Regulation
- By enhancing serotonin (a precursor pathway to melatonin) and exerting calming effects, saffron supports sleep onset and sleep quality
- Recent trials (including a 2025 RCT at 14mg twice daily) show improved sleep quality; the mechanism likely combines serotonergic/melatonergic support with anxiolytic effects
- This complements melatonin (which directly signals sleep timing) and l-theanine (which promotes relaxation)

### 4. Antioxidant Activity (Crocins & Crocetin)
- Crocins and crocetin are carotenoid antioxidants that scavenge reactive oxygen species and reduce oxidative stress
- Oxidative stress is implicated in mood disorders and neuronal health, so this antioxidant action may contribute to neuroprotective and mood effects
- The same carotenoid antioxidant activity underpins research into saffron for eye and metabolic health

### 5. Anti-Inflammatory Effects
- Saffron compounds reduce pro-inflammatory signaling; neuroinflammation is increasingly linked to depression, so this may contribute to mood benefits
- Reduced inflammatory tone may also relate to its metabolic and general-health research

### 6. Neurotrophic / BDNF Support
- Some research suggests saffron may support brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) signaling, relevant to neuroplasticity and the mechanisms underlying antidepressant effects
- This is an emerging area and part of why saffron is studied as a mood-supporting nootropic

### 7. HPA-Axis & Stress Modulation
- Saffron may modulate the stress response and cortisol signaling, contributing to reductions in anxiety and stress-related symptoms reported in some trials
- This calming effect supports both its mood and sleep applications
