# Saffron Extract
## Overview

Saffron extract is a standardized botanical from the stigmas of the Crocus sativus flower, best known as a mood and sleep supplement. Its active carotenoids (crocins and crocetin) and the aroma compound safranal enhance serotonin signaling, and a growing body of randomized controlled trials supports its use for low mood and, more recently, sleep quality. Standardized branded extracts such as affron are the basis of most positive research.

**Why it matters:**

- **Mood support with RCT backing** — Multiple randomized controlled trials and meta-analyses show standardized saffron extract (typically 28-30mg/day) improves symptoms of mild-to-moderate low mood, with several trials reporting efficacy comparable to low-dose conventional antidepressants in head-to-head comparisons. It is one of the better-evidenced botanicals for mood.
- **Serotonin signaling** — Saffron's compounds appear to increase serotonin availability (acting partly as a serotonin reuptake modulator) and influence dopamine and norepinephrine, which underlies its mood and emotional-well-being effects.
- **Sleep improvement** — More recent research supports saffron for sleep. A 2025 randomized controlled trial found that saffron extract dosed at 14mg twice daily improved sleep quality, complementing earlier studies showing improved sleep onset and quality with standardized extracts.
- **Antioxidant carotenoids** — Crocins and crocetin are potent antioxidants, contributing to broader effects on oxidative stress and possibly eye and metabolic health (areas of ongoing research).
- **Well tolerated** — At supplemental doses, saffron extract is generally well tolerated, with mild side effects. High doses far above supplemental ranges can be toxic, so dosing discipline matters.
- **Pairs naturally for mood and sleep** — Saffron complements melatonin (sleep timing/onset) and l-theanine (calm, relaxation) in mood-and-sleep stacks.
- **Standardization is essential** — The studied benefits come from standardized extracts at defined doses; culinary saffron is not a reliable substitute. Choose a clinically studied branded extract such as affron.
