# Inositol (Myo-Inositol)
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild, Dose-Dependent)
- **GI upset** — Nausea, gas, bloating, and loose stools are the most common effects, almost entirely at higher doses (above ~12g/day). At the metabolic 2-4g/day dose, GI effects are uncommon. Splitting doses and titrating up minimizes this.
- **Mild lightheadedness or fatigue** — Occasionally reported, usually transient.

### Uncommon
- **Headache** — Rarely reported.
- **Dizziness** — Uncommon, usually at high doses.

### Excellent Overall Tolerability
- **Very well tolerated** — Inositol has a strong safety record across many trials, including high-dose anxiety studies. It is generally better tolerated than metformin for metabolic indications.
- **No liver or kidney toxicity** documented at supplemental doses in healthy individuals.
- **Not a stimulant or hormone** — Inositol is a naturally occurring sugar alcohol the body produces in gram quantities daily.

### Blood Sugar Consideration
- **Hypoglycemia risk in combination** — Inositol improves insulin sensitivity. When combined with insulin, sulfonylureas, or other glucose-lowering drugs (or other insulin-sensitizers like berberine), there is a theoretical additive risk of low blood sugar. Monitor blood glucose, especially when starting. See drug interactions.

### Special Populations
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — Myo-inositol has actually been studied during pregnancy (e.g., for gestational diabetes prevention) and is generally considered low-risk, but it should be used during pregnancy only under medical supervision. Do not self-prescribe high doses.
- **Bipolar disorder** — Because inositol affects the phosphatidylinositol signaling system targeted by lithium and influences serotonergic signaling, individuals with bipolar disorder should use high-dose inositol cautiously and under psychiatric supervision (theoretical concern for mood destabilization).
- **Psychiatric medication users** — High-dose inositol affects serotonergic signaling; coordinate with a prescriber if on SSRIs or other psychiatric drugs.

### When to Adjust or Stop
- Persistent GI distress that does not resolve with dose reduction and splitting
- Symptoms of low blood sugar if combined with glucose-lowering medication

### Safety Summary
- One of the better-tolerated metabolic supplements; main issue is dose-dependent GI upset at high (mood) doses
- Key cautions are around combination with glucose-lowering drugs and use in bipolar disorder or with psychiatric medications
