# Inositol (Myo-Inositol)
## Natural Food Sources

Inositol is widely distributed in foods, both as free myo-inositol and as inositol-containing compounds (phytic acid/IP6 in plants, and phospholipids in animal foods). A typical mixed diet provides roughly 0.5-1g/day, and the body synthesizes several grams more. However, the therapeutic metabolic dose (2-4g) and especially the mood dose (12-18g) far exceed what food provides — hence supplementation.

### Highest Food Sources
- **Fruits** — Cantaloupe and citrus fruits (especially oranges and grapefruit) are among the richest fruit sources of free myo-inositol
- **Beans and legumes** — Kidney beans, lima beans, navy beans, and other legumes provide inositol (partly as phytate)
- **Whole grains** — Wheat bran, brown rice, oats, and whole-grain products; much is bound as phytic acid (IP6)
- **Nuts and seeds** — Almonds, walnuts, and other nuts and seeds contain inositol
- **Cantaloupe** — Notably high among common fruits

### Moderate Sources
- **Other fruits** — Bananas, peaches, berries
- **Vegetables** — Leafy greens, green beans, and others contain smaller amounts
- **Organ meats and liver** — Animal sources contain inositol as part of phospholipids

### Important Context: Free vs Bound Inositol
- **Phytic acid (IP6) in grains/legumes** — Much of plant inositol is bound as phytate, which has lower bioavailability of free inositol and can also bind minerals. This is a different form from the free myo-inositol used in supplements.
- **Free myo-inositol** — Fruits (especially citrus and cantaloupe) provide more readily usable free inositol

### Why Diet Alone Is Insufficient for Therapeutic Effects
- **Typical intake (~0.5-1g/day)** is far below the 2-4g metabolic dose and the 12-18g mood dose
- **Bound forms have lower availability** — Much dietary inositol is in phytate form
- **Supplementation provides a defined, high free-inositol dose** difficult to reach from food

### Practical Takeaway
- A diet rich in fruit (citrus, cantaloupe), legumes, whole grains, and nuts supports baseline inositol intake and overall metabolic health
- For PCOS, metabolic, or mood goals, dietary inositol complements but cannot replace the targeted doses provided by supplementation
- Combine an inositol-rich, whole-food diet with supplementation and other metabolic supports (berberine, chromium) for metabolic goals
