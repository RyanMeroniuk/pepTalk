# PQQ (Pyrroloquinoline Quinone)
## Overview

PQQ is a small, redox-active quinone that was first identified as a bacterial enzyme cofactor and has since been recognized as a critical nutrient for mammalian health. Its most remarkable property is its ability to stimulate mitochondrial biogenesis — the creation of entirely new mitochondria — making it fundamentally different from other mitochondrial supplements.

- **Why it matters:** Mitochondrial number and quality decline with age. Most mitochondrial supplements (CoQ10, B vitamins) support existing mitochondria. PQQ is one of the very few compounds shown to increase the total number of mitochondria per cell
- **Mitochondrial biogenesis:** PQQ activates PGC-1alpha (peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1-alpha), the master regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis. This triggers cells to produce new, functional mitochondria
- **Extraordinary antioxidant potency:** PQQ can undergo over 20,000 catalytic redox cycles before being degraded — compared to vitamin C, which is destroyed after 4 cycles. This makes PQQ one of the most efficient antioxidants known
- **Neuroprotection:** PQQ crosses the blood-brain barrier and protects neurons from oxidative damage, excitotoxicity, and mitochondrial dysfunction. It promotes nerve growth factor (NGF) synthesis
- **Essential nutrient debate:** While not yet classified as a vitamin, PQQ depletion in animal models causes growth impairment, immune dysfunction, and reproductive failure — characteristics of a vitamin deficiency
- **Ultra-low doses:** Unlike most supplements dosed in hundreds of milligrams, PQQ is effective at just 10-20mg/day, reflecting its catalytic (not stoichiometric) mechanism
- **Synergy with CoQ10:** PQQ makes new mitochondria; CoQ10 makes them work efficiently. The combination is considered the gold standard for mitochondrial support
