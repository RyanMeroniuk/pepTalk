# PQQ (Pyrroloquinoline Quinone)
## Natural Food Sources

PQQ is found in trace amounts in many plant and animal foods. Dietary intake is estimated at 0.1-1.0 mcg per day from food alone — far below supplemental doses.

- **Highest concentrations (mcg per 100g):**
  - **Natto (fermented soybeans)** — 61 mcg per 100g (the richest known dietary source)
  - **Green tea (brewed)** — 29 mcg per 100g
  - **Parsley** — 34 mcg per 100g
  - **Green peppers** — 28 mcg per 100g
  - **Kiwi fruit** — 27 mcg per 100g
- **Moderate concentrations:**
  - **Papaya** — 27 mcg per 100g
  - **Spinach** — 22 mcg per 100g
  - **Tofu** — 24 mcg per 100g
  - **Fava beans** — 17 mcg per 100g
  - **Celery** — 16 mcg per 100g
  - **Cocoa powder** — 8 mcg per 100g
- **Lower concentrations:**
  - **Carrot** — 17 mcg per 100g
  - **Potato** — 10 mcg per 100g
  - **Cabbage** — 10 mcg per 100g
  - **Sweet potato** — 9 mcg per 100g
  - **Banana** — 6 mcg per 100g
  - **Tomato** — 9 mcg per 100g
- **Animal sources:**
  - **Human breast milk** — 14-18 mcg per 100ml (significantly higher than cow's milk, suggesting importance in infant development)
  - **Egg yolk** — 4 mcg per 100g
  - **Cow's milk** — 3 mcg per 100ml

**Important context:** PQQ in food is measured in micrograms (mcg). Supplement doses are 10-20 milligrams (mg) — roughly 10,000-20,000x higher than daily dietary intake. Food sources are nutritionally valuable but cannot substitute for supplementation at therapeutic doses.
