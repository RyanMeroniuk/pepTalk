# Probiotics
## Dosing Protocols

- **General gut health maintenance** — 10-20 billion CFU daily of a multi-strain product containing Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium species. Take consistently at the same time each day. Morning or evening is fine — consistency matters more than specific timing.
- **During antibiotic treatment** — Saccharomyces boulardii 250-500mg (5-10 billion CFU) twice daily, starting on day 1 of antibiotics. Take the probiotic at least 2 hours away from each antibiotic dose. S. boulardii is preferred because antibiotics cannot kill it (it's a yeast, not bacteria). Continue for 2 weeks after completing antibiotics.
- **Post-antibiotic microbiome recovery** — Multi-strain probiotic (20-50 billion CFU) daily for 4-8 weeks after completing antibiotics. Include Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG, Bifidobacterium species, and S. boulardii. Combine with prebiotic fiber to feed recovering beneficial bacteria.
- **IBS management** — Bifidobacterium infantis 35624: 1 billion CFU daily (this is the researched dose — more is not better for this strain). Or Lactobacillus plantarum 299v: 10 billion CFU daily. Trial for minimum 4 weeks before assessing efficacy.
- **Mood and anxiety support** — Lactobacillus helveticus R0052 + Bifidobacterium longum R0175: 3 billion CFU daily (the Cerebiome researched dose). Take consistently for at least 4 weeks; mood benefits typically emerge at 2-4 weeks.
- **Immune support** — Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG: 10 billion CFU daily. Most studied during cold/flu season. Can also add Bacillus coagulans GBI-30, 6086 for spore-based immune support.
- **Ulcerative colitis maintenance** — VSL#3: 1-2 sachets daily (450-900 billion CFU). Must be under physician supervision. Refrigeration required.
- **Traveler's diarrhea prevention** — S. boulardii 250mg daily, starting 5 days before travel and continuing throughout the trip. Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG is an alternative.
- **Vaginal health** — L. rhamnosus GR-1 + L. reuteri RC-14: 1-5 billion CFU daily, taken orally (they migrate to the vaginal tract). Or vaginal capsules containing L. crispatus.
- **Infant/pediatric (under physician supervision)** — L. rhamnosus GG or B. infantis, typically 1-10 billion CFU. Available in drops or powder that can be mixed with breast milk or formula.

**Timing and administration:**

- **On an empty stomach or with a light meal** — Some research suggests better survival on an empty stomach (less acid); other research suggests a light meal buffers acid. Both approaches work. Avoid taking with very hot beverages (kills bacteria).
- **Separate from antibiotics by 2+ hours** — If taking both, space them apart to maximize probiotic survival.
- **Consistency over timing** — Daily, consistent intake is more important than whether you take probiotics morning vs. evening.
- **Duration** — Probiotics generally do not permanently colonize the gut (they are transient). Ongoing supplementation is needed for sustained benefits. After a course for a specific condition, assess whether continued use is warranted.
