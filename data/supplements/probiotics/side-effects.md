# Probiotics
## Side Effects

- **Gas and bloating (initial)** — The most common side effect, especially during the first 1-2 weeks. As probiotics colonize and shift the gut microbiome, increased gas production is normal. Usually resolves as the gut adapts. Start with lower CFU counts and increase gradually if sensitive.
- **Temporary change in stool consistency** — Looser or more frequent stools in the first week are common as the microbiome adjusts. Constipation is also possible with certain strains. Both typically normalize within 1-2 weeks.
- **Mild abdominal discomfort** — Cramping or discomfort during the initial adjustment period. If persistent beyond 2 weeks, the specific strains may not be appropriate for your gut environment.
- **Histamine production** — Certain strains (notably Lactobacillus casei, L. bulgaricus, and some L. reuteri strains) produce histamine. Individuals with histamine intolerance may experience headaches, nasal congestion, skin flushing, or digestive distress. Choose low-histamine strains: Bifidobacterium species, L. rhamnosus, L. plantarum, or L. gasseri.
- **D-lactic acidosis (rare)** — In individuals with short bowel syndrome or certain metabolic conditions, Lactobacillus species that produce D-lactic acid can cause neurological symptoms (brain fog, confusion, fatigue). Rare in the general population.
- **Infection risk in immunocompromised patients** — Extremely rare but documented cases of Lactobacillus bacteremia and Saccharomyces fungemia in severely immunocompromised individuals (transplant patients, those on chemotherapy, critically ill ICU patients). Probiotics should be used with physician supervision in these populations.
- **Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO) concern** — In susceptible individuals, certain probiotic strains may exacerbate SIBO symptoms. If you have diagnosed SIBO, work with a healthcare provider to select appropriate strains. Spore-based probiotics (Bacillus) may be better tolerated.
- **Die-off reaction (Herxheimer-like)** — When probiotics displace pathogenic bacteria, the dying pathogens can release endotoxins, temporarily worsening symptoms. Usually resolves within a few days. Start with low doses to minimize this effect.
- **Headache** — Occasionally reported, potentially related to biogenic amine production by certain strains. Switch strains if persistent.

**Overall safety profile:** Probiotics are remarkably safe for the general, healthy population. The adverse effects listed above are overwhelmingly mild and transient. Serious complications are essentially limited to severely immunocompromised individuals.
