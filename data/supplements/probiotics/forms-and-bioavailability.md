# Probiotics
## Forms and Bioavailability

**Delivery Forms:**

- **Capsules (enteric-coated or delayed-release)** — Most common supplement form. Enteric coating or acid-resistant capsules (DR caps, DRcaps) protect bacteria from stomach acid, significantly improving survival to the intestines. Look for products specifying acid-resistant delivery.
- **Capsules (standard)** — Less protection from stomach acid. Some strains (particularly Lactobacillus and Bacillus) have inherent acid resistance. Take on an empty stomach with water to minimize acid exposure time, or with a small meal that buffers stomach pH.
- **Spore-based (Bacillus strains)** — Bacillus coagulans, B. subtilis, and B. clausii form endospores that are naturally resistant to stomach acid, heat, and antibiotics. No refrigeration required. Excellent shelf stability. They germinate and become active in the alkaline small intestine.
- **Saccharomyces boulardii** — A probiotic yeast (not bacteria) that is inherently resistant to antibiotics, stomach acid, and temperature. Does not require refrigeration. Ideal for use during and after antibiotic courses.
- **Powder (sachets or bulk)** — Can be mixed into water, juice, or food. Useful for children or those who cannot swallow capsules. Exposure to moisture and air reduces viability over time — use quickly after opening.
- **Liquid probiotics** — Includes fermented beverages (kefir, kombucha) and liquid supplement formulations. Generally lower CFU counts than capsules but may have better initial viability.
- **Suppositories and vaginal capsules** — For direct delivery to vaginal or rectal microbiome. Relevant for vaginal health (L. crispatus, L. rhamnosus GR-1) and lower GI conditions.

**Strain-Specific Recommendations:**

- **Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG** — Best studied general gut health strain. Effective for diarrhea prevention, immune support, and pediatric applications. Available as Culturelle brand.
- **Lactobacillus helveticus R0052 + Bifidobacterium longum R0175** — The clinically studied "psychobiotic" combination for mood and anxiety. Available as Cerebiome or Probio'Stick.
- **Saccharomyces boulardii** — The go-to probiotic during and after antibiotic courses. Available as Florastor brand. Take throughout antibiotic treatment and for 2 weeks after completion.
- **Bifidobacterium infantis 35624** — Specifically studied for IBS. Available as Align (US) or Alflorex (EU).
- **Lactobacillus plantarum 299v** — Documented for IBS pain and bloating reduction.
- **Bacillus coagulans GBI-30, 6086** — Well-studied spore-forming probiotic for digestive health and immune support. Shelf-stable.
- **VSL#3** — High-dose multi-strain formulation (450-900 billion CFU) for IBD/ulcerative colitis. Requires refrigeration. Available by prescription in some regions.

**Refrigerated vs. Shelf-Stable:**

- **Refrigerated products** — Generally indicate live, non-spore-forming bacteria that are sensitive to heat and moisture. Higher viability at time of consumption if stored properly. The cold chain must be maintained from manufacturing to your refrigerator.
- **Shelf-stable products** — Either spore-based (Bacillus), yeast-based (S. boulardii), or lyophilized with moisture-barrier packaging. Convenient for travel. Ensure the product guarantees CFU at expiration, not just at manufacture.
- **"Guaranteed at manufacture" vs. "Guaranteed at expiration"** — This distinction matters enormously. A product claiming 50 billion CFU "at time of manufacture" may contain a fraction of that by the time you consume it. Look for products that guarantee CFU through the expiration date.

**CFU (Colony-Forming Units):**

- **General maintenance:** 5-20 billion CFU daily
- **Therapeutic use (IBS, antibiotic recovery):** 20-100 billion CFU daily
- **IBD protocols (VSL#3):** 450-900 billion CFU daily
- **More CFUs are not inherently better** — Strain selection and evidence base matter more than raw CFU count. 10 billion CFU of a clinically proven strain outperforms 100 billion CFU of unstudied strains.
