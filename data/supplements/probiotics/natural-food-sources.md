# Probiotics
## Natural Food Sources

**Fermented Dairy:**

- **Kefir** — One of the most diverse natural probiotic sources, containing 30-50 different microbial strains including Lactobacillus kefiri, L. kefiranofaciens, and various Bifidobacteria. Higher probiotic diversity than yogurt. Both dairy and water kefir (non-dairy) provide live cultures.
- **Yogurt (live culture)** — Contains Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus (starter cultures), with many brands adding additional strains like L. acidophilus, L. rhamnosus, and Bifidobacterium. Must say "live and active cultures" on the label. Avoid heat-treated yogurt products (kills bacteria).
- **Aged cheeses** — Gouda, cheddar, Parmesan, and Swiss cheese contain surviving Lactobacillus strains. The aging process selects for hardy bacteria. Not a primary probiotic source but contributes to diverse microbial exposure.
- **Lassi** — Traditional Indian yogurt drink, rich in Lactobacillus species.

**Fermented Vegetables:**

- **Sauerkraut (raw, unpasteurized)** — Naturally fermented cabbage rich in Lactobacillus plantarum, L. brevis, and Pediococcus. Must be raw/refrigerated — shelf-stable canned sauerkraut is pasteurized and contains no live cultures.
- **Kimchi** — Korean fermented cabbage and vegetables containing L. plantarum, L. brevis, Leuconostoc mesenteroides, and others. Also provides prebiotic fiber from garlic and onion.
- **Pickles (naturally fermented)** — Cucumbers fermented in salt brine (not vinegar) contain Lactobacillus species. Must be from the refrigerated section, not shelf-stable vinegar pickles.
- **Miso** — Fermented soybean paste containing Aspergillus oryzae and Lactobacillus species. Heat destroys cultures, so add miso to dishes after cooking or use in cold preparations.

**Fermented Soy:**

- **Tempeh** — Fermented soybeans bound by Rhizopus oligosporus mycelium. Also contains Lactobacillus species. Provides probiotics, complete protein, and enhanced mineral bioavailability.
- **Natto** — Japanese fermented soybeans containing Bacillus subtilis. Also a rich source of vitamin K2 (MK-7) and nattokinase (fibrinolytic enzyme).

**Fermented Beverages:**

- **Kombucha** — Fermented tea containing a SCOBY (symbiotic culture of bacteria and yeast). Contains Gluconacetobacter, Lactobacillus, and various yeasts. Choose low-sugar varieties. Probiotic content varies widely by brand and batch.
- **Kvass** — Traditional Eastern European fermented beet or bread beverage. Contains Lactobacillus species.
- **Apple cider vinegar (with "the mother")** — Contains Acetobacter and small amounts of Lactobacillus. Not a primary probiotic source but contributes to microbial diversity.

**Key principle:** Eat a diversity of fermented foods rather than relying on a single source. A Stanford study found that a high-fermented-food diet (6+ servings daily) increased microbiome diversity and reduced inflammatory markers more than a high-fiber diet.
