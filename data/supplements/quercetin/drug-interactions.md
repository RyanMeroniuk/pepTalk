# Quercetin
## Drug Interactions

Quercetin's most clinically meaningful interactions stem from its inhibition of cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes and the drug-efflux transporter P-glycoprotein. By slowing the metabolism or efflux of many medications, quercetin can raise their blood levels.

### Significant / Caution Interactions (CYP & Transporter Inhibition)
- **CYP3A4-metabolized drugs (broad class):** Quercetin inhibits CYP3A4. This can increase blood levels of many drugs metabolized by this enzyme — including certain statins (simvastatin, atorvastatin), calcium channel blockers, and benzodiazepines. **Use caution and monitor**; this is a quercetin enzyme interaction worth reviewing with a pharmacist.
- **CYP2C9 / CYP2D6 substrates:** Quercetin can inhibit other CYP enzymes as well, affecting drugs such as certain NSAIDs and some antidepressants. **Monitor** for enhanced effects.
- **P-glycoprotein substrates:** By inhibiting this efflux transporter, quercetin can raise levels of P-gp substrate drugs (e.g., digoxin). **Caution and monitoring** advised.

### Immunosuppressants (Caution)
- **Cyclosporine, tacrolimus:** These are CYP3A4 and P-gp substrates with narrow therapeutic windows. Quercetin can raise their levels, increasing toxicity risk. **Monitor drug levels closely; consult the prescribing physician.**

### Anticoagulants / Antiplatelets (Caution — Bleeding)
- **Warfarin and other anticoagulants/blood thinners, antiplatelet agents (aspirin, clopidogrel):** Quercetin may have mild antiplatelet activity and can affect drug metabolism, theoretically increasing bleeding risk. **Use caution, monitor**, and inform your physician — especially around surgery.

### Antibiotics
- **Fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin, etc.):** Quercetin may compete at bacterial DNA gyrase binding sites and theoretically reduce antibiotic effectiveness. **Take separated by several hours** to be safe, or avoid concurrent use during a course of treatment.

### Chemotherapy / Active Cancer (Caution)
- **Interaction:** Quercetin's antioxidant and enzyme-inhibiting properties could theoretically interfere with certain chemotherapy agents (either reducing efficacy or altering drug levels). Effects vary by agent.
- **Recommendation:** Do not take supplemental quercetin during active cancer treatment without explicit oncologist approval.

### Diabetes Medications (Monitor)
- **Drugs:** Metformin, insulin, sulfonylureas. Quercetin may modestly improve insulin sensitivity and lower glucose, creating a small additive hypoglycemic effect. **Monitor** blood glucose when starting.

### Blood Pressure Medications (Monitor)
- **Drugs:** ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers. Quercetin can modestly lower blood pressure; additive effect is possible. **Monitor** blood pressure.

### Grapefruit (Compounding Effect)
- **Grapefruit juice** inhibits the same CYP3A4 and P-gp pathways as quercetin. Combining both with CYP3A4-metabolized drugs can compound the interaction. **Separate or avoid** the combination on such medications.

### Contraindications / Avoid
- **Active chemotherapy** without oncologist clearance — contraindicated pending medical approval
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — avoid concentrated supplemental doses (insufficient safety data)
- **Severe kidney disease** — caution with high doses

### Blood Test / Surgical Considerations
- **Inform healthcare providers** that you take quercetin, especially before surgery (antiplatelet potential) and when on narrow-therapeutic-index drugs (cyclosporine, tacrolimus, digoxin, warfarin)
