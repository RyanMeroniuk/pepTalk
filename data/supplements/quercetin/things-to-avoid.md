# Quercetin
## Things to Avoid

### Ignoring the Bioavailability Problem
- **Taking standard quercetin powder and expecting high blood levels** — standard quercetin dihydrate is poorly absorbed. If consistent blood levels matter, use phytosome (Quercefit) or isoquercetin, and always take with a fat-containing meal.
- **Dosing on an empty stomach** — reduces absorption and increases GI upset

### Self-Administering Senolytic Protocols
- **Attempting the dasatinib + quercetin (D+Q) senolytic protocol on your own** — dasatinib is a prescription chemotherapy drug with serious risks. The D+Q protocol is investigational and conducted under medical supervision in clinical trials. Quercetin alone as a human senolytic lacks robust evidence.
- **Treating preliminary senolytic data as established** — most senolytic evidence is from animal models and small pilot trials; do not overstate it

### Drug-Interaction Blind Spots (Critical)
- **Combining high-dose quercetin with CYP-metabolized medications without checking** — quercetin inhibits several CYP enzymes and P-glycoprotein, which can raise levels of drugs like certain statins, calcium channel blockers, immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus), and others. Review your medication list with a pharmacist or physician.
- **Stacking quercetin with grapefruit** while on CYP3A4-metabolized drugs — additive enzyme inhibition

### Medical Cautions
- **Anticoagulants/blood thinners** — quercetin may have mild antiplatelet effects; use caution and inform your physician
- **Kidney disease** — high-dose quercetin has raised theoretical kidney concerns; consult a clinician
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — avoid concentrated supplemental doses due to insufficient safety data (dietary amounts are fine)
- **Active cancer / chemotherapy** — discuss with an oncologist before supplementing, given antioxidant and enzyme-inhibiting properties

### Quality and Dosing Mistakes
- **Buying unstandardized, low-quality products** — choose third-party-tested quercetin; for enhanced forms, look for branded ingredients (Quercefit) with published data
- **Chronic mega-dosing** — long-term safety above ~1000 mg/day is not well established; do not assume "more is better"
- **Expecting fast antihistamine action** — quercetin's allergy benefits build over weeks; it is not a rapid-acting antihistamine like cetirizine
