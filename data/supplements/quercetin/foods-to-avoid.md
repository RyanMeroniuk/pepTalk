# Quercetin
## Foods to Avoid

### Foods/Substances That Work Against Quercetin's Goals
- **Excessive alcohol** — increases oxidative stress and inflammation, working against quercetin's antioxidant and anti-inflammatory purpose; also burdens the liver where quercetin and many co-administered drugs are metabolized
- **Highly processed, pro-inflammatory foods** — fried foods, refined sugar, and ultra-processed snacks drive the inflammation and oxidative load that quercetin is meant to counter
- **High-fructose foods and sugary beverages** — promote oxidative stress and metabolic dysfunction, offsetting quercetin's cardiovascular and metabolic benefits

### Grapefruit and Quercetin's Enzyme Overlap (Important)
- **Grapefruit and grapefruit juice** inhibit CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein — the same enzymes/transporters quercetin inhibits. Combining the two with CYP-metabolized medications can compound the effect and unpredictably raise drug levels. If you take such medications, be cautious with both.

### Taking Quercetin Without Fat
- **Fat-free, empty-stomach dosing** — not a "food to avoid" so much as a timing mistake: it reduces absorption of an already poorly absorbed compound. Pair quercetin with a fat-containing meal.

### No Specific Food Blocks Quercetin
- **There are no common foods that destroy or fully block quercetin** in a meaningful way
- The bigger issue is overall dietary pattern: a diet high in pro-inflammatory, pro-oxidant foods undermines the very benefits quercetin provides

### Practical Guidance
- **Build a polyphenol-rich, whole-food diet** around quercetin rather than expecting the supplement to offset a poor diet
- **Be especially careful pairing quercetin with grapefruit if you take prescription medications** metabolized by CYP3A4 (see drug-interactions)
