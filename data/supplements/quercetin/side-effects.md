# Quercetin
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild)
- **GI discomfort** — nausea, stomach upset, or bloating, more likely at higher doses or on an empty stomach. Taking with food usually resolves this.
- **Headache** — occasionally reported, typically mild and transient
- **Tingling in extremities** — uncommon, reported with higher doses

### Uncommon
- **Mild flushing or warmth** — infrequent
- **Sleep disturbance** — rare; some users prefer earlier dosing

### Kidney Caution (High-Dose / Long-Term)
- **Theoretical kidney concern** — very high doses of quercetin have raised caution about potential kidney effects in some preclinical reports. People with kidney disease should be cautious and consult a clinician.
- **Long-term high-dose safety data is limited** — most human trials run weeks to a few months; chronic multi-year safety at high doses is not well characterized

### Drug-Interaction Risk (Important)
- **CYP enzyme and P-glycoprotein inhibition** — quercetin can raise blood levels of various CYP-metabolized drugs (see drug-interactions). This is the most clinically meaningful safety consideration, not direct toxicity.
- **Additive effects with blood thinners** — quercetin may have mild antiplatelet activity; caution with anticoagulants

### Special Populations
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data for supplemental (concentrated) doses; dietary amounts from food are fine, but supplementation should be avoided or discussed with an OB/GYN
- **Active cancer or cancer treatment** — quercetin's enzyme-inhibiting and antioxidant properties could theoretically interact with chemotherapy; consult an oncologist
- **Kidney disease** — use caution given high-dose kidney concerns

### Senolytic Protocol Note
- Adverse effects reported in D+Q senolytic trials are largely attributable to dasatinib (the prescription chemotherapy drug), not quercetin. Standalone quercetin at typical supplement doses has a benign side-effect profile in short-term trials.

### Overall Safety Profile
- **Well tolerated at typical doses (up to ~1000 mg/day) in short-to-medium-term trials**
- **Main practical concern is drug interactions**, not direct toxicity
- **Dietary quercetin** (from onions, apples, etc.) is consumed safely by populations worldwide
