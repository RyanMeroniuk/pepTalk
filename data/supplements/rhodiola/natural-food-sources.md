# Rhodiola Rosea
## Natural Food Sources

### Rhodiola Is a Medicinal Root, Not a Dietary Food
- Rhodiola rosea is harvested for its **root and rhizome**, which contain the active rosavins and salidroside. There is no common Western food that provides these compounds — they are essentially specific to the Rhodiola genus (and most reliably to Rhodiola rosea)
- Meaningful intake therefore comes from the prepared root (tea, tincture) or a standardized supplement, not from everyday foods

### Traditional Root Preparations
- **Rhodiola tea/decoction:** In traditional use across Siberia, Scandinavia, and Central Asia, the dried root is brewed as a tea or decoction to combat fatigue, cold, and altitude stress. Salidroside is water-soluble and extracts reasonably well; the rosavin complex extracts less completely in water alone
- **Tinctures:** Alcohol-based root tinctures extract a broader spectrum of actives and are a traditional and modern delivery form
- **Dried root chewed or powdered:** Historically used directly for stamina, though potency is variable

### Wild and Cultivated Sourcing
- Rhodiola grows in cold, high-altitude, rocky regions. Wild populations have been over-harvested in some areas, raising sustainability concerns — cultivated and responsibly sourced material is preferable
- Because wild material and species vary, **standardization (3% rosavins / 1% salidroside) and species verification (Rhodiola rosea)** matter more than for typical food-derived nutrients

### No Dietary Equivalent
- Other adaptogens (ashwagandha, ginseng, eleuthero) are different plants with different active compounds and do not substitute for rhodiola's specific rosavin/salidroside profile
- Other Rhodiola species (e.g., Rhodiola crenulata) contain salidroside but little rosavin and are sometimes used as cheaper, less-equivalent substitutes

### Practical Takeaway
- For the anti-fatigue and stress benefits documented in research, a **standardized Rhodiola rosea extract** is the reliable source. Traditional teas/tinctures provide variable amounts; there is no food-source alternative
