# Rhodiola Rosea
## Dosing Protocols

### Standard Anti-Fatigue / Stress Dose
- **Dose:** 200-400 mg/day of extract standardized to 3% rosavins / 1% salidroside (most clinical fatigue studies used ~200-400 mg/day, often as a single morning dose)
- **Timing:** Morning, on an empty stomach (rhodiola is activating — avoid late-day dosing)
- **Onset:** Anti-fatigue effects are often noticeable within a few days to ~2 weeks — faster than slow-build nootropics like bacopa
- **Notes:** Start at the low end (100-200 mg) to assess tolerance, as some people find rhodiola overstimulating

### Mood Support
- **Dose:** ~340-680 mg/day of standardized extract in some clinical depression/anxiety research
- **Timing:** Morning and early afternoon if split; avoid evening dosing
- **Notes:** Effects on mood may take 1-4 weeks. Milder than pharmaceutical antidepressants

### Acute Performance (Pre-Task / Pre-Exercise)
- **Dose:** ~200-300 mg taken 30-60 minutes before a demanding mental task or workout
- **Use case:** Single-dose acute use to reduce perceived fatigue/exertion and support focus or endurance
- **Notes:** This acute use is one of the few adaptogen applications where a single dose can help the same day

### Timing Considerations
- **Take early:** Because rhodiola is stimulating, dose it in the morning or early afternoon. Evening dosing commonly causes insomnia or vivid/disrupted sleep
- **Empty stomach:** Often recommended for best effect, though it can be taken with food if it causes stomach upset

### Cycling
- Many practitioners suggest cycling rhodiola (e.g., 5 days on / 2 days off, or several weeks on / 1-2 weeks off) rather than indefinite continuous use, partly because some users report diminished effect or restlessness with prolonged daily use
- Cycling is a common-practice recommendation rather than a firmly established requirement

### Finding Your Dose
- Rhodiola is dose-sensitive: too little may do nothing, too much can cause jitteriness, irritability, or overstimulation. The effective window is relatively narrow for many people. Titrate from a low dose upward
