# Rhodiola Rosea
## Drug Interactions

### Moderate Interactions (Use with Caution)
- **Antidepressants and serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs):** Rhodiola modulates serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine and may have mild MAO-inhibitory activity. Combined with serotonergic or MAOI medications, there is a theoretical risk of additive serotonergic effects (and, with MAOIs, a more serious concern). Use caution, watch for symptoms of excess serotonin (agitation, sweating, rapid heart rate, tremor), and coordinate with the prescribing physician.
- **Stimulant medications (e.g., ADHD stimulants):** Rhodiola is itself stimulating. Combined with prescription stimulants, additive overstimulation, elevated heart rate, anxiety, and insomnia are possible. Use caution and monitor.
- **Diabetes medications (metformin, sulfonylureas, insulin):** Rhodiola may modestly affect blood glucose. Monitor blood sugar when combined with diabetes medications; dose adjustments may be needed.

### Mild Interactions (Monitor)
- **Antihypertensive and cardiovascular drugs:** Rhodiola's stimulating effects could modestly influence blood pressure or heart rate. Monitor if on cardiovascular medications.
- **CYP-metabolized drugs (e.g., some via CYP2C9/CYP3A4):** Preclinical data suggest rhodiola may influence certain cytochrome P450 enzymes and transporters (such as P-glycoprotein), theoretically altering levels of co-administered drugs. Clinical significance is uncertain; monitor with narrow-therapeutic-index medications.
- **Anticoagulants/antiplatelets:** Theoretical additive effects via possible mild platelet effects; monitor for unusual bruising/bleeding and disclose use.

### Timing Considerations
- Take rhodiola in the morning, hours apart from any evening sedatives or sleep medications, so its stimulating effect does not work against them.
- If using a stimulant medication, separating it in timing from rhodiola does not eliminate additive stimulation — the concern is pharmacodynamic, so caution and dose moderation matter more than spacing.

### Conditions Requiring Caution
- **Bipolar disorder:** Stimulating, antidepressant-like effects could theoretically trigger agitation or mania. Use only under medical guidance.
- **Anxiety/panic disorders:** May be overstimulating; introduce cautiously at low doses.
- **Autoimmune conditions:** Theoretical immune modulation warrants caution; discuss with a physician.
- **Pregnancy:** Insufficient safety data — avoid unless directed by a clinician.
- **Breastfeeding:** Insufficient safety data — avoid unless directed by a clinician.
- **Liver disease:** Given possible CYP interactions, discuss with a hepatologist before use in significant liver disease.

### General Guidance
- Rhodiola's most important interaction cautions involve serotonergic/MAOI antidepressants and stimulant medications. Always disclose rhodiola use to your healthcare providers, especially before surgery or when on psychiatric, cardiovascular, or diabetes medications.
