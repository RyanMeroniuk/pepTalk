# Rhodiola Rosea
## Foods to Avoid

### Excessive Caffeine and Stimulants
- Rhodiola is itself a stimulating adaptogen. Stacking it with large amounts of coffee, energy drinks, or other stimulants can cause overstimulation, jitteriness, anxiety, elevated heart rate, and insomnia. Moderate caffeine is usually fine; large amounts compound rhodiola's activating effect
- If combining with caffeine, consider l-theanine to smooth the stimulation, and keep both early in the day

### Alcohol (Especially Evening)
- Alcohol disrupts sleep and works against the recovery and stress-resilience rhodiola supports. It also adds depressant load that counteracts rhodiola's energizing, mood-supporting profile. Heavy or regular drinking undermines the benefits
- Note: tinctures are alcohol-based, but the alcohol amount per dose is small; this caution is about beverage alcohol

### Tyramine-Rich Foods (Theoretical MAOI Caution)
- Because rhodiola may have mild monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitory activity, there is a theoretical concern about combining it with very high-tyramine foods (aged cheeses, cured/fermented meats, large amounts of fermented soy) — the same foods cautioned with MAOI drugs. The clinical significance at supplement doses is likely low, but those who are sensitive or also taking serotonergic/MAOI medications should be mindful (see drug-interactions)

### High-Sugar, Ultra-Processed Diets
- Blood-sugar swings from high-glycemic, ultra-processed foods worsen fatigue and mood instability — the very things rhodiola is used to address. A diet driving energy crashes works against rhodiola's anti-fatigue purpose

### Late-Day Stimulating Foods/Drinks Generally
- Since rhodiola is activating, combining it with other late-day stimulants (caffeine, sugar) increases the risk of disrupted sleep

### Foods That Are Irrelevant (Don't Overthink It)
- There are no common foods that meaningfully block rhodiola absorption. Fiber, acidic foods, and a normal diet do not impair it
- The main practical concerns are stimulant stacking and timing, not nutrient blocking
