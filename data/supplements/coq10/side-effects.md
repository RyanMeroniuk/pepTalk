# CoQ10 (Coenzyme Q10)
## Side Effects

- **Gastrointestinal upset:** The most common side effect. Nausea, diarrhea, stomach discomfort, and appetite loss occur in a small percentage of users, typically at doses above 200mg. Taking with food usually resolves this
- **Insomnia / energy boost:** Some users report difficulty sleeping if CoQ10 is taken in the evening. This is due to enhanced mitochondrial ATP production. Solution: take doses earlier in the day (morning or lunch)
- **Headache:** Occasionally reported, usually mild and transient during the first week of supplementation
- **Skin rash:** Rare allergic reaction. Discontinue if rash develops
- **Mild dizziness:** Uncommon, may relate to blood pressure lowering effects in sensitive individuals
- **Heartburn:** Occasionally reported, mitigated by taking with a full meal rather than on an empty stomach
- **Blood sugar lowering:** CoQ10 may reduce blood sugar levels. Diabetics on insulin or sulfonylureas should monitor glucose more frequently when starting CoQ10
- **Blood pressure lowering:** Additive hypotensive effect when combined with antihypertensive medications. Monitor BP, especially during the first few weeks
- **Fatigue (paradoxical):** Rare and usually transient. Some individuals experience initial fatigue as mitochondrial function recalibrates. Typically resolves within 1-2 weeks
- **Drug interaction concern (warfarin):** CoQ10 has a chemical structure similar to vitamin K and may reduce the anticoagulant effect of warfarin. INR monitoring is essential (see drug interactions)
- **Overall safety profile:** Excellent. CoQ10 is generally recognized as safe (GRAS). Serious adverse events are extremely rare even at high doses (up to 1200mg/day in trials)
