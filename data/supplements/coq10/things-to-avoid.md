# CoQ10 (Coenzyme Q10)
## Things to Avoid

- **Skipping CoQ10 while on statins:** This is the single most important point. Statins (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, pravastatin, lovastatin) block HMG-CoA reductase in the mevalonate pathway — the same pathway that produces CoQ10. Statin users are actively depleting CoQ10 and should supplement without exception
- **Using powder-filled capsules without fat:** Dry powder CoQ10 in hard capsules has very poor bioavailability (2-5%). If using this form, always take with a fat-rich meal. Better yet, switch to oil-based softgels or ubiquinol
- **Taking large single doses:** Doses above 200mg in a single serving have diminishing absorption returns. Split doses of 100-200mg across meals for optimal blood levels
- **Evening dosing (if sleep-sensitive):** CoQ10 enhances cellular energy production, which can cause alertness and difficulty sleeping in some individuals. Take morning or midday
- **Storing improperly:** CoQ10 (especially ubiquinol) is sensitive to heat, light, and oxygen. Store in a cool, dark place. Refrigeration extends shelf life for ubiquinol
- **Expecting overnight results:** CoQ10 takes 2-4 weeks to reach steady-state blood levels and 4-12 weeks for clinical benefits. Do not discontinue prematurely
- **Ignoring the ubiquinol/ubiquinone distinction after 40:** The body's ability to reduce ubiquinone to the active ubiquinol form declines with age. After 40, ubiquinol is the preferred form
- **Chronic high-dose NSAID use:** Regular NSAID use (ibuprofen, naproxen) may increase mitochondrial oxidative stress. CoQ10 supplementation becomes more important, not less
- **Excessive endurance training without support:** Intense prolonged exercise increases mitochondrial ROS production. Athletes should consider CoQ10 supplementation for mitochondrial protection
- **Smoking:** Cigarette smoke massively depletes antioxidants including CoQ10 and increases mitochondrial damage. Quitting is essential; CoQ10 supplementation helps but does not fully compensate
