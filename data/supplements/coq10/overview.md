# CoQ10 (Coenzyme Q10)
## Overview

Coenzyme Q10 is a naturally occurring compound found in every cell of the body, concentrated in the mitochondria where it plays an irreplaceable role in cellular energy production. It functions as an electron carrier in the electron transport chain, shuttling electrons between Complex I/II and Complex III to drive ATP synthesis — the universal energy currency of life.

- **Why it matters:** Without adequate CoQ10, cells cannot efficiently produce ATP. This directly impacts high-energy-demand organs: the heart, brain, kidneys, and liver
- **Age-related decline:** The body's CoQ10 production peaks around age 20-25 and progressively declines. By age 40, levels may be 30% lower; by age 80, levels can drop by 50-65%
- **Statin depletion (critical):** Statin medications (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, etc.) block the mevalonate pathway, which is the same pathway the body uses to synthesize CoQ10. Anyone on a statin is actively depleting their CoQ10 and should strongly consider supplementation
- **Dual role:** CoQ10 serves two functions simultaneously — energy production (ATP synthesis) and antioxidant protection (neutralizing free radicals in mitochondrial membranes)
- **Heart health cornerstone:** The heart beats ~100,000 times per day and has the highest mitochondrial density of any organ. It is exquisitely sensitive to CoQ10 status
- **Universal presence:** Found in all cell membranes, CoQ10 protects lipids, proteins, and DNA from oxidative damage
- **Research depth:** One of the most extensively studied supplements, with robust clinical trial data supporting cardiovascular, neurological, and metabolic benefits
