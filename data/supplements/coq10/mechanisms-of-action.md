# CoQ10 (Coenzyme Q10)
## Mechanisms of Action

- **Electron transport chain (ETC) shuttle:** CoQ10 accepts electrons from Complex I (NADH dehydrogenase) and Complex II (succinate dehydrogenase) and transfers them to Complex III (cytochrome bc1). This electron transfer drives proton pumping across the inner mitochondrial membrane, creating the electrochemical gradient that ATP synthase uses to produce ATP
- **ATP production:** Approximately 95% of the body's energy (ATP) is generated through oxidative phosphorylation, which cannot function without CoQ10 as the mobile electron carrier
- **Lipid-soluble antioxidant:** In its reduced form (ubiquinol), CoQ10 donates electrons to neutralize reactive oxygen species (ROS) — particularly lipid peroxyl radicals in cell membranes and mitochondrial membranes. It is one of the only endogenously produced lipid-soluble antioxidants
- **Vitamin E regeneration:** Ubiquinol regenerates alpha-tocopherol (vitamin E) from its oxidized form, extending vitamin E's antioxidant capacity in cell membranes
- **Mitochondrial membrane stabilization:** CoQ10 integrates into the inner mitochondrial membrane, maintaining membrane fluidity and integrity, which is essential for efficient electron transport
- **Anti-inflammatory effects:** CoQ10 reduces NF-kB activation and downstream inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-6, CRP). This is partly mediated through reduced mitochondrial ROS leakage
- **Endothelial function:** Improves nitric oxide (NO) bioavailability by reducing superoxide-mediated NO destruction, supporting vasodilation and blood pressure regulation
- **Gene expression modulation:** CoQ10 influences the expression of genes involved in cell signaling, metabolism, and inflammation — over 100 genes may be affected by CoQ10 status
- **Redox cycling:** CoQ10 continuously cycles between its oxidized form (ubiquinone) and reduced form (ubiquinol). In the ETC, it is oxidized; as an antioxidant, it is reduced. Enzymes like NADH and NADPH reductases regenerate ubiquinol from ubiquinone
