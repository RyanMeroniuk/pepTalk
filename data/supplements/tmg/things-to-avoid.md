# TMG (Trimethylglycine)
## Things to Avoid

### Product Confusion to Avoid
- **Don't buy betaine HCl when you want TMG** — betaine HCl is a stomach-acid (digestive) supplement; for methylation, homocysteine, and ergogenic benefits you want **betaine anhydrous / TMG**. They contain related molecules but serve different purposes and are dosed differently.
- **Don't assume "betaine" alone is clear** — check whether the label says betaine anhydrous (TMG) or betaine HCl

### Dosing Mistakes
- **Ignoring the lipid effect at high doses** — high-dose TMG (>3 g/day, especially ~6 g) can raise LDL and triglycerides in some people. Monitor lipids if using higher doses long-term; use the lower effective dose for your goal.
- **Over-methylation from stacking too many methyl donors** — combining TMG with high-dose methylfolate, SAMe, and choline can cause over-methylation symptoms (anxiety, irritability, insomnia) in sensitive people. Start low and balance.
- **Megadosing for ergogenic effect** — 2.5 g/day is the studied ergogenic dose; more does not clearly add benefit and increases GI and lipid concerns
- **Skipping B-vitamins for homocysteine goals** — TMG addresses only the betaine remethylation pathway; pair with folate, B12, and B6 for comprehensive homocysteine management

### Population-Specific Cautions
- **Existing high LDL/triglycerides** — the lipid-raising effect at higher doses is directly relevant; favor lower doses and monitor
- **Trimethylaminuria ("fish odor syndrome")** — avoid; impaired metabolism of trimethylamine can cause body odor
- **Kidney disease** — discuss with a physician (TMG is osmotically active and renally relevant)
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data for high-dose use; consult a healthcare provider

### Behaviors That Undermine Benefits
- **Heavy alcohol use** — depletes methyl donors and stresses the liver, opposing TMG's methylation and liver benefits
- **Neglecting lipid monitoring at high doses** — the main avoidable risk
- **Expecting creatine-level muscle gains** — TMG's ergogenic effect is modest; it complements rather than replaces creatine

### Medical Note
- **This is educational information, not medical advice.** People with dyslipidemia, kidney disease, trimethylaminuria, or who are pregnant/breastfeeding should consult a healthcare provider, and anyone on higher doses should monitor lipids.
