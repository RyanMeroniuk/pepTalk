# TMG (Trimethylglycine)
## Drug Interactions

### Moderate Interactions (Use with Caution / Monitor)
- **Lipid-affecting medications / statins** — high-dose TMG (>3 g/day) can raise LDL and triglycerides in some people. If you take statins or other lipid-lowering drugs, this is a relevant counter-effect; monitor your lipid panel and inform your physician.
- **Methotrexate and other antifolates** — these drugs interfere with folate-dependent methylation. TMG provides a folate-independent remethylation route, which is generally complementary, but anyone on methotrexate should discuss supplementation with their physician, as the methylation context is medically managed.
- **Vitamin B-vitamin therapies (prescribed folate/B12)** — not a harmful interaction; rather, these work together with TMG for homocysteine management. Coordinate with your physician for comprehensive homocysteine treatment.

### Mild Interactions (Monitor)
- **Anticoagulants / blood thinners (warfarin)** — no well-established direct interaction; as with any supplement, inform your physician and monitor
- **Other methyl-donor supplements (SAMe, methylfolate, high-dose choline)** — not a drug interaction per se, but cumulative methylation load can cause over-methylation symptoms in sensitive individuals; balance and start low

### Timing-Sensitive Notes
- **Take with meals** — improves tolerance; no strict separation from most medications is required
- **Levothyroxine / thyroid meds** — no documented direct interaction with TMG; standard practice is to take thyroid medication on an empty stomach away from other supplements, so keep TMG to a different time of day
- **Separate from medications with narrow therapeutic windows when uncertain**, though no strong TMG-specific absorption conflict is documented

### Positive / Neutral Interactions
- **NAD+ precursors (NMN, NR)** — strongly complementary; TMG replenishes methyl groups consumed by NAD+ metabolism (see peptide-synergies)
- **B-vitamins (folate, B12, B6)** — complementary for homocysteine management
- **Creatine** — complementary; creatine spares methyl groups, TMG replenishes them

### Population Cautions
- **Dyslipidemia** — the lipid-raising effect at higher doses is the key consideration; favor lower doses and monitor
- **Kidney disease** — discuss with a physician (TMG is osmotically active and renally relevant)
- **Trimethylaminuria** — avoid (risk of trimethylamine-related body odor)
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data for high-dose use; consult a healthcare provider
- **Liver disease** — TMG is generally hepatoprotective, but discuss higher-dose use with a hepatologist

### Safety Notes
- **The lipid effect at high doses is the most practically important interaction-adjacent concern** — monitor lipids if taking >3 g/day, especially with lipid medications
- **Always inform healthcare providers** of TMG use, particularly if on lipid-lowering drugs, methotrexate, or managing homocysteine medically
- **This is educational information, not medical advice** — review interactions with a pharmacist or physician
