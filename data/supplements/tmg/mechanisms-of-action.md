# TMG (Trimethylglycine)
## Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Methyl Donation & Homocysteine Remethylation (Primary Mechanism)
- **TMG donates a methyl group to homocysteine**, converting it back to methionine via the enzyme betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase (BHMT)
- This is the **betaine pathway**, a folate-independent route for homocysteine remethylation (the folate/B12 pathway is the other)
- **Lowering homocysteine** reduces a cardiovascular and neurological risk marker
- In the process, TMG becomes dimethylglycine (DMG) after giving up one methyl group

### 2. SAMe Regeneration (Methylation Cycle)
- **Methionine produced from homocysteine is converted to SAMe (S-adenosylmethionine)**, the body's universal methyl donor
- SAMe methylates DNA, neurotransmitters, phospholipids, creatine synthesis, and many other substrates
- **By regenerating methionine, TMG helps keep the SAMe pool full**, supporting all downstream methylation reactions
- This is the basis for TMG's role in supporting methylation-heavy processes

### 3. NAD+ Metabolism & Methyl Group Sparing (Key for NMN/NR Stacks)
- **NAD+ precursors (NMN, NR) are ultimately broken down via nicotinamide, which is methylated** by nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT) to form methyl-nicotinamide (MeNAM) for excretion
- **This methylation consumes SAMe-derived methyl groups**, and high-dose NAD+ precursor supplementation can theoretically deplete methyl groups and raise homocysteine
- **TMG replenishes the methyl pool** by remethylating homocysteine to methionine → SAMe, offsetting the methyl drain from NAD+ metabolism
- This mechanistic link is why TMG is commonly paired with NMN in longevity protocols

### 4. Cellular Osmolyte (Osmoprotection)
- **TMG is an organic osmolyte** — cells accumulate it to maintain volume and protein stability under osmotic and thermal stress
- It stabilizes proteins and cellular structures without disrupting their function (a "compatible solute")
- This osmoregulatory role contributes to cell hydration in muscle, part of its ergogenic effect

### 5. Ergogenic Effects (Power & Strength)
- **Proposed mechanisms include:** enhanced cellular hydration (osmolyte effect supporting cell volume), increased creatine synthesis (methionine → SAMe feeds endogenous creatine production), and improved protein synthesis signaling
- Multiple trials show **improved power output, strength, and body composition** at ~2.5 g/day in resistance-trained subjects, though the precise mechanism is still debated

### 6. Liver Fat Metabolism
- **TMG supports hepatic phospholipid (phosphatidylcholine) synthesis** via the methylation pathway, aiding the export of fat from the liver (VLDL assembly)
- This underlies TMG's role in supporting healthy liver fat handling
