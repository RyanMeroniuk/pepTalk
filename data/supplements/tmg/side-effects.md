# TMG (Trimethylglycine)
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild, Rare)
- **Generally well tolerated** — TMG has a good safety profile at typical doses (1-6 g/day)
- **Mild GI discomfort** — nausea, bloating, or diarrhea, more likely at higher doses (3+ g) or on an empty stomach; taking with food and splitting doses resolves it
- **Body odor (fishy smell)** — rare; at high doses TMG can be converted to trimethylamine (TMA), which has a fishy odor, particularly in people with trimethylaminuria or impaired TMA metabolism

### Uncommon
- **Elevated LDL cholesterol and triglycerides** — high doses (typically >3 g/day, and especially ~6 g/day) have raised LDL and triglycerides in some studies. This is the most notable concern — monitor lipids if using higher doses long-term.
- **Mild stimulation / sleep disturbance** — uncommon; some people report feeling slightly stimulated, possibly via methylation effects on neurotransmitters. Dose earlier in the day if this occurs.

### Population-Specific Cautions
- **Existing dyslipidemia (high LDL/triglycerides)** — the lipid-raising effect at higher doses is relevant; monitor and favor lower doses
- **Kidney disease** — discuss with a physician; TMG is osmotically active and renally relevant
- **Trimethylaminuria ("fish odor syndrome")** — avoid; impaired ability to metabolize trimethylamine
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data for high-dose supplementation; consult a healthcare provider

### Methylation Balance Note
- **Over-methylation symptoms** — in sensitive individuals, aggressive methyl-donor supplementation (TMG plus other methyl donors like methylfolate, SAMe) can cause anxiety, irritability, or insomnia. Balance methyl donors thoughtfully and start low.

### Safety Profile Summary
- **No established toxic dose** at supplemental ranges in healthy people; the main practical limits are GI tolerance and the lipid effect at high doses
- **No liver toxicity** — TMG is generally hepatoprotective
- **Not banned** by WADA or athletic organizations
- **Long history of use** as both a food component (beets, grains) and supplement

### When to Seek Guidance
- If you have **dyslipidemia, kidney disease, trimethylaminuria**, or are **pregnant/breastfeeding**, consult a healthcare provider before higher-dose use
- **Monitor lipids** if taking >3 g/day long-term
