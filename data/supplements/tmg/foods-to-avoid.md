# TMG (Trimethylglycine)
## Foods to Avoid

### There Are No Foods That Block TMG
- **No common food meaningfully impairs TMG absorption** — it is water-soluble and rapidly absorbed
- **Acidic foods, fiber, and fat do not interfere** with TMG uptake

### Lipid Context (Relevant at Higher Doses)
- **High-saturated-fat, high-refined-carb diets** — not a chemical interaction, but because high-dose TMG (>3 g/day) can itself raise LDL and triglycerides in some people, a diet already pushing lipids up compounds the concern. For those using higher doses, a lipid-supportive diet (fiber, unsaturated fats, lower refined carbs) is sensible.

### Alcohol
- **Heavy alcohol use** stresses the liver and depletes methyl donors (alcohol metabolism consumes methylation capacity). While TMG can support the liver, chronic heavy drinking works against the methylation and liver benefits TMG provides.

### Foods That Are Irrelevant (Don't Overthink It)
- **There are no foods that destroy or block TMG** in any meaningful way
- **Caffeine** — no interaction with TMG
- **Refined grains** — contain less betaine than whole grains (milling removes it), so they contribute less dietary TMG, but they don't block supplemental TMG

### Timing With Other Supplements
- **TMG can be taken with virtually anything** — no significant supplement-supplement absorption conflicts
- **Pairs well with B-vitamins, NMN/NR, and creatine** (see peptide-synergies)
- **Balance with other methyl donors** — if also taking methylfolate, SAMe, or high choline, be mindful of cumulative methylation load in sensitive individuals (a balance issue, not an absorption one)

### Bottom Line
- No food chemically blocks TMG. The only diet-related considerations are **supporting lipids at higher doses** and **avoiding heavy alcohol**, which works against TMG's liver and methylation benefits.
