# Citicoline (CDP-Choline)
## Natural Food Sources

### Citicoline Itself Is Not Abundant in Food
- Citicoline (CDP-choline) is an intracellular intermediate, not something consumed in meaningful amounts directly from food. The body makes it endogenously from dietary choline via the Kennedy pathway
- Therefore, dietary support for citicoline's effects comes primarily from **choline-rich foods** that supply the raw material

### Choline-Rich Foods (Building Blocks)
- **Egg yolks** — one of the richest dietary choline sources (~115 mg choline per large egg, mostly in the yolk)
- **Beef liver** — extremely high in choline (~350+ mg per 3 oz)
- **Beef, chicken, and fish** — good sources of choline and protein
- **Soybeans and soy lecithin** — plant source of phosphatidylcholine
- **Cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cauliflower)** — modest choline
- **Wheat germ** — contains choline
- **Shiitake mushrooms** — modest choline
- **Dairy and milk** — modest amounts

### Cytidine / Uridine Sources (the Other Half of Citicoline)
- The cytidine component of citicoline is converted to uridine in humans. Dietary uridine sources include:
  - **Beer and brewer's yeast** (RNA-rich — though alcohol is not recommended; see foods-to-avoid)
  - **Organ meats** (liver, again)
  - **Broccoli and other vegetables** (modest)
- Most circulating uridine in humans actually comes from the liver rather than diet, so dietary uridine is a minor contributor

### Practical Note
- A diet adequate in choline (eggs, meat, fish, or soy lecithin for plant-based eaters) supports the body's ability to make and use citicoline
- Supplementation provides a standardized, concentrated dose of the assembled molecule, bypassing the need to eat large amounts of choline-rich food
- Most people, especially those eating few eggs or organ meats, fall below the Adequate Intake for choline (~425 mg/day women, ~550 mg/day men), making supplemental choline donors like citicoline relevant
