# Citicoline (CDP-Choline)
## Foods That Enhance Absorption

### Citicoline Is Already Highly Bioavailable
- Citicoline absorbs nearly completely whether taken with or without food — it is rapidly broken into choline and cytidine in the gut. No special pairing is required for absorption itself
- The focus here is on foods and nutrients that support its downstream functions (acetylcholine synthesis and membrane health)

### B-Vitamins (Cofactors for Choline Metabolism)
- **Vitamin B5 (pantothenic acid):** Required to form acetyl-CoA, which combines with choline to make acetylcholine. Found in eggs, avocado, meat, whole grains
- **Vitamin B12 and folate:** Support one-carbon (methylation) metabolism that interacts with choline. Found in meat, fish, eggs, leafy greens
- Adequate B-vitamin status helps the choline from citicoline get converted into acetylcholine efficiently

### Omega-3 Fatty Acids (DHA) — Synergistic Membrane Support
- **DHA from fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel)** is a major structural fat in neuronal membranes. Combined with the phosphatidylcholine pathway that citicoline supports, DHA and citicoline work together on membrane integrity
- This is a well-recognized pairing for brain-membrane support

### Healthy Fats (General Membrane Building Blocks)
- Olive oil, avocado, nuts, and other healthy fats supply the fatty acids that, together with choline, form membrane phospholipids
- A meal containing some fat supports the broader membrane-synthesis role of citicoline

### Adequate Protein
- Acetylcholine synthesis and overall neuronal function depend on adequate dietary protein and the amino acids/cofactors it provides

### Practical Pairing
- **Simplest approach:** Take citicoline in the morning with a breakfast containing eggs (extra choline), some healthy fat, and a B-vitamin-rich whole food — this supports both acetylcholine synthesis and membrane function
- No food is required for citicoline to absorb, but a nutrient-dense diet maximizes what the body does with it
