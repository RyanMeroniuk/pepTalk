# Citicoline (CDP-Choline)
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild, Infrequent)
- **Headache:** The most commonly reported effect, often linked to shifting cholinergic tone. Frequently resolves with a lower dose or by ensuring adequate hydration
- **Mild GI upset:** Nausea, stomach discomfort, or loose stools, usually mild and transient. Taking with food helps
- **Insomnia or overstimulation:** Citicoline can be mildly activating; dosing too late in the day may interfere with sleep in sensitive individuals

### Uncommon
- **Jaw tension or muscle tightness:** A classic sign of excess cholinergic tone, more likely when stacking multiple choline sources (e.g., citicoline + alpha-gpc). Lower the total choline load if this occurs
- **Low blood pressure / lightheadedness:** Citicoline may have mild blood-pressure-lowering effects in some people
- **Irritability or restlessness:** Reported occasionally, possibly dose-related

### Cholinergic Excess (Dose-Dependent)
- Symptoms of too much cholinergic stimulation can include headache, nausea, sweating, jaw clenching, increased salivation, and a "wired but tired" feeling
- This is more likely at high doses or when combining several cholinergic supplements. Reduce dose to resolve

### Overall Safety Profile
- **Well tolerated in trials:** Human studies of Cognizin report a favorable safety profile at typical doses (250-500 mg/day), with adverse effects similar to placebo
- **Low toxicity:** No serious adverse effects established at supplement doses in healthy adults
- **Choline byproduct (TMAO) consideration:** Choline-rich supplements can raise trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), a marker studied in relation to cardiovascular risk. Clinical significance at citicoline doses is uncertain, but worth noting for those tracking cardiovascular markers

### When to Be Cautious
- **Bipolar disorder:** Cholinergic/dopaminergic modulation could theoretically affect mood states — use under medical guidance
- **Low blood pressure:** Monitor if you are prone to hypotension
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** Insufficient safety data — avoid unless directed by a clinician
