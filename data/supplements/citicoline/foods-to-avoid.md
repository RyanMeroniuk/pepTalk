# Citicoline (CDP-Choline)
## Foods to Avoid

### Excessive Alcohol
- Alcohol is neurotoxic, lowers BDNF, and impairs the cholinergic and membrane systems that citicoline supports. Chronic heavy drinking undermines cognitive benefits and damages neuronal membranes — directly opposing citicoline's purpose
- Occasional moderate drinking is unlikely to negate all benefits, but regular heavy intake works against supplementation

### High-Sugar, High-Glycemic Diets
- Chronic hyperglycemia and insulin resistance promote neuroinflammation and impair cognitive function, reducing the net benefit of any nootropic
- Refined sugar, sugary drinks, and high-glycemic refined carbs create conditions that work against cognitive support

### Ultra-Processed and Inflammatory Foods
- Ultra-processed foods, trans fats, and excessive omega-6 seed oils promote systemic and neuroinflammation, which counteracts the membrane-protective and cognitive effects of citicoline
- Trans fats in particular incorporate into neuronal membranes and impair their function — directly opposing the phosphatidylcholine support citicoline provides

### Choline-Heavy Combinations (Caution, Not a Food to "Avoid")
- Stacking citicoline with other high-choline foods/supplements (e.g., very high egg intake plus alpha-gpc plus citicoline) can push total choline high enough to cause headache, jaw tension, or GI upset. Be mindful of total cholinergic load rather than avoiding choline foods outright

### TMAO Consideration
- Choline-rich foods (red meat, egg yolks) and choline supplements are metabolized by gut bacteria into TMAO, a marker studied in relation to cardiovascular risk. If you are tracking cardiovascular markers, be aware of total choline intake from food plus supplements
- This is a monitoring consideration, not a directive to avoid choline foods

### Foods That Are Irrelevant (Don't Overthink It)
- There are no common foods that block citicoline absorption — it is highly bioavailable
- Acidic foods, fiber, and normal coffee intake do not meaningfully impair citicoline
