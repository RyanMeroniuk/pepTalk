# Zinc Carnosine
## Natural Food Sources

Zinc carnosine as a specific chelated compound does not occur naturally in food. However, its component parts — zinc and L-carnosine — are found in dietary sources. Note that consuming zinc-rich and carnosine-rich foods together does NOT replicate the chelated zinc carnosine molecule, but supporting overall zinc and carnosine status is beneficial for gut health.

**Zinc-rich foods:**

- **Oysters** — The richest dietary zinc source (74mg per 3oz serving of cooked oysters). A single serving exceeds the daily requirement.
- **Red meat (beef, lamb)** — 4-7mg zinc per 3oz cooked serving. One of the most bioavailable dietary zinc sources due to the absence of phytate inhibitors.
- **Pumpkin seeds** — 2.2mg per oz. One of the best plant-based zinc sources. Also provides magnesium.
- **Crab and lobster** — 3-6mg per 3oz serving.
- **Chicken and turkey (dark meat)** — 2-3mg per 3oz serving. Dark meat has more zinc than white meat.
- **Chickpeas and lentils** — 1-2mg per cup cooked. Contains phytates that reduce zinc absorption; soaking and cooking help.
- **Cashews** — 1.6mg per oz. Among the highest zinc nuts.
- **Eggs** — 1mg per large egg. Moderate zinc source.

**L-Carnosine-rich foods:**

- **Red meat (beef, bison)** — The richest dietary carnosine source (1,500-2,000mg per pound of raw beef). Carnosine is concentrated in skeletal muscle tissue.
- **Poultry (chicken, turkey)** — 500-1,000mg per pound. White meat (breast) contains carnosine; dark meat contains a related compound, anserine.
- **Pork** — Moderate carnosine content.
- **Fish** — Lower carnosine content than red meat and poultry. Some fish contain anserine (a carnosine analog).
- **Note for vegetarians/vegans** — Carnosine is found almost exclusively in animal muscle tissue. Vegetarians and vegans have significantly lower muscle carnosine levels and may benefit more from supplemental zinc carnosine.

**Gut-healing foods (complementary):**

- **Bone broth** — Rich in glutamine, glycine, and collagen that support intestinal mucosal integrity
- **Cabbage juice** — Traditional ulcer remedy containing glutamine and growth factor-like compounds
- **Slippery elm** — Demulcent that coats and soothes the GI lining
- **Aloe vera (inner gel)** — Supports mucosal healing when taken internally
- **Marshmallow root** — Demulcent herb that supports mucosal protection
