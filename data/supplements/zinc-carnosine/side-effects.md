# Zinc Carnosine
## Side Effects

- **Nausea (uncommon)** — Some individuals experience mild nausea when taking zinc carnosine on an empty stomach. Taking with a small amount of food usually resolves this. Paradoxically, the empty stomach dosing is preferred for gut repair, so finding a balance between mucosal contact and tolerability may require experimentation.
- **Metallic taste** — A metallic or copper taste in the mouth is occasionally reported with zinc-containing supplements. This is usually transient and mild.
- **Copper depletion (long-term use)** — The most clinically significant concern with any zinc supplementation. Zinc and copper compete for absorption via the same intestinal transporters (DMT-1 and ZIP4). Long-term zinc carnosine use at full doses (150mg/day, providing ~34mg elemental zinc) can gradually deplete copper stores, leading to copper deficiency anemia, neutropenia, and neurological symptoms. Monitor with serum copper and ceruloplasmin levels every 3-6 months during long-term use. Consider supplementing 1-2mg copper daily.
- **Constipation** — Occasionally reported. Ensure adequate hydration and fiber intake. If persistent, reduce the dose.
- **Headache** — Infrequently reported. Usually mild and transient.
- **Interaction with iron absorption** — Zinc can compete with iron for absorption. If managing iron deficiency, separate zinc carnosine from iron supplements by at least 2 hours.
- **Abdominal discomfort** — Mild stomach discomfort or cramping in some individuals, particularly at initiation. Usually resolves within a few days.

**Overall safety profile:** Zinc carnosine has an excellent safety record, having been used as a prescription medication in Japan since 1994. Clinical trials report minimal adverse events. The primary long-term concern is copper depletion, which is manageable with monitoring and copper co-supplementation.

**Signs of zinc excess:**

- Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea (acute excess)
- Copper deficiency symptoms: fatigue, weakness, frequent infections, neurological tingling (chronic excess)
- Reduced HDL cholesterol
- Impaired immune function (paradoxically, excess zinc suppresses immunity)
