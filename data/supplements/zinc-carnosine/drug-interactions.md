# Zinc Carnosine
## Drug Interactions

- **Antibiotics (tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones)** — Zinc chelates with tetracycline antibiotics (doxycycline, minocycline) and fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin), dramatically reducing their absorption and efficacy. Separate zinc carnosine from these antibiotics by at least 2-4 hours (take zinc carnosine 2 hours before or 4 hours after the antibiotic). This interaction is clinically significant and can lead to treatment failure.
- **Penicillamine (Wilson's disease, rheumatoid arthritis)** — Zinc reduces penicillamine absorption. Separate by at least 2 hours. Inform your prescriber.
- **Proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole, esomeprazole)** — PPIs reduce stomach acid, which may alter zinc carnosine's dissolution and mucosal contact behavior. The clinical significance is unclear, but if using both for ulcer healing, discuss timing with your gastroenterologist. PPIs also reduce zinc absorption long-term.
- **Iron supplements** — Zinc and iron compete for absorption via shared transporters (DMT-1). Separate zinc carnosine from iron supplements by at least 2 hours. Important for individuals managing iron deficiency anemia.
- **Copper supplements** — Zinc inhibits copper absorption. If supplementing both (recommended for long-term zinc carnosine use), take copper at a different time of day — e.g., zinc carnosine in the morning and evening, copper at lunch.
- **Bisphosphonates (alendronate, risedronate)** — Zinc may reduce bisphosphonate absorption. Separate by at least 2 hours. Bisphosphonates are typically taken on an empty stomach first thing in the morning with 30 minutes before food or other supplements.
- **ACE inhibitors (enalapril, lisinopril, captopril)** — Captopril and other ACE inhibitors contain sulfhydryl groups that can chelate zinc, potentially reducing the efficacy of both the medication and zinc carnosine. Separate by 2 hours.
- **Diuretics (thiazides)** — Thiazide diuretics increase urinary zinc excretion. Individuals on long-term thiazide therapy may need higher zinc intake, making zinc carnosine supplementation beneficial but also requiring copper monitoring.
- **NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen, indomethacin)** — This is a beneficial co-administration, not a harmful interaction. Zinc carnosine protects the GI mucosa from NSAID-induced damage. Take zinc carnosine before or with NSAIDs for gastroprotection.
- **Immunosuppressants** — Zinc has immunomodulatory effects. While zinc carnosine acts primarily locally in the GI tract, individuals on systemic immunosuppression should inform their physician.
- **Antacids containing aluminum or magnesium** — These may alter zinc carnosine's local pH environment and dissolution characteristics. Separate by 1-2 hours.

**General guidance:** The most clinically important interactions are with tetracycline antibiotics, fluoroquinolones, and iron. The common solution for most interactions is simple timing separation. Keep a 2-hour minimum window between zinc carnosine and interacting medications.
