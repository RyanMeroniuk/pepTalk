# Zinc Carnosine
## Things to Avoid

- **Confusing zinc carnosine with regular zinc** — Zinc picolinate, zinc citrate, zinc gluconate, and zinc oxide are designed for systemic zinc delivery (immune function, testosterone, enzyme support). Zinc carnosine is specifically designed for GI mucosal repair. They serve different purposes. Do not substitute one for the other if gut healing is the goal.
- **Taking zinc carnosine and L-carnosine separately as a substitute** — Taking zinc picolinate + L-carnosine separately does NOT replicate zinc carnosine's effects. The chelated molecule has unique pharmacokinetics and tissue-targeting properties. The 1:1 molar chelation is specifically engineered for slow GI dissociation and mucosal adherence.
- **Exceeding zinc upper limits without monitoring** — 150mg zinc carnosine provides ~34mg elemental zinc. Combined with dietary zinc and any multivitamin, you may approach or exceed the 40mg/day upper tolerable limit. Track total zinc intake from all sources.
- **Long-term use without copper supplementation** — Chronic zinc supplementation depletes copper over weeks to months. If using zinc carnosine for more than 8 weeks at full dose, add 1-2mg copper daily and monitor serum copper and ceruloplasmin every 3-6 months.
- **Ignoring the underlying cause of gut damage** — Zinc carnosine heals mucosal damage, but if the source of damage continues (chronic NSAID use, ongoing H. pylori infection, food sensitivities, excessive alcohol, chronic stress), healing will be incomplete. Address root causes alongside supplementation.
- **Taking with medications that require specific stomach pH** — Zinc carnosine may influence local gastric pH through mucosal protection. If taking medications that require acidic stomach conditions for activation (certain antifungals, some HIV medications), consult your pharmacist about timing.
- **Skipping meals entirely while on a gut-healing protocol** — While zinc carnosine is best taken between meals for mucosal contact, overall nutrition is essential for gut repair. Ensure adequate protein, vitamins A, D, and C, and overall caloric intake to support tissue regeneration.
- **Chronic stress without management** — Stress increases gut permeability, reduces mucosal blood flow, and suppresses GI repair mechanisms via cortisol. Zinc carnosine cannot overcome chronic stress-induced gut damage alone. Stress management is a required component of gut healing.
