# Glucosamine/Chondroitin
## Drug Interactions

- **Warfarin and anticoagulants** — Chondroitin sulfate has structural similarity to heparin, a potent anticoagulant. While chondroitin at supplemental doses has minimal anticoagulant activity, case reports and pharmacological concern exist for interaction with warfarin. Monitor INR closely when initiating chondroitin in patients on warfarin. Several case reports document elevated INR after starting glucosamine/chondroitin. Inform your prescriber before starting.
- **Diabetes medications (insulin, metformin, sulfonylureas)** — Early concerns that glucosamine (an amino sugar) might impair glucose metabolism have been largely dispelled by clinical trials showing no significant HbA1c or fasting glucose changes. However, theoretical concern remains, and monitoring blood sugar when initiating supplementation is prudent, particularly in poorly controlled diabetes.
- **NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen, celecoxib)** — This is a beneficial combination, not a harmful interaction. Glucosamine/chondroitin may allow gradual NSAID dose reduction over time as structural benefits emerge. Discuss a planned NSAID tapering strategy with your physician.
- **Acetaminophen (Tylenol)** — No known interaction. Can be used concurrently for breakthrough pain while waiting for glucosamine/chondroitin effects to develop. Monitor total daily acetaminophen dose (do not exceed 3g/day, less with any liver concerns).
- **Chemotherapy drugs (doxorubicin, etoposide)** — In vitro data suggests glucosamine may alter the activity of certain chemotherapy agents. Cancer patients should not take glucosamine/chondroitin without oncologist approval.
- **Topoisomerase II inhibitors** — Glucosamine has been shown to affect topoisomerase II activity in cell studies. Clinical significance is uncertain, but caution is warranted in cancer patients on these drugs.
- **Diuretics** — Some formulations of glucosamine sulfate contain significant sodium or potassium (from the stabilizing salt). This is relevant for individuals on potassium-sparing or potassium-depleting diuretics. Check the specific product formulation.
- **Antihypertensive medications** — Sodium-containing glucosamine sulfate formulations could theoretically counteract blood pressure-lowering medications in sodium-sensitive individuals. Choose potassium-stabilized formulations or glucosamine HCl if this is a concern.
- **Tetracycline antibiotics** — Glucosamine may enhance the absorption of tetracycline antibiotics. Clinical significance is uncertain, but be aware of potential increased antibiotic effects.
- **Statin medications** — No significant interaction documented. Can be used concurrently. Both statins and glucosamine/chondroitin may contribute to the cardiovascular risk reduction observed in observational studies.

**Shellfish allergy note:** Glucosamine derived from shellfish shells is generally considered safe for individuals with shellfish allergy (the allergens are in the flesh, not the shell). However, if severe shellfish allergy exists, use synthetic (corn-derived) glucosamine to eliminate any risk. Inform your allergist about glucosamine use.

**Pre-surgical consideration:** Discontinue chondroitin 2 weeks before scheduled surgery due to theoretical anticoagulant effects. Inform your surgical team about all supplements.
