# Glucosamine/Chondroitin
## Foods to Avoid

- **Pro-inflammatory foods** — Foods that drive systemic inflammation counteract the anti-inflammatory and cartilage-protective effects of glucosamine/chondroitin:
  - **Refined seed oils** — Soybean oil, corn oil, cottonseed oil, sunflower oil, and safflower oil are high in omega-6 fatty acids that promote pro-inflammatory eicosanoid production. These oils dominate processed and restaurant foods.
  - **Trans fats** — Partially hydrogenated oils (declining but still present in some processed foods) directly promote inflammation.
  - **Refined sugar and high-fructose corn syrup** — Excess sugar drives advanced glycation end products (AGEs), increases inflammatory cytokines, and promotes insulin resistance, all of which accelerate joint degradation.
  - **Ultra-processed foods** — The combination of refined carbohydrates, seed oils, emulsifiers, and artificial additives in ultra-processed foods promotes systemic inflammation.
- **Excessive alcohol** — Alcohol promotes inflammation, dehydrates tissues (including joint cartilage and synovial fluid), and impairs nutrient absorption. Chronic alcohol use is associated with accelerated joint degeneration.
- **Nightshade vegetables (for sensitive individuals)** — Tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, and potatoes contain solanine and related alkaloids that some individuals report worsen joint pain and inflammation. This is not universal and lacks strong clinical evidence, but some people experience clear symptom improvement upon eliminating nightshades. Worth a 4-week elimination trial if joint pain persists despite supplementation.
- **Excessive red meat and processed meat** — While moderate red meat provides beneficial nutrients, excessive consumption promotes inflammation through arachidonic acid, advanced glycation end products, and Neu5Gc. Processed meats (bacon, sausage, deli meats) contain nitrates and preservatives that add to inflammatory burden.
- **Excessive caffeine** — High caffeine intake may reduce calcium absorption and contribute to bone demineralization. Moderate coffee consumption (2-3 cups/day) is not problematic and may even have anti-inflammatory benefits, but excessive intake should be moderated.
- **High-sodium foods** — Excessive sodium promotes fluid retention and may worsen joint swelling. Particularly relevant if using sodium-stabilized glucosamine sulfate.
- **Foods that trigger individual sensitivities** — Gluten, dairy, or other personal food sensitivities that cause systemic inflammation should be identified and managed. An elimination diet can reveal hidden inflammatory triggers.
