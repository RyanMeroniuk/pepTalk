# Glucosamine/Chondroitin
## Lifestyle Recommendations

- **Move daily — joint loading is essential** — Cartilage is avascular and depends on compressive loading (movement) to receive nutrients via synovial fluid diffusion. Walking, cycling, swimming, and gentle resistance training all drive nutrients into cartilage. Aim for 30+ minutes of movement daily. "Motion is lotion" for joints.
- **Strengthen muscles around affected joints** — Muscle weakness (particularly quadriceps weakness for knee OA) is a primary contributor to joint pain and instability. Targeted strength training reduces joint loading forces by improving muscular shock absorption. Quad strengthening, hip abductor work, and core stability are essential for knee OA.
- **Maintain a healthy body weight** — Excess weight dramatically accelerates joint degeneration. Losing just 10 pounds reduces knee joint loading by approximately 40 pounds per step. Weight management amplifies glucosamine/chondroitin's protective effects more than any other single intervention.
- **Prioritize low-impact exercise during flares** — When joints are acutely inflamed, switch to low-impact activities: swimming, water aerobics, cycling, elliptical, or walking on soft surfaces. Avoid high-impact activities (running, jumping, heavy plyometrics) during flares.
- **Apply cold therapy after activity** — 10-15 minutes of ice or cold pack application after exercise reduces post-activity inflammation and swelling. Compression wraps provide additional support.
- **Eat an anti-inflammatory diet** — Emphasize fatty fish (2-3 servings weekly), olive oil, vegetables, berries, nuts, turmeric, and ginger. Reduce refined sugar, seed oils, and ultra-processed foods. The Mediterranean diet pattern has the strongest evidence for reducing systemic inflammation and supporting joint health.
- **Stay hydrated** — Synovial fluid and cartilage are heavily dependent on water content for their shock-absorbing properties. Adequate hydration (2-3 liters daily) supports joint lubrication.
- **Consider collagen supplementation** — Adding hydrolyzed collagen peptides (10-15g/day) or type II collagen (40mg/day) alongside glucosamine/chondroitin provides a more complete set of cartilage building blocks. Emerging evidence supports oral collagen for joint health.
- **Use proper footwear** — Cushioned, supportive shoes reduce impact forces transmitted to joints. Consider orthotic inserts for biomechanical imbalances. Avoid flat, unsupportive footwear for extended walking or standing.
- **Practice joint-friendly exercise form** — Proper squat depth, knee tracking, and movement mechanics reduce abnormal joint stress. Consider working with a physical therapist or qualified trainer to optimize movement patterns.
- **Get adequate sleep** — Tissue repair, including cartilage maintenance, occurs primarily during sleep. Growth hormone release during deep sleep supports connective tissue repair. Aim for 7-9 hours nightly.
- **Consider physical therapy** — A physical therapist can design a joint-specific exercise program, provide manual therapy, and teach self-management strategies. PT is one of the highest-evidence interventions for OA, synergistic with supplementation.
