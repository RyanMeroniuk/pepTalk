# Turmeric/Curcumin
## Things to Avoid

- **Taking curcumin without a bioavailability enhancer:** Standard curcumin extract has only ~1% oral bioavailability. Taking curcumin capsules without piperine, phospholipid complexation, or another enhancement technology means >99% passes through unabsorbed. Always choose an enhanced form (piperine, Meriva phytosome, liposomal, NovaSOL, BCM-95, or Theracurmin)
- **Taking curcumin on an empty stomach:** Even enhanced forms benefit significantly from dietary fat. Absorption drops meaningfully without fat. Always take with a meal containing fat (eggs, avocado, olive oil, nuts, coconut oil)
- **Using curcumin before surgery:** Curcumin's blood-thinning (antiplatelet, anticoagulant) effects create surgical bleeding risk. **Discontinue curcumin at least 2 weeks before any scheduled surgery.** Inform your surgeon and anesthesiologist that you take curcumin
- **Combining curcumin with blood thinners without medical supervision:** The combination of curcumin with warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel, or DOACs (apixaban, rivareloxaban) increases bleeding risk. If on blood thinners, consult your prescriber before adding curcumin
- **Using piperine-enhanced curcumin with medications:** Piperine inhibits CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein, which metabolize ~50% of all drugs. If you take prescription medications, choose a non-piperine bioavailability enhancer (Meriva, liposomal, NovaSOL) to avoid drug interactions. This is one of the most common and preventable supplement-drug interaction scenarios
- **Taking curcumin with gallstones or bile duct obstruction:** Curcumin stimulates bile production and gallbladder contraction. This can trigger a gallbladder attack in individuals with gallstones (cholelithiasis) or bile duct obstruction. Contraindicated in active gallbladder disease
- **Consuming curcumin simultaneously with iron supplements:** Curcumin chelates iron, reducing absorption. If you take iron supplements or have iron deficiency, separate curcumin and iron by 2+ hours
- **Assuming "more is better" with dosing:** Clinical benefits plateau at 1,000-2,000mg/day of enhanced curcumin. Higher doses increase side effect risk (GI distress, bleeding) without proportional benefit. Ultra-high doses of very-high-absorption formulations can reach pharmaceutical blood levels, increasing liver stress and other risks
- **Using low-quality turmeric products from contaminated sources:** Turmeric from certain regions (particularly some Indian and Bangladeshi sources) has documented lead contamination issues — lead chromate is sometimes added as a color enhancer. Choose products with third-party heavy metal testing. Look for USP, NSF, or ConsumerLab verification
- **Ignoring the staining:** Curcumin permanently stains clothing, countertops, teeth, and skin a vivid yellow-orange. Handle powder and liquid forms carefully. Capsules are cleaner. If taking liquid curcumin, use a straw and rinse your mouth afterward
- **Chronic high-dose use without monitoring:** While curcumin is generally safe, long-term high-dose use of enhanced-bioavailability forms should include periodic liver enzyme monitoring (ALT, AST), especially given emerging case reports of hepatotoxicity with very high blood levels
- **Taking during pregnancy at supplemental doses:** Culinary turmeric (as a spice in cooking) is safe during pregnancy. Supplemental curcumin doses, especially enhanced forms, are not recommended due to potential uterine stimulation and lack of safety data. Avoid supplemental curcumin during pregnancy
