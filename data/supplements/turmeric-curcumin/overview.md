# Turmeric/Curcumin
## Overview

- **What it is:** Curcumin is the primary bioactive compound in turmeric (Curcuma longa), the bright yellow-orange spice that gives curry its color. While turmeric has been used as a spice and medicine for thousands of years, curcumin itself constitutes only 2-5% of turmeric powder by weight. Supplemental curcumin is a concentrated extract standardized to 95% curcuminoids, providing 20-50x more curcumin than culinary turmeric. It is one of the most studied natural compounds in biomedical literature, with over 15,000 published studies.
- **Historical use:** Turmeric has been used in Ayurvedic medicine for over 4,000 years as an anti-inflammatory, digestive aid, wound healer, and "blood purifier." In Traditional Chinese Medicine, Jiang Huang has been used for pain, blood stasis, and liver conditions. It is a foundational spice in South Asian, Middle Eastern, and Southeast Asian cuisines.
- **Why it matters:** Chronic inflammation is the common thread connecting cardiovascular disease, neurodegenerative disease, cancer, metabolic syndrome, autoimmune conditions, and accelerated aging. Curcumin is one of the most potent natural anti-inflammatory compounds known, targeting the NF-kB pathway (the master inflammatory transcription factor) more broadly than most pharmaceutical anti-inflammatories. It simultaneously acts as a powerful antioxidant and modulates hundreds of molecular targets.
- **The bioavailability problem:** Raw curcumin is notoriously poorly absorbed — only about 1% bioavailability due to poor water solubility, rapid hepatic metabolism (glucuronidation and sulfation), and rapid elimination. This is the single most important factor in curcumin supplementation. Without a bioavailability enhancer, most oral curcumin passes through the GI tract unabsorbed. Multiple technologies have been developed to solve this problem (piperine, liposomal, phytosome, nano-emulsion), with absorption increases ranging from 7x to 185x compared to standard curcumin.
- **Scale of evidence:** Over 15,000 published papers and 300+ clinical trials. Despite the massive research base, many early studies used poorly absorbed formulations, leading to inconsistent results. Modern enhanced-bioavailability formulations have produced much more consistent clinical outcomes.
