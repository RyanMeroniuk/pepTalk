# Turmeric/Curcumin
## Side Effects

- **Blood thinning (most clinically significant concern):** Curcumin inhibits platelet aggregation and has anticoagulant properties through COX-2 inhibition and thromboxane reduction. At supplemental doses, this creates a meaningful bleeding risk:
  - Increased bruising, especially at higher doses
  - Prolonged bleeding from cuts
  - Risk of excessive bleeding during surgery — **discontinue curcumin 2 weeks before any scheduled surgery**
  - Potentiates blood-thinning medications (warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel, DOACs) — see drug interactions
  - Potential risk during heavy menstrual periods

- **GI discomfort:** Nausea, diarrhea, bloating, and stomach upset — most common at higher doses (>2,000mg/day) or on an empty stomach. Curcumin stimulates bile secretion, which can cause loose stools in some individuals. Taking with food significantly reduces GI symptoms

- **Gallbladder contraction:** Curcumin stimulates bile production and gallbladder contraction. Contraindicated in individuals with gallstones or bile duct obstruction — could trigger a gallbladder attack or biliary colic

- **Iron absorption interference:** Curcumin chelates iron and may reduce iron absorption when taken simultaneously. Individuals with iron deficiency anemia should separate curcumin and iron supplement/iron-rich meal timing by 2+ hours

- **Kidney stone risk (theoretical):** Turmeric (not curcumin extract) contains oxalates. High-dose turmeric powder may increase urinary oxalate levels, potentially increasing kidney stone risk in susceptible individuals. Standardized curcumin extracts have much lower oxalate content than whole turmeric powder

- **Allergic reactions (rare):** Contact dermatitis (topical use) and, rarely, anaphylaxis have been reported. More common with occupational exposure to turmeric powder. Cross-reactivity possible in people allergic to ginger family plants

- **Headache:** Occasionally reported, usually mild and transient. May be related to initial detoxification effects or blood pressure changes

- **Yellow staining:** Curcumin stains everything it contacts — teeth, skin, clothing, countertops. Capsules avoid this issue; powders and liquids can be messy. Not a health concern but a practical annoyance

- **Liver concerns (rare but emerging):** While curcumin is generally hepatoprotective, a small number of case reports have documented liver injury (elevated liver enzymes, cholestatic hepatitis) with high-dose curcumin supplementation. Risk may be higher with very high-absorption formulations that achieve pharmaceutical-level blood concentrations. Individuals with existing liver disease should use caution

- **Hypoglycemia potential:** Curcumin modestly lowers blood sugar. Combined with diabetes medications or berberine, there is a potential for additive hypoglycemia. Monitor blood sugar when starting curcumin if diabetic

- **Hormone effects:** Curcumin has mild phytoestrogenic and anti-androgenic activity in some in vitro studies. Clinical significance at normal supplemental doses is unclear, but individuals with hormone-sensitive conditions should be aware

- **Pregnancy:** Limited safety data during pregnancy. Culinary turmeric amounts are considered safe, but supplemental doses (especially enhanced-bioavailability forms) are not recommended during pregnancy due to uterine stimulation potential and lack of safety data
