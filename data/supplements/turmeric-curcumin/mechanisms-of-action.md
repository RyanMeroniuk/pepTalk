# Turmeric/Curcumin
## Mechanisms of Action

- **NF-kB inhibition (primary anti-inflammatory mechanism):** Curcumin directly inhibits NF-kB (nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells), the master transcription factor that controls the expression of over 500 pro-inflammatory genes. NF-kB activation drives chronic inflammation in virtually every inflammatory disease. Curcumin blocks NF-kB at multiple points: it inhibits IKK (IkB kinase) which normally activates NF-kB, prevents IkB degradation, and blocks NF-kB nuclear translocation. This is a broader anti-inflammatory mechanism than most pharmaceutical anti-inflammatories, which typically target only one or two downstream mediators
- **COX-2 and LOX inhibition:** Curcumin inhibits cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) and lipoxygenase (LOX) enzymes, reducing production of pro-inflammatory prostaglandins (PGE2) and leukotrienes — the same targets as NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen). Unlike NSAIDs, curcumin does not significantly inhibit COX-1, which protects the gastric mucosa, resulting in a better GI safety profile
- **Cytokine modulation:** Directly reduces production of pro-inflammatory cytokines including TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, IL-6, IL-8, and MCP-1. Also increases anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10. This cytokine rebalancing addresses chronic low-grade inflammation (inflammaging)
- **Antioxidant activity (direct and indirect):** Curcumin directly scavenges reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reactive nitrogen species (RNS). More importantly, it upregulates endogenous antioxidant enzymes through Nrf2 (nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2) activation: superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase, glutathione peroxidase, heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), and glutathione S-transferase. This Nrf2-mediated response provides sustained antioxidant protection beyond the direct scavenging effect
- **AMPK activation:** Curcumin activates AMPK, the cellular energy sensor, promoting fat oxidation, glucose uptake, mitochondrial biogenesis, and autophagy. This overlaps mechanistically with berberine, metformin, and exercise
- **mTOR modulation:** Curcumin inhibits mTOR (mechanistic target of rapamycin) signaling in the context of cancer and excessive cell proliferation, while supporting appropriate mTOR activation for muscle protein synthesis in healthy tissues. This selective modulation contributes to anti-cancer and anti-aging properties
- **Epigenetic modulation:** Curcumin modulates DNA methylation, histone acetylation (inhibits HDACs and HATs), and microRNA expression. These epigenetic effects influence gene expression patterns related to inflammation, tumor suppression, and cellular aging
- **Gut-brain axis modulation:** Curcumin modulates the gut microbiome composition, improves gut barrier integrity (tight junction proteins), and reduces intestinal inflammation. Even poorly absorbed curcumin acts locally in the GI tract, improving gut health. Gut microbiome changes may mediate some of curcumin's systemic effects
- **BDNF upregulation:** Curcumin increases brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) levels, promoting neuroplasticity, neurogenesis, and synaptic function. Low BDNF is implicated in depression, cognitive decline, and neurodegenerative diseases
- **Beta-amyloid and tau modulation:** Curcumin binds to beta-amyloid plaques, inhibits their aggregation, and promotes their clearance. It also inhibits tau protein hyperphosphorylation. These mechanisms are relevant to Alzheimer's disease prevention and treatment, though clinical results in established Alzheimer's have been mixed
- **Angiogenesis modulation:** Curcumin inhibits pathological angiogenesis (new blood vessel formation in tumors) while supporting healthy angiogenesis in wound healing — a context-dependent dual effect mediated by VEGF regulation
