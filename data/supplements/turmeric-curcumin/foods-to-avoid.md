# Turmeric/Curcumin
## Foods to Avoid

- **High-sugar and highly processed foods (counteract anti-inflammatory effects):** Curcumin fights inflammation, but a diet high in processed foods promotes it. Consuming curcumin while maintaining a pro-inflammatory diet reduces net benefit:
  - **Refined sugar** — activates NF-kB (the same pathway curcumin is trying to suppress)
  - **Ultra-processed foods** — contain inflammatory seed oils, additives, and preservatives
  - **Sugary beverages** — spike insulin and promote systemic inflammation

- **Excessive alcohol:**
  - Curcumin is metabolized by the liver. Heavy alcohol consumption burdens hepatic metabolism and can reduce curcumin's effectiveness
  - Both curcumin and alcohol have blood-thinning effects — combining them increases bleeding risk
  - Moderate alcohol (1 drink) with curcumin is generally fine; heavy drinking is counterproductive

- **Iron-rich meals (timing consideration):**
  - Curcumin chelates (binds) iron, reducing iron absorption. If you rely on dietary iron or take iron supplements, do not consume them at the same time as curcumin
  - **Separate by 2+ hours:** Take curcumin at a different meal than your iron-rich foods (red meat, liver, spinach, beans) or iron supplement
  - This is particularly important for people with iron deficiency anemia or those who are pregnant

- **Excessive omega-6 seed oils:**
  - Corn oil, soybean oil, sunflower oil, safflower oil — high in omega-6 fatty acids that promote pro-inflammatory prostaglandin production
  - While curcumin inhibits the enzymes that convert omega-6 to inflammatory prostaglandins, a diet excessively high in omega-6 overwhelms this inhibition
  - Prefer olive oil, avocado oil, or coconut oil when cooking with turmeric

- **Dairy with poor fat content (skim milk):**
  - If making golden milk, use full-fat milk, coconut milk, or add ghee/coconut oil. Skim milk provides no fat for curcumin absorption, making the golden milk preparation largely ineffective
  - Full-fat dairy or plant milks with added fat are preferred

- **Oxalate-rich foods (for kidney stone-prone individuals only):**
  - Turmeric powder (not curcumin extract) contains oxalates. If prone to calcium oxalate kidney stones, be cautious combining whole turmeric with other high-oxalate foods (spinach, rhubarb, beets, chocolate, sweet potatoes) in the same meal
  - Standardized curcumin extracts have minimal oxalate content and are not a concern

- **Foods that compete for glucuronidation pathways:**
  - Some compounds compete with curcumin for the same detoxification enzymes (UDP-glucuronosyltransferase). This is mostly theoretical, but high doses of acetaminophen (Tylenol) use the same glucuronidation pathway and could reduce curcumin metabolism efficiency while curcumin reduces acetaminophen clearance — a bidirectional interaction
