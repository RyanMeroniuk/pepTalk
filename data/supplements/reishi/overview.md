# Reishi
## Overview

Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum) is a woody, bitter polypore mushroom that has been revered in East Asian medicine for over two millennia as the "mushroom of immortality" — a tonic for calm, longevity, immune resilience, and spiritual well-being. Unlike its energizing siblings, reishi is the calming member of the functional-mushroom family, traditionally taken to quiet the mind, support sleep, and modulate stress. Its two main bioactive classes are immune-modulating beta-glucans (polysaccharides) and triterpenoids (ganoderic acids), the latter giving reishi its characteristic bitterness and much of its calming, anti-inflammatory, and liver-supporting activity. Modern research is most developed for its immune-modulating effects — including adjunctive use alongside conventional cancer therapy, where reviews suggest it may improve immune markers and quality of life (though it is not a cancer treatment) — and is more preliminary for its sleep, anti-anxiety, and stress-buffering claims, which rest largely on traditional use and animal data. Reishi sits alongside its siblings lion's mane (cognitive/neurotrophic) and cordyceps (energy/endurance) in the classic mushroom trio; within that trio, reishi is the one taken in the evening for its mildly sedating, restorative character. It is best understood as a calming adaptogen and immune modulator rather than a stimulant or a proven sleep drug.
