# Reishi
## Things to Avoid

- **Treating reishi as a cancer cure:** This is the most important caution. Reishi is studied only as a *supportive adjunct* alongside conventional cancer therapy, with limited-quality evidence for immune markers and quality of life. It is NOT a treatment, must never replace conventional care, and should not be started during cancer treatment without the oncologist's knowledge (it can interact with immune-modulating therapy)
- **Combining with blood thinners without medical awareness:** Reishi triterpenoids may inhibit platelet aggregation, creating a theoretical additive bleeding risk with anticoagulant/antiplatelet drugs. Inform your prescriber and discontinue 1-2 weeks before surgery
- **Use in autoimmune disease without medical guidance:** Beta-glucan immune activation is theoretically undesirable in some autoimmune conditions; consult a physician first
- **Relying on raw, unextracted powder:** Reishi's woody, chitin-bound bioactives are poorly bioavailable without extraction. Insist on a dual extract (hot water + alcohol) so you actually get both beta-glucans and triterpenoids. There are also rare liver-injury case reports tied to raw reishi powder — favor tested extracts
- **Buying mycelium-on-grain without verification:** Cheap "mycelium" products can be largely grain starch with little triterpenoid. Demand products specifying beta-glucan (and ideally triterpene) content, not just "total polysaccharides"
- **Daytime dosing if it makes you drowsy:** Reishi is mildly calming/sedating for some. Avoid high daytime doses before tasks requiring full alertness until you know your response; evening dosing suits it best
- **Stacking many platelet-affecting agents:** Reishi alongside other antiplatelet supplements (e.g., high-dose fish oil, garlic, ginkgo) plus medication can compound bleeding risk — be mindful of the cumulative load
- **Ignoring blood pressure / blood sugar:** Reishi may lower both. If you take antihypertensives or diabetes medication, monitor for additive effects
- **Use during pregnancy/breastfeeding:** Insufficient safety data — avoid unless directed by a physician
- **Expecting dramatic acute effects:** Reishi's calming and immune benefits are subtle and cumulative over weeks, not a same-day knockout sedative
