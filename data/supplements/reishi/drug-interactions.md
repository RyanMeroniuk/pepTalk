# Reishi
## Drug Interactions

- **Anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs (warfarin, heparin, aspirin, clopidogrel) — caution:** Reishi triterpenoids may inhibit platelet aggregation, creating a theoretical additive bleeding risk. Monitor for unusual bruising or bleeding, inform your prescriber, and discontinue 1-2 weeks before surgery. The concern is additive effect rather than absorption interference
- **Immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus, mycophenolate) and immune-modulating biologics — caution/contraindicated context:** Reishi beta-glucans stimulate innate immunity, which can theoretically oppose immunosuppressive therapy. Use with great caution in transplant recipients and those on immunosuppressants; this combination should only occur under physician guidance
- **Cancer therapy (chemotherapy/radiotherapy/immunotherapy) — only under oncologist supervision:** Reishi is studied as a supportive adjunct, but its immune-modulating activity can interact with cancer treatment (including immunotherapy). Do not combine without the oncology team's explicit knowledge and approval
- **Antihypertensive medications — caution/monitor:** Reishi may modestly lower blood pressure, potentially adding to antihypertensive drugs. Monitor blood pressure if combining
- **Diabetes medications (metformin, sulfonylureas, insulin) — caution/monitor:** Reishi may lower blood glucose. Additive hypoglycemic effect is possible; monitor blood sugar and adjust diabetes medication as needed under medical guidance
- **Sedatives, sleep aids, and CNS depressants — monitor / separate timing if drowsy:** Reishi is mildly calming and may add to the sedation of sleep medications, benzodiazepines, or alcohol. Be cautious combining; if excessive drowsiness occurs, separate timing or reduce dose
- **Hepatotoxic drugs — monitor:** Although reishi is traditionally hepatoprotective, rare liver-injury case reports exist (mostly with raw powder). Monitor liver enzymes if combining with drugs that stress the liver, and favor tested extracts
- **General timing note:** Some clinicians prefer to take herbal supplements a few hours apart from time-sensitive medications as general practice; this is precautionary rather than based on a documented reishi absorption interaction
- **Contraindicated contexts (consult a physician before use):**
  - **Active autoimmune disease** — immune stimulation is theoretically undesirable
  - **Active cancer treatment** without oncologist approval
  - **Scheduled surgery** — discontinue 1-2 weeks prior (bleeding risk)
  - **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data
- **Overall profile:** Reishi's most relevant interactions are with blood thinners, immunosuppressants/immunotherapy, antihypertensives, diabetes medications, and CNS depressants. Disclose reishi use to your healthcare providers
