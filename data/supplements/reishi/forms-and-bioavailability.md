# Reishi
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Dual-extract fruiting body — the gold standard:**
  - **What it is:** Extract of the actual fruiting body (the woody conk) using both hot water and alcohol. Reishi is the clearest case for dual extraction because its two key bioactive classes have opposite solubility — beta-glucans are water-soluble, while the triterpenoids (ganoderic acids) are alcohol-soluble. A water-only extract misses much of the triterpenoid content
  - **What to look for:** "Fruiting body" + "dual extract" / "double extract" on the label, standardized to **beta-glucan** content (ideally 20-30%+) and ideally specifying **triterpene** content too. Bitterness is a rough hallmark of triterpenoid content
  - **Best for:** Full-spectrum calming, immune, and anti-inflammatory effects

- **Hot-water extract:**
  - Captures water-soluble beta-glucans (good for immune support) but under-extracts the alcohol-soluble triterpenoids responsible for much of the calming/anti-inflammatory activity. Adequate for immune-focused use, suboptimal for the full effect

- **Mycelium-on-grain (MOG) — be cautious:**
  - As with other medicinal mushrooms, cheap "mycelium" products grown on grain can be largely starch (alpha-glucan) rather than mushroom beta-glucan, and contain little triterpenoid. Insist on products specifying beta-glucan (and ideally triterpene) content, not just "total polysaccharides"

- **Whole dried fruiting body / powder:**
  - Reishi is extremely woody, fibrous, and bitter — the bioactives are locked behind chitin and are poorly bioavailable without extraction. Traditional use involves long simmering (a hot-water extraction). Raw powder is the least bioavailable form

- **Reishi spore (cracked spore powder/oil):**
  - Spores have a hard wall that must be "cracked" to release contents; cracked-spore products are rich in triterpenoids and marketed as potent. Quality and standardization vary widely
  - **Verdict:** Can be potent if genuinely cracked and tested, but a standardized dual-extract fruiting body is a more reliable default

- **Capsules vs powder vs tincture:**
  - **Capsules:** Convenient, avoid the strong bitter taste
  - **Powder:** Cost-effective; very bitter, best masked in coffee/cacao/broth
  - **Dual-extract tincture:** Good full-spectrum extraction; check the extract ratio

- **Key label requirements for a quality product:**
  - Specifies "fruiting body"
  - "Dual extract" (hot water + alcohol)
  - Lists beta-glucan percentage (and ideally triterpene content)
  - Third-party tested; does not list grain as a filler
