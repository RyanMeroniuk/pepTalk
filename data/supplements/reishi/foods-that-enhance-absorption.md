# Reishi
## Foods That Enhance Absorption

- **Hot water (extraction of beta-glucans):** Reishi's water-soluble beta-glucans and polysaccharides are released by hot water. Adding reishi extract to hot tea, coffee, or broth aids dissolution and mirrors the traditional decoction. A long simmer of dried reishi is itself a hot-water extraction
- **A source of fat or alcohol context for triterpenoids:** The triterpenoids (ganoderic acids) are fat- and alcohol-soluble. Taking reishi with a meal containing dietary fat may aid absorption of these compounds:
  - Olive oil, avocado, nuts, MCT or coconut oil in a reishi coffee/cacao
  - This is also why dual extraction (which uses alcohol) captures triterpenoids that a water-only tea misses
- **Cacao / coffee:** Popular pairings that mask reishi's bitterness while the hot liquid aids beta-glucan extraction; the fat in milk/cream also supports triterpenoid uptake
- **Vitamin C-rich foods:** Vitamin C may help protect beta-glucans from degradation and supports the immune function beta-glucans enhance — citrus, bell peppers, kiwi, berries
- **Ginger, jujube, honey (traditional adjuncts):** Often simmered with reishi in traditional decoctions, primarily to soften the bitterness and as complementary tonics rather than proven absorption enhancers
- **Other medicinal mushrooms (the mushroom trio):** Combining reishi with cordyceps and lion's mane creates a functional-mushroom stack where beta-glucans from different species engage different immune receptors. This does not increase reishi absorption per se but broadens the overall protocol — calm/immune (reishi) + energy (cordyceps) + cognition (lion's mane)
- **Calming co-supplements (functional pairing):** Taken in the evening, reishi pairs well with L-theanine, magnesium, and glycine for sleep/relaxation — a functional synergy rather than an absorption effect
