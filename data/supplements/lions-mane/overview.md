# Lion's Mane
## Overview

- **What it is:** Lion's Mane (Hericium erinaceus) is an edible and medicinal mushroom native to North America, Europe, and Asia. It is distinctive in appearance — white, globe-shaped with long, cascading spines resembling a lion's mane or a white waterfall. Unlike most nootropics that work through neurotransmitter modulation, Lion's Mane is unique in that it stimulates the production of Nerve Growth Factor (NGF) and Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF), actually promoting the growth, repair, and myelination of neurons.
- **Historical use:** Used for centuries in traditional Chinese and Japanese medicine for digestive health, general vitality, and cognitive support. Buddhist monks reportedly consumed Lion's Mane tea to enhance concentration during meditation. It has been a prized culinary mushroom in East Asian cuisine, where it is valued for its lobster-like flavor and texture.
- **Why it matters:** Lion's Mane occupies a unique position among nootropics because it does not merely modulate existing neurotransmitter levels — it promotes actual neurogenesis and nerve repair. This makes it relevant for cognitive enhancement, neuroprotection, nerve injury recovery, and potentially neurodegenerative disease prevention. The NGF and BDNF stimulation has implications for the entire nervous system, not just the brain.
- **Critical quality issue — fruiting body vs. mycelium:** This is the single most important consideration when purchasing Lion's Mane. Products made from mycelium grown on grain (mycelium-on-grain or MOG) contain very little of the active compounds (hericenones and erinacines) and are largely composed of grain starch (often 50-70% alpha-glucans from the grain substrate, not beta-glucans from the mushroom). Only fruiting body extract or properly extracted mycelium contains meaningful amounts of the neurotrophic compounds. This distinction makes or breaks the supplement's effectiveness.
- **Growing research interest:** Clinical trials in mild cognitive impairment, depression, anxiety, nerve repair, and gut health are expanding. The neurotrophic mechanism makes Lion's Mane one of the most scientifically interesting supplements in the nootropic space.
