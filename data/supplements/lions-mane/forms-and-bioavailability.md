# Lion's Mane
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Fruiting body extract — the critical choice:**
  - **What it is:** Extract made from the actual mushroom (the visible structure that grows above the substrate). Contains hericenones, beta-glucans, and other bioactive polysaccharides
  - **Why it matters:** Hericenones are ONLY found in the fruiting body. These are the primary compounds that stimulate NGF production. A fruiting body extract is the only reliable way to get these compounds
  - **What to look for:** "Fruiting body" explicitly stated on the label. Hot water extraction or dual extraction (hot water + alcohol) to break down chitin cell walls and release bioactives. Standardized to beta-glucan content (minimum 20-30% beta-glucans). Some premium products also test for hericenones/erinacines specifically
  - **Best for:** Cognitive enhancement, neuroprotection, general supplementation

- **Mycelium extract (properly extracted):**
  - **What it is:** Extract from the root-like network (mycelium) of the fungus, grown in liquid fermentation (not on grain)
  - **Key compound:** Erinacines, particularly erinacine A, are found in the mycelium and are potent NGF inducers. Erinacines may cross the blood-brain barrier more effectively than hericenones
  - **The catch:** Only properly extracted mycelium from liquid fermentation contains meaningful erinacines. This is expensive to produce and rare in consumer products

- **Mycelium-on-grain (MOG) — AVOID:**
  - **The problem:** The vast majority of cheap Lion's Mane products are mycelium grown on grain (usually rice or oats). The mycelium cannot be separated from the grain, so the entire substrate is dried and powdered. The result is a product that is 50-70% grain starch with very little actual mushroom content
  - **How to identify:** Label says "mycelium" or "myceliated grain" or "mycelial biomass" without specifying fruiting body. May list "rice" or "oats" as "other ingredients." Claims of "full spectrum" or "whole lifecycle" are often used to disguise grain-heavy products
  - **Alpha-glucans vs beta-glucans:** Grain starch produces alpha-glucans, which have no medicinal value. True mushroom beta-glucans (1,3/1,6-glucans) are the bioactive compounds. Many MOG products test high in "polysaccharides" but it is grain starch, not mushroom beta-glucans. Insist on products that specify beta-glucan content, not just total polysaccharides

- **Dual extract (hot water + alcohol):**
  - **Best method:** Hot water extraction pulls out water-soluble beta-glucans and polysaccharides. Alcohol extraction pulls out fat-soluble hericenones and terpenoids. Dual extraction provides the fullest spectrum of bioactives
  - **Look for:** Products that specify "dual extraction" or "double extraction"

- **Whole dried mushroom powder:**
  - Simply dried and ground fruiting body without extraction. The bioactives are locked inside chitin cell walls, which humans cannot digest. Bioavailability is significantly lower than extracted products. Suitable for culinary use but not optimal for therapeutic supplementation

- **Capsules vs powder vs tincture:**
  - **Capsules:** Convenient, standardized dosing, no taste issues
  - **Powder:** Versatile (mix into coffee, smoothies, food), cost-effective per dose
  - **Tincture (liquid extract):** Fast absorption, easy to dose, but check if it is a dual extract and what the concentration ratio is (e.g., 1:1, 4:1, 8:1)

- **Key label requirements for a quality product:**
  - Specifies "fruiting body"
  - Lists beta-glucan percentage (minimum 20%, ideally 30%+)
  - Uses hot water or dual extraction
  - Third-party tested
  - Does NOT list grain as an ingredient or filler
