# Lion's Mane
## Drug Interactions

- **Anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs (warfarin, heparin, aspirin, clopidogrel):** Lion's Mane may have mild antiplatelet and anticoagulant properties based on in vitro and animal studies. Theoretical increased bleeding risk when combined with blood-thinning medications. Clinical significance at supplement doses is not well-established, but inform your prescriber and monitor for unusual bruising or bleeding
- **Diabetes medications (metformin, sulfonylureas, insulin):** Lion's Mane may lower blood glucose through AMPK activation and improved insulin sensitivity. Additive hypoglycemic effect when combined with diabetes medications. Monitor blood sugar more frequently when starting Lion's Mane. Dose adjustments to diabetes medications may be needed
- **Immunomodulatory drugs (immunosuppressants, biologics):** Lion's Mane's beta-glucans stimulate innate immune function. This could theoretically counteract immunosuppressive medications (cyclosporine, tacrolimus, mycophenolate) or complicate treatment with immune-modulating biologics (TNF inhibitors, IL inhibitors). Use with caution in transplant recipients and those on immunosuppressive therapy
- **Antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs):** Lion's Mane has mood-enhancing effects through BDNF upregulation and anti-inflammatory mechanisms. The combination with antidepressants is likely safe and potentially beneficial (complementary mechanisms), but monitor for any unexpected changes in mood or serotonergic symptoms. No strong evidence of adverse interaction
- **Anticonvulsants/antiepileptics:** Theoretical interaction based on Lion's Mane's neuroactive properties. No clinical evidence of problems, but individuals on seizure medications should introduce Lion's Mane cautiously and monitor seizure frequency
- **Chemotherapy drugs:** Lion's Mane has shown anticancer properties in vitro and may modulate immune function relevant to cancer treatment. The interaction with chemotherapy is complex and unstudied in humans. Do not use alongside cancer treatment without oncologist approval
- **Cholinergic medications (donepezil/Aricept, rivastigmine):** Lion's Mane may enhance cholinergic function through NGF stimulation and acetylcholinesterase inhibition. Theoretical additive cholinergic effect. Could be beneficial but may also increase cholinergic side effects (GI upset, bradycardia). Use under physician supervision in Alzheimer's patients already on cholinesterase inhibitors
- **Allergy medications (antihistamines):** Lion's Mane may increase NGF, which stimulates mast cells and histamine release. This could theoretically reduce the effectiveness of antihistamines, though the clinical significance is likely minimal

- **Overall drug interaction profile:** Lion's Mane has a relatively mild drug interaction profile compared to many herbal supplements. Most interactions are theoretical or based on mechanism rather than clinical case reports. The strongest cautions are for anticoagulants, diabetes medications, and immunosuppressants. Always disclose Lion's Mane use to your healthcare providers
