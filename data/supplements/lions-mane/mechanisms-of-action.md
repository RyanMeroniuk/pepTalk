# Lion's Mane
## Mechanisms of Action

- **Nerve Growth Factor (NGF) stimulation:** Hericenones (from fruiting body) and erinacines (from mycelium) cross the blood-brain barrier and stimulate the synthesis of NGF in the brain. NGF is essential for the growth, maintenance, and survival of cholinergic neurons in the basal forebrain — the same neurons that degenerate in Alzheimer's disease. Erinacine A is the most potent NGF inducer identified from Lion's Mane
- **Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) upregulation:** Lion's Mane increases BDNF expression, which supports synaptic plasticity, long-term potentiation (the cellular basis of learning and memory), neurogenesis in the hippocampus, and survival of existing neurons. BDNF is often called "Miracle-Gro for the brain"
- **Myelination support:** NGF stimulated by Lion's Mane promotes the growth and function of oligodendrocytes, the cells that produce myelin sheaths around nerve axons. Enhanced myelination improves nerve signal conduction speed and may support recovery from demyelinating conditions. This is relevant for both central and peripheral nervous system repair
- **Neurogenesis:** Through NGF and BDNF upregulation, Lion's Mane promotes the differentiation of neural stem/progenitor cells into new neurons, particularly in the hippocampus (the brain's memory center). This is one of very few supplements with evidence for promoting actual new neuron formation
- **Anti-inflammatory (neuroinflammation):** Reduces microglial activation and pro-inflammatory cytokine production (TNF-alpha, IL-6, IL-1beta) in the CNS. Chronic neuroinflammation is implicated in depression, cognitive decline, and neurodegenerative diseases. Lion's Mane may help resolve neuroinflammation through these pathways
- **Beta-glucan immune modulation:** Polysaccharides (beta-1,3/1,6-glucans) from the fruiting body activate innate immune cells — macrophages, NK cells, and dendritic cells — through dectin-1 and complement receptor 3 (CR3) pathways. This provides immunomodulatory, not immunostimulant, effects
- **Gut-brain axis:** Lion's Mane has prebiotic properties that support beneficial gut bacteria. It also contains compounds that protect gastric mucosa and may promote repair of damaged gut lining. Given the gut-brain connection via the vagus nerve, gut health improvements may contribute to cognitive and mood benefits
- **Antioxidant activity:** Lion's Mane exhibits free radical scavenging and reduces oxidative stress markers. Protects neurons from oxidative damage, which is a major contributor to neurodegeneration
- **Amyloid beta reduction:** In animal models, Lion's Mane extracts reduced amyloid beta plaque accumulation and the neurotoxicity of amyloid beta peptides — relevant to Alzheimer's disease pathology
