# Lion's Mane
## Side Effects

- **GI discomfort:** The most commonly reported side effect. Mild nausea, bloating, stomach discomfort, or diarrhea, especially at higher doses or when starting supplementation. Usually transient and resolves within a few days. Taking with food minimizes GI issues
- **Skin itching or rash:** Some users report itching or mild skin rash, potentially related to increased NGF levels. NGF stimulates mast cells, which release histamine. This is usually mild and may resolve with continued use or dose reduction. Antihistamines can manage symptoms if needed
- **Headache:** Occasionally reported, possibly related to increased neural activity or changes in blood flow. Usually mild and transient
- **Allergic reactions:** Rare but possible. Individuals with mushroom allergies should avoid Lion's Mane. Cross-reactivity with other Hericium species is expected. Discontinue if you experience hives, throat swelling, or difficulty breathing
- **Dizziness or lightheadedness:** Infrequently reported. May be related to blood pressure changes or individual sensitivity. Reduce dose if persistent
- **Increased dreaming or vivid dreams:** Reported anecdotally by some users, possibly related to enhanced neural activity and neuroplasticity during sleep. Not harmful but can be surprising
- **Potential concern for hormone-sensitive conditions:** Some in vitro research suggests Lion's Mane may have aromatase inhibitor activity and may influence DHT (dihydrotestosterone) levels. The clinical significance in humans at supplement doses is unclear, but individuals with hormone-sensitive conditions should be aware
- **Blood sugar reduction:** Lion's Mane may lower blood glucose. This is beneficial for most people but could cause hypoglycemia in those on diabetes medications. Monitor blood sugar if combining
- **Blood clotting:** Lion's Mane may have mild antiplatelet properties. Theoretical concern when combining with anticoagulant or antiplatelet medications. Discontinue before surgery as a precaution
- **Overall safety profile:** Lion's Mane has an excellent safety record both as a traditional food (centuries of consumption in Asia) and as a supplement in clinical trials. Serious adverse effects are extremely rare. It is generally considered one of the safest medicinal mushrooms
