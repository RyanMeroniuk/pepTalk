# Apigenin
## Drug Interactions

- **Benzodiazepines (diazepam, alprazolam, lorazepam, clonazepam)** — Apigenin binds to the same GABA-A benzodiazepine receptor sites. Additive sedation is possible. If taking prescribed benzodiazepines, discuss apigenin with your physician before combining. Dose reduction of either compound may be necessary.
- **Z-drugs (zolpidem/Ambien, zaleplon/Sonata, eszopiclone/Lunesta)** — These also act on GABA-A receptors. Combining with apigenin may intensify sedation, impair coordination, and increase risk of complex sleep behaviors (sleepwalking, sleep-driving). Use extreme caution.
- **Barbiturates** — Additive CNS depression. Do not combine without physician supervision.
- **Anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs (warfarin, heparin, clopidogrel, aspirin)** — Apigenin has demonstrated antiplatelet activity in vitro and may potentiate the effects of blood thinners. Monitor INR more closely if taking warfarin alongside regular apigenin supplementation. Inform your prescriber.
- **CYP1A2 substrates (caffeine, theophylline, clozapine, tizanidine)** — Apigenin is a CYP1A2 inhibitor, which may slow the metabolism of drugs cleared through this enzyme. This could increase blood levels and effects of these medications. Particularly relevant for theophylline (narrow therapeutic index) and clozapine.
- **CYP2C9 substrates (warfarin, phenytoin, NSAIDs)** — Apigenin may inhibit CYP2C9 at supplemental doses, potentially affecting metabolism of these drugs. The warfarin interaction is particularly concerning given apigenin's independent antiplatelet effects.
- **Hormone-sensitive medications (tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors, oral contraceptives)** — Apigenin has context-dependent estrogenic and anti-estrogenic activity. It may theoretically interfere with estrogen-modulating therapies. Women on tamoxifen, letrozole, or other hormonal cancer therapies should consult their oncologist.
- **Iron supplements** — Apigenin chelates iron and reduces its absorption. Separate administration by at least 2 hours. Important for individuals managing iron deficiency.
- **Sedating antihistamines (diphenhydramine/Benadryl, doxylamine, hydroxyzine)** — Additive sedation when combined with apigenin's GABAergic effects. Avoid combining for sleep unless aware of individual tolerance.
- **Antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclics)** — Theoretical interaction through shared effects on serotonin and GABA systems. Generally considered low risk at standard apigenin doses (50mg), but inform your prescriber.
- **Immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus)** — Apigenin has immunomodulatory effects and may theoretically interfere with immunosuppressive therapy. Transplant patients or those on immunosuppressants should avoid supplemental apigenin without physician approval.

**General precaution:** Discontinue apigenin supplementation at least 7-14 days before scheduled surgery due to its antiplatelet effects. Inform your anesthesiologist and surgeon about all supplements.
