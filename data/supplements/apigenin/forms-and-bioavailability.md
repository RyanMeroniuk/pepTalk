# Apigenin
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Chamomile extract (standardized to apigenin)** — The most common supplemental source. Chamomile naturally contains 1-2% apigenin by dry weight. Standardized extracts concentrate this to higher percentages. This delivers apigenin in its natural plant matrix alongside other bioactive chamomile compounds (bisabolol, chamazulene, luteolin).
- **Pure apigenin capsules/powder** — Isolated apigenin, typically synthetic or extracted. Available in 50mg capsules (the dose popularized by Andrew Huberman). Allows precise dosing independent of chamomile matrix. The 50mg dose is the most common for sleep support.
- **Chamomile tea** — A traditional delivery method. One cup of chamomile tea contains approximately 3-10mg of apigenin depending on brewing strength, steeping time, and chamomile quality. Multiple cups needed to approach supplemental doses. However, the ritual of tea preparation itself contributes to the relaxation effect.
- **Apigenin-rich food extracts** — Some supplements derive apigenin from parsley, celery seed, or citrus flavonoid complexes. These provide apigenin alongside other beneficial flavonoids.
- **Liposomal apigenin** — Encapsulated in lipid vesicles to improve bioavailability. A newer formulation that may address apigenin's naturally low water solubility.

**Bioavailability challenges and solutions:**

- **Poor water solubility** — Apigenin is hydrophobic, limiting its dissolution in the GI tract. This contributes to relatively low oral bioavailability (estimated at 20-30% for crystalline apigenin).
- **Extensive first-pass metabolism** — Apigenin undergoes significant Phase II metabolism (glucuronidation and sulfation) in the intestine and liver, reducing the amount of free apigenin reaching systemic circulation.
- **Take with fat** — Fat-containing foods or oils significantly improve apigenin absorption by promoting bile secretion and micelle formation. Take with a fat-containing evening snack, fish oil, or olive oil.
- **Piperine (black pepper extract)** — May improve apigenin bioavailability by inhibiting glucuronidation enzymes, though this specific combination has limited direct evidence.
- **Food matrix effect** — Apigenin consumed within the chamomile tea matrix (with other flavonoids and plant compounds) may have different absorption kinetics than isolated apigenin. The clinical evidence for chamomile's effects is based on the whole extract.
- **Micronized or nano-formulations** — Newer preparations that reduce particle size to improve dissolution and absorption. Check product specifications.

**Practical recommendation:** 50mg pure apigenin capsule taken with a small fat-containing snack, or 2-3 cups of strong chamomile tea in the evening. For NAD+ preservation goals, consistent daily dosing is more important than achieving high peak levels.
