# Apigenin
## Things to Avoid

- **Ragweed and Asteraceae family allergies** — Chamomile belongs to the Asteraceae/Compositae plant family, which includes ragweed, chrysanthemums, marigolds, and daisies. Individuals with known allergies to these plants may cross-react with chamomile-derived apigenin. If you have ragweed allergy, choose synthetic/non-chamomile-derived apigenin or start with a very small test dose and monitor for reactions.
- **Combining with sedative prescription drugs without physician awareness** — Apigenin enhances GABAergic signaling. While mild on its own, combining with benzodiazepines (diazepam, alprazolam, lorazepam), Z-drugs (zolpidem, zaleplon), barbiturates, or high-dose gabapentin could produce additive sedation. Inform your prescriber if taking apigenin alongside these medications.
- **High doses in women with estrogen-sensitive conditions** — Apigenin has demonstrated both estrogenic and anti-estrogenic activity in cell studies depending on concentration and receptor context. Women with ER-positive breast cancer, endometriosis, or uterine fibroids should discuss apigenin supplementation with their physician. Standard food intake (parsley, chamomile tea) is unlikely to be problematic; supplemental doses warrant conversation.
- **Taking apigenin with iron supplements simultaneously** — Flavonoid-iron chelation reduces iron bioavailability. Separate apigenin from iron supplements or iron-rich meals by 2+ hours. Important for those managing iron deficiency anemia.
- **Using apigenin as a sole cancer treatment** — While preclinical anti-cancer data is extensive and promising, apigenin is not a proven clinical cancer treatment. It should never replace evidence-based oncology care. It may be discussed with an oncologist as a potential adjunctive agent.
- **Assuming chamomile tea provides supplemental doses** — A cup of chamomile tea provides approximately 3-10mg of apigenin. The supplemental dose for sleep (50mg) requires either concentrated extract capsules or many cups of very strong tea. Be realistic about what tea vs. supplements deliver.
- **Blue light exposure after taking apigenin for sleep** — Apigenin opens the "relaxation gate," but bright screens and overhead lighting can override its effects. Maintain dim, warm lighting after taking your evening apigenin dose.
- **Driving or operating machinery before knowing your response** — While apigenin is mildly sedating at 50mg, individual responses vary. Test your personal reaction at home before relying on it in situations where alertness is critical.
