# Apigenin
## Natural Food Sources

- **Chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla)** — The richest common source. Dried chamomile flowers contain 0.8-1.2% apigenin by weight. Chamomile tea is the traditional way to consume apigenin, providing approximately 3-10mg per cup depending on brewing strength and time. Steep covered for 10-15 minutes to maximize extraction.
- **Fresh parsley** — One of the highest food sources at approximately 215-300mg apigenin per 100g of fresh herb. A tablespoon of fresh parsley provides roughly 10-15mg. Both flat-leaf (Italian) and curly varieties are rich sources.
- **Dried parsley** — Concentrated form, approximately 4,500mg per 100g dried. Even small amounts sprinkled on food contribute meaningful apigenin.
- **Celery** — Contains approximately 19-24mg apigenin per 100g. Both stalks and leaves contribute, with leaves having higher concentrations. Celery seed and celery seed extract are even more concentrated.
- **Celery seed** — Highly concentrated source of apigenin and related flavonoids. Used in traditional medicine and as a spice.
- **Artichokes** — Moderate apigenin content, approximately 7-12mg per 100g. Also rich in other beneficial flavonoids and prebiotic fiber (inulin).
- **Oregano** — Contains significant apigenin, approximately 3-7mg per 100g fresh herb. Dried oregano concentrates the content.
- **Thyme** — Contains apigenin alongside its primary flavonoids. Fresh and dried thyme both contribute.
- **Basil** — Moderate apigenin content, particularly in fresh sweet basil.
- **Cilantro (coriander leaves)** — Contains apigenin and related flavones.
- **Peppermint** — Contains apigenin in lower amounts. Peppermint tea provides small amounts alongside menthol.
- **Rutabagas/Swedes** — One of the higher vegetable sources, approximately 3-4mg per 100g.
- **Olives and olive oil** — Extra virgin olive oil contains small amounts of apigenin alongside its primary polyphenols (oleocanthal, hydroxytyrosol).
- **Citrus fruits** — Oranges, grapefruits, and lemons contain apigenin in the peel and pith. Citrus flavonoid supplements may include apigenin.
- **Red wine** — Contains small amounts of apigenin from grape skins, though alcohol consumption for apigenin is not recommended.
- **Wheat germ** — Contains measurable apigenin and related flavonoids.

**Maximizing dietary apigenin:**

- Use fresh parsley generously as a garnish and ingredient (not just decoration)
- Drink 2-3 cups of chamomile tea in the evening
- Include celery regularly in salads, soups, and juicing
- Cook with fresh herbs (oregano, thyme, basil) liberally
- Choose extra virgin olive oil as your primary cooking fat
