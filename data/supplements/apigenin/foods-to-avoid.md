# Apigenin
## Foods to Avoid

- **Caffeine-containing foods and beverages (when using for sleep)** — Coffee, caffeinated tea, dark chocolate, and energy drinks counteract apigenin's calming GABAergic effects. If using apigenin for sleep, avoid caffeine within 8-10 hours of bedtime. This is not a pharmacological interaction but a functional conflict — caffeine promotes alertness while apigenin promotes relaxation.
- **Alcohol** — Alcohol potentiates GABAergic activity, and combining with apigenin's GABA-A modulation could increase sedation unpredictably. More importantly, alcohol disrupts sleep architecture (suppresses REM, fragments sleep) regardless of apigenin use. If using apigenin for sleep optimization, alcohol is counterproductive.
- **High-iron meals taken simultaneously** — Apigenin, like many flavonoids, chelates iron (binds to iron ions), reducing iron absorption. If you rely on iron-rich meals (red meat, liver, fortified cereals, spinach) or take iron supplements, separate them from apigenin by at least 2 hours. This is particularly important for individuals with iron deficiency or anemia.
- **High-sugar and processed foods before bed** — Blood glucose spikes trigger cortisol and adrenaline responses that oppose the calming effects of apigenin. A cookie, ice cream, or sugary snack before bed can undermine the relaxation that apigenin provides.
- **Large heavy meals close to bedtime** — While a small fat-containing snack aids apigenin absorption, a large meal raises core body temperature (thermogenic effect of digestion), promotes acid reflux, and activates the sympathetic nervous system — all of which oppose sleep onset.

**Context matters:**

When using apigenin for its NAD+-preserving (CD38 inhibition) or anti-inflammatory effects rather than sleep, these food restrictions are less relevant. The iron chelation concern applies regardless of timing goals.
