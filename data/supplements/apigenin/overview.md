# Apigenin
## Overview

Apigenin is a naturally occurring flavone found in high concentrations in chamomile flowers, parsley, and celery. It has gained significant attention as a sleep aid and longevity compound, particularly after neuroscientist Andrew Huberman highlighted it as a key component of his nightly sleep stack alongside magnesium threonate and L-theanine.

**Why it matters:**

- **Mild, natural sedative** — Apigenin binds to benzodiazepine sites on GABA-A receptors, producing calming and mildly sedative effects. Unlike pharmaceutical benzodiazepines, apigenin is a partial agonist with no addiction potential, no rebound insomnia, and no significant next-day impairment. This is why chamomile tea has been used as a bedtime drink for centuries — apigenin is its primary active compound.
- **CD38 inhibition (NAD+ preservation)** — One of apigenin's most exciting properties is its ability to inhibit CD38, an enzyme that consumes NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide). NAD+ is critical for cellular energy production, DNA repair, sirtuin activation, and mitochondrial function. CD38 levels increase with age and inflammation, progressively depleting NAD+. By inhibiting CD38, apigenin helps preserve existing NAD+ levels — complementing NAD+ precursor strategies (NMN, NR) that increase NAD+ production.
- **Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant** — Apigenin is a potent inhibitor of NF-kB, COX-2, and iNOS signaling. It reduces inflammatory cytokine production and neutralizes reactive oxygen species.
- **Anti-cancer research** — Extensive preclinical research demonstrates apigenin's anti-proliferative, pro-apoptotic, and anti-angiogenic effects across multiple cancer cell lines. It modulates cell cycle progression and enhances the efficacy of certain chemotherapy agents in vitro.
- **Neuroprotection** — Reduces neuroinflammation, supports BDNF signaling, and protects against glutamate excitotoxicity.
- **Hormonal note for men** — Apigenin may have mild aromatase-inhibitory properties, potentially supporting testosterone levels by reducing conversion to estrogen. Research is preliminary but relevant for male health optimization.
