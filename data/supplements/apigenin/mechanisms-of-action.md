# Apigenin
## Mechanisms of Action

- **GABA-A receptor modulation (benzodiazepine site)** — Apigenin binds to the benzodiazepine binding site on GABA-A receptors as a positive allosteric modulator. This enhances the inhibitory effect of GABA without directly activating the receptor. As a partial agonist (not a full agonist like diazepam), it produces anxiolytic and mildly sedative effects without the heavy sedation, amnesia, respiratory depression, or addiction associated with benzodiazepines. This is the primary mechanism underlying its sleep-promoting effects.
- **CD38 enzyme inhibition** — CD38 is an NAD+-consuming ectoenzyme whose expression increases with age and chronic inflammation. CD38 is responsible for the majority of age-related NAD+ decline. Apigenin inhibits CD38 activity, preserving intracellular NAD+ levels. This supports sirtuin activation (SIRT1, SIRT3, SIRT6), PARP-mediated DNA repair, and mitochondrial function. This mechanism positions apigenin as a longevity compound independent of its sleep benefits.
- **NF-kB suppression** — Apigenin directly inhibits the NF-kB signaling pathway, one of the master regulators of inflammatory gene expression. This reduces production of TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, IL-6, and other pro-inflammatory mediators.
- **COX-2 and iNOS inhibition** — Suppresses cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) and inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) expression, reducing prostaglandin E2 and excessive nitric oxide production. These are key mediators in chronic inflammation and pain signaling.
- **Aromatase inhibition (CYP19A1)** — Apigenin has demonstrated mild inhibition of aromatase, the enzyme that converts testosterone to estrogen. This is dose-dependent and less potent than pharmaceutical aromatase inhibitors, but potentially relevant for maintaining hormonal balance in men.
- **Antioxidant activity** — Direct scavenging of superoxide, hydroxyl radicals, and peroxynitrite. Also upregulates endogenous antioxidant defenses through Nrf2 pathway activation.
- **Cell cycle regulation** — Induces G2/M cell cycle arrest in rapidly dividing cells, inhibits cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs), and promotes apoptosis via caspase activation in abnormal cells. These anti-proliferative effects underlie its preclinical anti-cancer research.
- **MAPK/ERK pathway modulation** — Inhibits aberrant MAPK/ERK signaling, which is involved in cell proliferation, survival, and inflammatory responses.
- **BDNF and neuroplasticity** — Preclinical evidence suggests apigenin supports BDNF expression and TrkB receptor signaling, contributing to neuroprotection and cognitive support.
- **Gut microbiome modulation** — Emerging research indicates apigenin can influence gut microbial composition, increasing beneficial Bacteroidetes and reducing inflammatory Firmicutes populations.
