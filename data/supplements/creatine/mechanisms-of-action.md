# Creatine
## Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Phosphocreatine Energy System (Primary Mechanism)
- **Creatine kinase reaction:** Phosphocreatine + ADP → Creatine + ATP
- This reaction regenerates ATP within seconds, providing immediate energy for high-intensity, short-duration efforts (0-10 seconds)
- Supplementation increases intramuscular phosphocreatine stores by 20-40%, extending the duration and power of the phosphocreatine energy system
- After phosphocreatine is depleted, it is resynthesized during rest periods — creatine supplementation accelerates this resynthesis

### 2. Cell Volumization (Osmotic Effect)
- Creatine is osmotically active — it draws water into muscle cells
- This intracellular hydration increases cell volume, which acts as an anabolic signal
- Cell swelling activates mTOR signaling and reduces protein breakdown (anti-catabolic)
- This is the "water weight" people notice in the first 1-2 weeks — it is intracellular water in muscle, NOT subcutaneous water retention or bloating

### 3. Enhanced Muscle Protein Synthesis
- By increasing training capacity (more reps, more weight, more volume), creatine indirectly drives greater muscle protein synthesis over time
- Some evidence for direct upregulation of IGF-1 locally in muscle tissue
- Increases satellite cell activity and myonuclear addition, supporting long-term muscle growth

### 4. Brain Creatine & Cognitive Function
- The brain maintains its own creatine pool via the creatine transporter (SLC6A8)
- Brain phosphocreatine buffers ATP during high cognitive demand, sleep deprivation, and hypoxia
- Supplementation can increase brain creatine levels by 5-10% (takes longer than muscle — 4-8 weeks)
- Vegetarians show larger brain creatine increases and cognitive improvements from supplementation (lower baseline)

### 5. Mitochondrial Function
- Creatine supports mitochondrial membrane integrity and function
- The phosphocreatine/creatine shuttle transports high-energy phosphate groups from mitochondria to cytoplasm (the "creatine shuttle")
- May reduce mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) production

### 6. Myostatin Reduction
- Some studies show creatine supplementation reduces myostatin levels (a negative regulator of muscle growth)
- This could enhance long-term muscle-building potential beyond the direct energy effects

### 7. Glycogen Supercompensation
- Creatine supplementation may enhance muscle glycogen storage when combined with carbohydrate loading
- Relevant for endurance performance and recovery between training sessions

### 8. Bone Density Support
- Creatine enhances the effects of resistance training on bone mineral density
- Increases osteoblast activity markers in some studies
- Particularly relevant for postmenopausal women and older adults at risk of osteoporosis
