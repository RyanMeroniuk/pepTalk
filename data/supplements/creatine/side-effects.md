# Creatine
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild, Expected)
- **Water weight gain (1-3 lbs / 0.5-1.5 kg in the first week)** — this is intracellular water drawn into muscle cells by creatine's osmotic effect. It is NOT fat, NOT subcutaneous bloating, and NOT unhealthy. It makes muscles look fuller and is part of creatine's anabolic signaling. This stabilizes after 1-2 weeks.
- **Mild GI discomfort** — only common during loading phases (20 g/day) or when taking large single doses (10+ g). At 5 g/day, GI issues are rare. If they occur, split into two 2.5 g doses or take with food.

### Uncommon
- **Muscle cramping** — anecdotally reported but NOT supported by research. Controlled studies show creatine does not increase cramping and may actually reduce it.
- **Mild bloating** — rare at standard doses; more common during loading. Usually resolves within a few days.

### Myth: Hair Loss / DHT Increase
- **The evidence:** One single study (van der Merwe et al., 2009) in rugby players showed increased DHT after a loading phase. This study has NEVER been replicated despite dozens of subsequent studies measuring testosterone and DHT.
- **Multiple studies show no change** in testosterone, free testosterone, or DHT with creatine supplementation (Farshidfar et al., 2017; Antonio et al., 2021; meta-analysis by Kreider et al., 2022)
- **DHT increase does not equal hair loss** — even if DHT were affected, hair loss depends on follicle sensitivity (genetics), not just DHT levels
- **The ISSN position stand (2017, updated 2021)** states there is insufficient evidence to support a link between creatine and hair loss
- **Bottom line:** If you are genetically predisposed to male pattern baldness, creatine is not going to meaningfully accelerate it. The fear is based on a single unreplicated study.

### Myth: Kidney Damage
- **The evidence:** Over 500 studies, including long-term studies (up to 5 years of daily use), have found NO adverse effects on kidney function in healthy individuals
- **Creatinine levels rise on blood tests** — this is expected and normal. Creatinine is a breakdown product of creatine metabolism. Higher creatine intake = higher creatinine output. This does NOT indicate kidney damage. Inform your doctor that you take creatine before blood work.
- **eGFR may appear lower** — because eGFR is calculated from creatinine, and creatinine is artificially elevated by supplementation, eGFR formulas may underestimate kidney function. Cystatin C-based eGFR is a more accurate measure for creatine users.
- **Pre-existing kidney disease:** Patients with CKD or significantly impaired renal function should consult their nephrologist. This is a precautionary measure, not because creatine has been shown to worsen kidney disease.
- **The ISSN, ACSM, and NCAA** all consider creatine safe for healthy individuals

### Myth: Dehydration
- **Research shows creatine does NOT cause dehydration** — it actually increases total body water and may improve thermoregulation during exercise in hot environments (Lopez et al., 2009)
- Drink adequate water (increase by 16-24 oz/day), but creatine is not dehydrating

### Myth: "It's a Steroid"
- **Creatine is not a steroid, not a hormone, and is not banned by any athletic organization** including WADA, NCAA, IOC, and all professional sports leagues
- It is a naturally occurring compound in meat and fish, and is produced endogenously

### Actual Contraindications
- **Pre-existing kidney disease** — consult nephrologist (precautionary, not proven harmful)
- **Very rare: compartment syndrome risk** — theoretical concern in individuals prone to acute compartment syndrome (creatine increases intracellular water in muscle). Extremely rare.

### Safety Profile Summary
- **One of the safest supplements available** — 30+ years of research
- **No liver toxicity** at any studied dose
- **No cardiovascular risk** — may actually be cardioprotective
- **Safe for adolescents** — ISSN considers creatine safe for young athletes when supervised
- **Safe for women** — no evidence of hormonal disruption or sex-specific risks
- **Safe for long-term use** — continuous daily use for years is well-documented as safe
