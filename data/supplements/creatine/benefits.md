# Creatine
## Benefits

### Strength & Power (Most Robust Evidence)
- **Increases maximal strength** — 5-10% increase in 1RM across multiple exercises (bench press, squat, deadlift) with creatine + resistance training vs resistance training alone
- **Increases power output** — 10-20% improvement in repeated sprint performance, jump height, and peak power
- **Increases lean body mass** — 1-2 kg more lean mass gain over 4-12 weeks of training vs placebo
- **Improves high-intensity exercise capacity** — more reps at a given weight, more sets before fatigue, faster recovery between sets

### Muscle Growth & Body Composition
- **Enhanced hypertrophy** — creatine + resistance training produces significantly more muscle growth than training alone across virtually every study
- **Works in all populations** — young men, young women, older men, older women, trained athletes, untrained beginners
- **Cell volumization** — immediate 1-3 lb increase in lean mass from intracellular water (within first week), followed by real tissue accretion over weeks

### Cognitive Performance
- **Improves working memory** — particularly under stress or sleep deprivation
- **Enhances processing speed** — faster reaction times and cognitive throughput in demanding tasks
- **Vegetarians see the largest benefits** — 10-15% improvement in memory and intelligence test scores (Rae et al., 2003)
- **Sleep deprivation resilience** — partially compensates for cognitive decline during sleep loss (McMorris et al., 2006)
- **Potential antidepressant effects** — augments SSRI response in treatment-resistant depression (Kondo et al., 2011; Lyoo et al., 2012)

### Anti-Aging & Longevity
- **Combats sarcopenia** — creatine + resistance training in older adults preserves and builds muscle more effectively than training alone
- **Bone density support** — creatine + resistance training improves bone mineral density in postmenopausal women
- **Brain aging** — maintaining brain creatine may protect against age-related cognitive decline
- **Reduces fatigue** in older adults, improving quality of life and functional independence

### Neuroprotection
- **Traumatic brain injury (TBI)** — pilot studies show reduced headache, dizziness, and fatigue post-TBI with creatine supplementation
- **Neurodegenerative disease** — preclinical evidence for neuroprotection in Parkinson's, Huntington's, and ALS models (clinical trials ongoing)
- **Stroke** — creatine's ability to buffer ATP may protect neurons during ischemic events

### Recovery
- **Reduces muscle damage markers** — lower CK levels after intense exercise
- **Faster glycogen replenishment** — when combined with carbohydrates post-exercise
- **Reduces inflammation** — some evidence for lower IL-6 and TNF-alpha after exercise

### Myth Busting
- **Hair loss:** One study from 2009 (Vatani et al.) in rugby players showed increased DHT (dihydrotestosterone) levels with creatine loading. This was a single study, never replicated, and DHT increase does not automatically mean hair loss. Multiple subsequent studies found NO change in DHT or testosterone. The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) position stand states there is no strong evidence linking creatine to hair loss.
- **Kidney damage:** Over 500 studies, including long-term studies (up to 5 years), show no adverse effects on kidney function in healthy individuals. People with pre-existing kidney disease should consult their nephrologist, but healthy kidneys handle creatine safely. The creatinine increase on blood tests is a byproduct of creatine metabolism and does NOT indicate kidney damage — inform your doctor you take creatine before blood work.
- **Dehydration/cramps:** No evidence supports this. Creatine may actually improve hydration status and thermoregulation during exercise.
- **"It's a steroid":** Creatine is not a hormone, not anabolic in the steroid sense, and is not banned by any sports organization. It is a natural compound found in meat and produced by the body.
