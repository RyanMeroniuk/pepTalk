# Creatine
## Forms and Bioavailability

### Creatine Monohydrate (The Gold Standard)
- **88% creatine by weight** (12% water molecule)
- **The most studied form** — virtually all positive research on creatine uses monohydrate
- **Nearly 100% bioavailable** when dissolved — absorption from the gut is highly efficient
- **Cheapest per gram** — no reason to pay more for other forms
- **Micronized creatine monohydrate** — finer particle size dissolves better in water but is chemically identical. Worth the small price premium for convenience.
- **Creapure** — a trademarked, German-manufactured creatine monohydrate known for the highest purity (99.99%). If you want to ensure zero contaminants, Creapure is the benchmark.
- **Bottom line: Monohydrate is king. Every other form is trying to solve problems that don't exist.**

### Creatine Hydrochloride (HCl)
- **Marketing claim:** More water-soluble, therefore better absorbed, requires smaller doses
- **Reality:** Higher solubility in a beaker does not mean higher absorption in the body. The gut absorbs monohydrate at ~99% efficiency already. There is no meaningful advantage.
- **Smaller doses (1-2 g):** Insufficient to saturate muscle stores as effectively as 3-5 g monohydrate
- **More expensive per equivalent creatine dose**
- **No bloating advantage** — the "bloating" from monohydrate is intracellular muscle hydration, not GI bloating. HCl doesn't avoid this.
- **Verdict:** Unnecessary. Saves no money, provides no proven benefit over monohydrate.

### Buffered Creatine (Kre-Alkalyn)
- **Marketing claim:** Buffered pH prevents conversion to creatinine in the stomach, requiring smaller doses
- **Reality:** A direct comparison study (Jagim et al., 2012) found Kre-Alkalyn at recommended doses was NO more effective than standard monohydrate and actually resulted in LOWER muscle creatine levels.
- **More expensive per equivalent dose**
- **Verdict:** Debunked. Monohydrate is superior.

### Creatine Ethyl Ester (CEE)
- **Marketing claim:** Better absorbed due to ester group
- **Reality:** Research shows CEE is LESS effective than monohydrate — it converts to creatinine (waste product) faster than monohydrate in the body (Spillane et al., 2009)
- **Verdict:** Actively worse than monohydrate. Avoid.

### Creatine Magnesium Chelate
- **Creatine bound to magnesium** — provides both nutrients
- **Some evidence** for comparable effectiveness to monohydrate at lower doses
- **Useful if you want creatine + magnesium in one product**, but more expensive
- **Verdict:** Fine but unnecessary — just take monohydrate + magnesium separately

### Creatine Nitrate
- **Creatine bound to nitrate** — more water-soluble
- **Limited research** — insufficient evidence to recommend over monohydrate
- **Verdict:** Unproven

### Liquid Creatine
- **Pre-dissolved creatine in liquid form**
- **Creatine degrades in liquid over time** — converts to creatinine
- **Inferior to powder** — you're paying for degraded creatine
- **Verdict:** Avoid

### Summary
| Form | Proven Effective | Cost-Effective | Recommended |
|------|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Monohydrate** | Yes | Yes | **YES** |
| HCl | No advantage proven | No | No |
| Kre-Alkalyn | No advantage proven | No | No |
| Ethyl Ester | Worse than monohydrate | No | No |
| Liquid | Degraded product | No | No |

### The Only Decision: Micronized or Regular Monohydrate?
- **Regular monohydrate:** Slightly grittier, may not dissolve completely in cold water. Works perfectly fine.
- **Micronized monohydrate:** Finer particles, dissolves better, slightly more expensive. The small premium is worth it for convenience.
- **Creapure (micronized):** Highest purity guarantee. Best option if budget allows.
