# Creatine
## Drug Interactions

### Moderate Interactions (Use with Caution)
- **Nephrotoxic drugs** — aminoglycoside antibiotics (gentamicin, tobramycin), NSAIDs (chronic high-dose), cyclosporine, and other drugs known to stress kidney function. While creatine itself is not nephrotoxic, the theoretical additive kidney burden warrants caution. Consult your prescribing physician.
- **Diuretics** — creatine increases intracellular water retention; diuretics increase water excretion. Theoretical conflict in hydration status. Monitor hydration carefully if taking both.
- **Probenecid** — used for gout; affects renal tubular transport of various compounds. Theoretical interaction with creatine/creatinine excretion. Low clinical significance but worth monitoring.

### Mild Interactions (Monitor)
- **Metformin** — both creatine and metformin can affect cellular energy metabolism. No direct interaction documented, but metformin users should be aware that creatinine levels on blood tests will be elevated from creatine supplementation, not from kidney issues. Inform your endocrinologist.
- **Cimetidine (Tagamet)** — competes with creatinine for renal tubular secretion, potentially raising serum creatinine further. Not dangerous, but blood test interpretation should account for both.
- **Trimethoprim (antibiotic)** — also inhibits renal creatinine secretion, elevating serum creatinine. Additive effect with supplemental creatine. Blood tests during trimethoprim treatment + creatine will show very elevated creatinine without indicating kidney damage.

### Positive Interactions (Beneficial)
- **Caffeine (moderate doses)** — despite one early study suggesting interference, the consensus is that moderate caffeine (1-3 cups of coffee) does not negate creatine's benefits. Some athletes combine both for enhanced performance.
- **Protein supplements** — creatine and protein are highly synergistic for muscle building. No interaction concerns.
- **Carbohydrate** — insulin from carbs enhances creatine uptake. Beneficial pairing.
- **HMB (beta-hydroxy beta-methylbutyrate)** — some evidence for additive anti-catabolic effects when combined with creatine
- **Beta-alanine** — complementary ergogenic aids; beta-alanine buffers hydrogen ions (muscular endurance) while creatine regenerates ATP (power/strength). No interaction.

### Contraindications
- **Diagnosed kidney disease (eGFR < 60)** — consult nephrologist before using. This is a precautionary measure; creatine has not been proven to worsen kidney disease, but theoretical concerns warrant medical supervision.
- **Dialysis patients** — avoid without nephrology guidance

### Blood Test Considerations (Critical)
- **Serum creatinine will be elevated** — this is expected and normal. It does NOT indicate kidney damage.
- **eGFR may appear artificially low** — because eGFR is calculated from creatinine. For accurate kidney function assessment, request **cystatin C-based eGFR** or direct GFR measurement.
- **BUN (blood urea nitrogen)** — not significantly affected by creatine supplementation
- **Always inform healthcare providers** that you supplement with creatine before blood work or medical procedures

### Safety Notes
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data for supplemental creatine during pregnancy. While creatine is a natural compound, high-dose supplementation should be discussed with an OB/GYN. Some preliminary research actually suggests creatine may protect the fetal brain during hypoxic events, but this is not yet a clinical recommendation.
- **Adolescents** — the ISSN considers creatine safe for young athletes with proper supervision, adequate hydration, and appropriate training programs
- **Liver disease** — creatine is synthesized in the liver; severe liver disease may affect endogenous production. Supplementation has not been shown to worsen liver function but discuss with hepatologist if liver disease is present.
