# Creatine
## Natural Food Sources

### Highest Concentration (Meat and Fish)
- **Herring** — ~6.5-10 g per kg (raw); one of the richest sources
- **Salmon** — ~4.5 g per kg (raw)
- **Tuna** — ~4.0 g per kg (raw)
- **Cod** — ~3.0 g per kg (raw)
- **Beef** — ~4.5 g per kg (raw); roughly 2 g per pound of raw beef
- **Pork** — ~5.0 g per kg (raw)
- **Chicken** — ~3.4 g per kg (raw)
- **Turkey** — ~3.0 g per kg (raw)
- **Rabbit** — ~3.4 g per kg (raw)
- **Venison** — ~4.5 g per kg (raw)

### Moderate Sources
- **Milk** — ~0.1 g per liter
- **Cranberries** — trace amounts (one of the few plant sources with any detectable creatine)

### Important Context
- **Cooking degrades creatine** — heat converts creatine to creatinine (biologically inactive). Raw or rare meat retains more creatine than well-done meat. Approximately 20-30% of creatine is lost during cooking.
- **Typical dietary intake (omnivores):** 1-2 g/day from food
- **Typical dietary intake (vegetarians/vegans):** ~0 g/day from food (no dietary creatine)
- **To get 5 g of creatine from food alone, you would need to eat approximately:**
  - 1 kg (2.2 lbs) of raw beef, OR
  - 500 g-1 kg of raw herring
  - This is impractical and expensive compared to supplementation ($0.03-0.05 per 5 g dose of creatine monohydrate)
- **Endogenous synthesis:** The body produces ~1 g/day from glycine + arginine + methionine (primarily in the liver and kidneys)

### Why Vegetarians/Vegans Should Especially Supplement
- Zero dietary creatine intake
- Lower baseline muscle creatine stores (by ~20-30%)
- Lower baseline brain creatine stores
- Show larger absolute improvements in both physical and cognitive performance when supplementing
- Creatine monohydrate is synthetically produced (not derived from animal sources) and is vegan-friendly

### The Argument for Supplementation (Everyone)
- Even omnivores consuming 1-2 g/day from food have muscle creatine stores at only ~60-80% of full capacity
- 5 g/day supplementation fills stores to ~100% capacity
- Cost is negligible (~$10-15/month for high-quality monohydrate)
- Risk is virtually zero in healthy individuals
- Benefit-to-cost ratio is arguably the highest of any supplement
