# Creatine
## Foods to Avoid

### Foods/Substances That May Reduce Creatine Effectiveness
- **Excessive caffeine (high acute doses)** — one early study (Vandenberghe et al., 1996) suggested that high-dose caffeine (5 mg/kg) negated creatine's ergogenic benefits on muscle relaxation time. However, subsequent studies have NOT consistently replicated this, and most researchers believe moderate caffeine intake (1-3 mg/kg) does not meaningfully interfere with creatine. **Practical advice:** Normal coffee consumption (1-3 cups/day) is fine with creatine. Don't worry about it.
- **Excessive alcohol** — chronic heavy alcohol use impairs muscle protein synthesis, reduces mTOR activation, and undermines the training adaptations that creatine supports. Creatine itself isn't affected, but the benefits are wasted if training adaptation is suppressed.

### Foods That Are Irrelevant (Don't Overthink It)
- **There are no foods that block creatine absorption** in any meaningful way
- **Acidic foods/beverages** do NOT destroy creatine — creatine is stable at normal stomach pH
- **Fiber** does NOT bind creatine or prevent absorption
- **Fat** does NOT impair creatine uptake

### The Real "Avoid" List (Behaviors, Not Foods)
- **Inconsistency** — the biggest enemy of creatine effectiveness is forgetting to take it. Creatine requires daily dosing to maintain saturated muscle stores. Missing days means stores drop.
- **Under-dosing** — 1-2 g/day is insufficient to fully saturate muscle stores. Stick to 3-5 g/day.
- **Inadequate water intake** — creatine draws water into muscle cells. Not drinking enough water won't make creatine dangerous, but it may reduce the fullness effect and could contribute to mild GI discomfort.
- **Not training** — creatine enhances training adaptations. Without a training stimulus, the performance benefits are greatly diminished (though cognitive benefits still apply).

### Timing of Creatine with Other Supplements
- **Creatine can be taken with virtually anything** — it does not compete with or interfere with other supplements
- **Can be mixed with protein powder, BCAAs, pre-workout, coffee, juice, or plain water**
- **No need to separate from medications** — creatine has no known drug-food interactions that require timing separation
