# Creatine
## Overview

Creatine monohydrate is the single most well-researched and effective sports supplement in existence. But its benefits extend far beyond athletics — emerging evidence shows significant cognitive, neuroprotective, and even anti-aging effects. If you could only take one supplement, many researchers would argue it should be creatine.

### Why It Matters

- **ATP regeneration:** Creatine donates a phosphate group to ADP to rapidly regenerate ATP — the cell's energy currency. This is the fastest pathway for energy production, critical during high-intensity efforts (sprints, heavy lifts, rapid cognitive processing).
- **Brain energy:** The brain consumes ~20% of the body's energy despite being ~2% of body mass. Creatine stores in the brain directly support cognitive performance, especially under stress, sleep deprivation, or complex task demands.
- **Muscle performance:** Increases strength, power output, and lean mass more consistently than any other legal supplement. Effect sizes are large and reproducible across hundreds of studies.
- **Neuroprotection:** Emerging evidence for benefits in TBI, depression, cognitive decline, and neurodegenerative conditions.
- **Safety:** Over 30 years of research with no credible evidence of harm in healthy individuals at recommended doses.

### Who Benefits Most

- **Strength and power athletes** — increases 1RM strength, power output, and lean mass
- **Anyone doing resistance training** — enhances workout capacity and recovery between sets
- **Older adults** — combats age-related muscle loss (sarcopenia), supports bone density, and enhances cognitive function
- **Vegetarians and vegans** — have lower baseline creatine stores (no dietary creatine from meat); respond more dramatically to supplementation
- **People under cognitive stress** — sleep-deprived individuals, high-stress jobs, students during exams
- **Those with TBI history** — emerging evidence for neuroprotective benefits
- **Women** — historically understudied but evidence shows equal or greater relative benefits, including potential mood and hormonal cycle-related cognitive support
