# Collagen Peptides
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild)
- **Feeling of fullness** — collagen is a protein and contributes to satiety. Taking 15-20 g can suppress appetite mildly. This can be a feature for weight management.
- **Mild digestive discomfort** — some people experience bloating or mild GI upset when first starting, especially with gelatin. Usually resolves within a few days. Starting with lower doses (5 g) and increasing gradually helps.
- **Aftertaste** — lower-quality collagen products may have a slight fishy (marine) or bovine taste. High-quality hydrolyzed peptides should be tasteless.

### Uncommon
- **Allergic reactions** — rare but possible, especially with marine collagen in people with fish/shellfish allergies. Bovine collagen can trigger reactions in people with beef sensitivity. Always check the source.
- **Hypercalcemia** — very rare; some collagen products (especially bone-derived) contain calcium. In combination with high calcium intake from other sources, this could theoretically contribute to excess calcium. Monitor if taking multiple calcium sources.
- **Headache** — very rare; possibly related to histamine content in some lower-quality collagen products (collagen from bone broth can be high in histamine)
- **Bad taste in mouth** — occasional complaint, particularly with marine collagen capsules

### Histamine Consideration
- **Bone broth and gelatin can be high in histamine** — the slow cooking process that extracts collagen also promotes histamine formation
- **People with histamine intolerance or MCAS** should choose pure hydrolyzed collagen peptides (processed quickly, lower histamine) over bone broth or gelatin
- Marine collagen from fish can also be histamine-rich if not processed properly

### Safety Profile
- **Very safe** — collagen has been consumed as food (bone broth, gelatin) for millennia
- **No toxicity** reported at any studied supplemental dose (up to 30 g/day in clinical trials)
- **No hormonal effects** — collagen is a structural protein, not a bioactive hormone
- **Safe for long-term use** — no adverse effects reported with chronic supplementation
- **GRAS status** confirmed by the FDA
- **No drug interactions** — collagen is a food-derived protein, not a pharmacological agent

### Quality Concerns (Not Side Effects, But Worth Noting)
- **Heavy metal contamination** — bovine and marine collagen can contain trace heavy metals (lead, cadmium, mercury) depending on sourcing. Choose third-party tested products from reputable brands.
- **BSE/prion disease** — theoretical concern with bovine collagen. Mitigated by sourcing from BSE-free countries (USA, Australia, New Zealand, Brazil). Look for "BSE-free" or "grass-fed, pasture-raised" designations.
- **Antibiotic/hormone residues** — grass-fed, pasture-raised (bovine) and wild-caught (marine) sources minimize this risk

### Contraindications
- **Fish/shellfish allergy** — avoid marine collagen
- **Beef allergy (alpha-gal syndrome)** — avoid bovine collagen; use marine instead
- **Phenylketonuria (PKU)** — collagen contains phenylalanine; those with PKU should consult their physician
- **Renal oxalate stones** — high-dose vitamin C taken with collagen could increase oxalate production in susceptible individuals. Use moderate vitamin C doses (250-500 mg) rather than megadoses.
