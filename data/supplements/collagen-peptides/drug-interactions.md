# Collagen Peptides
## Drug Interactions

### No Significant Drug Interactions
- **Collagen peptides are a food-derived protein** and do not have clinically significant drug interactions. They are metabolized as dietary protein and do not affect drug metabolism pathways (CYP450 enzymes, P-glycoprotein, etc.).

### Minor Considerations
- **Calcium channel blockers / blood pressure medications** — some bone-derived collagen products contain calcium. If taking calcium-containing collagen alongside calcium supplements and calcium-rich diet, monitor total calcium intake to avoid hypercalcemia. This is not a direct drug interaction but a nutrient-load consideration.
- **Antibiotics (tetracycline, fluoroquinolone class)** — calcium and minerals in some collagen products could theoretically reduce antibiotic absorption if taken simultaneously. Separate collagen from these antibiotics by 2 hours as a precaution.
- **Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs)** — collagen contains trace amounts of tyramine (especially bone broth-derived products). MAOIs + tyramine can cause hypertensive crisis. While the tyramine content in hydrolyzed collagen peptides is very low, patients on MAOIs should use pharmaceutical-grade hydrolyzed collagen rather than bone broth.
- **Blood thinners (warfarin, heparin)** — no direct interaction, but vitamin C (taken with collagen) at very high doses (>2000 mg/day) can theoretically affect warfarin metabolism. Standard doses (250-500 mg) are not a concern.

### Positive Interactions (Beneficial)
- **Vitamin C supplements** — essential co-factor for collagen synthesis. Always beneficial to combine.
- **Hyaluronic acid supplements** — synergistic with collagen for skin hydration and joint lubrication
- **Glucosamine and chondroitin** — complementary joint health supplements; no interaction concerns with collagen
- **Calcium and vitamin D** — synergistic with collagen for bone health

### Allergy Considerations (Not Drug Interactions)
- **Fish/shellfish allergy** — marine collagen is derived from fish. Avoid marine collagen if you have a fish allergy. Use bovine instead.
- **Alpha-gal syndrome (mammalian meat allergy)** — bovine collagen may trigger reactions. Use marine collagen instead.
- **Egg allergy** — eggshell membrane collagen products exist; avoid if egg-allergic
- **Soy allergy** — some collagen products contain soy-derived ingredients (check labels)

### Safety Notes
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — collagen is generally considered safe during pregnancy (it is a normal dietary protein). Therapeutic doses (10-20 g/day) have not been specifically studied in pregnant women, but there are no known concerns. Discuss with OB/GYN.
- **Kidney disease** — collagen is a protein and contributes to nitrogen load. Patients on protein-restricted diets for kidney disease should count collagen toward their protein allowance and consult their nephrologist.
- **Gout** — collagen supplements do not contain purines and are not expected to affect uric acid levels. Bone broth, however, can contain moderate purines and may need to be limited in gout-prone individuals.
- **Surgery** — no need to discontinue collagen before surgery. In fact, increased collagen intake post-surgery may support wound healing.
