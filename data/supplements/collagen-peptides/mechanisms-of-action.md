# Collagen Peptides
## Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Bioactive Peptide Signaling (Not Just Building Blocks)
- Collagen peptides are NOT simply broken down into individual amino acids and reassembled. Key di- and tripeptides (especially hydroxyproline-containing peptides like Pro-Hyp and Hyp-Gly) survive digestion and enter the bloodstream intact.
- These bioactive peptides accumulate in target tissues (skin, cartilage, bone) and act as **signaling molecules** — they stimulate resident cells to produce NEW collagen.
- This is the critical insight: collagen supplementation works as a biological signal, not just a protein source.

### 2. Fibroblast Stimulation (Skin)
- Collagen-derived peptides (especially Pro-Hyp) bind to fibroblast receptors and stimulate proliferation and collagen synthesis
- Increase production of Type I and Type III procollagen in dermal fibroblasts
- Upregulate hyaluronic acid synthase — increasing skin hydration from within
- Increase elastin fiber production — improving skin elasticity

### 3. Chondrocyte Stimulation (Cartilage/Joints)
- Type II collagen-derived peptides stimulate chondrocytes (cartilage-building cells) to produce new cartilage matrix
- Reduce matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) activity — enzymes that degrade cartilage
- Suppress inflammatory mediators in joint tissue (IL-1beta, TNF-alpha)
- Undenatured Type II collagen (UC-II) specifically works via oral tolerance — training the immune system to stop attacking joint cartilage (relevant for osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis)

### 4. Osteoblast Stimulation (Bone)
- Collagen peptides stimulate osteoblast differentiation and activity
- Increase bone mineral density by providing the organic scaffold for mineralization
- The organic matrix of bone is ~90% Type I collagen — minerals (calcium, phosphorus) deposit onto this collagen scaffold

### 5. Tendon and Ligament Support
- Collagen peptides increase collagen synthesis in tenocytes (tendon cells) and ligament fibroblasts
- Enhanced by mechanical loading — taking collagen before exercise increases collagen synthesis in tendons (Keith Baar research, UC Davis)
- Supports injury recovery and prevention in athletes

### 6. Gut Barrier Support
- Collagen provides glycine, glutamine, and proline — key amino acids for enterocyte function and tight junction maintenance
- Collagen-derived peptides may directly support mucosal repair
- Gelatin (partially hydrolyzed collagen) has been used historically for gut healing ("Gut and Psychology Syndrome" protocols)

### 7. Vitamin C Dependency (Critical)
- **Collagen synthesis absolutely requires vitamin C** as a cofactor for prolyl hydroxylase and lysyl hydroxylase
- These enzymes hydroxylate proline and lysine residues in procollagen — without hydroxylation, collagen cannot form stable triple-helix structures
- Vitamin C deficiency = scurvy = total collagen synthesis failure
- **ALWAYS co-supplement vitamin C with collagen peptides**
