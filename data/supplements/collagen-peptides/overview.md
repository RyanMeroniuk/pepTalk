# Collagen Peptides
## Overview

Collagen is the most abundant protein in the human body, comprising 25-35% of total protein content. It is the structural scaffold of skin, tendons, ligaments, cartilage, bone, blood vessels, gut lining, and the extracellular matrix. After age 25, collagen production declines at ~1-1.5% per year — leading to wrinkles, joint pain, slower wound healing, and weakened connective tissue.

Collagen peptides (hydrolyzed collagen) are enzymatically broken-down collagen proteins that are efficiently absorbed as di- and tripeptides, which can stimulate the body's own collagen-producing cells (fibroblasts, chondrocytes, osteoblasts).

### Why It Matters

- **Skin aging:** Collagen loss is the primary driver of skin wrinkles, sagging, and loss of elasticity. Supplementation with collagen peptides consistently shows improved skin hydration, elasticity, and wrinkle depth in RCTs.
- **Joint health:** Collagen provides the structural matrix of cartilage. Hydrolyzed collagen accumulates in cartilage tissue and stimulates chondrocyte activity.
- **Gut lining:** The intestinal mucosa is collagen-rich. Collagen peptides support gut barrier integrity, complementing gut-healing protocols.
- **Bone density:** The organic matrix of bone is primarily Type I collagen. Collagen supplementation supports bone mineral density, especially in postmenopausal women.
- **Tendons and ligaments:** Athletic injuries often involve collagen-rich tissues. Supplementation supports repair and resilience.

### Who Benefits Most

- **Anyone over 25** — collagen production is already declining
- **Women concerned with skin aging** — strongest evidence base is for skin hydration and elasticity
- **Athletes** — tendon, ligament, and joint support; injury prevention and recovery
- **People with joint pain or osteoarthritis** — Type II collagen for cartilage support
- **Post-surgical patients** — wound healing requires collagen deposition
- **Those on gut-healing protocols** — collagen supports mucosal repair alongside glutamine and BPC-157
- **People experiencing hair thinning or brittle nails** — collagen provides structural proteins for both
- **Older adults** — bone density support, sarcopenia prevention (with resistance training)
