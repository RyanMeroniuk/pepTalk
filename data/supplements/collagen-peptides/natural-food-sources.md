# Collagen Peptides
## Natural Food Sources

### Richest Whole-Food Sources
- **Bone broth** — the gold standard whole-food collagen source. Beef bone broth (simmered 12-24 hours) provides ~5-10 g collagen per cup, plus glycine, proline, glutamine, and minerals. Chicken bone broth is also excellent.
- **Pork skin (chicharrones)** — ~30 g collagen per 100 g. Extremely collagen-dense.
- **Chicken skin** — ~15 g collagen per 100 g. Eat skin-on poultry.
- **Chicken feet** — ~20 g collagen per 100 g. Used traditionally in Asian soups; one of the richest sources available.
- **Oxtail** — rich in Type I and III collagen from tendons and connective tissue
- **Short ribs** — collagen-rich connective tissue that breaks down during slow cooking
- **Beef tendon** — nearly pure collagen; used in Asian cuisine (pho, braised dishes)
- **Pork trotters (pig feet)** — extremely collagen-rich; traditional food in many cultures
- **Fish skin** — rich in Type I collagen (especially salmon skin, cod skin)
- **Sardines and small fish (eaten whole)** — bones and skin provide collagen
- **Shellfish (shrimp, crab shells)** — collagen in shells and connective tissue

### Moderate Sources
- **Beef (tough cuts)** — chuck roast, brisket, shank — collagen-rich cuts that benefit from slow cooking
- **Gelatin (Jell-O, gummies, panna cotta)** — cooked collagen; easily added to desserts and snacks
- **Egg whites** — contain proline (collagen amino acid) but minimal intact collagen
- **Dairy** — minimal collagen content

### Foods That SUPPORT Collagen Production (Not Direct Sources)
- **Vitamin C-rich foods (CRITICAL):** Bell peppers (highest per serving), kiwi, strawberries, citrus fruits, broccoli, papaya, guava
- **Proline-rich foods:** Egg whites, dairy, asparagus, mushrooms, cabbage
- **Lysine-rich foods:** Meat, fish, eggs, dairy, lentils, soybeans
- **Copper-rich foods (collagen cross-linking):** Oysters, dark chocolate, cashews, liver, sesame seeds
- **Zinc-rich foods (fibroblast function):** Oysters, beef, pumpkin seeds, lentils
- **Sulfur-rich foods (disulfide bonds):** Garlic, onions, eggs, cruciferous vegetables

### Ancestral Eating for Collagen
- **Nose-to-tail eating** is the ancestral approach — our ancestors consumed skin, tendons, cartilage, organs, and made bone broth from every animal. Modern diets that emphasize lean muscle meat miss the collagen-rich parts.
- **Slow-cooked tough cuts** break down collagen into gelatin, making it bioavailable — pot roast, braised short ribs, slow-cooker stews
- **Head cheese, aspic, bone marrow** — traditional collagen-rich preparations found in European, Asian, and Latin American cuisines

### Important Notes
- **Cooking converts collagen to gelatin** — this is actually beneficial for bioavailability (gelatin > raw collagen for absorption)
- **Long, slow cooking** extracts more collagen — pressure cooking (Instant Pot bone broth, 2-4 hours) or slow cooking (12-24 hours) are best
- **Add acid (vinegar, lemon juice)** to bone broth to help extract collagen and minerals from bones — 1-2 tablespoons of apple cider vinegar per batch
- **Food sources alone may not provide therapeutic doses** (10-20 g/day) — supplementation fills the gap
