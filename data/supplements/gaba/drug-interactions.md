# GABA
## Drug Interactions

### Moderate Interactions (Use with Caution / Monitor)
- **CNS depressants / sedatives (benzodiazepines, barbiturates, z-drugs like zolpidem, sedating antihistamines)** — these act on or alongside the GABAergic inhibitory system. Combining with GABA can produce additive drowsiness and sedation. Use caution, especially before driving or operating machinery.
- **Anti-anxiety medications** — additive calming/sedative effects possible; coordinate with your prescriber
- **Antihypertensives (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers, beta-blockers)** — GABA may modestly lower blood pressure; combined use could be additive. Monitor blood pressure.

### Mild Interactions (Monitor)
- **Antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs)** — no well-established dangerous interaction, but combining agents that affect mood/CNS warrants awareness; monitor and consult a prescriber
- **Other sedating supplements (valerian, high-dose melatonin, kava)** — additive drowsiness; a tolerability rather than dangerous interaction, but use caution when stacking

### Timing-Sensitive Notes
- **Take sedating combinations at bedtime, not before activities requiring alertness** — the additive drowsiness with CNS depressants is the main practical timing concern
- **Levothyroxine / thyroid meds** — no documented direct interaction; standard practice is to take thyroid medication on an empty stomach separate from other supplements, so keep GABA to a different time of day
- **Separate from large amino acid doses** — minor transport competition (efficacy, not safety)

### Important Distinction: Phenibut
- **Phenibut is NOT GABA** — it is a separate, BBB-penetrating GABA derivative with serious dependence and dangerous withdrawal potential, and it has significant, hazardous interactions with alcohol and CNS depressants. The interaction profile here applies to GABA supplements, not phenibut. Do not conflate them.

### Alcohol (Notable)
- **Alcohol acts on GABA-A receptors** — combining with GABA (and other sedatives) produces additive sedation and impairment. Avoid the combination, especially before driving or sleep.

### Population Cautions
- **On sedatives or anti-anxiety drugs** — additive sedation; the most relevant caution
- **On blood pressure medication** — monitor for additive BP lowering
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data; consult a healthcare provider
- **Driving / operating machinery** — assess individual drowsiness response, especially when combined with any sedating agent

### Safety Notes
- **The main interaction concern is additive sedation with CNS depressants and alcohol** — not organ toxicity
- **Always inform healthcare providers** of GABA use, especially if taking sedatives, anti-anxiety, or blood-pressure medications
- **This is educational information, not medical advice** — review interactions with a pharmacist or physician
