# L-Tryptophan
## Forms and Bioavailability

### L-Tryptophan (Free-Form Amino Acid)
- **The standard supplement form** — pharmaceutical/USP-grade free-form L-tryptophan in capsules, tablets, or powder
- **Quality is paramount:** Because of the 1989 EMS contamination event (traced to an impurity from one manufacturer's production process, not tryptophan itself), sourcing pharmaceutical-grade, third-party-tested product is especially important. Modern, properly manufactured L-tryptophan is considered safe

### Tryptophan vs 5-HTP (Form Comparison)
- **L-tryptophan:** Upstream precursor; conversion to serotonin is rate-limited and partly diverted to kynurenine/niacin pathways. Gentler and more regulated, but a smaller fraction reaches serotonin — larger doses needed
- **5-HTP:** Downstream metabolite that bypasses the rate-limiting step and crosses the blood-brain barrier without competing with LNAAs — more direct and potent serotonergically per mg. See the 5-htp entry. Both share serotonin-syndrome risk with serotonergic drugs

### Bioavailability and Blood-Brain Barrier Transport
- **LNAA competition:** Tryptophan competes with other large neutral amino acids for transport into the brain. This is the single most important factor in its real-world effectiveness
- **Take away from protein:** Taking tryptophan with a high-protein meal floods the blood with competing amino acids and reduces brain uptake. Taking it on a relatively empty stomach, or with a small amount of carbohydrate (which lowers competing LNAAs via insulin), improves the fraction that reaches the brain
- **Cofactors:** Vitamin B6 supports the conversion of 5-HTP to serotonin; adequate B6, iron, and magnesium support the pathway

### Capsules vs Powder
- **Capsules/tablets:** Convenient, precise dosing, no taste issues
- **Powder:** Cost-effective and flexible; mild taste, dissolves in water

### Storage
- Store sealed, cool, and dry, away from light and moisture
