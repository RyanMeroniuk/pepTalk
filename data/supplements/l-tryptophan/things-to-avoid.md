# L-Tryptophan
## Things to Avoid

### Combining With Serotonergic Medications (CRITICAL)
- **Never combine L-tryptophan with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, tricyclic antidepressants, triptans, tramadol, dextromethorphan, St. John's Wort, or other serotonergic agents** without explicit physician supervision. Adding a serotonin precursor on top of these can cause **serotonin syndrome**, a potentially life-threatening condition. This is the single most important thing to avoid (see drug-interactions and side-effects)
- Also avoid stacking tryptophan with **5-htp** (another serotonin precursor) — together they push serotonin synthesis harder and increase risk

### Daytime Use Before Driving or Focus Work
- Tryptophan can be sedating. Avoid taking it before driving, operating machinery, or tasks requiring full alertness

### Low-Quality / Unverified Products
- Given the historical 1989 EMS contamination event (traced to a manufacturing impurity), use **pharmaceutical-grade, third-party-tested** L-tryptophan. Avoid cheap, unverified sources

### Taking It With a High-Protein Meal
- Competing amino acids block brain uptake. Avoid dosing alongside protein-heavy meals; take it away from protein, optionally with a small carbohydrate

### Excessive Doses
- More is not better. High doses increase GI side effects and, if combined with serotonergic drugs, the danger of serotonin syndrome. Use the lowest effective dose

### Alcohol and Late Caffeine
- Both undermine the sleep and mood benefits tryptophan is used for. Avoid combining, especially in the evening

### Medical Cautions
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** Insufficient safety data — avoid unless directed by a clinician
- **Liver or kidney disease:** Affects amino-acid metabolism — consult a physician
- **Eosinophilic/scleroderma-spectrum conditions:** Historical EMS concern — avoid or use only under medical supervision
- **Bipolar disorder:** Serotonergic effects could theoretically affect mood states — use under medical guidance
- **Before surgery:** Disclose use; serotonergic effects can interact with certain anesthetics and analgesics

### Ignoring Persistent Symptoms
- Stop and seek medical evaluation if you develop unexplained, persistent muscle pain, swelling, or signs of serotonin excess (agitation, rapid heart rate, tremor, sweating, confusion)
