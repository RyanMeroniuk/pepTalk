# L-Tryptophan
## Foods to Avoid

### High-Protein Meals (Timing, Not Elimination)
- Don't take your tryptophan supplement *with* a high-protein meal. The competing large neutral amino acids in protein block tryptophan's transport into the brain, blunting its effect. Separate the supplement from protein-heavy meals (this is about timing the supplement, not avoiding protein in your diet)

### Alcohol
- Alcohol disrupts sleep architecture (especially REM) and serotonin signaling, working against the sleep and mood benefits tryptophan is used for. It also adds CNS depressant load. Avoid combining tryptophan with alcohol, particularly at night

### Excessive Caffeine (Evening)
- Caffeine late in the day opposes the sleep support tryptophan provides and can offset its calming effect. Keep caffeine moderate and early if using tryptophan for sleep

### High-Sugar, Ultra-Processed Diets
- While a *small* carbohydrate can aid brain uptake, a diet built on high-sugar, ultra-processed foods promotes inflammation, which upregulates the kynurenine pathway and diverts tryptophan away from serotonin synthesis — undermining mood benefits

### Tyramine and Serotonergic Caution (If on MAOIs)
- Anyone on an MAOI faces serious risk combining serotonergic precursors with both tyramine-rich foods and the drug itself. This is a medication issue more than a food issue, but it reinforces that tryptophan must not be combined with MAOIs (see drug-interactions)

### Foods That Are Irrelevant (Don't Overthink It)
- Beyond the protein-timing rule, there are no special foods that block tryptophan. Fiber and acidic foods do not meaningfully impair it
- The two practical food rules are simple: **take it away from protein, optionally with a little carbohydrate, and avoid alcohol/late caffeine**

### The Overriding Rule Is About Drugs, Not Food
- The single most important "avoid" for tryptophan is not a food but a drug class: **do not combine with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, or other serotonergic medications** due to serotonin syndrome risk (see drug-interactions)
