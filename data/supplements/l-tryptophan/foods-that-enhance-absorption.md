# L-Tryptophan
## Foods That Enhance Absorption

### Carbohydrates (The Key Enhancer for Brain Uptake)
- **Why it works:** Tryptophan competes with other large neutral amino acids (LNAAs) for transport across the blood-brain barrier. Carbohydrate intake triggers insulin, which drives the competing LNAAs into muscle tissue — leaving tryptophan with less competition and increasing the fraction that reaches the brain
- **Practical approach:** Taking tryptophan with a small carbohydrate snack (e.g., a few crackers, a small piece of fruit, or a little honey) can improve its conversion to brain serotonin
- **Caveat:** Keep it modest — a large, heavy meal or high-sugar load is not the goal; just enough carbohydrate to nudge insulin

### Take Away From High-Protein Meals
- This is the inverse of an "enhancer," but it is the most important practical rule: **avoid taking tryptophan with a high-protein meal**, because the protein supplies competing amino acids that block brain uptake. Separate tryptophan from protein-heavy meals

### Vitamin B6 (Cofactor)
- **Vitamin B6 (pyridoxal-5-phosphate)** is a cofactor for the decarboxylation step that turns 5-HTP into serotonin. Adequate B6 supports efficient conversion. B6-rich foods include bananas, potatoes, poultry, and fortified foods; some supplements pair tryptophan with B6
- Iron and magnesium also support the broader pathway

### Empty or Near-Empty Stomach
- Taking tryptophan when the stomach is relatively empty (with just a small carbohydrate) reduces amino-acid competition and improves brain entry, compared with taking it amid a full protein meal

### Practical Pairing
- **Simplest effective approach for sleep:** Take tryptophan ~30-60 minutes before bed with a small carbohydrate snack and away from a protein-heavy dinner, ensuring adequate B6 status. This optimizes the fraction reaching the brain for serotonin/melatonin synthesis
- Do NOT combine with serotonergic medications regardless of food timing (see drug-interactions)
