# L-Tryptophan
## Natural Food Sources

### Tryptophan Is Abundant in Protein Foods
- Unlike many supplement compounds, L-tryptophan is a normal dietary amino acid found in virtually all protein-containing foods. Typical diets provide roughly **0.5-1.5 g/day**

### High-Protein Sources (Richest)
- **Turkey** — the famous example (though its sleepiness reputation is overstated; see note below). ~250-350 mg per 100 g
- **Chicken** — comparable to turkey
- **Eggs** — high-quality protein with good tryptophan content
- **Cheese and dairy** — cheddar, parmesan, milk, yogurt
- **Fish** (salmon, tuna, cod) and **shellfish**
- **Beef and pork**
- **Soy products** (tofu, tempeh, edamame) — strong plant source
- **Pumpkin and squash seeds** — among the richest seed sources
- **Other seeds and nuts** (sesame, sunflower, cashews, peanuts)
- **Legumes** (lentils, beans, chickpeas)
- **Oats and whole grains** — moderate

### The "Turkey Makes You Sleepy" Myth
- Turkey is often blamed for post-Thanksgiving drowsiness, but its tryptophan content is similar to other meats. More importantly, **eating a large protein meal floods the blood with competing amino acids that REDUCE tryptophan's entry into the brain** — so a high-protein meal does not efficiently raise brain serotonin. Holiday drowsiness is better explained by large meals, carbohydrates, and alcohol

### Food vs Supplement (Key Distinction)
- **From food:** Tryptophan comes packaged with many competing large neutral amino acids (LNAAs), so the fraction reaching the brain is limited — eating tryptophan-rich protein does not reliably boost brain serotonin
- **From a supplement:** Free-form tryptophan taken away from protein (optionally with a small carbohydrate) faces less competition and more reliably raises brain tryptophan/serotonin
- This is why supplementation, timed away from protein, has different effects than simply eating high-tryptophan foods

### Niacin Note
- Tryptophan also contributes to the body's niacin (vitamin B3) supply (~60 mg tryptophan → ~1 mg niacin), so adequate dietary tryptophan supports more than just serotonin
