# L-Tryptophan
## Overview

L-tryptophan is an essential amino acid — meaning the body cannot make it and must obtain it from diet — that serves as the starting material for synthesizing serotonin and, downstream, melatonin. Because of this role, supplemental L-tryptophan is used to support mood and sleep. It sits one step "upstream" of 5-htp in the pathway (tryptophan → 5-HTP → serotonin → melatonin), and this matters: tryptophan's conversion is more tightly regulated by the body and is diverted into other pathways (notably the kynurenine pathway and niacin synthesis), so it tends to raise serotonin more gently and less directly than 5-HTP does. A key practical consideration is that tryptophan competes with other large neutral amino acids (LNAAs) for transport across the blood-brain barrier, which is why it is often taken away from high-protein meals. L-tryptophan also carries an important historical note: a 1989 outbreak of eosinophilia-myalgia syndrome (EMS) was traced to a contaminated batch from a single manufacturer, not to tryptophan itself — modern pharmaceutical-grade product is considered safe, but this history underlines the importance of quality sourcing. Like 5-HTP, tryptophan is serotonergic and carries a real risk of serotonin syndrome when combined with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, or other serotonergic drugs — this must be emphasized. It pairs conceptually with 5-htp and melatonin for sleep and mood support. This content is educational and research-oriented, not medical advice.
