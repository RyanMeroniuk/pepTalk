# L-Tryptophan
## Drug Interactions

### Contraindicated / Dangerous Combinations (Serotonin Syndrome Risk — CRITICAL)
- **MAOIs (monoamine oxidase inhibitors — phenelzine, tranylcypromine, selegiline, isocarboxazid):** **Contraindicated.** Combining a serotonin precursor with an MAOI can cause severe, potentially fatal serotonin syndrome. Do not combine.
- **SSRIs (fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine, citalopram, escitalopram):** High-risk. Adding L-tryptophan on top of an SSRI increases serotonin and can precipitate serotonin syndrome. Do not combine without explicit physician supervision.
- **SNRIs (venlafaxine, duloxetine, desvenlafaxine):** High-risk for serotonin syndrome — same caution as SSRIs. Do not combine without physician supervision.
- **Other serotonergic agents:** Tricyclic antidepressants, triptans (sumatriptan), tramadol, dextromethorphan, linezolid, lithium, and St. John's Wort all add serotonergic load. Combining with tryptophan raises serotonin-syndrome risk. Avoid or use only under medical supervision.
- **5-HTP (and other serotonin precursors):** Do not stack tryptophan with 5-htp — combining two serotonin precursors compounds the risk.

### Moderate Interactions (Use with Caution)
- **Sedatives, benzodiazepines, sleep medications, and other CNS depressants:** Tryptophan is sedating; combined with these it may cause additive drowsiness and impairment. Use caution and avoid additive oversedation.
- **Carbidopa (Parkinson's therapy):** Case reports describe skin/scleroderma-like reactions when tryptophan was combined with carbidopa. Use caution.
- **Serotonergic migraine and pain medications:** Triptans and tramadol (also listed above) — monitor closely; these are common real-world serotonergic co-exposures.

### Mild Interactions (Monitor)
- **Antihypertensives:** Possible additive effects on blood pressure in some individuals; monitor.
- **Drugs affecting the kynurenine/inflammatory pathway:** Inflammation and certain drugs can shift tryptophan metabolism toward kynurenine; clinical relevance is usually minor but worth awareness in inflammatory conditions.

### Timing Considerations
- Take tryptophan away from high-protein meals (amino-acid competition) and hours apart from daytime medications you don't want to compound with sedation. For sleep use, evening dosing is typical.
- Timing separation does NOT make it safe to combine with serotonergic drugs — the serotonin-syndrome risk is pharmacodynamic, not about absorption timing. Avoid the combination entirely unless a physician directs and monitors it.

### Conditions Requiring Caution
- **Pregnancy:** Insufficient safety data — avoid unless directed by a clinician.
- **Breastfeeding:** Insufficient safety data — avoid unless directed by a clinician.
- **Liver disease / kidney disease:** Affects amino-acid metabolism — consult a physician before use.
- **Bipolar disorder:** Serotonergic effects could theoretically influence mood states — use only under medical guidance.
- **Eosinophilic or scleroderma-spectrum conditions:** Historical EMS association — avoid or use only under medical supervision.

### General Guidance
- The defining interaction issue for L-tryptophan is **serotonin syndrome with serotonergic medications (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, and others)** — this is a serious, potentially life-threatening risk and the most important caution. Always disclose tryptophan use to your healthcare providers, and never combine serotonin precursors with serotonergic drugs without physician supervision, especially before surgery (anesthetics/analgesics can be serotonergic).
