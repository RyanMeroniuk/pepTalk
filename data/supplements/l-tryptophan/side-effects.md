# L-Tryptophan
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild)
- **Drowsiness/sedation:** Expected given the serotonin/melatonin pathway — useful at bedtime, undesirable during the day
- **GI upset:** Nausea, stomach discomfort, or loose stools, usually mild and dose-related
- **Headache, dizziness, dry mouth:** Reported by some users
- **Lightheadedness:** Occasionally reported

### Serotonin Syndrome (Serious — Emphasized)
- **The most important risk.** Combining L-tryptophan with serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, tricyclics, triptans, tramadol, dextromethorphan, St. John's Wort, and others) can cause **serotonin syndrome** — a potentially life-threatening excess of serotonin
- **Symptoms:** Agitation, confusion, rapid heart rate, high blood pressure, dilated pupils, muscle twitching/rigidity, heavy sweating, shivering, tremor, diarrhea, and in severe cases high fever, seizures, and loss of consciousness
- **This is a medical emergency.** Do not combine tryptophan with serotonergic medications without physician supervision (see drug-interactions)

### Historical: Eosinophilia-Myalgia Syndrome (EMS)
- In 1989, an outbreak of EMS (a serious condition with severe muscle pain and elevated eosinophils) was linked to L-tryptophan. Investigation traced it to a **contaminant from a single manufacturer's production process**, not to tryptophan itself. The product was temporarily restricted
- **Implication:** Pharmaceutical-grade, third-party-tested tryptophan is considered safe, but this history is why quality sourcing matters and why some people remain cautious

### Other Considerations
- **Daytime impairment:** Because it can be sedating, avoid driving or operating machinery if it makes you drowsy
- **Eosinophilia monitoring:** Given the historical EMS link, persistent unexplained muscle pain or swelling while supplementing warrants stopping and seeking medical evaluation

### When to Be Cautious
- **Anyone on serotonergic medication** — see above; this is the key contraindication
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** Insufficient safety data — avoid unless directed by a clinician
- **Liver or kidney disease:** Affects amino-acid metabolism; consult a physician
- **Scleroderma/eosinophilic conditions:** Historical concern — avoid or use only under medical supervision
- **Before surgery:** Disclose use; serotonergic effects can interact with certain anesthetics/analgesics

### Overall Safety Profile
- At appropriate doses, in healthy individuals NOT on serotonergic drugs, pharmaceutical-grade tryptophan is generally well tolerated. The defining safety issues are serotonin syndrome (drug combinations) and the historical EMS/quality concern
