# L-Tryptophan
## Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Serotonin Synthesis (Primary Pathway)
- L-tryptophan is hydroxylated by tryptophan hydroxylase (TPH) to form **5-HTP (5-hydroxytryptophan)**, which is then decarboxylated to **serotonin (5-HT)**
- Tryptophan hydroxylase is the rate-limiting enzyme of this pathway and is regulated — this is why supplemental tryptophan raises serotonin more gradually and is less likely to overshoot than supplemental 5-HTP, which bypasses this regulated step
- Increased serotonin availability underlies tryptophan's effects on mood, calm, and sleep onset

### 2. Melatonin Synthesis (Sleep)
- Serotonin produced from tryptophan is further converted (via the pineal gland, in darkness) to **melatonin**, the hormone that regulates sleep timing
- This tryptophan → serotonin → melatonin chain is the basis for tryptophan's sleep-supporting reputation

### 3. Blood-Brain Barrier Transport and LNAA Competition
- Tryptophan competes with other large neutral amino acids (LNAAs — e.g., valine, leucine, isoleucine, tyrosine, phenylalanine) for the same transporter into the brain
- A high-protein meal floods the blood with competing LNAAs and can paradoxically reduce tryptophan's entry into the brain; a carbohydrate-containing context raises insulin, which drives competing LNAAs into muscle, increasing the relative ratio of tryptophan that reaches the brain
- This transport competition is a defining feature of tryptophan pharmacology and explains many of its dosing/food recommendations

### 4. Kynurenine Pathway (Major Alternative Fate)
- A large fraction of tryptophan is metabolized down the **kynurenine pathway** (via indoleamine/tryptophan dioxygenase, IDO/TDO), producing kynurenine and downstream metabolites
- Inflammation and cortisol can upregulate this pathway, diverting tryptophan away from serotonin synthesis — relevant to mood in inflammatory states
- This diversion is another reason tryptophan is a less direct serotonin precursor than 5-HTP

### 5. Niacin (Vitamin B3) Synthesis
- Tryptophan can be converted to niacin (NAD+ precursor); roughly 60 mg tryptophan can yield ~1 mg niacin. This is a minor metabolic fate at supplement doses but part of tryptophan's biochemistry

### 6. Cofactor Dependence
- Conversion steps require cofactors including vitamin B6 (for the decarboxylation to serotonin), iron, and others — adequate micronutrient status supports efficient serotonin production
