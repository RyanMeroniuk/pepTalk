# MSM (Methylsulfonylmethane)
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild, Usually Transient)
- **GI upset** — Mild nausea, bloating, diarrhea, or loose stools, most common when starting at high doses. Taking MSM with food and titrating up from a low dose minimizes this.
- **Headache** — Occasionally reported, usually mild and transient during the first week.
- **Mild stimulation/sleep disturbance** — A minority of users report feeling slightly energized; taking MSM earlier in the day resolves this for most.

### Uncommon
- **Skin reactions** — Rare reports of itching or rash.
- **Fatigue or difficulty concentrating** — Uncommon, generally transient.
- **Increased flatulence** — Some individuals experience gas, usually resolving with continued use.

### Excellent Overall Safety Profile
- **Very wide safety margin** — Toxicology studies show MSM is non-toxic at doses far exceeding human supplemental amounts. Human trials up to 6g/day for several months report excellent tolerability.
- **No liver or kidney toxicity** documented at supplemental doses in healthy individuals.
- **Not a stimulant, hormone, or banned substance** — MSM is a naturally occurring sulfur compound and is not prohibited by athletic organizations.

### Special Populations
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — Insufficient safety data for supplemental MSM during pregnancy or breastfeeding. Avoid or use only under medical supervision (precautionary, not because harm is demonstrated).
- **Sulfa allergy confusion** — MSM is sometimes mistakenly avoided by people with sulfonamide (sulfa drug) allergies. MSM is organosulfur, structurally unrelated to sulfa antibiotics, and does not cross-react. A true sulfa allergy is not a contraindication to MSM.
- **Bleeding risk** — Theoretical only; see drug interactions regarding anticoagulants.

### When to Stop
- Persistent GI distress that does not resolve with dose reduction and food
- Any allergic-type reaction (rash, swelling)

### Safety Summary
- One of the better-tolerated joint supplements available
- Mild, dose-dependent GI effects are the main concern, easily managed by titration and taking with food
