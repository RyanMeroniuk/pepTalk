# Copper
## Dosing Protocols

### General Maintenance (With Zinc Supplementation)
- **Dose:** 1-2mg/day copper bisglycinate or copper gluconate
- **Purpose:** Prevent zinc-induced copper depletion
- **When to use:** Anytime you take 15mg+ zinc daily for more than 4 weeks
- **Zinc-to-copper ratio:** Maintain 10:1 to 15:1 (e.g., 30mg zinc = 2-3mg copper)
- **Timing:** Take copper at a DIFFERENT meal than zinc — they compete for absorption. Zinc with breakfast, copper with dinner (or vice versa).

### Zinc-Copper Balancing Examples
| Daily Zinc Dose | Recommended Copper Dose | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| 15mg | 1mg | 15:1 |
| 25mg | 2mg | 12.5:1 |
| 30mg | 2-3mg | 10-15:1 |
| 40mg | 3-4mg | 10-13:1 |
| 50mg+ | 3-5mg (monitor labs) | 10-15:1 |

### Copper Repletion (Correcting Deficiency)
- **Dose:** 2-4mg/day copper bisglycinate for 4-12 weeks
- **Monitoring:** Check serum copper and ceruloplasmin at baseline, 4 weeks, and 8 weeks
- **Target:** Serum copper 70-150 mcg/dL; ceruloplasmin 20-50 mg/dL
- **Anemia resolution:** If copper-deficiency anemia is present, expect improvement in CBC within 4-8 weeks of repletion
- **Neutropenia resolution:** Neutrophil count typically normalizes within 2-4 weeks of copper repletion
- **After repletion:** Drop to maintenance dose of 1-2mg/day

### For GHK-Cu Peptide Users
- **Topical GHK-Cu:** Minimal systemic copper impact — standard maintenance copper supplementation (1-2mg/day) is sufficient if also taking zinc
- **Subcutaneous GHK-Cu:** The copper in GHK-Cu injections is a very small amount and does NOT replace dietary/supplemental copper. Maintain 1-2mg/day copper supplementation independently.
- **If also taking zinc:** This is the most important consideration — ensure you are supplementing copper to offset zinc's depleting effect. GHK-Cu does not provide enough copper to balance zinc supplementation.

### For Those NOT Taking Zinc Supplements
- **Generally not needed:** If eating a varied diet including shellfish, organ meats, nuts, seeds, and legumes, copper supplementation is usually unnecessary
- **Exception:** Malabsorption conditions (Crohn's, celiac, post-bariatric surgery) — consider 1mg/day as insurance
- **Multivitamin check:** Many multivitamins contain 0.5-2mg copper; check your label before adding standalone copper

### Timing Guidelines
- **Separate from zinc:** Take copper and zinc at different meals, ideally 4+ hours apart
- **With food:** Always take copper with a meal to reduce nausea and improve absorption
- **With protein:** Amino acids enhance copper absorption — take with a protein-containing meal
- **Avoid with:** High-dose vitamin C (>500mg), high-dose iron (>45mg), high-phytate meals — all reduce copper absorption. Separate by 2+ hours.
- **Morning vs. evening:** No strong preference, but many people tolerate copper better with dinner (and take zinc with breakfast)

### Upper Limits and Safety
- **RDA:** 900mcg/day (0.9mg) for adults
- **UL:** 10mg/day for adults
- **Practical ceiling:** Most people should stay at 1-3mg/day from supplements
- **Doses above 5mg/day:** Not recommended without medical supervision and lab monitoring
- **Acute toxicity:** Single doses above 10mg can cause nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain
- **Chronic toxicity:** Doses of 3-10mg/day for extended periods can cause liver damage in susceptible individuals (particularly those with Wilson's disease gene carriers)

### Lab Monitoring
- **Who should monitor:**
  - Anyone on >3mg/day copper supplementation
  - Anyone on high-dose zinc (>40mg/day) for extended periods
  - Anyone with symptoms of copper deficiency or excess
  - GHK-Cu peptide users who are also supplementing zinc
- **Labs to order:**
  - Serum copper (normal: 70-150 mcg/dL)
  - Serum ceruloplasmin (normal: 20-50 mg/dL)
  - CBC with differential (to monitor neutrophil count and anemia markers)
  - Liver enzymes (AST, ALT) if concerned about copper excess
- **Frequency:** Baseline, then every 3-6 months if on ongoing supplementation above 2mg/day

### Cycling
- **Not typically required** at maintenance doses (1-2mg/day)
- **For repletion doses (3-5mg/day):** Use for 4-12 weeks then reassess and drop to maintenance
- **Periodic reassessment:** Every 6 months, evaluate whether ongoing copper supplementation is still needed based on zinc intake and dietary copper sources

### Special Populations
- **Pregnancy:** RDA increases to 1mg/day (from 0.9mg); UL remains 10mg/day. Standard prenatal vitamins contain 1-2mg copper.
- **Lactation:** RDA is 1.3mg/day to account for copper in breast milk
- **Children:** Lower RDA (ages 1-3: 340mcg; ages 4-8: 440mcg; ages 9-13: 700mcg) — do not use adult doses
- **Kidney disease:** Copper is primarily excreted via bile, not kidneys, so kidney disease does not typically cause copper accumulation. However, consult with a nephrologist.
- **Liver disease:** Copper is processed and excreted by the liver. Liver disease can impair copper clearance — use lower doses and monitor.
