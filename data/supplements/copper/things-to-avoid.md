# Copper
## Things to Avoid

### Supplementing Without Checking Zinc-Copper Balance
- **Why:** The most important principle in copper supplementation is context — are you taking it to balance zinc, or adding it to an already-adequate copper intake? Supplementing copper when you don't need it can push you into copper excess, which generates oxidative stress.
- **Do instead:** Only supplement copper if (a) you are taking >15mg zinc daily, (b) you have documented copper deficiency, or (c) your diet is very low in copper-rich foods

### Taking Copper and Zinc at the Same Time
- **Why:** Zinc and copper compete for absorption via the same intestinal transport mechanisms. Taking them together reduces absorption of both, but copper is affected more severely.
- **Do instead:** Take zinc and copper at separate meals, at least 4 hours apart (e.g., zinc with breakfast, copper with dinner)

### Taking on an Empty Stomach
- **Why:** Copper supplements, especially non-chelated forms, can cause significant nausea when taken without food. Protein-rich food also enhances copper absorption.
- **Do instead:** Always take copper with a protein-containing meal

### High-Dose Vitamin C at the Same Time
- **Why:** Mega-dose vitamin C (>500mg) can reduce copper bioavailability and potentially generate oxidative stress when excess ascorbate reacts with copper
- **Do instead:** Separate high-dose vitamin C supplements from copper by 2+ hours. Moderate vitamin C from food is fine.

### Supplementing If You Have Wilson's Disease
- **Why:** Wilson's disease is a genetic disorder of copper metabolism that causes pathological copper accumulation in the liver, brain, and cornea. Copper supplementation is absolutely contraindicated and could be life-threatening.
- **Do instead:** If you have a family history of Wilson's disease, get screened (ceruloplasmin, 24-hour urine copper, slit-lamp exam) before any copper supplementation

### Ignoring Symptoms of Copper Excess
- **Symptoms:** Persistent nausea, metallic taste, abdominal pain, new-onset anxiety/insomnia, headaches, mood changes, elevated liver enzymes
- **Why:** Copper has a narrower therapeutic window than many minerals. The gap between "adequate" and "too much" is smaller than for zinc or magnesium.
- **Do instead:** Start at 1mg/day, monitor symptoms, and do not exceed 3mg/day without medical guidance and lab monitoring

### Using Copper Oxide Supplements
- **Why:** Copper oxide has the poorest bioavailability of all supplemental forms (~10-20% absorption). One study found it was essentially not absorbed. Many cheap multivitamins use copper oxide because it has a high elemental copper content by weight.
- **Do instead:** Choose copper bisglycinate, copper gluconate, or copper citrate

### Long-Term High-Dose Zinc Without Copper Co-Supplementation
- **Why:** This is the single most common cause of acquired copper deficiency in the supplement community. Zinc doses of 30-50mg+/day for months to years can cause frank copper deficiency, presenting as unexplained anemia, neutropenia, and eventually myelopathy (spinal cord damage).
- **Do instead:** ALWAYS pair zinc supplementation (>15mg/day) with copper supplementation (1-3mg/day) at a 10:1 to 15:1 ratio

### Self-Treating Neurological Symptoms
- **Why:** Copper deficiency myelopathy (posterior column degeneration) is a serious neurological condition that mimics B12 deficiency. If you develop numbness, tingling, balance problems, or gait changes while taking zinc, seek medical evaluation immediately — do not attempt to self-treat.
- **Do instead:** See a neurologist; get serum copper, ceruloplasmin, B12, and possibly an MRI of the spine

### Relying on GHK-Cu Peptide for Copper Status
- **Why:** GHK-Cu (whether topical or injectable) delivers a very small amount of copper bound to a specific tripeptide. This is a signaling molecule, not a nutritional copper source. It does NOT provide enough copper to maintain systemic copper status.
- **Do instead:** Maintain dietary/supplemental copper independently of GHK-Cu peptide use

### Cooking Exclusively in Unlined Copper Cookware
- **Why:** Cooking acidic foods (tomato sauce, citrus-based dishes, vinegar) in unlined copper pots leaches significant amounts of copper into food — potentially enough to cause toxicity with chronic use
- **Do instead:** Use lined copper cookware (tin or stainless steel lined), or reserve unlined copper for non-acidic preparations

### Ignoring Estrogen-Copper Connection
- **Why:** Estrogen increases ceruloplasmin production and raises serum copper. Women on oral contraceptives, hormone replacement therapy, or who are pregnant may already have elevated copper levels. Additional supplementation could push copper status too high.
- **Do instead:** Women on estrogen therapy should check serum copper before supplementing. During pregnancy, rely on the copper in prenatal vitamins (typically 1-2mg) without additional standalone copper supplements.

### Drinking Water from Corroded Copper Pipes Without Testing
- **Why:** Older homes with corroded copper pipes, especially when water is acidic (low pH), can leach significant copper into drinking water — sometimes exceeding the EPA's action level of 1.3 ppm
- **Do instead:** If you have copper pipes and acidic well water, test your water for copper content. Run the tap for 30 seconds before drinking to flush standing water from pipes.

### Combining Multiple Copper Sources Without Accounting
- **Sources to tally:** Standalone copper supplement + multivitamin copper + copper in zinc-copper combination supplements + copper in food + copper from water
- **Why:** It is easy to accidentally double or triple copper intake by not realizing multiple supplements contain it
- **Do instead:** Add up ALL sources of supplemental copper before determining your standalone copper dose
