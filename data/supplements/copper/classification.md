# Copper
## Classification

- **Category:** Essential Trace Mineral
- **Type:** Micronutrient
- **Essentiality:** Cannot be synthesized by the body — must be obtained through diet or supplementation
- **Chemical Symbol:** Cu (from Latin: cuprum)
- **Atomic Number:** 29
- **Biologically active forms:** Cu+ (cuprous, reduced) and Cu2+ (cupric, oxidized); redox cycling between these states is central to copper's enzymatic roles
- **Biological Role:** Cofactor for over 30 enzymes (cuproenzymes); essential for energy production, iron metabolism, connective tissue formation, neurotransmitter synthesis, and antioxidant defense
- **Supplement Class:** Mineral supplement
- **Regulation:** Available over-the-counter as a dietary supplement; regulated by the FDA under DSHEA
- **RDA:** 900mcg/day (0.9mg) for adults (both men and women)
- **Upper Tolerable Intake Level (UL):** 10mg/day for adults
- **Key relationship:** Copper and zinc have an inverse relationship — excess zinc depletes copper, and proper balance is critical for health
- **Peptide connection:** Copper is the metallic component of GHK-Cu (copper peptide), a key peptide in the PepTalk database for skin regeneration and anti-aging
