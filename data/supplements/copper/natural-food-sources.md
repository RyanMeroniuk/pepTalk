### Daily Copper Need from Food (Examples)
To meet the 0.9mg RDA from food alone:
- **Option 1:** 3oz beef liver (12.4mg — more than enough for the week)
- **Option 2:** 1oz cashews (0.6mg) + 1 cup lentils (0.5mg) = 1.1mg
- **Option 3:** 3oz oysters (4.8mg — one serving covers several days)
- **Option 4:** 1oz dark chocolate (0.5mg) + 1 cup shiitake mushrooms (1.3mg) = 1.8mg

### Important Notes

#### Copper-Rich Diet vs. Supplementation
- **Most people eating a varied diet** that includes nuts, seeds, legumes, and occasional shellfish or organ meats will meet the RDA without supplementation
- **The primary reason to supplement** is when taking zinc at doses >15-30mg/day, which depletes copper regardless of dietary intake
- **Vegetarians/vegans:** Can meet copper needs through nuts, seeds, legumes, mushrooms, and dark chocolate — copper is actually easier to obtain on a plant-based diet than zinc

#### Cooking and Preparation Effects
- **Copper cookware:** Cooking acidic foods (tomatoes, vinegar) in unlined copper pots can add significant copper to food — enough to potentially cause toxicity with chronic use. Lined copper cookware does not have this issue.
- **Cooking losses:** Moderate — some copper leaches into cooking water. Using cooking liquids (soups, stews) retains copper.
- **Soaking and sprouting:** Reduces phytates that bind copper, improving bioavailability from grains and legumes

#### Soil and Regional Variation
- **Copper content of plant foods** varies based on soil copper levels
- **Animal foods** have more consistent copper content regardless of geography (the animal concentrates it)
- **Water supply:** Some areas have copper pipes that contribute trace amounts to drinking water (usually safe but can be measured if concerned)
