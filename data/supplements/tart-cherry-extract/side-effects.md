# Tart Cherry Extract
## Side Effects

- **Gastrointestinal discomfort** — The high sorbitol content in tart cherry juice can cause bloating, gas, abdominal cramps, and diarrhea in sensitive individuals. Sorbitol is a sugar alcohol that draws water into the intestines. Start with half doses and increase gradually. Capsule forms largely avoid this issue.
- **Blood sugar impact** — Tart cherry juice concentrate contains significant natural sugars (12-17g per 30ml serving). Diabetics and those managing blood sugar should prefer capsule forms or account for the carbohydrate content. The glycemic impact is moderate, with the polyphenols partially offsetting the sugar load.
- **Possible headache** — Rare reports of headache, potentially related to the natural tyramine content in cherries. Relevant for individuals sensitive to tyramine or those on MAO inhibitors.
- **Allergic reactions** — Uncommon but possible. Individuals with stone fruit allergies (peaches, plums, apricots) may cross-react with cherry products. Symptoms could include oral itching, hives, or more severe allergic responses.
- **Mild sedation** — Due to the melatonin and polyphenol content, some people notice drowsiness. Avoid driving or operating heavy machinery until you understand your personal response, especially at higher doses.
- **Kidney stone concern (theoretical)** — Tart cherries contain moderate oxalate levels. Individuals with a history of calcium-oxalate kidney stones should be aware, though the overall oxalate load from supplement doses is typically modest.
- **Weight gain potential** — Long-term daily consumption of tart cherry juice (not capsules) adds 100-140 calories per day if taking the common twice-daily protocol. This is unlikely to cause weight gain in most people but should be factored into caloric intake.
- **Drug interactions** — See drug-interactions.md for detailed information. Key concerns include blood thinners, diabetes medications, and sedative drugs.

**Overall safety profile:** Tart cherry extract is very well-tolerated in most people. It has been used safely in clinical trials lasting up to 12 weeks. The most common complaints relate to the GI effects of the juice form, which are dose-dependent and manageable.
