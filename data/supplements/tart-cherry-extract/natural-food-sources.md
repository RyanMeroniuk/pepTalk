# Tart Cherry Extract
## Natural Food Sources

- **Montmorency tart cherries (fresh)** — The gold standard variety. Grown primarily in Michigan, USA (which produces ~75% of US tart cherries). Available fresh in limited season (mid-July to early August). Approximately 100 cherries provide a research-equivalent dose of anthocyanins. Extremely tart — not typically eaten fresh like sweet cherries.
- **Frozen Montmorency tart cherries** — Available year-round. Flash-frozen at harvest, retaining most anthocyanins and nutrients. Add to smoothies, yogurt, or thaw for baking. A practical way to get tart cherries outside the brief fresh season.
- **Balaton tart cherries** — A Hungarian variety grown in the US. Slightly sweeter than Montmorency with deep red flesh. Contains anthocyanins and melatonin, though at somewhat lower concentrations than Montmorency.
- **Morello cherries** — A European sour cherry variety common in traditional preserves and baking. Contains anthocyanins and melatonin but less studied than Montmorency for specific health benefits.
- **Other anthocyanin-rich foods** — While not substitutes for tart cherry's unique melatonin + anthocyanin combination, these foods provide related benefits:
  - **Blueberries** — High in anthocyanins (malvidin-based), excellent for cognitive health
  - **Blackberries** — Rich in cyanidin-3-glucoside, the same primary anthocyanin in tart cherries
  - **Black currants** — Exceptionally high anthocyanin content
  - **Purple/black grapes** — Anthocyanins plus resveratrol
  - **Pomegranate** — Punicalagins and anthocyanins with anti-inflammatory effects
  - **Red cabbage** — Surprisingly high anthocyanin content among vegetables
  - **Elderberries** — Very high anthocyanin density, also supports immune function

**Melatonin-containing foods (for comparison):**

- **Pistachios** — Highest known melatonin content of any food (~233mcg/g in some analyses)
- **Tart cherries** — ~13.5ng/g melatonin
- **Walnuts** — Contain melatonin and increase serum melatonin levels
- **Tomatoes** — Small amounts of melatonin
- **Grapes and wine** — Contain melatonin, highest in Nebbiolo and Merlot varieties
- **Mushrooms** — Some varieties contain melatonin
