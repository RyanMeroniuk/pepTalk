# Tart Cherry Extract
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Tart cherry juice concentrate** — The most studied form. Typically diluted 30ml (1 oz) concentrate in water. Provides the full spectrum of anthocyanins, melatonin, and phenolics. The Montmorency variety specifically is used in most clinical research. Higher sugar content than capsules (approximately 15-20g per serving). Best for those who want the whole-food matrix and don't mind the calories.
- **Tart cherry juice (not from concentrate)** — 8-12 oz of 100% tart cherry juice. Lower concentration than the concentrate, requiring larger volumes. More expensive per active dose. Taste is very tart; some people blend with other juices.
- **Freeze-dried tart cherry capsules** — Concentrated powder in capsule form, typically 480-1,000mg per capsule. Retains most anthocyanins and phenolics. Melatonin content may be reduced during processing. Convenient, calorie-free alternative to juice. Best for those managing sugar intake.
- **Tart cherry powder** — Freeze-dried or spray-dried powder that can be added to smoothies, yogurt, or water. Quality varies significantly by manufacturer. Look for Montmorency variety, freeze-dried processing, and standardized anthocyanin content.
- **CherryPURE (branded extract)** — A patented tart cherry skin powder used in several clinical trials. Standardized to anthocyanin content. Available in supplement form, often 480mg per capsule equivalent to approximately 60 cherries.

**Bioavailability considerations:**

- **Anthocyanin absorption** — Anthocyanins are absorbed rapidly but have relatively low bioavailability (1-5% of ingested dose reaches plasma). However, their metabolites remain bioactive and continue to exert anti-inflammatory effects.
- **Melatonin absorption** — The naturally occurring melatonin in tart cherry is well-absorbed, with studies showing significant increases in urinary 6-sulfatoxymelatonin (melatonin metabolite) after consumption.
- **Food matrix effect** — The polyphenol matrix of tart cherry may enhance melatonin bioavailability compared to synthetic melatonin alone. The combination of compounds works synergistically.
- **Fat pairing** — Anthocyanins are somewhat lipophilic. Taking tart cherry with a small amount of healthy fat may improve absorption, though this is less critical than for fat-soluble vitamins.
- **Montmorency vs. other varieties** — Montmorency tart cherries have the highest documented melatonin and anthocyanin content. Balaton and other sour cherry varieties also contain these compounds but at lower concentrations. Sweet cherries (Bing, Rainier) have significantly less.
- **Processing matters** — Heat processing degrades anthocyanins. Freeze-dried and cold-pressed products retain more active compounds than heat-pasteurized juices. Look for products that specify minimal heat processing.
