# Tart Cherry Extract
## Overview

Tart cherry extract, specifically from the **Montmorency variety** (Prunus cerasus), is one of the richest natural sources of melatonin combined with potent anti-inflammatory anthocyanins. This dual action makes it uniquely valuable for both sleep support and exercise recovery.

**Why it matters:**

- **Natural melatonin source** — Montmorency tart cherries contain significant amounts of naturally occurring melatonin (up to 13.5ng/g). Unlike synthetic melatonin supplements, the melatonin in tart cherry is delivered alongside a matrix of synergistic polyphenols that enhance its bioavailability and effectiveness.
- **Powerful anti-inflammatory anthocyanins** — The deep red pigment in tart cherries comes from anthocyanins (primarily cyanidin-3-glucoside and cyanidin-3-rutinoside), which inhibit COX-1 and COX-2 enzymes similarly to ibuprofen and naproxen — but without the gastrointestinal side effects.
- **Exercise recovery** — Multiple clinical trials demonstrate that tart cherry juice reduces muscle soreness, strength loss, and inflammatory markers (CRP, IL-6) following intense exercise. Marathon runners, resistance athletes, and weekend warriors all show measurable recovery benefits.
- **Sleep quality improvement** — Clinical studies show tart cherry juice increases sleep time by an average of 84 minutes and improves sleep efficiency. The combination of melatonin and anti-inflammatory compounds addresses both circadian signaling and the inflammation-insomnia connection.
- **Uric acid reduction** — Tart cherry has documented uric acid-lowering effects, making it relevant for gout management and prevention. It inhibits xanthine oxidase and enhances renal uric acid excretion.
- **Antioxidant density** — Tart cherries rank among the highest ORAC (Oxygen Radical Absorbance Capacity) fruits, with antioxidant activity comparable to blueberries and superior to sweet cherries.
- **Whole-food advantage** — Unlike isolated melatonin supplements, tart cherry provides the full spectrum of bioactive compounds. The synergy between melatonin, anthocyanins, proanthocyanidins, and phenolic acids may explain why tart cherry outperforms equivalent doses of synthetic melatonin in some studies.
