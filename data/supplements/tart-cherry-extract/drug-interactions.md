# Tart Cherry Extract
## Drug Interactions

- **Anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs (warfarin, heparin, clopidogrel, aspirin)** — Tart cherry anthocyanins have mild antiplatelet activity and may potentiate blood-thinning medications. Monitor INR more frequently if taking warfarin. Inform your prescriber about tart cherry supplementation.
- **NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin)** — Tart cherry inhibits the same COX enzymes as NSAIDs. While this is generally additive rather than dangerous, combining high-dose tart cherry with regular NSAID use could theoretically increase bleeding risk or GI irritation. For athletes, tart cherry may be a partial NSAID replacement.
- **Diabetes medications (insulin, metformin, sulfonylureas)** — Tart cherry juice contains significant sugars that could affect blood glucose management. Anthocyanins also have mild glucose-lowering effects. Monitor blood sugar more closely when starting tart cherry. Capsule forms avoid the sugar concern.
- **MAO inhibitors (phenelzine, tranylcypromine, selegiline)** — Cherries contain small amounts of tyramine. While typically insufficient to trigger a hypertensive crisis, individuals on MAO inhibitors should exercise caution and start with small amounts.
- **Sedative medications (benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, barbiturates)** — The melatonin content of tart cherry, while modest compared to supplements, adds to the sedative burden when combined with prescription sleep aids. Use caution when initiating tart cherry alongside existing sedative prescriptions.
- **Melatonin supplements** — Combining tart cherry (which contains natural melatonin) with synthetic melatonin supplements can lead to excessive melatonin intake. If using both, reduce the melatonin supplement dose or use tart cherry as a replacement.
- **Gout medications (allopurinol, colchicine, febuxostat)** — Tart cherry inhibits xanthine oxidase through a similar mechanism to allopurinol. This interaction is generally beneficial (additive uric acid reduction) but could theoretically increase the risk of hypouricemia. Inform your rheumatologist.
- **Immunosuppressants** — Anthocyanins have immunomodulatory effects. While generally mild, individuals on immunosuppressive therapy should discuss tart cherry supplementation with their physician.
- **Iron supplements** — Polyphenols in tart cherry can significantly reduce non-heme iron absorption. Separate tart cherry from iron supplements by at least 2 hours.
- **Statin medications** — Some evidence suggests anthocyanins may interact with CYP3A4 metabolism. While interactions at dietary doses are unlikely, inform your physician if taking high-dose tart cherry extract alongside statins.

**Pre-surgical precaution:** Discontinue tart cherry supplementation 7-14 days before scheduled surgery due to mild antiplatelet effects. Inform your surgical team.
