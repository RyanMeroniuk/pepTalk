# Ashwagandha
## Natural Food Sources

- **Ashwagandha root:** The primary source. Not commonly consumed as a food in Western diets but used extensively in Ayurvedic culinary preparations:
  - **Ashwagandha churna (powder):** Dried, ground root powder — the traditional preparation. Mixed into warm milk with honey, ghee, and spices (cardamom, cinnamon). This "ashwagandha milk" or "moon milk" is a traditional Ayurvedic tonic
  - **Fresh root:** Occasionally available in Indian markets. Can be boiled into a decoction or added to soups
  - **Root tea/decoction:** Simmered root pieces or powder in water for 10-15 minutes. Earthy, slightly bitter taste

- **Ashwagandha berries:** The fruit of the plant contains withanolides but is less studied than the root. Traditionally used in Ayurvedic preparations. Not commercially available as a supplement

- **Ashwagandha leaves:** Used in some traditional preparations and poultices. Higher in withaferin A than the root. Not recommended as a primary food source for the reasons discussed in forms and bioavailability

- **No meaningful dietary equivalent:** Unlike some supplements (e.g., omega-3 from fish, vitamin C from citrus), there is no common food that provides meaningful amounts of withanolides. Ashwagandha is a supplementation-dependent compound — you must take a supplement or prepare the root directly

- **Traditional Ayurvedic preparations:**
  - **Ashwagandharishta:** A fermented liquid preparation of ashwagandha used in traditional Ayurvedic medicine
  - **Ashwagandha ghee:** Root cooked into clarified butter for enhanced absorption of fat-soluble withanolides
  - **Ashwagandha lehyam:** A paste-like preparation mixed with honey and other herbs

- **Modern food products:** Ashwagandha powder is increasingly added to smoothie powders, adaptogenic coffee blends, protein bars, and functional foods, but doses are typically sub-therapeutic (50-100mg vs. the 300-600mg used in clinical trials)
