# Ashwagandha
## Foods to Avoid

- **Excessive caffeine:** Ashwagandha modulates the stress response and reduces cortisol. High caffeine intake (400mg+/day) stimulates the HPA axis and raises cortisol, directly opposing ashwagandha's primary mechanism. Moderate caffeine (1-2 cups of coffee) is generally fine and may even complement the alertness benefits, but excessive caffeine undermines the adaptogenic effect
- **Alcohol (in excess):** Ashwagandha has mild sedative/GABAergic properties. Combining with alcohol can potentiate sedation. Additionally, heavy alcohol use stresses the liver, and ashwagandha is metabolized hepatically. Moderate alcohol is unlikely to be problematic, but avoid heavy drinking
- **High-sugar and highly processed foods:** These drive inflammation, insulin resistance, and cortisol dysregulation — the very conditions ashwagandha is trying to correct. Consuming a diet high in refined sugar and processed foods reduces the net benefit of supplementation
- **Excessive raw cruciferous vegetables (if thyroid-sensitive):** For individuals using ashwagandha specifically for thyroid support, large amounts of raw goitrogenic vegetables (raw kale, raw broccoli, raw cabbage, raw Brussels sprouts) contain compounds that can interfere with thyroid hormone synthesis. Cooking largely eliminates goitrogens. This is only relevant for those specifically managing thyroid issues
- **Grapefruit and grapefruit juice (theoretical):** Grapefruit inhibits CYP3A4 enzymes. Some withanolides may be metabolized by CYP3A4. The interaction is theoretical and not well-characterized, but if you are taking ashwagandha alongside other CYP3A4-sensitive medications, be aware of this potential compounding effect
- **Nightshade-reactive individuals:** If you are sensitive to nightshades (tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, potatoes), ashwagandha belongs to the same botanical family (Solanaceae) and may trigger similar reactions. Monitor for joint pain, GI upset, or inflammatory symptoms
