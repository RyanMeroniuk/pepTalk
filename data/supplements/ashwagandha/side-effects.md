# Ashwagandha
## Side Effects

- **Anhedonia and emotional blunting (important concern):** Some users report decreased emotional range, reduced motivation, feeling "flat," or loss of pleasure with prolonged continuous use (typically after 2-3+ months). This is likely related to sustained cortisol suppression and GABAergic modulation reducing both stress AND the emotional engagement that comes with normal cortisol fluctuation. This effect is reversible upon discontinuation and is a key reason cycling is recommended. Not everyone experiences this — it appears more common in individuals who are not chronically stressed (i.e., those whose cortisol is already normal)
- **Thyroid stimulation:** Ashwagandha meaningfully increases T3 and T4 thyroid hormones. This is beneficial for hypothyroid individuals but potentially dangerous for:
  - **Hyperthyroidism/Graves' disease:** Contraindicated — can worsen hyperthyroid symptoms
  - **Hashimoto's thyroiditis:** Complex — may help or worsen depending on disease stage. Use only under endocrinologist supervision
  - **Those on thyroid medication (levothyroxine/Synthroid):** May alter medication requirements. Monitor labs closely
- **GI discomfort:** Nausea, stomach upset, diarrhea, or cramping — usually mild and often resolves by taking with food. More common at higher doses or on an empty stomach
- **Drowsiness/sedation:** Particularly with Sensoril or higher doses. Can impair alertness if taken in the morning for sensitive individuals. Generally considered a benefit when taken at bedtime
- **Headache:** Reported in some clinical trials, usually transient and mild
- **Liver concerns (rare but serious):** A small number of case reports have documented liver injury (elevated liver enzymes, jaundice) associated with ashwagandha, particularly with high doses or products of uncertain quality. While causality is debated, individuals with liver disease should use caution. Discontinue if you develop jaundice, dark urine, or upper abdominal pain
- **Immune stimulation:** Ashwagandha stimulates immune function, which is contraindicated in autoimmune conditions where the immune system is already overactive (lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, multiple sclerosis). May worsen flares
- **Nightshade sensitivity:** Ashwagandha is in the Solanaceae (nightshade) family. Individuals with nightshade sensitivities may react to ashwagandha
- **Pregnancy:** Traditionally classified as an abortifacient in high doses. Contraindicated during pregnancy
- **Blood sugar reduction:** Can lower blood glucose, which is beneficial for diabetics but may cause hypoglycemia in combination with diabetes medications. Monitor blood sugar
