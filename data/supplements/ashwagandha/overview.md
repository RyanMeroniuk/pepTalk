# Ashwagandha
## Overview

- **What it is:** Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is a small evergreen shrub native to India, the Middle East, and parts of Africa. The root and, to a lesser extent, the leaves contain bioactive withanolides that give the plant its medicinal properties. It is classified as a rasayana (rejuvenator) in Ayurvedic medicine and as an adaptogen in Western herbal medicine — meaning it helps the body resist and adapt to physical, chemical, and biological stressors.
- **Historical use:** One of the most important herbs in Ayurveda, used for over 3,000 years. Traditionally prescribed for stress resilience, vitality, longevity, reproductive health, and as a general tonic. The name "ashwagandha" translates to "smell of the horse" in Sanskrit, referring both to the root's odor and the traditional belief that it imparts the strength and vigor of a horse.
- **Why it matters:** Ashwagandha is one of the most clinically studied adaptogens, with robust evidence for cortisol reduction, stress/anxiety relief, testosterone support, thyroid modulation, and physical performance enhancement. In an era of chronic stress, it addresses the HPA (hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal) axis dysfunction that underlies many modern health complaints.
- **Modern research surge:** Since 2010, the number of human clinical trials on ashwagandha has grown dramatically. Standardized extracts like KSM-66 and Sensoril have enabled consistent dosing across studies, producing a strong evidence base that distinguishes ashwagandha from many other herbal supplements.
- **Important caution:** While generally well-tolerated, ashwagandha has clinically meaningful effects on thyroid hormones (can increase T3 and T4) and may cause emotional blunting or anhedonia with prolonged use in some individuals. These are not trivial considerations and are addressed in the side effects section.
