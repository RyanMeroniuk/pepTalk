# Ashwagandha
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **KSM-66 (root extract) — gold standard:**
  - **Extraction:** Full-spectrum root extract using a proprietary milk-based extraction process (no alcohol or chemical solvents)
  - **Standardization:** Minimum 5% withanolides by HPLC
  - **Why it's preferred:** Most clinically studied ashwagandha extract — used in 24+ human clinical trials. Root-only extract (no leaves), which matters because withaferin A is more concentrated in leaves and may have cytotoxic properties at higher doses. Consistent potency batch to batch
  - **Dose:** Typically 300-600mg/day
  - **Best for:** Stress, anxiety, testosterone, physical performance, cognitive function

- **Sensoril (root + leaf extract):**
  - **Extraction:** Aqueous extraction from both roots and leaves
  - **Standardization:** Minimum 10% withanolides (higher than KSM-66 due to leaf inclusion)
  - **Characteristics:** Higher total withanolide content but includes more withaferin A from leaves. Tends to be more calming/sedating than KSM-66, making it better for sleep and acute anxiety. Some concerns about long-term leaf extract safety (withaferin A cytotoxicity), though clinical trials have not shown issues at recommended doses
  - **Dose:** Typically 125-250mg/day (lower dose needed due to higher withanolide concentration)
  - **Best for:** Sleep, acute anxiety, calming effect

- **Generic/unbranded root powder:**
  - **Standardization:** Highly variable — may range from 1.5-5% withanolides or not standardized at all
  - **Issues:** Inconsistent potency, potential adulteration, often bulked with fillers. Impossible to compare dosing to clinical trial results. Some products use leaf powder marketed as root
  - **Use case:** Acceptable for traditional Ayurvedic preparation (churna mixed with warm milk) but not recommended for reliable therapeutic effects

- **Ashwagandha leaf extract (avoid for daily use):**
  - Contains significantly more withaferin A, which has cytotoxic/pro-apoptotic properties. While this is studied for anti-cancer applications, it is not desirable for general supplementation. Some cheaper products use leaf extract because it's cheaper to produce
  - **Rule of thumb:** Choose root extract (KSM-66) or root + leaf at studied doses (Sensoril) — avoid pure leaf extracts

- **Gummy and liquid forms:** Convenient but often under-dosed (100-150mg vs. the 300-600mg used in studies). May contain added sugars and artificial ingredients. Check actual withanolide content, not just total ashwagandha weight

- **Bioavailability considerations:** Ashwagandha withanolides are reasonably well-absorbed orally. Fat-soluble withanolides benefit from being taken with food containing fat. Some newer formulations use cyclodextrin complexes or liposomal delivery to enhance absorption, but the standard extracts (KSM-66, Sensoril) have proven bioavailability in clinical trials without enhancement
