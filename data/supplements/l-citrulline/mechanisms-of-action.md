# L-Citrulline
## Mechanisms of Action

### 1. Arginine and Nitric Oxide Production (Primary Mechanism)
- **Citrulline is converted to L-arginine in the kidneys** via the enzymes argininosuccinate synthase and argininosuccinate lyase
- **L-arginine is the substrate for nitric oxide synthase (NOS)**, which produces nitric oxide (NO)
- **NO is a potent vasodilator** — it relaxes vascular smooth muscle, widening blood vessels and increasing blood flow
- This increased blood flow delivers more oxygen and nutrients to working muscle and improves waste/metabolite clearance

### 2. Why Citrulline Beats Arginine
- **Oral arginine undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism** — gut bacteria and the enzyme arginase break it down before it reaches systemic circulation, blunting its NO effect
- **Citrulline bypasses this** — it is not significantly degraded by intestinal/hepatic arginase and is efficiently converted to arginine downstream
- **Result:** A given dose of citrulline raises plasma arginine MORE than the same dose of arginine itself

### 3. Ammonia Clearance via the Urea Cycle
- **Citrulline is a key intermediate in the urea cycle**, the pathway that detoxifies ammonia
- During intense exercise, ammonia accumulates and contributes to fatigue
- By supporting urea cycle flux, citrulline aids ammonia clearance, helping reduce the central and peripheral fatigue associated with ammonia buildup

### 4. The Malate Component (Citrulline Malate)
- **Malic acid (malate) is a tricarboxylic acid (TCA / Krebs) cycle intermediate**
- Malate may support aerobic energy production and the regeneration of ATP
- This is part of the rationale for citrulline malate's use in performance, though the independent contribution of malate is debated

### 5. Vasodilation and Blood Pressure
- **Enhanced NO availability lowers vascular resistance**, producing a modest reduction in blood pressure
- Improves endothelial function — the health and responsiveness of the blood vessel lining
- Particularly relevant in older adults and those with endothelial dysfunction

### 6. Enhanced Nutrient and Oxygen Delivery ("The Pump")
- **Increased blood flow to muscle** creates the sustained fullness and vascularity ("pump") during and after training
- Beyond aesthetics, improved perfusion supports nutrient delivery and may aid recovery
- Synergizes with substrates like creatine that depend on muscle delivery

### 7. Growth Hormone and Recovery (Secondary)
- Some evidence suggests citrulline (via arginine) may modestly support growth hormone response to exercise, though this effect is smaller and less consistent than the vascular effects
- Reduced post-exercise soreness has been reported with citrulline malate in several trials
