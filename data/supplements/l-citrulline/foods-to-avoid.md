# L-Citrulline
## Foods to Avoid

### There Are No Foods That Block Citrulline
- **No common food meaningfully impairs citrulline absorption** — it is water-soluble and efficiently converted to arginine
- **Acidic foods, fiber, and fat do not interfere** with citrulline uptake

### Things That Undermine the Nitric Oxide Effect
- **High-oxidative-stress diets (heavily fried, ultra-processed foods)** — excess oxidative stress degrades nitric oxide, blunting the very effect citrulline is taken to produce
- **Excessive alcohol** — impairs endothelial function and NO signaling, working against citrulline's vascular benefits
- **Antiseptic mouthwash (not a food, but relevant)** — kills the oral bacteria that convert dietary nitrate to nitrite, reducing the complementary nitrate→NO pathway. If you rely on dietary nitrates alongside citrulline, frequent antibacterial mouthwash can blunt that pathway.

### High-Sodium / Processed Diets
- **Not a direct chemical interaction**, but a high-sodium, processed diet works against the blood-pressure benefits citrulline provides
- For vascular/BP goals, pair citrulline with a whole-food, lower-sodium diet

### Timing With Other Supplements
- **Citrulline can be taken with virtually anything** — no significant supplement-supplement absorption conflicts
- **Stacks freely with creatine, beta-alanine, caffeine, and most pre-workout ingredients**
- **Arginine** — redundant to combine large doses of both, since citrulline raises arginine more effectively anyway; no harm, just unnecessary

### Bottom Line
- There is no food that chemically blocks citrulline. The real "avoid" list is **oxidative, ultra-processed, high-sodium, high-alcohol patterns** that degrade nitric oxide and oppose citrulline's vascular and blood-pressure benefits.
