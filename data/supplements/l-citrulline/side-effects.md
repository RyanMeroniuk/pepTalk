# L-Citrulline
## Side Effects

### Common (Mild, Rare)
- **Very well tolerated** — citrulline has an excellent safety profile and rarely causes side effects at recommended doses
- **Mild GI discomfort** — possible at high single doses (8+ g), usually minor; citrulline is actually gentler on the gut than equivalent arginine, which more commonly causes GI upset and diarrhea

### Uncommon
- **Mild drop in blood pressure** — because citrulline lowers BP via nitric oxide, sensitive individuals or those on antihypertensives may notice lightheadedness; usually benign
- **Tart/sour taste** — citrulline malate in particular is quite sour in powder form; not a side effect but a common complaint

### Notable Advantage Over Arginine
- **Far better GI tolerance than L-arginine** — arginine at effective NO doses frequently causes nausea, cramping, and diarrhea; citrulline achieves higher plasma arginine with much less GI distress. This is a major practical reason to choose citrulline.

### Population-Specific Cautions
- **Low blood pressure / on antihypertensives** — additive BP-lowering; monitor and consult a physician
- **PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil/Viagra, tadalafil/Cialis) and nitrates** — citrulline enhances the same NO pathway these drugs act on; combining can excessively lower blood pressure (see drug-interactions)
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data for supplemental doses; consult a healthcare provider

### Safety Profile Summary
- **No established toxic dose** in healthy individuals; excess is handled by normal metabolism and renal excretion
- **No liver toxicity** at supplemental doses
- **Not banned** by WADA or athletic organizations
- **Not a stimulant** — does not cause jitteriness, elevated heart rate, or insomnia; safe to use in the evening

### When to Seek Guidance
- If you have **cardiovascular disease, low blood pressure**, take **nitrates or PDE5 inhibitors**, or are **pregnant/breastfeeding**, discuss with a healthcare provider before use
