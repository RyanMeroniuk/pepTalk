# Selenium
## Drug Interactions

### Anticoagulants / Blood Thinners
- **Warfarin (Coumadin):** Selenium may have mild antiplatelet effects; high-dose selenium (>200mcg/day) could theoretically increase bleeding risk when combined with warfarin
- **Aspirin, Clopidogrel, DOACs:** Monitor for increased bruising or bleeding if supplementing selenium above 200mcg/day
- **Recommendation:** Standard doses (100-200mcg) are generally safe; inform your prescribing physician

### Cholesterol-Lowering Medications (Statins)
- **HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin):** Some evidence suggests selenium combined with other antioxidants (vitamin E, C, beta-carotene) may blunt the HDL-raising effects of statin-niacin combinations (HATS trial, Brown et al., 2001)
- **Mechanism:** Antioxidant combinations may interfere with the oxidative signaling pathways that statins use to upregulate HDL
- **Recommendation:** Standard-dose selenium alone is unlikely to be problematic; avoid mega-dose antioxidant cocktails alongside statin therapy without physician guidance

### Chemotherapy Drugs
- **Cisplatin, carboplatin:** Selenium may reduce cisplatin nephrotoxicity by scavenging ROS, but there is concern that antioxidants could theoretically reduce chemotherapy efficacy
- **General principle:** Some oncologists recommend avoiding antioxidant supplements during active chemotherapy; others argue selenium specifically may be protective. This is actively debated.
- **Recommendation:** Do NOT supplement selenium during chemotherapy without explicit oncologist approval

### Immunosuppressants
- **Cyclosporine, tacrolimus, mycophenolate:** Selenium enhances immune function, which could theoretically counteract immunosuppressive therapy in transplant recipients or autoimmune patients on immunosuppressants
- **Recommendation:** Use with caution and physician oversight in transplant recipients

### Thyroid Medications
- **Levothyroxine (Synthroid):** Selenium does not directly interact with levothyroxine absorption but affects thyroid hormone metabolism by supporting T4-to-T3 conversion via deiodinase enzymes
- **Methimazole / PTU (antithyroid drugs):** Selenium supplementation (200mcg/day) has been shown to benefit Graves' disease patients alongside antithyroid drugs (EUGOGO trial)
- **Recommendation:** Selenium is generally beneficial for thyroid patients but may necessitate thyroid medication dose adjustments over time — monitor TSH, free T3, free T4

### Barbiturates and Sedatives
- **Phenobarbital:** May increase selenium excretion, potentially lowering selenium levels with chronic use
- **Recommendation:** Consider selenium status monitoring in patients on long-term barbiturate therapy

### Niacin (Vitamin B3)
- **Interaction:** The HATS trial found that a combination of selenium + vitamin E + vitamin C + beta-carotene blunted the beneficial HDL effects of simvastatin-niacin therapy
- **Recommendation:** If taking niacin for lipid management, avoid high-dose selenium combined with other antioxidants

### Heavy Metal Chelation Agents
- **DMSA, DMPS, EDTA:** Chelation therapy may also chelate selenium, reducing body stores
- **Recommendation:** Replenish selenium after chelation rounds; monitor selenium levels during chelation protocols

### Oral Contraceptives
- **Combined oral contraceptives:** Some evidence suggests oral contraceptives may lower selenium levels
- **Recommendation:** Women on oral contraceptives should ensure adequate dietary selenium intake or supplement with 100-200mcg/day

### Corticosteroids
- **Prednisone, dexamethasone:** Long-term corticosteroid use increases oxidative stress and may deplete selenium stores
- **Recommendation:** Selenium supplementation (100-200mcg/day) is reasonable during prolonged corticosteroid therapy

### Pregnancy and Lactation
- **Pregnancy:** Selenium requirements increase slightly during pregnancy (RDA: 60mcg/day vs. 55mcg for non-pregnant women). Selenium deficiency during pregnancy is associated with preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, and miscarriage risk.
- **Supplementation in pregnancy:** 60-100mcg/day from selenomethionine is generally considered safe. Do NOT exceed 400mcg/day (UL).
- **Lactation:** RDA increases to 70mcg/day. Breast milk selenium content reflects maternal intake.
- **Key caution:** Selenium has a narrow therapeutic window — toxicity is more of a concern than with most minerals. Pregnant women should not exceed 200mcg/day from supplements without medical supervision.

### Gold Salts (Auranofin)
- **Used in rheumatoid arthritis:** Gold compounds inhibit selenium-dependent thioredoxin reductase, which may reduce selenium's antioxidant function
- **Recommendation:** Monitor selenium status in patients on gold therapy

### General Notes
- **Therapeutic window:** Selenium's beneficial range is narrow — 55-200mcg/day is optimal; above 400mcg/day is toxic. This makes drug interactions more clinically relevant than with minerals that have wider safety margins.
- **Lab monitoring:** Serum selenium or plasma selenoprotein P can be measured to guide supplementation and monitor interactions
- **Always disclose:** Inform all healthcare providers about selenium supplementation, especially before surgery, chemotherapy, or starting new medications
