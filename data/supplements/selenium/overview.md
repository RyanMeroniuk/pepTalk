# Selenium
## Overview

Selenium is an essential trace mineral that plays a critical role in antioxidant defense, thyroid function, immune health, and DNA synthesis. It is unique among minerals in that it is incorporated into proteins as the amino acid selenocysteine (the "21st amino acid"), making selenoproteins some of the most important functional proteins in the body.

### Why It Matters
- **Thyroid function:** The thyroid gland contains the highest concentration of selenium per gram of tissue in the body. Selenium is required for the conversion of T4 (inactive) to T3 (active thyroid hormone) via deiodinase enzymes.
- **Antioxidant defense:** Glutathione peroxidase (GPx), the body's master antioxidant recycling enzyme, is a selenoprotein. Without selenium, the glutathione system cannot function properly.
- **Immune function:** Selenium enhances both innate and adaptive immunity, supports NK cell activity, and modulates inflammatory response
- **Cancer risk reduction:** Adequate selenium status is associated with reduced risk of several cancers, though supplementation beyond adequacy does not add benefit
- **Male fertility:** Selenium is essential for sperm maturation and motility; the selenoprotein GPx4 is critical for structural integrity of sperm cells
- **Cognitive health:** Selenium status declines with age and is associated with cognitive decline; adequate levels support neuroprotection

### Who Needs It Most
- **People with thyroid conditions** — Hashimoto's thyroiditis, Graves' disease, and hypothyroidism all involve selenium-dependent pathways
- **Those in low-selenium soil regions** — Selenium content in food depends entirely on soil selenium where it was grown
- **People with GI disorders** — Crohn's, celiac, and short bowel syndrome impair selenium absorption
- **HIV-positive individuals** — HIV depletes selenium; low selenium is associated with faster disease progression
- **Vegans in low-selenium regions** — Plant-based selenium varies dramatically by soil; animal sources are more reliable
- **Older adults** — Selenium status tends to decline with age
- **Men focused on fertility** — Selenium is critical for sperm function

### Signs of Deficiency
- Thyroid dysfunction (particularly hypothyroid symptoms: fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, brain fog)
- Weakened immune function and frequent illness
- Hair loss or nail abnormalities (white nail beds)
- Muscle weakness or pain
- Male infertility (poor sperm motility)
- Cognitive decline
- Mood disturbances

### The Narrow Therapeutic Window
- **Deficiency:** <55mcg/day increases risk of thyroid dysfunction, immune impairment, cancer, and cardiomyopathy (Keshan disease)
- **Optimal range:** 55-200mcg/day from all sources
- **Toxicity (selenosis):** >400mcg/day chronically; symptoms begin at sustained intakes above 300-400mcg/day
- **Key point:** Unlike many minerals where "more is better" within a wide range, selenium has a U-shaped risk curve. Both deficiency AND excess increase disease risk.
