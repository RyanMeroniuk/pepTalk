# Selenium
## Natural Food Sources

### Important Note on Variability
Selenium content in food depends almost entirely on the selenium content of the soil where the food was grown or the animal was raised. Values below are US averages and can vary significantly by region.

### Top Selenium-Rich Foods (per standard serving)

**Nuts and Seeds:**
- **Brazil nuts:** 68-91mcg per single nut (1 nut = ~5g) — by far the richest food source. However, values range from 8 to 160mcg per nut depending on soil. Just 1-2 nuts can meet the RDA.

**Seafood (consistently high, less soil-dependent):**
- **Yellowfin tuna (cooked):** 92mcg per 3oz serving
- **Sardines (canned):** 45mcg per 3oz serving
- **Halibut (cooked):** 47mcg per 3oz serving
- **Shrimp (cooked):** 40mcg per 3oz serving
- **Salmon (Atlantic, cooked):** 40mcg per 3oz serving
- **Crab (cooked):** 41mcg per 3oz serving
- **Oysters (cooked):** 56mcg per 3oz serving
- **Lobster (cooked):** 36mcg per 3oz serving

**Meat and Poultry:**
- **Ham (roasted):** 42mcg per 3oz serving
- **Pork chop (cooked):** 33mcg per 3oz serving
- **Turkey (roasted):** 31mcg per 3oz serving
- **Chicken breast (cooked):** 24mcg per 3oz serving
- **Beef (ground, cooked):** 18mcg per 3oz serving
- **Liver (beef, cooked):** 28mcg per 3oz serving

**Eggs and Dairy:**
- **Eggs:** 15mcg per large egg (concentrated in the yolk)
- **Cottage cheese:** 20mcg per 1 cup
- **Milk (whole):** 8mcg per 8oz

**Grains (highly variable by region):**
- **Enriched bread:** 10mcg per slice
- **Brown rice (cooked):** 19mcg per 1 cup
- **Oatmeal (cooked):** 13mcg per 1 cup
- **Whole wheat bread:** 13mcg per 2 slices

**Vegetables (generally low, but some exceptions):**
- **Garlic:** 14mcg per 1oz (also contains methylselenocysteine)
- **Broccoli:** 2.5mcg per 1 cup (but contains selenoglucosinolates with cancer-protective properties)
- **Mushrooms (shiitake, cooked):** 18mcg per 1 cup
- **Spinach (cooked):** 11mcg per 1 cup

### Best Dietary Strategy
- **Eat 2-3 servings of seafood per week** — Seafood is the most reliable and consistent selenium source because ocean selenium is more uniformly distributed than soil selenium
- **Include 1-2 Brazil nuts daily** — But do not exceed 3-4 nuts due to toxicity risk from high-selenium batches
- **Eat eggs regularly** — 2 eggs provide ~30mcg selenium
- **Variety matters** — Different foods contain different forms of selenium (selenomethionine in grains/meat, selenocysteine in animal proteins, methylselenocysteine in garlic/cruciferous vegetables), and each form has different metabolic fates

### Regional Considerations
- **High-selenium US regions:** Great Plains states (Nebraska, South Dakota) — foods grown here are naturally selenium-rich
- **Low-selenium US regions:** Pacific Northwest, parts of the Great Lakes region
- **Low-selenium global regions:** Parts of China (Keshan disease belt), Finland (before national soil fortification), New Zealand, parts of the UK and Europe
- **Note:** The US, Canada, and Australia have generally adequate soil selenium in most regions. European countries, particularly the UK and Scandinavia, tend to have lower soil selenium.
