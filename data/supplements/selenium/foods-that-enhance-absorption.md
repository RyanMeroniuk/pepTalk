# Selenium
## Foods That Enhance Absorption

### Protein-Rich Foods
- **Mechanism:** Selenium is primarily absorbed as selenoamino acids (selenomethionine, selenocysteine), which use amino acid transport pathways. A protein-rich meal upregulates these transporters, improving selenium absorption.
- **Foods:** Eggs, chicken, fish, beef, dairy, legumes
- **Practical tip:** Take selenium supplements with a protein-containing meal for optimal absorption

### Vitamin E-Rich Foods
- **Mechanism:** Selenium and vitamin E are synergistic antioxidants — vitamin E scavenges lipid peroxyl radicals in cell membranes, while selenium-dependent GPx4 reduces the resulting lipid hydroperoxides. Each "spares" the other by reducing the oxidative burden.
- **Foods:** Almonds, sunflower seeds, hazelnuts, avocado, olive oil, wheat germ
- **Impact:** The combination provides more antioxidant protection than either alone; also reduces the amount of each needed for optimal defense
- **Practical tip:** Take selenium with a handful of almonds or sunflower seeds

### Vitamin C-Rich Foods (with organic selenium forms)
- **Mechanism:** Vitamin C enhances the reduction of selenite to selenide for selenoprotein synthesis. However, high-dose vitamin C can reduce selenite to elemental selenium (poorly absorbed) if taken simultaneously.
- **Nuance:** Vitamin C ENHANCES the bioactivity of organic selenium forms (selenomethionine) and food-source selenium, but can REDUCE absorption of inorganic selenite if taken at the same time in high doses (>500mg).
- **Foods:** Citrus fruits, bell peppers, strawberries, kiwi, broccoli
- **Practical tip:** Eat vitamin C-rich foods with selenium-rich meals (this is beneficial); but if taking selenite supplements, separate from high-dose vitamin C supplements by 1-2 hours

### Healthy Fats
- **Mechanism:** Selenium absorption is modestly enhanced by dietary fats, which slow gastric emptying and increase contact time with intestinal absorptive surfaces. Lipid-soluble selenium metabolites also benefit from fat co-ingestion.
- **Foods:** Olive oil, avocado, nuts, fatty fish, coconut oil
- **Practical tip:** This is why Brazil nuts (high in selenium AND fat) are such an efficient selenium source

### Iodine-Containing Foods
- **Mechanism:** Selenium and iodine work together in thyroid function — selenium activates thyroid hormones (T4 to T3 conversion), and iodine is the raw material for thyroid hormone synthesis. They are synergistic for thyroid health.
- **Foods:** Seaweed (kelp, nori), iodized salt, seafood, eggs, dairy
- **Important caveat:** Supplement selenium BEFORE or simultaneously with iodine in people with Hashimoto's. Adding iodine without adequate selenium can worsen thyroid autoimmunity by increasing H2O2 production without adequate GPx protection.

### Garlic and Cruciferous Vegetables
- **Mechanism:** Garlic contains methylselenocysteine, a unique organic selenium compound that is efficiently converted to methylselenol (a potent anti-cancer metabolite). Cruciferous vegetables contain selenoglucosinolates with additional cancer-protective properties.
- **Foods:** Garlic, onions, broccoli, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, kale
- **Practical tip:** Eating garlic and cruciferous vegetables alongside selenium supplementation may enhance the cancer-protective metabolic pathways of selenium

### Methionine-Rich Foods
- **Mechanism:** Selenomethionine and methionine share the same absorption and incorporation pathways. Adequate dietary methionine ensures the amino acid transport systems are active and available for selenomethionine uptake.
- **Foods:** Eggs, fish, sesame seeds, Brazil nuts, chicken, turkey
- **Note:** Extremely high methionine intake could theoretically compete with selenomethionine for incorporation, but at normal dietary levels this enhances rather than inhibits selenium utilization
