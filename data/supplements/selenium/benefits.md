# Selenium
## Benefits

### Thyroid Health
- **Hashimoto's thyroiditis:** Selenium supplementation (200mcg/day as selenomethionine) reduced anti-TPO antibodies by 21-40% over 3-12 months in multiple clinical trials (Toulis et al., 2010, meta-analysis)
- **T4-to-T3 conversion:** Adequate selenium is required for deiodinase enzyme activity; supplementation in deficient individuals improves thyroid hormone conversion
- **Thyroid protection:** GPx3 protects the thyroid from oxidative damage during hormone synthesis
- **Graves' disease:** Selenium supplementation (200mcg/day) improved quality of life and reduced orbitopathy progression in Graves' patients (EUGOGO trial, Marcocci et al., 2011)

### Antioxidant Defense
- **Glutathione system:** Selenium is required for glutathione peroxidase activity — the body's primary endogenous antioxidant defense system
- **Oxidative stress reduction:** Selenium supplementation reduces markers of oxidative stress (MDA, 8-OHdG) and increases total antioxidant capacity
- **Synergy with vitamin E:** Selenium and vitamin E work synergistically — vitamin E neutralizes lipid peroxyl radicals, and selenium-dependent GPx4 reduces lipid hydroperoxides. Together they provide comprehensive membrane protection.

### Immune Function
- **Viral defense:** Selenium deficiency allows normally benign viruses to mutate into more virulent strains (demonstrated with Coxsackievirus B3 mutating into a cardiotropic strain in selenium-deficient mice — Beck et al., 1995)
- **HIV progression:** Selenium supplementation in HIV-positive individuals reduced viral load and improved CD4 counts in several trials (Hurwitz et al., 2007)
- **General immunity:** 100-200mcg/day improves NK cell activity, T-cell proliferation, and vaccine response in elderly populations

### Cancer Risk Reduction
- **Prostate cancer:** The NPC trial (Clark et al., 1996) showed 200mcg selenium/day reduced prostate cancer incidence by 52% — however, the SELECT trial later found that supplementation in selenium-replete men did NOT reduce risk and may have increased risk in those with already-high selenium
- **Colorectal cancer:** Higher selenium status is associated with 30-40% lower colorectal cancer risk (meta-analysis, Jacobs et al., 2004)
- **Lung cancer:** Inverse association between selenium status and lung cancer risk in observational studies
- **Key insight:** Selenium supplementation benefits those who are deficient or suboptimal. Supplementing above optimal levels does NOT add cancer protection and may be harmful.

### Male Fertility
- **Sperm motility:** Selenium supplementation (200mcg/day) improved sperm motility in subfertile men (Safarinejad & Safarinejad, 2009)
- **Sperm count:** Selenium combined with N-acetylcysteine improved sperm count and morphology
- **Mechanism:** GPx4 selenoprotein is a structural component of sperm; adequate selenium is required for normal sperm development

### Cardiovascular Health
- **Keshan disease prevention:** Selenium supplementation eliminated Keshan disease (endemic cardiomyopathy) in selenium-deficient regions of China
- **Endothelial function:** Selenium supports nitric oxide production and reduces vascular oxidative stress
- **Note:** Supplementation benefits are primarily seen in those with low baseline selenium; supplementation in replete individuals does not add cardiovascular benefit

### Cognitive Health
- **Age-related decline:** Low selenium status is associated with accelerated cognitive decline in elderly populations
- **Neuroprotection:** Selenoproteins protect neurons from oxidative damage; the brain prioritizes selenium retention even during deficiency
- **Mood:** Low selenium is associated with increased anxiety, depression, and fatigue; supplementation may improve mood in deficient individuals

### Heavy Metal Protection
- **Mercury binding:** Selenium forms insoluble selenide complexes with mercury, reducing mercury's toxicity
- **Practical relevance:** High-selenium fish (tuna, swordfish) contain selenium that partially offsets their mercury content
- **Cadmium and arsenic:** Selenium also provides partial protection against cadmium and arsenic toxicity
