# Selenium
## Lifestyle Recommendations

### Test Before Supplementing
- **Serum selenium** is the most accessible test: optimal range 100-150mcg/L
- **Plasma selenoprotein P** is the best functional marker (saturates at ~120mcg/L serum selenium)
- Testing is especially important for selenium because of the narrow therapeutic window
- If serum selenium is already >120mcg/L, additional supplementation is unlikely to provide benefit and may cause harm

### Build a Selenium-Rich Diet First
- **Eat 2-3 Brazil nuts daily** — The most efficient whole-food selenium source (but do not exceed 3-4 due to variability)
- **Include seafood 2-3 times per week** — Salmon, sardines, tuna, and shrimp are reliable selenium sources
- **Eat eggs regularly** — 2 eggs provide ~30mcg selenium
- **Use garlic generously** — Contains methylselenocysteine, the most cancer-protective form of selenium
- **Choose whole grains** over refined — whole wheat retains 4x the selenium of white flour

### Pair Selenium With Synergistic Nutrients
- **Vitamin E:** Selenium and vitamin E are synergistic antioxidants; they spare each other and provide complementary protection
- **Iodine:** For thyroid health, selenium and iodine must be adequate together; neither works optimally alone
- **Zinc:** Selenium and zinc work together for immune function and antioxidant defense (Cu/Zn-SOD + GPx)
- **Vitamin D:** Emerging evidence suggests selenium status influences vitamin D metabolism

### Support Thyroid Health Holistically
- If supplementing selenium for thyroid support:
  - Address iodine status (150mcg/day from food or supplements)
  - Optimize iron status (required for thyroid peroxidase enzyme)
  - Manage stress (cortisol impairs T4-to-T3 conversion)
  - Ensure adequate zinc (required for thyroid hormone receptor binding)
  - Reduce exposure to thyroid-disrupting chemicals (BPA, perchlorate, fluoride)
  - Consider gluten elimination trial if Hashimoto's (molecular mimicry theory)

### Reduce Oxidative Stress Burden
- Since selenium's primary role is antioxidant defense, reducing oxidative stress means you need less selenium to stay protected:
  - **Reduce exposure:** Minimize air pollution, pesticides, processed food, and excessive alcohol
  - **Anti-inflammatory diet:** Emphasize colorful vegetables, berries, fatty fish, olive oil
  - **Exercise moderately:** Regular exercise upregulates antioxidant defenses; excessive exercise generates oxidative stress
  - **Manage stress:** Chronic psychological stress increases oxidative damage
  - **Sleep adequately:** Sleep is when the body performs much of its oxidative repair

### Minimize Mercury Exposure
- Mercury binds selenium and reduces its availability for selenoprotein synthesis
- Choose low-mercury fish (salmon, sardines, anchovies, herring, trout) over high-mercury fish (shark, swordfish, king mackerel, bigeye tuna)
- Consider selenium's mercury-protective effect as a bonus when eating seafood — the selenium in fish partially protects against the mercury in the same fish
- Reduce other mercury sources: dental amalgams, contaminated water

### Monitor Long-Term Supplementation
- Test serum selenium every 6-12 months if supplementing at 200mcg/day
- Watch for early selenosis signs: garlic breath, hair changes, nail brittleness
- Track total selenium intake from ALL sources (supplements, food, multivitamins)
- Adjust dosing seasonally if you eat more or fewer selenium-rich foods at different times of year
