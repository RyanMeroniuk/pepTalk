# Selenium
## Foods to Avoid (When Taking Selenium)

### Heavy Metals in Food
- **What:** High-mercury fish (shark, king mackerel, tilefish, bigeye tuna), contaminated water sources
- **Why:** Mercury binds selenium with extremely high affinity, forming insoluble mercury-selenide complexes. While this is actually a protective mechanism (selenium detoxifies mercury), it also sequesters selenium and reduces its bioavailability for selenoprotein synthesis.
- **How much impact:** Chronic high mercury exposure can functionally deplete selenium, even if dietary selenium intake appears adequate
- **Mitigation:** Choose lower-mercury seafood (salmon, sardines, anchovies, mackerel) which still provide selenium without excessive mercury. Selenium-to-mercury molar ratio matters — as long as selenium exceeds mercury in the food, the net effect is still positive.

### High-Dose Vitamin C Supplements (with inorganic selenium only)
- **What:** Vitamin C supplements above 500mg, taken at the same time as sodium selenite or selenate
- **Why:** High-dose ascorbic acid reduces inorganic selenium (selenite, Se4+) to elemental selenium (Se0), which is poorly absorbed and biologically inactive
- **How much impact:** Can reduce selenite absorption by 30-50% if taken simultaneously
- **Mitigation:** This does NOT apply to organic selenium forms (selenomethionine, selenium yeast). If using selenite, separate from high-dose vitamin C by 2 hours. If using selenomethionine, there is no concern.

### Excessive Sulfur Compounds
- **What:** Very high intake of sulfur-containing supplements (MSM, N-acetylcysteine at very high doses, sulfur-rich mineral waters)
- **Why:** At very high concentrations, sulfur compounds can compete with selenium for incorporation into amino acids and proteins, since selenocysteine and cysteine, and selenomethionine and methionine, are structural analogs
- **How much impact:** Minor at normal dietary levels; only relevant at very high supplemental doses
- **Mitigation:** Standard NAC supplementation (600-1200mg/day) is not a concern and actually pairs well with selenium

### Phytate-Rich Foods (minimal impact)
- **What:** Whole grains, legumes, nuts
- **Why:** Phytates primarily bind divalent cations (zinc, iron, calcium, magnesium). Selenium is absorbed as selenoamino acids using amino acid transporters, so phytates have minimal direct impact on selenium absorption.
- **How much impact:** Minimal — selenium is less affected by phytates than other minerals
- **Note:** This is listed for completeness; phytates are NOT a major concern for selenium specifically

### Processed and Refined Foods
- **What:** White bread, white rice, processed snacks, fast food
- **Why:** Food processing and refining removes selenium. White flour retains only ~25% of the selenium in whole wheat. Processed foods also lack the co-nutrients (vitamin E, methionine) that support selenium utilization.
- **How much impact:** Not an absorption blocker per se, but contributes to dietary selenium insufficiency
- **Mitigation:** Choose whole grains, whole foods, and minimally processed options

### Alcohol
- **What:** All alcoholic beverages
- **Why:** Chronic alcohol consumption impairs selenium absorption, increases oxidative stress (depleting selenium-dependent antioxidant enzymes faster), and may impair selenoprotein synthesis in the liver
- **How much impact:** Moderate; chronic alcoholics often have low selenium status
- **Mitigation:** Moderate alcohol consumption; ensure adequate selenium intake if drinking regularly
