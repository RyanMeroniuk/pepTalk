# Selenium
## Classification

- **Category:** Essential Trace Mineral
- **Type:** Micronutrient
- **Essentiality:** Cannot be synthesized by the body — must be obtained through diet or supplementation
- **Biological role:** Component of 25+ selenoproteins including glutathione peroxidases (antioxidant defense), thioredoxin reductases (redox regulation), and iodothyronine deiodinases (thyroid hormone metabolism)
- **Storage:** Primarily stored in skeletal muscle (~28-46% of total body selenium); also concentrated in thyroid, kidneys, and liver
- **RDA:** 55mcg/day (adults of both sexes), 60mcg/day (pregnant), 70mcg/day (lactating)
- **Upper Tolerable Intake Level (UL):** 400mcg/day for adults
- **Deficiency prevalence:** Highly geography-dependent — soil selenium content varies dramatically. Low-selenium regions include parts of China, Europe, New Zealand, and parts of the US Pacific Northwest. An estimated 500 million to 1 billion people worldwide have inadequate selenium intake.
- **CRITICAL NOTE:** Selenium has a narrow therapeutic window. The difference between beneficial and toxic doses is smaller than for most other minerals. More is NOT better.
