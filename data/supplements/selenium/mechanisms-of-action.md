# Selenium
## Mechanisms of Action

### Selenoprotein Synthesis
- Selenium is incorporated into proteins as **selenocysteine** (Sec), often called the 21st amino acid
- The human genome encodes **25 selenoproteins**, each containing at least one selenocysteine residue at its active site
- Selenocysteine is encoded by a UGA codon (normally a stop codon) with a specialized RNA structure (SECIS element) that directs selenocysteine insertion
- This unique biology makes selenium essential — no other element can substitute

### Glutathione Peroxidase (GPx) Family
- **GPx1-6** are selenoenzymes that reduce hydrogen peroxide and organic hydroperoxides to water and alcohols, using glutathione (GSH) as the electron donor
- **GPx1 (cytoplasmic):** Protects cells from oxidative damage; most abundant selenoprotein
- **GPx3 (plasma):** Extracellular antioxidant; protects blood vessels and tissues
- **GPx4 (phospholipid hydroperoxide):** Unique ability to reduce lipid peroxides in cell membranes; critical for sperm maturation, prevents ferroptosis (iron-dependent cell death)
- **Net effect:** Without selenium, the entire glutathione antioxidant system is compromised, leading to accumulation of reactive oxygen species

### Thioredoxin Reductase (TrxR) System
- **TrxR1, TrxR2, TrxR3** are selenoenzymes that regenerate thioredoxin, a key intracellular antioxidant and redox signaling molecule
- The thioredoxin system regulates: cell proliferation, DNA synthesis, gene expression, and apoptosis
- TrxR is also involved in regenerating other antioxidants including vitamin C and lipoic acid
- **Cancer relevance:** TrxR modulation is one proposed mechanism for selenium's cancer-preventive effects

### Thyroid Hormone Metabolism
- **Iodothyronine deiodinases (DIO1, DIO2, DIO3)** are selenoenzymes that convert thyroid hormones:
  - **DIO1 and DIO2:** Convert inactive T4 to active T3 (activating enzymes)
  - **DIO3:** Converts T4 and T3 to inactive forms (inactivating enzyme)
- The thyroid gland has the highest selenium concentration of any organ because it requires selenium for both hormone production and protection from H2O2 generated during thyroid hormone synthesis
- **GPx3** in the thyroid protects thyrocytes from oxidative damage caused by hydrogen peroxide (used in the iodination reaction)
- **Clinical significance:** Selenium deficiency impairs T4-to-T3 conversion, contributing to hypothyroid symptoms even with adequate iodine

### Immune Modulation
- **Selenoprotein P (SELENOP):** The major selenium transport protein; delivers selenium to tissues including immune cells
- **Selenoprotein K (SELENOK):** Required for calcium flux in immune cells during activation
- Selenium enhances:
  - NK cell cytotoxic activity
  - T-cell proliferation and differentiation
  - Macrophage phagocytic activity
  - Antibody production
- Selenium modulates NF-kB signaling, reducing excessive inflammatory cytokine production (IL-6, TNF-alpha)
- Selenium supports the shift from Th2-dominant to balanced Th1/Th2 immune response

### DNA Repair and Cancer Prevention
- Selenoproteins participate in DNA damage repair pathways
- Selenium induces phase II detoxification enzymes that neutralize carcinogens
- Selenium promotes apoptosis in damaged cells (reducing mutation accumulation)
- At physiological doses, selenium is protective; at supraphysiological doses, pro-oxidant effects can occur — explaining the U-shaped cancer risk curve

### Reproductive Biology
- **GPx4 (sperm-specific form):** Structural protein in sperm midpiece; essential for sperm motility and structural integrity
- **Selenoprotein P:** Required for selenium delivery to testes
- Selenium deficiency in males causes impaired spermatogenesis and reduced fertility
