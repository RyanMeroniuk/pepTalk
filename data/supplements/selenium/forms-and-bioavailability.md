# Selenium
## Forms and Bioavailability

### Bioavailability Ranking

#### 1. Selenomethionine
- **Bioavailability:** ~90% absorption
- **What it is:** Selenium incorporated into the amino acid methionine — the predominant form in food (especially plant sources)
- **Best for:** General supplementation, thyroid support, antioxidant defense, building selenium stores
- **Why:** Non-specifically incorporated into body proteins in place of methionine, creating a selenium reservoir that is released during normal protein turnover. Provides sustained, long-term selenium supply.
- **Dose:** 100-200mcg/day
- **Advantage:** Highest bioavailability; builds tissue selenium stores effectively
- **Consideration:** Because it incorporates non-specifically into proteins, it takes longer to show acute functional effects compared to selenite

#### 2. Selenium Yeast (Se-Yeast)
- **Bioavailability:** ~80-90% absorption
- **What it is:** Yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) grown in selenium-enriched media, converting inorganic selenium to organic forms — primarily selenomethionine (~60-70%) plus other selenoamino acids
- **Best for:** General supplementation — essentially a food-form selenium
- **Why:** Provides a spectrum of organic selenium compounds similar to what you would get from a selenium-rich diet
- **Dose:** 100-200mcg/day
- **Note:** Quality varies by manufacturer. Look for standardized products (e.g., SelenoExcell brand).

#### 3. Methylselenocysteine (Se-Methylselenocysteine)
- **Bioavailability:** ~80-85% absorption
- **What it is:** An organic selenium compound found naturally in selenium-accumulating plants (garlic, broccoli, onions)
- **Best for:** Cancer-preventive applications, targeted antioxidant support
- **Why:** Unlike selenomethionine, methylselenocysteine is NOT incorporated into body proteins. Instead, it is directly metabolized to methylselenol, a metabolite with demonstrated anti-cancer properties (apoptosis induction, angiogenesis inhibition).
- **Dose:** 100-200mcg/day
- **Advantage:** More direct conversion to bioactive metabolites; preferred form in cancer prevention research

#### 4. Sodium Selenite
- **Bioavailability:** ~50-60% absorption
- **What it is:** Inorganic selenium (Se4+)
- **Best for:** Acute functional needs; cheaper option; IV clinical use
- **Why:** Directly available for selenoprotein synthesis without needing to be released from protein turnover. Faster-acting than selenomethionine for raising functional selenoprotein levels.
- **Dose:** 50-200mcg/day
- **Disadvantage:** Lower absorption; can cause GI upset; at high doses, can generate free radicals during reduction (pro-oxidant at excess doses)
- **Note:** More likely to cause toxicity at high doses compared to organic forms

#### 5. Sodium Selenate
- **Bioavailability:** ~90% absorption, but significant urinary excretion
- **What it is:** Inorganic selenium (Se6+)
- **Best for:** Not typically recommended for supplementation; used in some animal feeds and clinical research
- **Why:** Well-absorbed but poorly retained — much is excreted in urine before being incorporated into selenoproteins
- **Net bioavailability (retention):** Significantly lower than selenomethionine despite high initial absorption

### Recommended Forms by Goal
- **General daily supplementation:** Selenomethionine or selenium yeast (100-200mcg)
- **Thyroid support (Hashimoto's):** Selenomethionine (200mcg) — the form used in most thyroid clinical trials
- **Cancer prevention:** Methylselenocysteine or selenium yeast (100-200mcg)
- **Acute immune support:** Sodium selenite (100-200mcg) for faster selenoprotein synthesis
- **Building long-term selenium stores:** Selenomethionine (incorporated into body proteins as a reservoir)

### CRITICAL: Dosing Safety
- **Do NOT exceed 200mcg/day from supplements** without medical supervision
- **Total intake (food + supplements) should not exceed 400mcg/day**
- Organic forms (selenomethionine, Se-yeast) are generally safer at higher doses than inorganic forms (selenite)
- **Brazil nuts:** 1-2 Brazil nuts provide approximately 68-91mcg selenium per nut — eating just 2-3 nuts daily can meet the RDA. However, selenium content varies enormously (from 8 to 160mcg per nut depending on soil), making consistent dosing unreliable.
