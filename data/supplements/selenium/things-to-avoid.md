# Selenium
## Things to Avoid

### Exceeding 200mcg/day From Supplements
- **Why:** This is the most critical thing to avoid with selenium. The SELECT trial showed that 200mcg/day in men with ALREADY adequate selenium was associated with increased type 2 diabetes risk. The NPC trial showed benefits at 200mcg/day in DEFICIENT populations. The lesson: supplementation above the optimal range is not only unhelpful — it may be harmful.
- **Solution:** Limit supplemental selenium to 100-200mcg/day. Test serum selenium to confirm your status before supplementing. If your serum selenium is >120mcg/L, you may not need supplementation at all.

### Assuming "More Is Better"
- **Why:** Selenium has a clear U-shaped risk curve. Below ~80mcg/day, risk of cancer, thyroid disease, and immune dysfunction increases. Above ~300mcg/day, risk of diabetes, certain cancers, and selenosis increases. The optimal window is narrow.
- **Solution:** Target 100-200mcg/day from all sources (food + supplements). Get tested. Do not megadose.

### Eating Too Many Brazil Nuts
- **Why:** Brazil nuts are the richest food source of selenium, but their selenium content is wildly variable (8-160mcg per nut). Eating 5-6 high-selenium Brazil nuts could provide 400-900mcg — well into the toxic range.
- **Solution:** Limit to 1-2 Brazil nuts per day. If using Brazil nuts as your primary selenium source, buy from a consistent supplier and do not exceed 3 per day.

### Stacking Multiple Selenium-Containing Supplements
- **Why:** Many supplements contain selenium: multivitamins (55-70mcg), thyroid support formulas (100-200mcg), antioxidant blends (50-100mcg), prostate health formulas (200mcg). Taking multiple products can easily push total intake above 400mcg/day.
- **Solution:** Audit ALL supplements for selenium content. Add up the total. Adjust individual supplement doses to keep total selenium intake in the 100-200mcg/day range.

### Taking Inorganic Selenium (Selenite) With High-Dose Vitamin C
- **Why:** Ascorbic acid reduces selenite to elemental selenium, which is poorly absorbed. This interaction does not occur with organic forms (selenomethionine).
- **Solution:** If using selenite, separate from vitamin C by 2+ hours. Or simply use selenomethionine instead.

### Ignoring Regional Soil Selenium Content
- **Why:** Selenium status varies enormously by geography. Someone eating a whole-foods diet in Nebraska (high-selenium soil) may already be getting 200+mcg/day from food alone. Someone eating the same diet in the UK or Pacific Northwest may be getting <50mcg/day.
- **Solution:** Test your serum selenium level, especially if you eat locally grown food. Adjust supplementation based on actual status, not assumptions.

### Long-Term High-Dose Selenium Without Monitoring
- **Why:** Chronic selenium excess accumulates gradually. Selenosis symptoms (hair loss, nail changes, garlic breath, neuropathy) develop insidiously over weeks to months.
- **Solution:** If supplementing above 100mcg/day, test serum selenium every 6-12 months. Target serum selenium of 100-150mcg/L. If above 150mcg/L, reduce or stop supplementation.

### Supplementing Selenium Without Adequate Iodine (for thyroid conditions)
- **Why:** Selenium alone may worsen hypothyroidism in iodine-deficient individuals by accelerating T4 degradation via increased DIO1 activity without providing the iodine needed to make more T4.
- **Solution:** Ensure adequate iodine status (150mcg/day from food or supplements) before or concurrently with selenium supplementation for thyroid health.

### Supplementing Iodine Without Adequate Selenium (for thyroid conditions)
- **Why:** The reverse is also dangerous. Iodine supplementation increases H2O2 production in the thyroid (necessary for hormone synthesis). Without adequate selenium to activate GPx for H2O2 clearance, this causes oxidative damage to thyrocytes and can trigger or worsen Hashimoto's thyroiditis.
- **Solution:** Always ensure selenium status is adequate before starting iodine supplementation. Ideally, start selenium 2-4 weeks before adding iodine.
