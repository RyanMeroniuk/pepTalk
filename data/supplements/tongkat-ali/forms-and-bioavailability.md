# Tongkat Ali
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Standardized water extract (LJ100) — the clinical benchmark:**
  - **What it is:** A patented hot-water root extract (developed from MIT/Malaysian government research) standardized to ~22% eurypeptides, 40% glycosaponins, and 30% polysaccharides
  - **Why it's preferred:** Most of the positive human trials (testosterone, cortisol, libido, fertility) used LJ100 or comparable standardized water extracts. Consistent potency batch to batch
  - **Typical dose:** 200-400 mg/day of the standardized extract
  - **Best for:** Free testosterone, stress/cortisol, libido, fertility

- **Eurycomanone-standardized extracts:**
  - Some products standardize to a specific eurycomanone percentage (e.g., 1-2%+ eurycomanone) rather than the LJ100 eurypeptide profile. Eurycomanone is the most-studied single quassinoid for fertility and steroidogenesis
  - Useful for fertility-focused protocols; confirm the actual eurycomanone content rather than relying on raw extract ratios

- **Extract ratio products (e.g., "100:1", "200:1"):**
  - Marketed by concentration ratio rather than active-compound standardization. A higher ratio does NOT guarantee more active compounds and is not directly comparable to clinical doses
  - Quality is inconsistent; prefer products that disclose eurypeptide or eurycomanone content

- **Raw root powder:**
  - The traditional preparation (boiled into a bitter tonic). Highly variable potency, large doses required, and impractical for reliable effects. Acceptable culturally but not recommended for dependable supplementation

- **Bioavailability considerations:**
  - The active quassinoids and eurypeptides are water-soluble; hot-water extraction (as in LJ100) concentrates them. Oral absorption is reasonable but the compounds are cleared relatively quickly, which is one rationale for daily dosing
  - Heavy-metal and adulteration concerns exist with cheap, unstandardized imports. Third-party tested, standardized extracts are strongly preferred

- **Quality red flags:** Unstandardized "pure root," exaggerated extract ratios without active-compound disclosure, proprietary blends that hide the actual Tongkat Ali dose, and products lacking heavy-metal testing
