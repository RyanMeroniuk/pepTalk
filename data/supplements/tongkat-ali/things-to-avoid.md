# Tongkat Ali
## Things to Avoid

- **Buying cheap, unstandardized "pure root" products:** Low-grade Tongkat Ali has documented heavy-metal contamination (mercury and others) and wildly variable potency. Use standardized, third-party-tested extracts (e.g., LJ100) that disclose eurypeptide or eurycomanone content
- **Trusting extract ratios alone ("100:1", "200:1"):** A high concentration ratio does not guarantee a high active-compound content and is not comparable to clinical doses. Look for actual standardization percentages
- **Evening dosing:** The alerting effect can disrupt sleep. Take it in the morning
- **Megadosing:** More than ~400-600 mg/day of standardized extract provides no proven added benefit and increases restlessness, irritability, and sleep problems
- **Expecting steroid-like results:** Tongkat Ali gently raises *free* testosterone by lowering SHBG; it is not exogenous hormone. Effects are modest and accumulate over weeks, and are largest in stressed or older men
- **Using it with hormone-sensitive conditions without medical oversight:** Because it raises free testosterone and may modestly inhibit aromatase, anyone with prostate cancer, prostate conditions, or other hormone-sensitive cancers should consult a physician first
- **Combining with exogenous hormones without a plan:** Stacking with TRT, DHEA, or other hormonal agents changes the risk profile and can complicate monitoring. Coordinate with a knowledgeable clinician
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** Avoid entirely — hormonally active with insufficient safety data
- **Ignoring sleep, alcohol, and diet:** Tongkat Ali optimizes endogenous signaling, but it cannot overcome the testosterone-suppressing effects of poor sleep, heavy drinking, and a junk-food diet
- **Stacking multiple stimulating testosterone herbs blindly:** Combining Tongkat Ali with several other alerting/hormonal items at once (e.g., aggressive fadogia-agrestis dosing) makes it hard to attribute effects or side effects. Add one variable at a time
