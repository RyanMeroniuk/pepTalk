# Tongkat Ali
## Natural Food Sources

- **No dietary food equivalent:** Tongkat Ali is the root of Eurycoma longifolia. The bioactive quassinoids (eurycomanone) and eurypeptides are not present in any common food. You cannot obtain a meaningful dose through diet — it is a supplementation-dependent botanical

- **Traditional preparations (root-derived, not foods in the Western sense):**
  - **Root decoction / "tonic":** In Malaysia and Indonesia the sliced or powdered root is boiled in water for an extended time to produce a bitter tonic, traditionally taken for vitality, libido, and fatigue
  - **Tongkat Ali coffee:** A popular commercial preparation in Southeast Asia where root extract is added to instant coffee. Doses are usually low and unstandardized, and the caffeine may compound the alerting effect
  - **Tinctures and syrups:** Traditional alcohol or honey-based extractions of the root

- **Why supplementation is necessary:**
  - The active compounds are concentrated in the root and require extended hot-water extraction to release in meaningful amounts
  - Standardized extracts (e.g., LJ100) deliver a consistent, quantified eurypeptide/eurycomanone dose that traditional preparations cannot reliably match
  - Traditional decoctions are bitter, time-consuming, and of uncertain potency

- **Sourcing note:** Wild-harvested Eurycoma longifolia from contaminated soil can accumulate heavy metals in the root. This makes third-party-tested, standardized commercial extracts both more reliable for potency and safer than informally sourced raw root
