# Chromium
## Forms and Bioavailability

### Overview
Chromium absorption from all forms is very low (0.4-2.5% of ingested dose), making form selection and absorption optimization important. The picolinate form has the most clinical research and the highest bioavailability among common supplemental forms.

### Chromium Picolinate
- **Bioavailability:** Highest among chromium supplements — estimated 2-5% absorption
- **Description:** Chromium bound to picolinic acid (a natural amino acid metabolite)
- **Best for:** Blood sugar management, insulin sensitivity, weight management, food cravings, PCOS, atypical depression
- **Clinical evidence:** The most studied form by far — used in the majority of clinical trials showing benefits for diabetes, weight loss, and lipid profiles
- **Dose range:** 200-1,000mcg/day
- **Controversy:** Early concerns about genotoxicity in cell culture studies (Stearns et al., 2002) have not been confirmed in human studies; considered safe at recommended doses
- **Brand examples:** NOW Foods, Solgar, Nature's Way

### Chromium Polynicotinate (Niacin-Bound Chromium)
- **Bioavailability:** Good — estimated 1.5-3% absorption
- **Description:** Chromium bound to niacin (vitamin B3); marketed as ChromeMate
- **Best for:** General metabolic support, lipid management
- **Advantage:** The niacin component may provide additional lipid benefits
- **Clinical evidence:** Some studies show lipid improvements, but fewer trials than picolinate
- **Dose range:** 200-600mcg/day

### Chromium GTF (Glucose Tolerance Factor)
- **Bioavailability:** Moderate — varies by source
- **Description:** Chromium complexed with niacin and amino acids (glycine, cysteine, glutamic acid) in a form that mimics the natural glucose tolerance factor
- **Best for:** General blood sugar support
- **Note:** The exact structure of endogenous GTF is still debated; supplemental GTF chromium attempts to replicate it
- **Dose range:** 200-500mcg/day

### Chromium Chloride
- **Bioavailability:** Low — estimated 0.4-0.5% absorption
- **Description:** Simple inorganic chromium salt
- **Best for:** Not recommended for supplementation due to poor absorption
- **Clinical evidence:** The early Anderson et al. (1997) China diabetes study used chromium chloride at high doses and still showed benefits, but this required 1,000mcg to achieve what picolinate does at 200-400mcg
- **Note:** Often used in research settings because it is inexpensive and the chromium content is precisely known

### Chromium Histidinate
- **Bioavailability:** High — potentially comparable to or exceeding picolinate
- **Description:** Chromium chelated with the amino acid histidine
- **Best for:** Insulin sensitivity, metabolic support
- **Clinical evidence:** Newer form with growing research; some animal studies suggest superior absorption and efficacy compared to picolinate
- **Dose range:** 200-400mcg/day

### Chromium Nicotinate Glycinate
- **Bioavailability:** Good
- **Description:** Chromium chelated with nicotinic acid and glycine; marketed as Chromax
- **Best for:** General metabolic support
- **Clinical evidence:** Limited compared to picolinate

### Chromium Yeast (High-Chromium Yeast)
- **Bioavailability:** Moderate to good — organically bound chromium in a whole-food matrix
- **Description:** Brewer's yeast or nutritional yeast grown in chromium-rich media; chromium is incorporated into organic complexes
- **Best for:** Those who prefer whole-food-derived supplements
- **Clinical evidence:** The original GTF research used brewer's yeast as the chromium source
- **Dose range:** Varies; typically provides 100-200mcg chromium per serving

### Bioavailability Ranking (Highest to Lowest)
1. **Chromium picolinate** — ~2-5% absorption, most clinical evidence
2. **Chromium histidinate** — potentially comparable to picolinate; newer research
3. **Chromium polynicotinate** — ~1.5-3% absorption, niacin co-benefit
4. **High-chromium yeast** — moderate, whole-food matrix advantage
5. **Chromium GTF** — moderate, variable by formulation
6. **Chromium nicotinate glycinate** — good, limited data
7. **Chromium chloride** — ~0.4-0.5% absorption, not recommended for supplementation

### Practical Recommendations
- **For blood sugar and diabetes:** Chromium picolinate, 200-1,000mcg/day — most evidence-based choice
- **For lipid support:** Chromium polynicotinate (ChromeMate), leveraging the niacin component
- **For general metabolic health:** Chromium picolinate or polynicotinate at 200-400mcg/day
- **For food cravings / appetite:** Chromium picolinate, 400-600mcg/day
- **Avoid:** Chromium chloride for supplementation (poor absorption, requires very high doses)
