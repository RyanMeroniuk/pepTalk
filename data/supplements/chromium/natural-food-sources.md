# Chromium
## Natural Food Sources

### Top Food Sources (Ranked by Chromium Content)

#### Highest Chromium Foods
- **Broccoli:** 11mcg per 1/2 cup (cooked) — one of the richest vegetable sources
- **Grape juice:** 7.5mcg per 8oz serving
- **Turkey breast:** 1.7mcg per 3oz serving
- **Whole wheat English muffin:** 3.6mcg per muffin
- **Red wine:** 1-13mcg per 5oz serving (highly variable depending on soil and production)
- **Brewer's yeast:** 3.3-15mcg per tablespoon — the original GTF chromium source; content varies significantly by brand and preparation

### Important Notes About Food Chromium

#### Soil Dependency
- **Chromium content varies significantly** based on the soil where food was grown, making precise food-based intake calculations difficult
- **Regional variation:** Some agricultural regions have chromium-depleted soil, producing lower-chromium crops
- **Organic vs. conventional:** No consistent difference in chromium content

#### Food Processing Effects
- **Refining depletes chromium:** Processing whole grains into white flour removes up to 83% of the chromium
- **Stainless steel cookware:** Cooking acidic foods (tomato sauce, vinegar-based dishes) in stainless steel can increase chromium content of food by leaching Cr3+ from the cookware — this is a harmless, bioavailable form
- **High-sugar diets:** Refined sugar consumption increases urinary chromium excretion, effectively reducing chromium status even if dietary intake appears adequate

#### Practical Strategy for Food-Based Chromium
- **Eat broccoli regularly** — one of the few reliably chromium-rich foods
- **Choose whole grains** over refined grains
- **Include brewer's yeast** in smoothies or meals if tolerated
- **Cook in stainless steel** with acidic ingredients occasionally
- **Minimize refined sugar** to reduce chromium excretion
- **Realistic assessment:** Given the low and variable chromium content of most foods and typical Western diets, supplementation is a reasonable approach for individuals seeking specific metabolic benefits
