# Fisetin
## Drug Interactions

Like its relative quercetin, fisetin can inhibit certain cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes and drug transporters, which is the basis for most of its interactions. By slowing metabolism or efflux of some medications, fisetin can raise their blood levels. High-dose "hit-and-run" senolytic windows are when these interactions matter most.

### Caution Interactions (CYP & Transporter Inhibition)
- **CYP3A4-metabolized drugs (broad class):** Fisetin can inhibit CYP3A4, potentially increasing levels of many drugs metabolized by it — including certain statins, calcium channel blockers, and benzodiazepines. **Use caution and monitor**, especially during high-dose windows.
- **CYP2C9 / other CYP substrates:** Fisetin may affect additional CYP-handled drugs (some NSAIDs, certain others). **Monitor** for enhanced effects.
- **P-glycoprotein substrates:** By modulating this efflux transporter, fisetin may raise levels of P-gp substrate drugs (e.g., digoxin). **Caution and monitoring** advised.

### Anticoagulants / Antiplatelets (Caution — Bleeding)
- **Warfarin and other anticoagulants/blood thinners, antiplatelet agents (aspirin, clopidogrel):** Fisetin may have mild antiplatelet activity, theoretically increasing bleeding risk. **Use caution, monitor**, and inform your physician — especially around surgery.

### Chemotherapy / Active Cancer (Caution)
- **Interaction:** Fisetin's antioxidant and enzyme-modulating properties could theoretically interfere with certain chemotherapy agents (altering efficacy or drug levels). Of note, fisetin is also being studied for potential anti-cancer and senescence-clearing roles, but this does not make self-combination safe.
- **Recommendation:** Do not take supplemental fisetin during active cancer treatment without explicit oncologist approval.

### Immunosuppressants (Caution)
- **Cyclosporine, tacrolimus:** These are CYP3A4 and P-gp substrates with narrow therapeutic windows. Fisetin's enzyme/transporter effects could raise their levels. **Monitor closely; consult the prescribing physician.**

### Diabetes Medications (Monitor)
- **Drugs:** Metformin, insulin, sulfonylureas. Fisetin's metabolic and anti-inflammatory effects may modestly support glucose control; a small additive effect is possible. **Monitor** blood glucose when starting.

### Blood Pressure Medications (Monitor)
- **Drugs:** ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers. Fisetin may modestly support vascular function; **monitor** blood pressure, though the interaction is minor for most people.

### Grapefruit (Compounding Effect)
- **Grapefruit juice** inhibits the same CYP3A4 and P-gp pathways. Combining it with fisetin and CYP3A4-metabolized drugs can compound the interaction. **Separate or avoid** on such medications.

### Contraindications / Avoid
- **Active chemotherapy** without oncologist clearance — contraindicated pending medical approval
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — avoid concentrated supplemental doses (insufficient safety data)
- **Bleeding disorders / immediately pre-surgery** — caution due to potential antiplatelet effect

### Blood Test / Surgical Considerations
- **Inform healthcare providers** that you take fisetin, particularly before surgery and when on narrow-therapeutic-index drugs (cyclosporine, tacrolimus, digoxin, warfarin)
- **Time high-dose windows away from critical medication dosing** when feasible
