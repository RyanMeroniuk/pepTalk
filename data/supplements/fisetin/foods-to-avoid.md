# Fisetin
## Foods to Avoid

### Foods/Substances That Work Against Fisetin's Goals
- **Excessive alcohol** — increases oxidative stress and inflammation, working against fisetin's antioxidant and anti-inflammatory purpose; also burdens the liver where fisetin and many drugs are metabolized
- **Highly processed and pro-inflammatory foods** — fried foods, refined sugar, and ultra-processed snacks drive the "inflammaging" that fisetin is meant to counter, and accelerate the senescence burden it aims to reduce
- **High-fructose and sugary foods/beverages** — promote oxidative and metabolic stress, including AGE formation that contributes to cellular senescence

### Grapefruit (Enzyme Overlap)
- **Grapefruit and grapefruit juice** inhibit CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein — pathways fisetin (like quercetin) also affects. Combining both with CYP-metabolized medications can compound interactions and unpredictably alter drug levels. Be cautious if you take such medications.

### Timing Mistake (Not a Food, But Critical)
- **Taking fisetin without fat** — substantially limits absorption of an already poorly bioavailable compound. Always pair with a fat-containing meal, especially during high-dose senolytic windows.

### No Specific Food Blocks Fisetin
- **There are no common foods that destroy or fully block fisetin** in a meaningful way
- The larger issue is dietary pattern: a diet high in pro-inflammatory, pro-oxidant foods undermines the antioxidant and senolytic benefits fisetin provides

### Practical Guidance
- **Build a polyphenol-rich, whole-food diet** (including strawberries, a natural fisetin and vitamin C source) around supplementation
- **Be cautious combining fisetin with grapefruit** if you take prescription medications metabolized by CYP3A4 (see drug-interactions)
