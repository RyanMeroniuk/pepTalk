# Fisetin
## Foods That Enhance Absorption

Fisetin's low and short-lived bioavailability makes absorption support especially important — particularly during high-dose senolytic windows.

### Dietary Fat (Most Important)
- **Fisetin is poorly water-soluble and absorbs better with fat** — take it with a high-fat meal or with oils
- **Practical fat sources:** olive oil, avocado, nuts, eggs, full-fat yogurt, fatty fish, coconut oil
- Mixing fisetin powder into a fatty food or taking it alongside a fat-rich meal is the simplest, most effective enhancer

### Piperine (Black Pepper Extract)
- **Piperine inhibits enzymes that metabolize many polyphenols**, potentially slowing fisetin's rapid clearance and extending its presence
- Some fisetin products co-formulate piperine for this reason
- **Food source:** black pepper

### Other Polyphenols (Network Effect)
- **Quercetin** (onions, apples) is fisetin's close structural relative and shares antioxidant/senolytic pathways — a natural companion
- **Resveratrol** (red grapes, berries) and other polyphenols support overlapping longevity pathways (SIRT1/AMPK)
- Eating a polyphenol-rich diet supports the broader antioxidant network fisetin participates in

### Vitamin C and Antioxidant Foods
- **Vitamin C-rich foods** (citrus, peppers, strawberries — which also contain fisetin) help maintain the antioxidant environment in which fisetin works
- Strawberries conveniently provide both fisetin and vitamin C

### Gut Health (Indirect)
- **Fermented foods and fiber** support a healthy microbiome, which influences flavonoid metabolism and absorption in the intestine

### What Doesn't Help
- **Taking standard fisetin powder dry, on an empty stomach** — wastes much of an already poorly absorbed dose
- **Relying on food sources alone** to reach senolytic doses — impossible in practice (see natural-food-sources); use a supplement with fat, or a liposomal/phospholipid form
