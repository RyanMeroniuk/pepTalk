# Fisetin
## Dosing Protocols

### Important Framing
- Fisetin dosing in longevity circles is based primarily on **extrapolation from animal studies and ongoing (unfinished) human trials**. There is no established, evidence-confirmed human dose. The protocols below reflect common practice in the longevity community, not validated clinical guidelines.

### Periodic High-Dose "Hit-and-Run" Senolytic Protocol (Most Common)
- **Concept:** Senolytics aim to clear a population of senescent cells in a short burst rather than maintain steady blood levels — hence intermittent high dosing followed by long breaks
- **Common dose:** ~20 mg/kg/day taken for 2-3 consecutive days, then repeated periodically (e.g., monthly or quarterly). For a ~70-80 kg adult, this works out to roughly 1,000-1,500 mg/day for 2-3 days.
- **Some users round to:** 1,500 mg/day for 2-3 consecutive days
- **Frequency:** Monthly, quarterly, or a few times per year — there is no consensus
- **Take with fat** — fisetin's poor absorption means high-fat meals or oils meaningfully improve uptake during the dosing window

### Low-Dose Daily (Antioxidant/General Support)
- **Dose:** ~100-500 mg/day with food
- **Rationale:** For general antioxidant/anti-inflammatory support rather than senolysis
- **Note:** Daily dosing does not match the "hit-and-run" senolytic model; it is a different goal

### Timing & Absorption
- **Take with a high-fat meal** to maximize the limited absorption
- **Liposomal or phospholipid forms** can lower the dose needed to reach comparable blood levels
- **No need for special timing of day** for senolytic dosing; convenience and consistency with food matter most

### Cycling
- **The "hit-and-run" approach is inherently cyclic** — short bursts with long off-periods
- This mirrors the senolytic philosophy: clear senescent cells, then allow them to slowly reaccumulate before the next clearance

### Safety-First Notes
- **Start conservatively** if self-experimenting and assess tolerance
- **Medical caveat:** Anyone on prescription medications, with chronic disease, pregnant/breastfeeding, or with active cancer should consult a clinician before high-dose protocols
- **Long-term safety of repeated high-dose fisetin in humans is not established** — this is an area of active research
