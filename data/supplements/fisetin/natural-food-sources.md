# Fisetin
## Natural Food Sources

Fisetin occurs in a range of fruits and vegetables, but in small amounts relative to supplement doses. Strawberries are by far the richest common dietary source.

### Highest Concentration
- **Strawberries** — the standout source, ~160 mcg per gram (~16 mg per 100 g); the most fisetin-dense common food

### Moderate Sources
- **Apples** — ~27 mcg per gram (~2.7 mg per 100 g)
- **Persimmons** — ~10.6 mcg per gram
- **Lotus root** — ~5.8 mcg per gram
- **Onions** — ~4.8 mcg per gram
- **Grapes** — ~3.9 mcg per gram

### Lower Sources
- **Kiwi** — ~2 mcg per gram
- **Peaches** — modest amounts
- **Cucumbers** — modest amounts
- **Tomatoes** — trace to modest

### Important Context
- **Dietary fisetin is far below supplemental/senolytic doses.** Even strawberries, the richest source at ~16 mg per 100 g, would require eating roughly **6-9 kg (13-20 lbs) of strawberries to reach a single ~1,000-1,500 mg "hit-and-run" dose** — clearly impractical.
- **Typical dietary intake is on the order of a few milligrams per day**, useful for general antioxidant intake but nowhere near research-level senolytic doses
- **This gap is the rationale for supplementation** when targeting senolytic or high-dose antioxidant effects
- **Fisetin is structurally related to quercetin** (another dietary flavonol found in onions and apples), and the two often appear together in the same foods
