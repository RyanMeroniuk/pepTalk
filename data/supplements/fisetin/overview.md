# Fisetin
## Overview

Fisetin is a flavonol — a flavonoid closely related to quercetin — found most abundantly in strawberries. It is a capable antioxidant and anti-inflammatory compound, but its rise to prominence comes from longevity research: in a widely cited 2018 study (Yousefzadeh et al., published in EBioMedicine), fisetin was identified as the most potent senolytic among the natural flavonoids tested in mice, selectively clearing senescent ("zombie") cells and extending healthspan and lifespan in aged animals. This launched interest in periodic high-dose "hit-and-run" dosing in humans — taking large doses for a few consecutive days, then nothing for weeks. Crucially, human evidence is still preliminary: clinical trials (including work led by the Mayo Clinic) are ongoing, and the dramatic results come primarily from animal models.

### Why It Matters

- **Leading natural senolytic candidate:** Fisetin selectively triggers death in senescent cells, which accumulate with age and secrete inflammatory factors (the senescence-associated secretory phenotype, or SASP) that drive age-related dysfunction.
- **"Hit-and-run" rationale:** Because senolytics aim to clear a population of cells rather than maintain a steady drug level, fisetin is often dosed intermittently in short high-dose bursts rather than continuously.
- **Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory:** Independent of senolysis, fisetin scavenges free radicals, inhibits NF-kB inflammatory signaling, and supports glutathione levels.
- **Neuroprotective interest:** Fisetin promotes neurotrophic and pro-cognitive pathways in preclinical models, drawing interest for brain aging.
- **Better safety margin than drug senolytics:** As a dietary flavonoid, fisetin is generally well tolerated, unlike the chemotherapy drug dasatinib used in the D+Q senolytic combination.

### Who Might Be Interested

- **Longevity-focused users** — interested in senescent-cell clearance (with the strong caveat that human data is preliminary)
- **People building an antioxidant/polyphenol stack** — alongside quercetin and resveratrol
- **Those interested in neuroprotection** — based on early preclinical cognitive findings
