# Fisetin
## Side Effects

### Generally Well Tolerated
- **As a dietary flavonoid, fisetin has a benign safety profile in short-term use** — it is one reason it is favored over drug senolytics like dasatinib
- Strawberries and other food sources are consumed safely worldwide

### Common (Mild)
- **GI discomfort** — nausea, bloating, or loose stools, more likely during high-dose "hit-and-run" days or on an empty stomach. Taking with a fatty meal helps.
- **Headache** — occasionally reported, usually mild and transient

### Uncommon
- **Fatigue or "off" feeling** during high-dose senolytic days — anecdotal; may relate to a transient inflammatory response as senescent cells are cleared
- **Mild dizziness** — infrequent

### Drug-Interaction Risk (Important)
- **Like quercetin, fisetin can inhibit certain CYP enzymes and drug transporters**, potentially altering blood levels of co-administered medications (see drug-interactions). This is the most relevant safety consideration, more than direct toxicity.
- **Possible mild antiplatelet effect** — theoretical additive bleeding risk with anticoagulants/blood thinners

### Special Populations
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data for concentrated supplemental doses; avoid (dietary strawberry amounts are fine)
- **Active cancer / chemotherapy** — fisetin's antioxidant and enzyme-modulating effects could theoretically interact with treatment; consult an oncologist
- **Bleeding disorders / pre-surgery** — caution due to potential antiplatelet activity
- **Chronic disease or polypharmacy** — review high-dose protocols with a clinician

### Long-Term & High-Dose Safety
- **Long-term safety of repeated high-dose fisetin in humans is not established** — most human data is short-term and from ongoing trials
- The intermittent "hit-and-run" model limits cumulative exposure, but multi-year safety remains uncharacterized

### Overall Safety Profile
- **Low toxicity at typical doses; main practical concern is drug interactions and the unknowns of high-dose chronic use**
- Adverse events in the literature are generally mild; serious effects are not characteristic of fisetin itself at studied doses
