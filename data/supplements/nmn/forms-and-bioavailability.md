# NMN (Nicotinamide Mononucleotide)
## Forms and Bioavailability

- **Standard oral NMN (capsules/powder):** The most common form. NMN is water-soluble and absorbed in the gut. Some NMN is converted to NR (nicotinamide riboside) in the gut before absorption; some may be transported directly via the Slc12a8 transporter. Oral bioavailability is estimated at 20-50%
- **Sublingual NMN (strongly recommended):** Sublingual delivery (dissolving under the tongue) bypasses first-pass liver metabolism and allows NMN to enter the bloodstream directly through the sublingual mucosa. This may significantly increase bioavailability compared to swallowed capsules. Sublingual tablets or powder held under the tongue for 1-3 minutes before swallowing is the preferred method among longevity practitioners
- **Liposomal NMN:** NMN encapsulated in phospholipid vesicles for enhanced cellular uptake. Liposomal delivery protects NMN from degradation in the GI tract and may improve cellular delivery. Some evidence suggests 2-4x improved bioavailability vs standard oral
- **Enteric-coated capsules:** Designed to bypass stomach acid and dissolve in the small intestine. Protects NMN from acid degradation. A reasonable option for those who prefer capsules over sublingual
- **IV / injection NMN:** Used in clinical settings. Provides 100% bioavailability. Not practical for daily supplementation but used in some anti-aging clinics
- **Beta-NMN vs other isomers:** The biologically active form is beta-NMN (beta-nicotinamide mononucleotide). Ensure the supplement specifies beta-NMN. Alpha-NMN is not biologically active
- **Stability concerns (critical):**
  - NMN degrades in heat and moisture. Store in a cool, dry place. Refrigeration is recommended, especially in warm climates
  - Bulk NMN powder exposed to air and humidity can degrade rapidly
  - Look for products with desiccant packets and opaque, sealed containers
  - Some manufacturers use nitrogen-flushed packaging to reduce oxidation
- **Purity and testing:** Third-party tested NMN with certificates of analysis (CoA) showing >98% purity is essential. The NMN market has had quality control issues with some products containing significantly less NMN than labeled
- **NMN vs NR (form comparison):** NR (nicotinamide riboside, sold as Niagen/TRU NIAGEN) is the other major NAD+ precursor supplement. NR has more published human clinical data. NMN is one enzymatic step closer to NAD+. Both effectively raise NAD+. NMN is generally more expensive. NR is more shelf-stable. The debate is ongoing; both are valid choices
