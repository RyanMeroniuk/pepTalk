# NMN (Nicotinamide Mononucleotide)
## Side Effects

- **Generally well tolerated:** Clinical trials at doses up to 1250mg/day have shown excellent safety profiles with few adverse events
- **Mild GI discomfort:** The most commonly reported side effect. Nausea, bloating, or diarrhea occasionally occur, especially at higher doses. Usually transient and resolved by taking with food or reducing dose
- **Flushing / warmth:** Some users report a mild warming sensation or skin flushing, similar to niacin flush but much milder. This is related to NAD+'s role in metabolism and is typically transient
- **Headache:** Occasionally reported in the first week. May relate to increased cerebral metabolism. Usually resolves without intervention
- **Insomnia / increased energy:** NMN can increase cellular energy, causing wakefulness if taken too late in the day. Solution: take in the morning only
- **Vivid dreams:** Commonly reported, likely related to improved NAD+ levels and their effect on circadian rhythm and sleep architecture
- **Skin sensitivity (rare):** Rare reports of increased sun sensitivity or mild skin reactions
- **Potential concern — cancer risk (theoretical and debated):**
  - NAD+ is required by all cells, including cancer cells. The theoretical concern is that raising NAD+ could fuel existing cancers
  - Counterargument: NAD+ also fuels DNA repair (PARPs) and tumor suppression (SIRT1, SIRT6). Improved DNA repair may reduce cancer initiation
  - Current evidence does not show increased cancer risk from NMN supplementation in animal studies. Multiple studies show reduced cancer markers
  - **Recommendation:** Individuals with active cancer should consult their oncologist before taking NMN
- **Nicotinamide accumulation:** NMN metabolism produces nicotinamide (NAM) as a byproduct. Excess NAM can inhibit sirtuins (the opposite of the intended effect). This is a theoretical concern at very high doses but may be mitigated by the body's methylation of NAM to MeNAM
- **Methylation demand:** NAM is cleared by methylation (via NNMT enzyme), which consumes methyl groups (SAMe). Very high NMN doses could theoretically increase methylation demand. Ensure adequate methyl donor intake (B12, folate, betaine/TMG)
