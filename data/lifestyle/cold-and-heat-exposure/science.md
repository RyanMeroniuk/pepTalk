# Temperature Therapy

## Science & Mechanisms

### Cold Exposure

- **Cold shock proteins** — Cold triggers the release of RNA-binding motif protein 3 (RBM3), which protects synapses and may prevent neurodegeneration
- **Norepinephrine release** — Cold water immersion produces a 200-300% increase in norepinephrine, a neurotransmitter and hormone that drives alertness, focus, and mood elevation
- **Brown adipose tissue activation** — Cold activates brown fat thermogenesis, burning calories to generate heat. Regular cold exposure increases brown fat volume and activity over time.
- **Vasoconstriction and vasodilation cycle** — Cold triggers vasoconstriction followed by vasodilation upon rewarming, improving vascular tone and circulation
- **Dopamine increase** — Cold water immersion raises dopamine levels significantly, with effects lasting 2-3 hours post-exposure. This is a sustained, gradual rise rather than a spike-and-crash.
- **Anti-inflammatory effects** — Cold reduces inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-alpha) and shifts the immune system toward anti-inflammatory pathways
- **Mitochondrial biogenesis** — Cold activates PGC-1alpha, the master regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis, increasing mitochondrial density and function
- **The Soberg Principle** — End on cold to maximize metabolic effects. Allowing the body to reheat naturally forces it to generate heat through thermogenesis, burning more calories.

### Heat Exposure

- **Heat shock proteins (HSP70, HSP90)** — Cellular repair chaperones that refold damaged proteins and clear misfolded proteins. Increased by sauna use and associated with longevity.
- **Cardiovascular effects** — Sauna increases heart rate and cardiac output, mimicking moderate cardiovascular exercise. Regular use improves endothelial function and reduces blood pressure.
- **Finnish sauna studies** — Landmark research: 4-7 sauna sessions per week associated with 40% reduction in all-cause mortality, 50% reduction in cardiovascular death, and reduced risk of dementia
- **Growth hormone** — Sauna can increase GH release 2-5x as an acute response. Multiple sauna sessions in a single day with cooling breaks can produce even larger GH spikes.
- **Detoxification** — Sweat eliminates heavy metals (arsenic, cadmium, lead, mercury), BPA, phthalates, and other stored toxins. Infrared sauna may be particularly effective for mobilizing stored toxins.
- **BDNF** — Heat stress increases brain-derived neurotrophic factor, supporting neuroplasticity, learning, and mood regulation
