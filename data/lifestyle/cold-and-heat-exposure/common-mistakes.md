# Temperature Therapy

## Common Mistakes

- **Starting too extreme** — Jumping into an ice bath on day 1 is unnecessary and potentially dangerous. Start with cold showers and work up gradually over weeks.
- **Cold plunging immediately after resistance training** — May reduce the hypertrophy signal by blunting the inflammatory response needed for muscle growth. Save cold plunges for non-training days or at least 4+ hours after lifting.
- **Not hydrating before sauna** — Risk of dehydration, dizziness, and fainting. Drink water with electrolytes before and after every sauna session.
- **Staying in too long trying to be tough** — Diminishing returns after the optimal window, and increasing safety risk. More is not always better with temperature therapy.
- **Doing contrast therapy in the wrong order for their goal** — Ending on cold maximizes metabolic benefits; ending on hot promotes relaxation. Choose based on your goal for that session.
- **Using cold to "hack" inflammation when the real issue needs medical attention** — Cold reduces inflammation symptomatically but does not address underlying injuries or conditions that require proper diagnosis and treatment.
- **Not getting medical clearance with cardiovascular conditions** — Both extreme cold and extreme heat place significant stress on the cardiovascular system. Anyone with heart conditions, blood pressure issues, or a history of stroke should consult their doctor first.
- **Expecting cold showers to replace exercise** — Cold exposure has metabolic benefits but does not replace the full spectrum of adaptations from resistance training and cardiovascular exercise. It is a complement, not a substitute.
