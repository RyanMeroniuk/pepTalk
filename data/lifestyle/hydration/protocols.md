# Hydration & Electrolytes

## Hydration Protocols

### Baseline Daily Intake

- **General formula** — Drink half your body weight (in lbs) in ounces of water daily (e.g., 180 lbs = 90 oz)
- **Exercise adjustment** — Add 16-24 oz per hour of exercise, more in heat or high humidity
- **Minimum floor** — Most adults should aim for at least 64 oz (8 cups) regardless of body weight

### GLP-1 Agonist Users

- **Sip between meals** — Do not drink large amounts during meals, as this worsens nausea and bloating
- **Fluid-rich foods count** — Soups, broths, cucumbers, watermelon, celery, and berries all contribute to hydration
- **Set hydration alarms** — Appetite suppression often causes people to forget to eat AND drink; set phone reminders every 1-2 hours
- **Electrolyte priority** — GLP-1-related constipation is worsened by dehydration; electrolytes help retain water in the gut

### Electrolyte Protocol

- **Morning ritual** — Add a pinch of high-quality sea salt or Himalayan salt to your first glass of water
- **Commercial electrolyte mixes** — LMNT, Liquid IV, Drip Drop, Re-Lyte, or Nuun tablets
- **DIY electrolyte recipe** — Per 32 oz of water: 1/4 tsp sea salt + 1/4 tsp potassium chloride (NoSalt/Nu-Salt) + squeeze of lemon or lime for taste
- **Frequency** — 1-2 electrolyte servings per day for most people; more if sweating heavily or on GLP-1 agonists

### Timing

- **Morning priority** — You lose 1-2 lbs of water overnight through breathing and perspiration; rehydrate first thing with 16 oz of water
- **Pre-exercise** — Drink 16-20 oz of water 2 hours before exercise and another 8 oz 15-30 minutes before
- **During exercise** — 4-8 oz every 15-20 minutes during activity
- **Evening cutoff** — Limit fluid intake 2 hours before bed to prevent sleep disruption from nighttime urination
- **Consistent sipping** — Spread intake throughout the day rather than consuming large amounts at once

### Water Quality

- **Minimum filtration** — Filter chlorine and chloramine at a minimum (standard carbon filter handles chlorine; catalytic carbon for chloramine)
- **Optimal setup** — Reverse osmosis filtration + mineral drops or remineralization filter to add back beneficial minerals
- **Container choice** — Use glass or stainless steel bottles; avoid plastic containers, especially in heat
- **BPA/phthalate avoidance** — Never leave plastic water bottles in a hot car, in direct sunlight, or microwave them
- **Municipal water** — Request your local water quality report (required by law) to know what contaminants are present
