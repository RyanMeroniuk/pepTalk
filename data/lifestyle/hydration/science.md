# Hydration & Electrolytes

## The Science of Hydration

### Intracellular vs Extracellular Fluid Balance

- **Intracellular fluid (ICF)** — About 60% of total body water resides inside cells, where it participates in metabolic reactions and maintains cell structure
- **Extracellular fluid (ECF)** — The remaining 40% exists outside cells in blood plasma, interstitial fluid, and lymph, serving as the transport medium for nutrients and waste
- **Fluid balance** — The body constantly regulates the movement of water between these compartments based on electrolyte concentrations (osmotic pressure)

### The Sodium-Potassium Pump

- **Na+/K+ ATPase** — This pump actively transports 3 sodium ions out and 2 potassium ions into the cell, using ATP energy
- **Drives cellular hydration** — This pump creates the electrochemical gradient that governs water movement across cell membranes
- **Energy cost** — The sodium-potassium pump consumes approximately 20-40% of your resting energy expenditure, underscoring how fundamental fluid balance is to survival

### ADH (Antidiuretic Hormone)

- **Vasopressin** — Released by the posterior pituitary gland when blood osmolality rises (dehydration signal)
- **Kidney action** — ADH tells the kidneys to reabsorb water rather than excrete it, concentrating urine
- **Feedback loop** — When you drink enough water, ADH secretion decreases, and urine becomes dilute and pale

### Effects of Dehydration

- **Cognitive function** — A loss of just 2% body weight in water produces measurable declines in attention, working memory, reaction time, and mood
- **Exercise performance** — Dehydration of 2-3% reduces endurance, strength, and power output; perceived effort increases significantly
- **Kidney function** — Chronic mild dehydration increases the risk of kidney stones and may contribute to long-term kidney damage
- **Blood pressure** — Dehydration reduces blood volume, potentially causing orthostatic hypotension (dizziness when standing)
- **Skin elasticity** — Dehydrated skin loses turgor, appears dull, and is more prone to wrinkles and slow wound healing

### Electrolyte Roles

- **Sodium** — Primary extracellular electrolyte; regulates fluid balance, blood pressure, and nerve impulse transmission
- **Potassium** — Primary intracellular electrolyte; critical for heart rhythm, muscle contraction, and nerve signaling
- **Magnesium** — Cofactor in over 300 enzymatic reactions including energy production, protein synthesis, muscle/nerve function, and blood glucose control

### Osmolality and "Hydrated but Dehydrated"

- **Osmolality** — The concentration of dissolved particles in a fluid; your body tightly regulates blood osmolality between 275-295 mOsm/kg
- **Why plain water isn't always enough** — Drinking large amounts of plain water without electrolytes dilutes blood sodium (hyponatremia), triggering the kidneys to excrete the excess water rather than retain it
- **Cellular dehydration** — You can drink plenty of water yet remain dehydrated at the cellular level if electrolyte concentrations are insufficient to drive water into cells
- **Practical implication** — Adding even a small amount of sodium and potassium to water dramatically improves cellular absorption and retention
