# Hydration & Electrolytes

## Supplement Connections

- **Magnesium** — Both a critical hydration cofactor and one of the most commonly under-consumed electrolytes. Magnesium is required for the sodium-potassium pump to function, meaning cellular hydration literally depends on adequate magnesium levels. Forms like magnesium glycinate or magnesium L-threonate are well-absorbed and gentle on the gut
- **Sodium / Potassium** — The core electrolyte pair that governs fluid distribution between intracellular and extracellular compartments. Most people need more potassium (4,700 mg/day recommended), not less sodium. Potassium-rich foods include avocados, bananas, potatoes, and leafy greens. Potassium chloride (NoSalt) can be added to water
- **Creatine** — Increases intracellular water through osmotic draw into muscle cells. When supplementing creatine (typically 3-5g daily), add an extra 16-24 oz of water per day to compensate for the increased intracellular water demand
- **Collagen** — Collagen peptide powder mixes easily into water, making it a convenient way to combine supplementation with hydration. Dissolve 10-20g in a large glass of water for a two-in-one habit
- **Vitamin C** — Adding vitamin C powder or a squeeze of citrus to electrolyte water improves taste and provides an antioxidant boost. Vitamin C also supports collagen synthesis and iron absorption
- **Zinc** — Adequate hydration supports zinc absorption in the small intestine. Zinc is best taken with water on a mildly full stomach to prevent nausea, and proper hydration ensures it reaches absorptive surfaces efficiently
