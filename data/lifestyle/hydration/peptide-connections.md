# Hydration & Electrolytes

## Peptide Connections

- **Semaglutide / Tirzepatide / Retatrutide** — Nausea and reduced appetite make people forget to drink, leading to dehydration that worsens constipation (already a common GLP-1 side effect). Sip fluids between meals rather than during meals. Electrolytes help retain water in the gut and reduce constipation severity
- **MK-677 (Ibutamoren)** — Causes water retention and bloating as a common side effect. Paradoxically, staying well-hydrated with adequate electrolytes helps the body manage this by reducing aldosterone-driven sodium retention. Limiting excess sodium while maintaining potassium intake can ease bloating
- **Creatine** — Draws water into muscle cells through osmotic action, increasing intracellular water content. This requires an additional 16-24 oz of daily water intake to prevent dehydration of other tissues and support kidney function
- **CJC-1295 / Ipamorelin** — Adequate hydration supports the transport of growth hormone through the bloodstream and optimizes cellular uptake. GH-mediated tissue repair and recovery processes are water-dependent
- **BPC-157** — Tissue healing and angiogenesis (new blood vessel formation) require robust blood flow and nutrient delivery, both of which depend on proper hydration. Dehydration slows healing
- **Semax** — The most commonly reported side effect of Semax is headache, which is frequently dehydration-related. Ensuring adequate hydration before and during Semax use significantly reduces headache incidence
- **All injectable peptides** — Proper hydration supports subcutaneous blood flow and tissue perfusion, improving absorption of injected peptides from the injection site. Dehydrated tissue absorbs peptides more slowly and less consistently
- **TB-500 (Thymosin Beta-4)** — Wound healing and anti-inflammatory effects depend on adequate blood flow and lymphatic drainage, both impaired by dehydration
- **AOD-9604** — Fat metabolism requires adequate water for lipolysis and for the kidneys to process metabolic byproducts of fat breakdown
