# Hydration & Electrolytes

## Common Mistakes

- **Drinking too much plain water without electrolytes** — This dilutes blood sodium levels (hyponatremia), which paradoxically can worsen cellular dehydration and cause headaches, fatigue, and confusion
- **Chugging large amounts at once** — The kidneys can only process about 27-33 oz per hour; excess is simply excreted. Sipping throughout the day is far more effective for absorption
- **Drinking lots of water with meals** — Large amounts of water during meals dilute digestive enzymes and stomach acid, impairing nutrient absorption. This also worsens nausea in GLP-1 agonist users
- **Relying on thirst alone** — By the time you feel thirsty, you are already 1-2% dehydrated, which is enough to impair cognitive and physical performance. Drink proactively on a schedule
- **Counting coffee as full hydration** — Coffee is a mild diuretic at higher doses (300+ mg caffeine). While moderate coffee consumption is not significantly dehydrating, it should not be your primary fluid source
- **Ignoring urine color** — Urine should be pale straw/light yellow. Clear urine means you are over-hydrating or flushing electrolytes. Dark yellow or amber means you need more fluids
- **Not increasing water with creatine supplementation** — Creatine pulls water into muscle cells, increasing total body water requirements by at least 16-24 oz per day
- **Drinking from plastic bottles left in heat** — Heat accelerates the leaching of BPA, phthalates, and microplastics into water. This applies to bottles left in cars, in direct sunlight, or near heat sources
