# Hydration & Electrolytes

## Why Hydration Matters Beyond "Drink More Water"

Hydration is foundational to nearly every biological process in the body, yet most people think of it as simply "drinking enough water." True hydration is about maintaining optimal fluid and electrolyte balance at the cellular level.

- **Cellular function** — Every cell in your body requires adequate water to perform metabolic reactions, transport nutrients, and remove waste products
- **Nutrient transport** — Water is the medium through which vitamins, minerals, amino acids, and glucose travel to cells throughout the body
- **Temperature regulation** — Sweating and blood flow adjustments depend on adequate hydration to prevent overheating during exercise and daily activity
- **Joint lubrication** — Synovial fluid in joints is primarily water; dehydration leads to increased friction, stiffness, and joint pain
- **Cognitive function** — The brain is approximately 75% water; even mild dehydration (1-2% body weight loss) causes measurable declines in focus, memory, and mood

## Special Considerations for Peptide Users

- **GLP-1 agonist users** (Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, Retatrutide) — Nausea and reduced appetite frequently cause people to forget to drink, worsening constipation and other side effects
- **MK-677 users** — Water retention is a common side effect; proper hydration and electrolyte balance help manage bloating
- **Creatine users** — Creatine draws water into muscle cells, increasing total water requirements

## Electrolytes Are as Important as Water Volume

Drinking water without adequate electrolytes can actually worsen dehydration at the cellular level. Sodium, potassium, and magnesium are the key players that allow your cells to absorb and retain the water you drink. Without them, water passes through without being properly utilized.
