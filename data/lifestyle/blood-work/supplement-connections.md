# Blood Work & Health Monitoring

## Supplement Connections

- **Vitamin D3** — test 25-OH vitamin D before and after supplementation; optimal range is 40-60 ng/mL; most people need 2,000-5,000 IU daily to reach optimal levels; retest after 8-12 weeks of consistent supplementation to confirm you've reached target
- **B-Complex / B12 / Folate** — check serum B12 and folate levels; homocysteine is a functional marker that indicates whether methylation is working properly; elevated homocysteine (above 8 umol/L) suggests B-vitamin insufficiency or MTHFR polymorphism requiring methylated forms (methylfolate, methylcobalamin)
- **Iron** — ferritin is the primary storage marker; do not supplement iron without testing first because iron overload (hemochromatosis) is dangerous and more common than people realize; optimal ferritin is 40-100 ng/mL for most adults; also check serum iron, TIBC, and transferrin saturation for a complete picture
- **Magnesium** — serum magnesium is unreliable because only 1% of total body magnesium is in the blood; RBC magnesium (magnesium inside red blood cells) is a better indicator of true magnesium status; optimal RBC magnesium is 5.0-6.5 mg/dL
- **Omega-3 (Fish Oil / EPA+DHA)** — the Omega-3 Index test measures the percentage of omega-3 fatty acids in red blood cell membranes; target is 8-12%; most people without supplementation are at 4-5%, which is associated with higher cardiovascular risk
- **Zinc / Copper** — test both if supplementing zinc long-term because zinc and copper compete for absorption; chronic zinc supplementation without copper can cause copper deficiency, leading to anemia and neurological symptoms; maintain a zinc-to-copper ratio of roughly 10:1
- **CoQ10** — no standard blood test is widely available, but supplementation is particularly important for statin users because statins deplete CoQ10; typical dose is 100-200mg daily in ubiquinol form
- **Vitamin K2** — no routine blood test; important to pair with vitamin D3 supplementation to direct calcium into bones rather than arteries; check calcium score if cardiovascular risk is a concern
- **Iodine** — urinary iodine is the standard test; important for thyroid function; if thyroid markers (TSH, T3, T4) are suboptimal, iodine status should be evaluated before supplementing
