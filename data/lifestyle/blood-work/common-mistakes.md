# Blood Work & Health Monitoring

## Common Mistakes

- **Never getting baseline labs before starting peptides** — without a pre-peptide baseline, you have no way to know what changed, what improved, or what may have worsened; always draw blood before day one of any protocol
- **Only looking at "normal" ranges** — lab reference ranges include the general population, many of whom are metabolically unhealthy; a fasting insulin of 20 uIU/mL is "normal" by lab standards but indicates significant insulin resistance
- **Testing non-fasted when fasting is required** — glucose, insulin, and triglycerides are significantly affected by recent food intake; eating before a fasting blood draw produces unreliable results and wastes money
- **Testing IGF-1 too soon** — IGF-1 takes 4-6 weeks to stabilize after starting GH peptides; testing at 2 weeks gives you an incomplete and misleading picture
- **Not testing frequently enough when changing protocols** — when you change a peptide, adjust a dose, or add something new, you need follow-up labs at 8-12 weeks to assess the impact
- **Ignoring trends** — a marker moving consistently in the wrong direction matters even if every individual reading falls within the reference range; a fasting glucose climbing from 78 to 82 to 88 to 94 is a clear trajectory toward metabolic dysfunction
- **Only looking at individual markers instead of patterns** — high fasting glucose alone tells one story, but high glucose combined with high insulin, high triglycerides, and high hs-CRP tells a much more urgent story about metabolic syndrome
- **Not bringing peptide use up with your doctor** — your physician needs to know what you are taking to properly interpret your lab results; elevated IGF-1 in a patient on CJC-1295 is expected, but in an uninformed doctor it may trigger unnecessary concern or testing
- **Drawing blood at inconsistent times** — cortisol, testosterone, and other markers fluctuate throughout the day; always draw in the morning at roughly the same time for comparable results
- **Training hard before a blood draw** — intense exercise temporarily elevates liver enzymes (AST, ALT), CRP, and creatine kinase; avoid heavy training 48 hours before your draw to avoid false elevations
