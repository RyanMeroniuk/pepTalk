# Nutrition & Eating Patterns

## The Science Behind Nutritional Optimization

- **Muscle protein synthesis (MPS) and the leucine threshold** — MPS is maximally stimulated when a meal contains approximately 2.5-3g of leucine, which corresponds to roughly 30-50g of high-quality protein per meal. Below this threshold, you get suboptimal muscle-building signaling. Spreading protein across 3-4 meals per day maximizes total daily MPS compared to consuming the same amount in 1-2 meals.
- **Thermic effect of food (TEF)** — protein has the highest thermic effect of any macronutrient at 20-30%, meaning your body burns 20-30% of protein calories just digesting and processing it. Carbohydrates have a TEF of 5-10%, and fat only 0-3%. This is one reason high-protein diets support body composition even at similar calorie levels.
- **Glycemic response and insulin dynamics** — rapid blood sugar spikes trigger large insulin responses, which promote fat storage and create energy crashes. Eating protein and fiber before carbohydrates in a meal flattens the glucose curve by 30-40%. This is especially relevant for metabolic peptide users where insulin sensitivity is a primary target.
- **Inflammatory pathways and the omega-6/omega-3 ratio** — the modern diet delivers omega-6 to omega-3 ratios of 15:1 or higher, when the optimal range is closer to 2:1 to 4:1. Excess omega-6 (from seed oils, processed foods) drives pro-inflammatory eicosanoid production. Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA) produce anti-inflammatory resolvins and protectins that support every healing and immune peptide protocol.
- **The gut-brain axis and food choices** — the enteric nervous system contains over 500 million neurons. Gut bacteria produce approximately 90% of the body's serotonin and significant amounts of GABA, dopamine, and short-chain fatty acids. Dietary fiber, fermented foods, and polyphenols feed beneficial bacteria, while processed foods, artificial sweeteners, and emulsifiers damage the gut barrier and shift the microbiome toward inflammatory species.
- **How GLP-1 drugs change gastric emptying and appetite signaling** — semaglutide and tirzepatide slow gastric emptying by 30-50%, meaning food sits in the stomach much longer. This is why large meals cause nausea and why high-fat foods are poorly tolerated. The drugs also act centrally in the hypothalamus to reduce hunger signals and increase satiety. This fundamentally changes how and what you should eat.
- **Fasting and autophagy** — fasting periods of 16+ hours activate autophagy, the cellular recycling process that clears damaged proteins and organelles. This is particularly relevant for longevity peptides and anti-aging protocols. Growth hormone secretion increases 2-5x during fasting, which synergizes with GH peptides administered in a fasted state.
- **mTOR and AMPK pathways** — these are the body's two master metabolic switches. mTOR (mechanistic target of rapamycin) is activated by protein and insulin, driving muscle growth and cellular proliferation. AMPK (AMP-activated protein kinase) is activated by fasting, exercise, and energy deficit, driving fat oxidation, autophagy, and mitochondrial biogenesis. MOTS-c peptide directly activates AMPK. Strategic cycling between fed (mTOR) and fasted (AMPK) states optimizes both muscle building and cellular cleanup.
