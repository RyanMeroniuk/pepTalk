# Nutrition & Eating Patterns

## Protocols

### Protein-First Eating (Universal)

- **Core rule** — eat protein first at every meal before touching carbs or fats
- **Daily target** — 1.6-2.2g of protein per kilogram of body weight (0.7-1.0g per pound)
- **Per-meal target** — 30-50g of protein per meal to reliably hit the leucine threshold for maximal muscle protein synthesis
- **Meal frequency** — spread protein across 3-4 meals per day rather than backloading into one or two
- **Best sources** — chicken breast, fish (salmon, tuna, cod), eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, whey protein isolate, lean beef, turkey
- **Leucine-rich options** — whey protein, chicken breast, and eggs have the highest leucine content per gram of protein
- **Tracking** — weigh and track protein for at least one week to calibrate your intuition, then adjust as needed

### GLP-1 Eating Protocol (Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, Retatrutide Users)

- **Protein first, always** — with reduced appetite, every gram of food matters; prioritize protein above all else
- **Small, frequent meals** — eat 4-6 smaller meals rather than 2-3 large ones to avoid nausea and maximize nutrient absorption with slowed gastric emptying
- **Avoid high-fat greasy foods** — fried foods, heavy sauces, and greasy meals cause nausea, dumping syndrome, and gastrointestinal distress due to delayed stomach emptying
- **Separate fluids from meals** — drink water and other liquids between meals, not during, to avoid filling already-limited stomach capacity and worsening nausea
- **Walk 10 minutes after eating** — gentle movement aids the slowed digestion process and reduces post-meal discomfort
- **Stop eating before feeling full** — the fullness signal is significantly delayed on GLP-1 drugs; by the time you feel full, you have likely overeaten
- **Bad nausea day options** — protein shakes, bone broth, Greek yogurt, scrambled eggs, cottage cheese, and smoothies provide nutrition when solid food is intolerable
- **Minimum calorie floor** — never drop below 1200 calories per day regardless of appetite suppression; malnutrition causes muscle loss, hair loss, and metabolic damage
- **Supplement gaps** — use a quality multivitamin and electrolytes to cover micronutrient shortfalls from reduced food intake

### Anti-Inflammatory Diet

- **Foundation foods** — vegetables, fruits, fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel), extra virgin olive oil, nuts (walnuts, almonds), seeds (flax, chia, hemp)
- **Foods to avoid** — seed oils (soybean, corn, canola, sunflower), highly processed foods, added sugar, excess alcohol, refined carbohydrates
- **Plant diversity** — aim for 30 or more different plant species per week for maximum microbiome diversity and short-chain fatty acid production
- **Fermented foods** — target 6 or more servings per week of yogurt, kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi, kombucha, or miso
- **Spices and polyphenols** — turmeric (with black pepper for absorption), ginger, green tea, dark berries, and dark chocolate (85%+) provide concentrated anti-inflammatory compounds

### Fasting Protocols

- **Pre-injection fasting for GH peptides** — no food for at least 2 hours before injecting CJC-1295, ipamorelin, or other GH secretagogues, and no food for 30 minutes after; carbohydrates and fats blunt growth hormone release
- **16:8 intermittent fasting** — eat within an 8-hour window, fast for 16 hours; compatible with most peptide protocols and activates AMPK and autophagy pathways
- **Extended fasting caution with GLP-1 drugs** — fasting beyond 16 hours while on semaglutide, tirzepatide, or retatrutide carries hypoglycemia risk due to altered glucose regulation; monitor blood sugar and break fast if symptomatic
- **Strategic refeeding** — break fasts with protein and moderate carbohydrates to activate mTOR for muscle preservation while transitioning out of the fasted state
