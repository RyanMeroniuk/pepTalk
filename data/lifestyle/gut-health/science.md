# Gut & Digestive Health

## The Science of Gut Health

## Gut Barrier

- **Tight junctions** — protein complexes (claudins, occludins, zonula occludens) that seal the spaces between intestinal epithelial cells; they control what passes from the gut lumen into the bloodstream
- **Zonulin pathway** — zonulin is a protein that modulates tight junction permeability; gluten and certain bacteria trigger zonulin release, which loosens tight junctions and increases intestinal permeability
- **Intestinal permeability** — when tight junctions are compromised, the gut becomes "leaky," allowing lipopolysaccharides (LPS), undigested food proteins, and bacterial fragments to enter circulation, triggering immune responses and systemic inflammation

## The Microbiome

- **100 trillion organisms** — the gut houses bacteria, fungi, archaea, and viruses that collectively outnumber human cells; this ecosystem is called the microbiome
- **Diversity matters more than specific strains** — research consistently shows that microbiome diversity (the number of different species) correlates more strongly with health outcomes than the presence of any single "good" strain
- **Firmicutes and Bacteroidetes** — the two dominant phyla in the human gut; their ratio shifts with diet, health status, and body composition
- **Keystone species** — certain bacteria like Akkermansia muciniphila and Faecalibacterium prausnitzii play outsized roles in maintaining gut barrier integrity and reducing inflammation

## Gut-Brain Axis

- **Vagus nerve** — the primary communication highway between the gut and brain; it carries signals bidirectionally, meaning gut state affects brain function and brain state affects gut function
- **Serotonin production** — approximately 90% of the body's serotonin is produced in the gut by enterochromaffin cells; gut inflammation and dysbiosis directly affect serotonin availability
- **GABA production** — certain gut bacteria (Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium species) produce GABA, the brain's primary inhibitory neurotransmitter; this is one mechanism by which probiotics can influence anxiety and mood
- **Inflammation-cognition link** — gut-derived inflammation crosses the blood-brain barrier and contributes to brain fog, depression, and neurodegeneration

## Short-Chain Fatty Acids (SCFAs)

- **Butyrate** — the primary fuel for colonocytes (colon lining cells); strengthens gut barrier, reduces inflammation, and regulates immune function; produced by bacterial fermentation of dietary fiber
- **Propionate** — involved in gluconeogenesis and appetite regulation; supports metabolic health
- **Acetate** — the most abundant SCFA; crosses the blood-brain barrier and influences appetite regulation through the hypothalamus
- **Fiber is the raw material** — without adequate dietary fiber, gut bacteria cannot produce SCFAs; this is why fiber diversity directly impacts gut health

## Dysbiosis

- **Definition** — an imbalance between beneficial and pathogenic organisms in the gut; can be caused by antibiotics, poor diet, chronic stress, infections, or environmental toxins
- **Consequences** — increased intestinal permeability, reduced SCFA production, immune dysregulation, increased inflammation, and impaired nutrient absorption
- **SIBO (Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth)** — bacteria that normally reside in the large intestine migrate to the small intestine, causing bloating, gas, malabsorption, and nutrient deficiencies

## Migrating Motor Complex (MMC)

- **The cleansing wave** — a cyclical pattern of electrical activity that sweeps through the stomach and small intestine during fasting, moving debris, bacteria, and undigested material toward the large intestine
- **Requires 4+ hours between meals** — the MMC only activates in a fasted state; constant snacking prevents it from firing
- **SIBO connection** — impaired MMC function is one of the primary causes of SIBO because bacteria are not being swept out of the small intestine
- **Meal spacing matters** — eating 2-3 meals with adequate spacing (4-5 hours) supports MMC function better than grazing throughout the day
