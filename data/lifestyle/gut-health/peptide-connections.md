# Gut & Digestive Health

## Peptide Connections

- **BPC-157** — the premier gut healing peptide, originally isolated from human gastric juice; oral dosing (500-750mcg) targets gut-specific effects including ulcer healing, gut barrier repair, counteracting NSAID damage, and promoting angiogenesis in damaged tissue; shown to accelerate healing of esophageal, gastric, and intestinal lesions in research
- **KPV** — a tripeptide derived from alpha-MSH that inhibits NF-kB, reducing intestinal inflammation at the cellular level; best paired with BPC-157 for a comprehensive approach combining barrier repair (BPC-157) with inflammation reduction (KPV)
- **Larazotide** — a tight junction regulator that directly addresses the mechanism of leaky gut by blocking the zonulin pathway; zonulin is the protein that loosens tight junctions, and Larazotide prevents this process; studied primarily in the context of celiac disease but applicable to general intestinal permeability
- **VIP (Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide)** — a neuropeptide with broad gut effects including vasodilation, anti-inflammatory action, and immune regulation in the intestinal mucosa; critical for gut recovery in CIRS (Chronic Inflammatory Response Syndrome) and mold illness, where gut dysfunction is a central feature
- **Semaglutide / Tirzepatide / Retatrutide** — GLP-1 agonists slow gastric emptying, which changes digestive patterns significantly; common GI side effects (nausea, constipation, diarrhea) are managed more effectively when foundational gut health practices are in place; adequate hydration, fiber, and meal timing become especially important on these drugs
- **Thymosin Alpha-1** — with 70% of the immune system residing in the gut, immune-modulating peptides have direct relevance to gut immune function; Thymosin Alpha-1 supports T-cell maturation and function in the GALT (gut-associated lymphoid tissue)
- **LL-37** — an antimicrobial peptide of the innate immune system with anti-biofilm properties; relevant for addressing pathogenic bacterial overgrowth in the gut and disrupting biofilms that protect harmful organisms from both the immune system and antimicrobial treatments
