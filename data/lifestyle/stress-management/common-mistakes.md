# Stress & Cortisol Management

## Common Mistakes

- **Thinking stress management is "soft" or optional** — cortisol directly suppresses GH release, immune function, gut healing, and sleep quality. Ignoring stress management while spending money on peptides is like ignoring a leak in the boat while bailing water. It is pharmacologically relevant, not a lifestyle luxury.
- **Only addressing stress when it is already overwhelming** — stress management works best as a daily preventive practice, not an emergency response. By the time you feel overwhelmed, cortisol has already done significant damage to sleep, immunity, and metabolic function. Consistent daily practices (even 5 minutes) prevent accumulation.
- **Using alcohol or cannabis as stress relief** — both disrupt sleep architecture, even if they help you fall asleep. Alcohol suppresses REM sleep and fragments sleep cycles. Cannabis suppresses deep slow-wave sleep. Both impair the nighttime GH pulse and undermine recovery that peptide protocols depend on.
- **Ignoring the cortisol-GH antagonism** — this is the most costly mistake for GH peptide users. Elevated cortisol directly suppresses growth hormone release from the pituitary. If you are chronically stressed, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, sermorelin, and other GH secretagogues are working against a headwind. Stress literally makes your peptides less effective.
- **Overexercising as stress relief** — intense exercise is itself a physiological stressor that raises cortisol. Using daily intense workouts to "burn off" mental stress adds physical stress on top of psychological stress. The result is elevated total cortisol, impaired recovery, and overtraining. Include low-intensity movement (walking, yoga, swimming) as stress relief instead.
- **Doom scrolling and news consumption as "relaxation"** — passive social media and news consumption activates the sympathetic nervous system through constant novelty-seeking and threat detection. Your brain cannot distinguish between a real threat and a threatening headline. This is chronic low-grade stress that most people have completely normalized.
- **Not recognizing chronic low-grade stress** — acute stress is obvious (argument, deadline, near-miss in traffic). Chronic low-grade stress (unfulfilling work, relationship tension, financial worry, information overload) becomes the water you swim in. It feels normal but cortisol remains elevated. HRV tracking can reveal this hidden stress load objectively.
- **Skipping rest days** — recovery is when adaptation happens. Rest days are not laziness; they are when muscles repair, the nervous system resets, and cortisol returns to baseline. Peptide protocols that support recovery (BPC-157, TB-500, GH peptides) require actual rest periods to do their work.
