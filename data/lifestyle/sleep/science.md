# Sleep & Circadian Rhythm

## The Science of Sleep

### Sleep Architecture

- **N1 (Light Sleep)**: The transition stage between wakefulness and sleep, lasting 1-5 minutes. Muscle activity slows, and you can be easily awakened. Comprises about 5% of total sleep.
- **N2 (Intermediate Sleep)**: Heart rate and body temperature drop. Sleep spindles and K-complexes appear on EEG, which are critical for memory consolidation. Comprises about 45-55% of total sleep.
- **N3 (Deep/Slow-Wave Sleep)**: The most restorative stage. Delta brain waves dominate. This is when growth hormone is released in its largest pulse of the day, tissue repair occurs, the immune system strengthens, and energy is restored. Comprises about 15-25% of total sleep, concentrated in the first half of the night.
- **REM (Rapid Eye Movement)**: The dreaming stage. The brain is highly active while the body is temporarily paralyzed. Critical for memory consolidation, emotional processing, creativity, and learning. Comprises about 20-25% of total sleep, concentrated in the second half of the night.

### Why Deep Sleep Matters

- **Growth hormone release**: The largest GH pulse of the day occurs during the first bout of N3 sleep, typically within the first 90 minutes of falling asleep. This is why GH secretagogue peptides are often dosed at bedtime.
- **Tissue repair**: Muscle repair, bone growth, and cellular regeneration are concentrated during deep sleep. Athletes who get more deep sleep recover faster.
- **Immune restoration**: Deep sleep drives the production of cytokines and enhances T-cell adhesion to infected cells.

### Why REM Matters

- **Memory consolidation**: REM sleep integrates new information with existing knowledge, forming long-term memories and creative insights.
- **Emotional regulation**: REM sleep processes emotional experiences, reducing the emotional charge of memories. Insufficient REM is linked to anxiety, depression, and emotional reactivity.

### Circadian Rhythm Biology

- **Suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN)**: A tiny cluster of about 20,000 neurons in the hypothalamus that serves as the master clock. It receives light input directly from the retina via melanopsin-containing retinal ganglion cells.
- **Melatonin production**: The SCN signals the pineal gland to produce melatonin as light diminishes in the evening. Melatonin does not induce sleep directly but signals to the body that it is time to prepare for sleep. Production begins about 2 hours before habitual bedtime (dim light melatonin onset, or DLMO).
- **Cortisol awakening response**: Cortisol peaks within 30-45 minutes of waking, providing alertness and energy. This natural spike is healthy and necessary. It should decline throughout the day, reaching its lowest point around midnight.
- **Light exposure sets the clock**: Bright light (especially blue wavelengths around 480nm) in the morning advances the clock and suppresses melatonin. Light exposure in the evening delays the clock and suppresses melatonin production, making it harder to fall asleep.

### The Glymphatic System

- **Brain cleanup during sleep**: The glymphatic system is a waste-clearance pathway that is primarily active during deep sleep. Cerebrospinal fluid flows through the brain along paravascular channels, flushing out metabolic waste products including amyloid-beta (associated with Alzheimer's disease) and tau proteins.
- **Sleep position matters**: Research suggests lateral (side) sleeping may enhance glymphatic clearance compared to supine or prone positions.
- **Implications**: Chronic sleep deprivation impairs glymphatic function, potentially accelerating neurodegenerative processes. This is one reason why sleep is considered neuroprotective and relevant to peptides like Pinealon and Semax that target brain health.
