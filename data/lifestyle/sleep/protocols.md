# Sleep & Circadian Rhythm

## Protocols

### Beginner Protocol

- **Consistent bedtime**: Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day, including weekends. Choose a bedtime that allows for 7-9 hours of sleep. Consistency is more important than the exact time.
- **Dark room**: Eliminate all light sources. Use blackout curtains or a sleep mask. Cover LED indicators on electronics. Even small amounts of light can suppress melatonin and reduce deep sleep.
- **No screens 30 minutes before bed**: The combination of blue light and mental stimulation from screens is a double hit to sleep quality. Read a physical book, stretch, or do breathing exercises instead.
- **Cool temperature**: Set your bedroom to 65-68°F (18-20°C). Core body temperature must drop by about 2-3°F to initiate and maintain sleep. A cool room facilitates this process.

### Intermediate Protocol

- **Morning sunlight within 30 minutes of waking**: Get outside and expose your eyes to natural light for 10-15 minutes (longer on cloudy days). Do not wear sunglasses. This is the single most powerful circadian signal and anchors your entire sleep-wake cycle.
- **Blue light glasses after sunset**: Wear amber or orange-tinted blue light blocking glasses after the sun goes down. This protects melatonin production from artificial indoor lighting.
- **No caffeine after noon**: Caffeine has a half-life of 5-7 hours. A coffee at 2pm still has 25% of its caffeine in your system at midnight. Even if you can fall asleep, caffeine reduces deep sleep by up to 20%.
- **Evening wind-down routine**: Start dimming lights 2 hours before bed. Establish a consistent 30-60 minute routine (reading, stretching, journaling, meditation). This trains your brain to recognize pre-sleep cues.
- **Bedroom as sleep-only sanctuary**: Remove TVs, work materials, and clutter from the bedroom. The bed is for sleep and intimacy only. This creates a strong psychological association between the bedroom and sleep.

### Advanced Protocol

- **Sleep tracking**: Use a wearable device (Oura Ring, Whoop, Apple Watch) to track sleep stages, HRV, respiratory rate, and sleep efficiency. Use data to identify patterns and optimize. Aim for 1.5+ hours of deep sleep and 1.5+ hours of REM.
- **Temperature manipulation**: Take a hot bath or sauna 1-2 hours before bed. The subsequent drop in core body temperature mimics the natural thermoregulatory signal for sleep onset. Combine with a cool bedroom (65-68°F) and a cooling mattress pad if available.
- **Supplement stack**: Magnesium glycinate (400mg) + L-theanine (200mg) + apigenin (50mg) taken 30-60 minutes before bed. This combination supports GABA activity, promotes alpha brain waves, and mildly inhibits CD38 to preserve NAD+.
- **Mouth taping**: Use medical-grade mouth tape to encourage nasal breathing during sleep. Nasal breathing produces nitric oxide, filters air, and promotes deeper sleep. Start with a small strip and work up to full coverage.
- **Humidity optimization**: Maintain bedroom humidity at 40-50%. Dry air irritates airways and disrupts sleep. Use a hygrometer to monitor and a humidifier or dehumidifier as needed.

### Special Protocols

- **Jet lag protocol**: For eastward travel, start shifting bedtime 30 minutes earlier per day for several days before departure. Use bright morning light at your destination. Short-acting melatonin (0.3-0.5mg) at the new target bedtime. For westward travel, seek evening light at your destination and delay bedtime gradually.
- **Shift work protocol**: Use blackout curtains and a sleep mask for daytime sleep. Wear blue light blocking glasses on the commute home. Keep the same sleep schedule on days off if possible. Use bright light at the start of your shift.
- **Napping protocol**: Naps should be either 20 minutes (power nap, avoids deep sleep inertia) or 90 minutes (full sleep cycle). Take naps before 2pm to avoid disrupting nighttime sleep. A "nappuccino" (coffee immediately before a 20-min nap) uses the 20-minute caffeine absorption delay to wake you up alert.
