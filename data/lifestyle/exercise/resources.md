# Exercise & Movement

## Resources

### Workout Tracking

- **Strong**: Clean, intuitive workout logging app. Track exercises, sets, reps, and weight. View progress over time with charts. Free tier is sufficient for most users. Available on iOS and Android.
- **Hevy**: Similar to Strong with a social component. Share workouts and follow friends for accountability. Includes routine templates and progress tracking.
- **JEFIT**: Large exercise database with animated demonstrations. Built-in programs and workout plans. Good for beginners who need guidance on exercise form.

### Step Counting

- **Apple Health**: Built into every iPhone. Automatically tracks steps, distance, and flights of stairs. Set daily goals and view trends over weeks and months.
- **Google Fit**: The Android equivalent of Apple Health. Tracks steps and active minutes. Integrates with most fitness wearables.
- **Pedometer apps**: Lightweight alternatives for those who want simple step counting without the complexity of a full health platform.

### Training Programs

- **Starting Strength (beginner)**: Mark Rippetoe's linear progression program. Three compound lifts per session (squat, press, deadlift/bench/clean), 3x5 reps, 3x/week. Add weight every session. The simplest and most effective beginner program.
- **GZCLP (beginner to intermediate)**: A flexible linear progression program with a tiered structure: heavy compound lifts, moderate accessory work, and light isolation work. Good transition from Starting Strength.
- **5/3/1 (intermediate)**: Jim Wendler's program based on monthly periodization. Four main lifts with submaximal training and planned progression. Sustainable long-term and highly adaptable to different goals and schedules.

### Heart Rate Monitoring

- **Polar H10 chest strap**: The gold standard for accurate heart rate monitoring. Essential for Zone 2 training where precise heart rate zones matter. Wrist-based monitors (watches) are less accurate during exercise. Connects via Bluetooth to any fitness app.
- **Zone 2 estimation**: A simple starting point is 180 minus your age for the upper limit of Zone 2. Refine with a chest strap and perceived exertion (you should be able to hold a conversation but prefer not to).

### Body Composition

- **DEXA scan**: Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry. The gold standard for measuring body fat percentage, lean mass, bone density, and regional body composition. Get a baseline scan, then repeat every 3-6 months to track changes. Especially valuable for GLP-1 users to verify they are losing fat, not muscle.
- **InBody scan**: Bioelectrical impedance analysis available at many gyms. Less accurate than DEXA but more accessible and affordable. Useful for tracking trends over time when using the same machine under consistent conditions (same time of day, hydration state).

### Metrics to Track

- **Progressive overload**: The total weight moved (weight x reps x sets) should trend upward over weeks and months. This is the primary driver of muscle growth and strength gains. Track in a training log.
- **Step count**: Daily steps as a measure of overall movement and NEAT. Aim for 8,000-12,000+ depending on your level.
- **VO2 max estimate**: Available through Apple Watch, Garmin, and other wearables. The single strongest predictor of all-cause mortality. Track trends over months. Improve with Zone 2 cardio and HIIT.

### Books

- **"Starting Strength" by Mark Rippetoe**: The definitive guide to barbell training. Covers squat, bench press, deadlift, overhead press, and power clean with detailed technical instruction. Essential for anyone learning to lift.
- **"Outlive" by Peter Attia**: Focuses on exercise as medicine for longevity. Covers the evidence for resistance training, Zone 2 cardio, VO2 max, and stability training as the four pillars of exercise for a long, healthy life. Provides the "why" behind exercise recommendations for health optimization.
