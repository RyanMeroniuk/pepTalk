# Exercise & Movement

## Supplement Connections

- **Creatine (5g daily)**: The most studied supplement in existence with decades of safety data. Creatine increases phosphocreatine stores in muscle, providing energy for short bursts of high-intensity effort (heavy lifts, sprints). It improves strength, power output, and muscle hydration. It supports lean mass preservation during caloric deficits, making it especially valuable for GLP-1 users. No loading phase is necessary; 5g daily reaches saturation within 3-4 weeks. Mix in water and take at any time of day. Creatine monohydrate is the only form with robust evidence.
- **Collagen Peptides (10-15g) + Vitamin C before training**: The Keith Baar protocol for tendon and ligament health. Consuming collagen peptides with 50mg vitamin C 30-60 minutes before training increases collagen synthesis in tendons, ligaments, and cartilage. This supports joint health, reduces injury risk, and complements healing peptides like BPC-157 and TB-500. Especially important for those over 30 or with a history of connective tissue injuries.
- **Omega-3 Fatty Acids (2-4g EPA/DHA daily)**: Reduces exercise-induced inflammation and supports recovery between training sessions. Omega-3s also improve cell membrane fluidity, which enhances nutrient uptake and insulin sensitivity in muscle cells. Look for products standardized to EPA and DHA content rather than total fish oil. Take with a fat-containing meal for absorption.
- **Magnesium (400mg daily)**: Supports over 300 enzymatic reactions including muscle contraction, relaxation, and energy production. Magnesium deficiency causes muscle cramps, impaired recovery, and poor sleep quality. Glycinate form is preferred for sleep and relaxation; citrate for those who need GI motility support. Take in the evening.
- **Vitamin D3 (4,000-5,000 IU daily)**: Supports muscle function, testosterone production, bone density, and immune health. Deficiency is common (estimated 40-50% of adults) and is associated with muscle weakness, increased injury risk, and impaired recovery. Test blood levels and supplement to maintain 40-60 ng/mL. Take with a fat-containing meal. Pair with Vitamin K2 (100-200mcg MK-7) for calcium metabolism.
- **Ashwagandha (KSM-66, 600mg daily)**: An adaptogen that may improve VO2 max, muscular endurance, and recovery from training. KSM-66 is the most studied extract. It reduces cortisol, which supports recovery and body composition. Some studies show improvements in testosterone in men. Take in the morning or split between morning and evening.
- **CoQ10 (200mg daily)**: Supports mitochondrial energy production (the electron transport chain) and acts as a powerful antioxidant. Particularly relevant for endurance exercise and Zone 2 training, where mitochondrial function is the limiting factor. The ubiquinol form is better absorbed than ubiquinone. Take with a fat-containing meal.
- **Protein (1.6-2.2g/kg bodyweight daily)**: The most important "supplement" for anyone who exercises. Protein provides the amino acids necessary for muscle protein synthesis. Distribute intake across 3-5 meals with at least 30g per meal to hit the leucine threshold (~2.5g leucine) that maximizes MPS. Whey protein is the gold standard for post-workout due to rapid absorption and high leucine content. For those with dairy sensitivity, egg white or pea/rice protein blends are alternatives.
