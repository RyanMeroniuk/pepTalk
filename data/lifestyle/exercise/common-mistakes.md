# Exercise & Movement

## Common Mistakes

- **Only doing cardio (especially on GLP-1s)**: Cardio alone does not preserve muscle mass. On GLP-1 agonists, relying solely on walking or running accelerates lean mass loss, resulting in a "skinny fat" outcome where the scale drops but body composition worsens. Resistance training must be the priority.
- **Skipping resistance training**: Many people, especially women and older adults, avoid weights due to misconceptions about "getting bulky." Building significant muscle mass requires years of dedicated training and specific nutrition. Resistance training at any intensity improves bone density, metabolic rate, functional strength, and longevity.
- **Not tracking progressive overload**: Progressive overload means gradually increasing the weight, reps, or sets over time. Without tracking, most people unconsciously use the same weights for months or years and wonder why they stop progressing. Use a training log or app to record every session and ensure you are progressing.
- **Overtraining without recovery**: More is not always better. Training 7 days a week without rest days, deload weeks, or adequate sleep leads to elevated cortisol, suppressed immune function, joint pain, and stalled progress. Recovery is when adaptation occurs. Training is the stimulus; rest is the response.
- **Exercising too close to bedtime**: Intense exercise within 2-3 hours of sleep raises core body temperature, cortisol, and sympathetic nervous system activation, all of which oppose sleep onset. If evening is the only option, choose low-intensity activities like walking or yoga.
- **Ignoring walking and steps as real exercise**: Daily steps contribute significantly to total energy expenditure through NEAT. The difference between 4,000 and 12,000 steps per day can be 300-500 calories. Walking after meals improves blood sugar regulation. Steps are the easiest, lowest-barrier form of health-promoting movement.
- **Training through injury instead of recovering**: Pushing through pain leads to chronic injury and extended time away from training. Healing peptides like BPC-157 and TB-500 exist specifically to accelerate tissue repair. Taking 1-2 weeks to properly recover (while using healing peptides if appropriate) results in less total training time lost than pushing through and causing a more severe injury.
- **Not eating enough protein**: Resistance training without adequate protein is like building a house without materials. Muscle protein synthesis requires amino acids. Aim for 1.6-2.2g protein per kilogram of bodyweight daily, distributed across 3-5 meals with at least 30g per meal to hit the leucine threshold for MPS activation.
- **Chronic long-duration cardio**: Running for 60-90+ minutes multiple times per week without resistance training raises cortisol chronically, promotes muscle breakdown, and can impair immune function. For longevity and body composition, shorter Zone 2 sessions (30-45 minutes) combined with resistance training are superior to long endurance sessions.
