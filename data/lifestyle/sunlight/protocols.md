# Sunlight & Light Exposure

## Light Exposure Protocols

### Morning Light Protocol

- **Timing** — Get outside within 30-60 minutes of waking
- **Duration** — 10 minutes on sunny days, 20-30 minutes on overcast days
- **No sunglasses** — Do not wear sunglasses during this window; your eyes need the light signal to reach melanopsin receptors in the retina
- **Face toward the sun** — Orient yourself toward the sun but do not stare directly at it; peripheral and indirect light is sufficient and safe
- **Combine with movement** — A morning walk outdoors provides the light signal plus exercise for a compounding benefit
- **Through a window** — Approximately 50% less effective due to glass filtering, but still better than nothing on days when getting outside is not possible
- **Consistency** — Do this every day, including weekends. Circadian rhythm disruption from inconsistent timing (social jet lag) is as harmful as too little light

### Evening Light Protocol

- **Dim overhead lights after sunset** — Switch to lower-wattage warm bulbs, table lamps, or floor lamps instead of bright ceiling fixtures
- **Blue light blocking glasses** — Wear amber or orange-tinted glasses if you must use screens after sunset. Clear "blue light" lenses do not block enough of the relevant wavelengths
- **Warm/amber lighting** — Salt lamps, candles, low-wattage warm-white LED bulbs (2700K or lower) for evening ambient light
- **Screen settings** — Enable Night Shift (iOS/Mac), f.lux (desktop), or Android night mode. Reduce screen brightness to the lowest comfortable level
- **2-hour rule** — No bright overhead lights for 2 hours before your target bedtime
- **Bathroom lighting** — Use a dim nightlight or red-tinted bulb for nighttime bathroom trips to avoid resetting melatonin

### Vitamin D Protocol

- **Midday exposure** — 15-30 minutes of midday sun (10am-2pm) with arms and legs exposed for optimal vitamin D synthesis
- **No sunscreen during D window** — Sunscreen blocks UVB and prevents vitamin D production. Brief unprotected exposure during this window, then apply sunscreen for extended time outdoors
- **Supplementation** — When sun exposure is insufficient (winter, high latitude, dark skin, indoor lifestyle), supplement with vitamin D3
- **Target blood levels** — 40-60 ng/mL of 25-hydroxyvitamin D, tested via blood draw (25-OH vitamin D test)
- **Always pair D3 with K2** — Vitamin K2 (MK-7 form) directs calcium mobilized by vitamin D into bones and teeth rather than soft tissues and arteries

### Seasonal and Latitude Adjustments

- **Northern latitudes (above 37 degrees N)** — No UVB radiation reaches the surface for vitamin D synthesis approximately October through March. Supplementation is essential during these months
- **Light therapy lamp** — Use a 10,000 lux light therapy lamp for 20-30 minutes each morning during winter months, positioned 16-24 inches from your face
- **SAD management** — Combine light therapy + vitamin D supplementation + regular exercise for the strongest effect against seasonal mood disruption
- **Travel across time zones** — Use morning light at the destination to reset your clock. Seek light in the morning of the new time zone, avoid light during what would be evening in your origin zone
