# Sunlight & Light Exposure

## Quick Wins

1. **Step outside for 10 minutes within 30 minutes of waking tomorrow** — No preparation needed, no equipment required. Just walk outside and face the general direction of the sun. This single action sets your cortisol peak, starts your melatonin timer, and improves alertness for the entire day
2. **Do not wear sunglasses during that morning window** — Your eyes need unfiltered outdoor light to activate melanopsin receptors. Prescription glasses and contacts are fine; tinted or UV-blocking sunglasses are not. Save sunglasses for later in the day
3. **Turn on Night Shift or dark mode on your phone right now** — Go to Settings > Display and enable Night Shift (iOS) or Night Light (Android). Set it to activate automatically at sunset. This reduces blue light emission from your screen in the evening
4. **Dim your overhead lights after sunset tonight** — Turn off bright ceiling lights and switch to table lamps, floor lamps, or dimmer settings. This single change reduces melatonin suppression and makes it noticeably easier to fall asleep
5. **Get a vitamin D blood test at your next checkup** — Ask your doctor to add 25-hydroxyvitamin D to your next blood panel. Knowing your baseline level tells you whether supplementation is needed and at what dose. Target range is 40-60 ng/mL
