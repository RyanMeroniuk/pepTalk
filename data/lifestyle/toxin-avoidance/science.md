# Environmental & Toxin Avoidance

## Science & Mechanisms

- **Alcohol metabolism** — Ethanol is converted to acetaldehyde (a known carcinogen) by alcohol dehydrogenase, then to acetate by aldehyde dehydrogenase. This process creates massive oxidative stress and diverts liver resources from other detoxification tasks.
- **Alcohol and sleep** — Alcohol suppresses REM sleep, fragments sleep architecture, increases sympathetic nervous system activity during sleep, and disrupts circadian rhythm. Even moderate intake degrades sleep quality for 2-3 nights.
- **Alcohol and gut barrier** — Ethanol and acetaldehyde directly damage intestinal epithelial cells, increase gut permeability ("leaky gut"), promote endotoxemia (LPS translocation), and disrupt the gut microbiome.
- **Alcohol and hormones** — Elevates cortisol, suppresses testosterone production, increases aromatase activity (converting testosterone to estrogen), and suppresses growth hormone release for up to 24 hours.
- **Endocrine disruptors (BPA, phthalates, parabens)** — These xenoestrogens mimic estrogen at receptor sites, disrupt the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis, interfere with thyroid function, and accumulate in adipose tissue. Even "BPA-free" products often contain BPS or BPF, which have similar effects.
- **Mold and mycotoxins** — Mycotoxins (aflatoxins, ochratoxin, trichothecenes) trigger Chronic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (CIRS) in genetically susceptible individuals (roughly 25% of the population with HLA-DR mutations). They activate the innate immune system, suppress VIP (vasoactive intestinal peptide), elevate TGF-beta-1, C4a, and MMP-9, and disrupt MSH (melanocyte-stimulating hormone) pathways.
- **Pesticides (organophosphates, glyphosate)** — Organophosphates inhibit acetylcholinesterase, disrupting neurotransmission. Glyphosate disrupts the shikimate pathway in gut bacteria, damages the gut microbiome, and may chelate essential minerals. Both classes are associated with neurological damage and endocrine disruption.
- **Volatile organic compounds (VOCs)** — Off-gassing from furniture, paint, carpeting, cleaning products, and building materials. Includes formaldehyde, benzene, toluene, and xylene. Chronic low-level exposure causes respiratory irritation, headaches, neurological effects, and contributes to total toxic burden.
