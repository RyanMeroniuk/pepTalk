# CJC-1295

## Things to Avoid

- **Eating within 2 hours before injection** — this is the single most common mistake. Food-induced insulin and free fatty acids directly suppress GH release at the pituitary, potentially reducing the GH pulse by 50-80%
- **Alcohol on injection days** — ethanol suppresses GH secretion acutely and disrupts deep sleep architecture, eliminating two of the primary benefits of CJC-1295
- **Chronic sleep deprivation** — sleeping less than 6 hours dramatically reduces nocturnal GH output. CJC-1295 amplifies your natural GH pulse; if you have no pulse to amplify, the peptide underperforms
- **Excessive steady-state cardio** — running or cycling for 60+ minutes at moderate intensity elevates cortisol and can become catabolic, counteracting GH-driven muscle preservation and recovery
- **High-dose caffeine after 2pm** — disrupts sleep architecture and reduces deep sleep, where the majority of GH is released. Limit caffeine to morning hours
- **NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) chronically** — regular NSAID use may blunt the IGF-1 response and impair GH-mediated tissue repair
- **Skipping resistance training** — CJC-1295 creates an optimal hormonal environment for muscle growth and repair, but without the training stimulus, you miss the primary benefit
- **Inconsistent injection schedule** — especially with the no-DAC form, consistent timing maintains stable GH pulsatility. Random dosing reduces cumulative benefit
- **Stress without management** — chronic psychological stress elevates cortisol, which directly antagonizes GH at both the hypothalamic and pituitary level. Unmanaged stress can negate much of the peptide's benefit
