# DSIP (Delta Sleep-Inducing Peptide)

## Foods to Avoid

- **Caffeine After 12 PM (all sources)**: Coffee, tea, energy drinks, chocolate, and some medications contain caffeine. With a half-life of 5-7 hours, afternoon caffeine remains active at bedtime and reduces delta-wave sleep by up to 20% — directly opposing DSIP's primary mechanism
- **Alcohol**: Despite its sedating feel, alcohol suppresses delta sleep by 20-40%, causes sleep fragmentation in the second half of the night, suppresses REM, and triggers nighttime cortisol spikes. It directly counteracts every mechanism DSIP supports
- **Heavy, High-Fat Meals Within 3 Hours of Bed**: Fat slows gastric emptying, keeps core temperature elevated from digestion, and increases risk of acid reflux — all of which fragment sleep
- **Spicy Foods in the Evening**: Capsaicin raises core body temperature (opposing the temperature drop needed for sleep onset) and can cause gastric discomfort and heartburn
- **Excess Sugar Before Bed**: Causes blood glucose spikes followed by crashes that trigger cortisol and adrenaline — stress hormones that fragment sleep and oppose DSIP's cortisol-lowering effects
- **Dark Chocolate After 4 PM**: Contains caffeine (20-30mg per serving) and theobromine, both of which are stimulants
- **Tyramine-Rich Foods Late at Night (aged cheese, cured meats, soy sauce)**: Tyramine triggers norepinephrine release, which is alerting and delays sleep onset
- **Excess Fluids in Final 2 Hours Before Bed**: Nocturia fragments sleep. Even brief awakenings disrupt delta-wave sleep cycles that DSIP promotes
- **MSG-Heavy Foods**: Monosodium glutamate is an excitatory neurotransmitter (glutamate) that can increase alertness and impair sleep onset in sensitive individuals
