# NAD+

## Overview

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a master redox coenzyme found in every living cell — it is NOT a peptide, but a dinucleotide built from nicotinamide and adenine. It is included in this peptide-focused library because it is widely used in injectable and IV "biohacker" longevity protocols and frequently stacked alongside peptides. NAD+ shuttles electrons in metabolism (cycling between NAD+ and NADH) to drive ATP production, and serves as an essential substrate for sirtuins and PARPs, the enzymes governing DNA repair and longevity signaling. Cellular NAD+ levels decline substantially with age, which is the rationale behind supplementation. Oral NAD+ is poorly absorbed because it is broken down in digestion, so it is typically administered by IV, intramuscular, or subcutaneous routes — and IV infusion is notorious for causing flushing, nausea, and chest tightness if run too fast. Its direct precursor NMN is also catalogued in this library.
