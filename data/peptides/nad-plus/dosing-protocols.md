# NAD+

## Dosing Protocols

- **IV Infusion**: Common protocols use 250–1000mg infused slowly over 2–4 hours; slow drip rate is essential to limit flushing and chest tightness.
- **Subcutaneous Injection**: Anecdotal protocols use ~50–100mg daily or several times weekly to bypass poor oral absorption.
- **Intramuscular**: Smaller volumes (e.g., 50–100mg) sometimes used; can sting at the injection site.
- **Oral Limitation**: Oral NAD+ is largely broken down in digestion and poorly absorbed — precursors like NMN or NR are preferred for oral use.
- **Frequency**: IV courses often run as a series (e.g., several infusions over 1–2 weeks) then periodic maintenance.
- **Infusion Pace**: Slowing the IV drip is the primary way to reduce the characteristic flushing, nausea, and chest pressure.
- **Cycle**: No firmly established cycling standard; periodic courses are common in biohacker practice.
- **Reconstitution / Storage**: Injectable NAD+ is light- and temperature-sensitive; refrigerate and protect from light.
- **Note**: Not a peptide — dosing conventions differ from microgram/milligram peptide protocols.
