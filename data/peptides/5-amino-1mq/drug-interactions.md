# 5-Amino-1MQ

## Drug Interactions & Contraindications

- **SAM Cycle / Methylation Drugs**: 5-Amino-1MQ affects S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) metabolism through NNMT inhibition. Use caution when combining with SAMe supplements, methotrexate, or other drugs that affect the methylation cycle. NNMT normally metabolizes nicotinamide using SAM as a methyl donor — inhibiting NNMT alters SAM/SAH ratios, which could affect other methylation-dependent processes
- **Methyl Donors (SAMe, TMG, Methylfolate, Methyl-B12)**: Theoretical interaction — NNMT inhibition shifts the SAM cycle, and adding exogenous methyl donors could amplify or unpredictably alter methylation patterns. Use with caution and monitor for mood changes or other methylation-related symptoms
- **Psychiatric Medications Affecting Methylation (MAOIs, certain antidepressants)**: NNMT inhibition affects monoamine metabolism indirectly through SAM cycle changes. Exercise caution combining with MAO inhibitors or medications sensitive to methylation status
- **Chemotherapy / Cancer Treatment**: NNMT is overexpressed in some tumors and plays a role in cancer metabolism. While inhibiting NNMT may have anti-tumor properties in some contexts, the interaction with chemotherapy agents is unstudied. Consult oncologist before use
- **Diabetes Medications**: No direct glucose-lowering effect established, but metabolic changes from enhanced fat cell metabolism could modestly affect insulin sensitivity over time. Monitor blood glucose if on diabetes medications
- **Pregnancy / Breastfeeding**: Contraindicated. Insufficient safety data and the effects of NNMT inhibition on fetal development and methylation during pregnancy are completely unknown — methylation is critical for embryonic development
- **Active Cancer**: Use with caution — NNMT plays complex roles in cancer metabolism. Effects of inhibition on existing tumors are not well characterized
- **Children / Adolescents**: No pediatric safety data. Not recommended for use under 18, particularly given the methylation cycle effects during development
