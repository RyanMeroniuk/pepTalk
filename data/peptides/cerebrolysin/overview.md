# Cerebrolysin

## Overview

Cerebrolysin is not a single peptide but a complex mixture of low-molecular-weight peptides and free amino acids produced by the enzymatic breakdown of purified porcine brain proteins. This distinction matters: its effects come from a blend of fragments with BDNF-, NGF-, and GDNF-like neurotrophic activity rather than one defined molecule, which complicates standardization and mechanism attribution. It is administered intravenously or intramuscularly and has been studied in stroke, traumatic brain injury, vascular dementia, and Alzheimer's disease, with human trial results that are mixed and often modest. Cerebrolysin is an approved pharmaceutical in a number of countries (parts of Europe, Asia, and the CIS) but is NOT approved by the US FDA. Because it is derived from animal brain tissue, it carries a theoretical concern about animal-sourced contamination, including prion-related caution.
