# PEG-MGF

## Overview

PEG-MGF (PEGylated Mechano Growth Factor) is a synthetic peptide based on the C-terminal portion of IGF-1Ec, a splice variant of IGF-1 that is produced locally in muscle in response to mechanical loading and damage. Its defining role is activating muscle satellite (stem) cells, prompting them to proliferate and fuse to damaged fibers — a key step in muscle repair and growth following resistance training. Native MGF has a very short half-life (minutes), so the molecule is PEGylated (attached to polyethylene glycol) to slow degradation and extend its window of action to hours or days. PEG-MGF is frequently paired conceptually with IGF-1 LR3, with MGF priming satellite cells and IGF-1 LR3 driving their maturation. Like most peptides in this category, the human evidence is thin and it is sold only as a gray-market research chemical; because it acts on the IGF axis, there is a theoretical cancer/proliferation concern with chronic use.
