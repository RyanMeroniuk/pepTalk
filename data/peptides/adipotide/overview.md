# Adipotide

## Overview

Adipotide (also called FTPP, for "fat-targeted pro-apoptotic peptide") is a highly experimental peptidomimetic that works by a completely different mechanism than the incretin weight-loss drugs. It is a fusion of a targeting peptide that binds prohibitin on the surface of blood vessels feeding white fat tissue, joined to a pro-apoptotic sequence that triggers cell death in those vessels. By destroying the blood supply to fat depots, it causes adipocytes to die and fat tissue to shrink. Critically, the entirety of the meaningful evidence comes from animal studies, including a study in obese rhesus monkeys; there are no completed controlled human efficacy trials. It carries a documented risk of nephrotoxicity (kidney damage) seen in animal work, and it is not approved, not in routine clinical development, and should be regarded as research-only and dangerous for human use.
