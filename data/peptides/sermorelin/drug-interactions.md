# Sermorelin

## Drug Interactions & Contraindications

- **Diabetes medications (insulin, metformin, sulfonylureas)**: Sermorelin elevates GH, which increases blood glucose and reduces insulin sensitivity. May require dose adjustments of diabetes medications; monitor fasting glucose and HbA1c regularly
- **Glucocorticoids (prednisone, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone)**: Chronic glucocorticoid use suppresses GH release at the hypothalamic-pituitary level, directly opposing sermorelin's mechanism and reducing its effectiveness. Additionally, glucocorticoids promote muscle wasting and fat gain — the opposite of sermorelin's benefits
- **Thyroid medications (levothyroxine, liothyronine)**: GH elevation alters thyroid hormone metabolism, particularly T4-to-T3 conversion. Patients on thyroid replacement may need dose adjustments. Monitor TSH, free T4, and free T3 regularly during sermorelin use
- **Anti-obesity medications (semaglutide, tirzepatide, phentermine)**: Sermorelin's GH elevation affects glucose metabolism and body composition through different pathways than weight loss drugs. Concurrent use requires careful metabolic monitoring but is not necessarily contraindicated
- **Exogenous growth hormone (rhGH)**: Combining sermorelin with direct GH injection bypasses the self-limiting somatostatin feedback that makes sermorelin safe. This negates sermorelin's built-in safety mechanism and risks GH excess
- **Somatostatin analogs (octreotide, lanreotide)**: These drugs directly suppress GH release, completely opposing sermorelin's mechanism. Concurrent use renders sermorelin ineffective
- **Cyclooxygenase inhibitors and aspirin**: May blunt GH response to sermorelin in some individuals
- **Contraindicated: Active cancer or history of cancer** — GH and IGF-1 elevation may promote tumor growth and angiogenesis
- **Contraindicated: Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data; GH axis manipulation during pregnancy poses unknown risks to fetal development
- **Contraindicated: Active pituitary tumors or significant pituitary damage** — sermorelin requires functional pituitary somatotroph cells. Tumors could be stimulated; severely damaged pituitary will not respond
- **Contraindicated: Acute critical illness** — GH elevation during acute illness (surgery, trauma, respiratory failure) has been associated with increased mortality in some studies
- **Caution: Intracranial hypertension** — GH elevation may worsen or precipitate this condition
