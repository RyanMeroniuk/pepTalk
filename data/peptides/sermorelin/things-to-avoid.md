# Sermorelin

## Things to Avoid

- **Eating within 2 hours of your bedtime injection** — the most common mistake that undermines sermorelin's effectiveness. Even a small snack can elevate insulin enough to blunt the GH pulse
- **Alcohol, especially in the evening** — destroys the deep sleep window that is sermorelin's primary mechanism of action. Even 1-2 drinks significantly reduce GH release
- **Sleep deprivation or irregular schedule** — sermorelin amplifies a natural process. If your sleep is poor or inconsistent, the peptide has less to work with
- **Expecting immediate dramatic results** — sermorelin works by gradually restoring natural GH production. It is gentler and slower than direct GH injection but safer for long-term use. Give it 8-12 weeks for meaningful body composition changes
- **High caffeine after noon** — caffeine's 6-hour half-life means afternoon intake disrupts the deep sleep where sermorelin does its work
- **Chronic overtraining** — excessive exercise without recovery raises cortisol, which directly opposes GH at the pituitary. Sermorelin improves recovery, but you must allow rest days
- **Skipping blood work** — while sermorelin is self-limiting (somatostatin prevents GH excess), monitoring IGF-1 ensures optimal dosing, and glucose monitoring catches any metabolic changes early
- **Using sermorelin if pituitary function is severely impaired** — sermorelin requires functional somatotroph cells to work. If the pituitary is damaged or atrophied, direct GH therapy may be more appropriate
- **Chronic unmanaged stress** — cortisol suppresses GH release at the hypothalamic and pituitary level, directly opposing sermorelin's mechanism
