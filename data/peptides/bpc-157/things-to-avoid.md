# BPC-157 (Body Protection Compound-157)

## Things to Avoid

- **Overtraining Injured Areas**: Pushing through pain or returning to full training too early disrupts the healing cascade BPC-157 promotes. Follow a progressive rehabilitation protocol — start at 50% intensity and increase gradually
- **NSAIDs During Healing**: Ibuprofen, naproxen, and aspirin inhibit the inflammatory phase of healing that is necessary for proper tissue repair. BPC-157 modulates inflammation without blocking it entirely. Use BPC-157 instead of NSAIDs when possible
- **Chronic Stress**: Elevated cortisol directly impairs wound healing, increases gut permeability, and suppresses growth factor expression. Address chronic stressors and practice daily stress management
- **Sleep Deprivation**: Less than 6 hours of sleep significantly reduces growth hormone output and impairs immune-mediated tissue repair. Protect sleep as a non-negotiable part of your healing protocol
- **Smoking & Nicotine**: Nicotine is a potent vasoconstrictor that reduces blood flow to healing tissues, directly opposing BPC-157's angiogenic mechanism. Avoid all nicotine products during healing
- **Excessive Alcohol**: Even "moderate" drinking (1-2 drinks) damages gut epithelium and disrupts tight junctions. Zero alcohol during gut healing protocols
- **Ice/Cryotherapy on Acute Injuries (Prolonged)**: While brief icing is fine, prolonged cryotherapy reduces blood flow and may impair BPC-157's delivery to the injury site. Limit icing to 10-15 minutes at a time
- **Corticosteroid Injections**: Corticosteroids impair tendon and muscle healing. BPC-157 has been shown to counteract corticosteroid damage, but avoiding cortisone shots during a BPC-157 protocol is preferable
- **High-Dose Vitamin E Supplements**: Doses above 400 IU may interfere with the initial inflammatory healing response
- **Fasting During Healing**: Extended fasting (24+ hours) deprives the body of amino acids and nutrients needed for tissue repair. Maintain consistent protein-rich meals during healing protocols
