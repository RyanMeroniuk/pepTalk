# Semaglutide

## Drug Interactions & Contraindications

- **Sulfonylureas (Glipizide, Glyburide, Glimepiride)**: High risk of hypoglycemia when combined with semaglutide. Sulfonylurea dose reduction is typically required when initiating semaglutide. Monitor blood glucose frequently
- **Insulin**: Increased hypoglycemia risk. Insulin dose reduction (often 20-50%) is usually necessary when starting semaglutide. Close glucose monitoring required during dose escalation
- **Oral Contraceptives**: Semaglutide slows gastric emptying, which may reduce absorption of oral birth control pills. Use backup contraception (barrier method) for 4 weeks after each dose escalation. Consider non-oral contraceptive methods
- **Thyroid Medications (Levothyroxine)**: Delayed gastric emptying may alter levothyroxine absorption. Take levothyroxine on an empty stomach, ideally 60 minutes before any food. Monitor TSH every 6-8 weeks during semaglutide dose changes
- **Warfarin / Anticoagulants**: Delayed absorption may alter warfarin pharmacokinetics. Monitor INR more frequently when starting semaglutide or changing doses
- **Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma / MEN2**: Absolute contraindication (FDA black box warning). GLP-1 agonists caused thyroid C-cell tumors in rodent studies. Do not use in patients with personal or family history of MTC or MEN2
- **Pancreatitis History**: Contraindicated in patients with history of pancreatitis. Discontinue immediately if pancreatitis is suspected (severe persistent abdominal pain)
- **Gastroparesis / Severe GI Disorders**: Use with caution — semaglutide further slows gastric emptying and can worsen pre-existing gastroparesis or severe GI motility disorders
- **Pregnancy / Breastfeeding**: Contraindicated. Discontinue semaglutide at least 2 months before planned conception (long half-life ~1 week). Weight loss during pregnancy is harmful to fetal development
- **Rybelsus (Oral) Specific**: Must be taken on empty stomach with no more than 4oz plain water, 30 minutes before any food or other oral medications. Other medications, food, and larger water volumes drastically reduce absorption
- **Diabetic Retinopathy**: Rapid glucose improvement can temporarily worsen diabetic retinopathy. Patients with existing retinopathy should have ophthalmologic monitoring
