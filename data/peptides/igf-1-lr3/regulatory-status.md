# IGF-1 LR3 (Long R3 Insulin-like Growth Factor-1)

## Regulatory Status

- **FDA Status**: Not approved for human use; sold as "for research purposes only" — no pharmaceutical-grade human formulation exists
- **Recombinant IGF-1 (Mecasermin/Increlex)**: The native (non-LR3) form of IGF-1 is FDA-approved under the brand name Increlex for severe primary IGF-1 deficiency in children; IGF-1 LR3 is a distinct modified analog not covered by this approval
- **DEA Scheduling**: Not a scheduled/controlled substance in the United States
- **WADA Prohibited List**: Banned in and out of competition under S2 (Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances, and Mimetics); athletes subject to testing cannot use this peptide
- **Clinical Research**: Primarily studied in cell culture and animal models for cancer biology, wound healing, and muscle physiology; no human clinical trials for performance or body composition
- **International Status**: Regulatory status varies by country; generally unregulated for research sale but illegal to sell for human consumption in most jurisdictions
- **Quality Concerns**: Absence of pharmaceutical regulation means purity and dosing accuracy vary widely between suppliers; third-party testing (HPLC, mass spectrometry) is strongly recommended before use
- **Liability**: Users assume all risk — no regulatory body has evaluated safety or efficacy for any human use case outside of native IGF-1 (Mecasermin) for pediatric IGF-1 deficiency
