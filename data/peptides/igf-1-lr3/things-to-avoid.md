# IGF-1 LR3 (Long R3 Insulin-like Growth Factor-1)

## Things to Avoid

- **Fasted Training**: Never train in a fasted state while using IGF-1 LR3 — the combination of exercise-induced glucose depletion and the peptide's glucose-lowering effects can cause dangerous hypoglycemia; always eat carbohydrates before training
- **Extended Cycles (>6 weeks)**: Prolonged use increases the risk of IGF-1 receptor desensitization, organ growth (intestinal smooth muscle, cardiac tissue), and amplified tumor risk; strict cycle discipline is essential
- **High Doses Without Monitoring**: Doses above 50 mcg/day without blood glucose monitoring are reckless; hypoglycemic events can escalate quickly and become medical emergencies
- **Combining with Insulin**: Stacking IGF-1 LR3 with exogenous insulin dramatically amplifies hypoglycemia risk — this combination has caused hospitalizations and deaths; avoid unless under direct medical supervision
- **Ignoring Hypoglycemia Symptoms**: Dizziness, shakiness, sweating, confusion, or rapid heartbeat during use should be treated immediately with fast-acting carbohydrates (glucose tabs, juice); do not push through symptoms
- **Skipping Meals**: Irregular eating patterns cause unpredictable blood glucose levels; maintain consistent meal timing with balanced macronutrients throughout the day
- **Using with Active or Suspected Cancer**: IGF-1 LR3 is a potent mitogen — it will accelerate the growth of any existing tumors; get cleared with appropriate screening before use
- **Aggressive Reconstitution**: IGF-1 LR3 denatures easily; never shake the vial, spray directly into the powder, or use non-bacteriostatic water; inject slowly along the glass wall and swirl gently
- **Sharing Vials or Needles**: Basic injection hygiene — use a new sterile needle for every injection, never share reconstituted vials, and always swab injection sites with alcohol
- **Neglecting Off-Cycle Recovery**: The 4-week off period between cycles allows IGF-1 receptors to resensitize and gives the body time to normalize; skipping off-cycles leads to diminishing returns and increased risk
