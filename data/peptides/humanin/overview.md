# Humanin

## Overview

Humanin (HN) is a 24-amino acid mitochondrial-derived peptide encoded within the 12S rRNA region of mitochondrial DNA — the same gene region that encodes MOTS-c — making it a sibling "mitokine." First identified in 2001 from the brain of an Alzheimer's patient, it acts as a cytoprotective and anti-apoptotic signaling molecule, protecting cells from oxidative stress and programmed cell death. A more potent synthetic analog, HNG (where the 14th residue serine is replaced with glycine), is far more biologically active and is the form used in most research. Circulating humanin levels decline with age and have been studied for roles in neuroprotection, metabolic regulation, and cardiovascular protection. Evidence is overwhelmingly preclinical (cell and animal studies), with minimal human data, so its use remains highly experimental.
