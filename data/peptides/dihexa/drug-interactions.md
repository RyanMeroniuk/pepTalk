# Dihexa

## Drug Interactions & Contraindications

- **Active Cancer or Cancer History**: CRITICAL CONCERN. Dihexa activates the HGF/c-Met pathway, which is a well-established oncogenic signaling pathway involved in tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis. Multiple cancers (lung, breast, liver, gastric, glioblastoma) exploit HGF/c-Met for proliferation. Absolutely contraindicated in patients with active cancer. Use extreme caution with any personal or family history of cancer
- **Chemotherapy / Anti-Cancer Drugs**: Several cancer drugs specifically target the HGF/c-Met pathway (cabozantinib, crizotinib, capmatinib). Dihexa activates the same pathway these drugs are designed to block — direct pharmacological antagonism. Never combine
- **Blood-Brain Barrier Penetrating Drugs**: Dihexa crosses the BBB. Co-administration with other BBB-crossing CNS drugs (certain antidepressants, antipsychotics, benzodiazepines, opioids, stimulants) creates unpredictable neurochemical interactions in a context where Dihexa is potently forming new synapses. Exercise extreme caution with any CNS-active medication
- **MAO Inhibitors and Serotonergic Drugs**: The combination of potent synaptogenesis with altered monoamine metabolism is unstudied and theoretically risky. New synapses formed under abnormal neurotransmitter conditions could develop dysfunctional signaling patterns
- **Growth Factors and Angiogenic Agents**: HGF is an angiogenic factor. Combining with other growth-factor-stimulating compounds (e.g., full-dose GH therapy, VEGF-related treatments) may amplify proliferative risks
- **Immunosuppressants**: HGF/c-Met signaling interacts with immune function. Effects of combining with immunosuppressant drugs are unknown
- **Pregnancy / Breastfeeding**: Absolutely contraindicated. HGF/c-Met pathway is critical in embryonic development — exogenous activation could cause developmental abnormalities. No safety data exists
- **Children / Adolescents**: Contraindicated. The developing brain is actively forming synapses — introducing a compound 10 million times more potent than BDNF during neurodevelopment carries unquantifiable risk
- **Individuals Without Access to Regular Health Monitoring**: Given the HGF/c-Met cancer concern and the extremely limited human safety data, Dihexa should only be used by individuals who can access regular health screenings and blood work
