# VIP (Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide)

## Drug Interactions & Contraindications

- **Blood pressure medications (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers, beta-blockers, diuretics)**: VIP is a potent vasodilator. Combining with antihypertensive drugs creates additive blood pressure lowering, risking symptomatic hypotension (dizziness, fainting, syncope). Requires close blood pressure monitoring and potential dose adjustments of existing medications
- **Nitrates (nitroglycerin, isosorbide)**: Both VIP and nitrates cause vasodilation. Combined use may cause dangerous hypotension. Avoid concurrent use without cardiologist supervision
- **PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil)**: Additive vasodilatory effects via overlapping nitric oxide/cGMP pathways. Risk of severe hypotension
- **Heart medications (antiarrhythmics, digoxin)**: VIP affects cardiac function, heart rate, and vascular tone. May interact with antiarrhythmic drugs and alter cardiac medication efficacy. Requires cardiology consultation
- **Alpha-blockers (prazosin, doxazosin, tamsulosin)**: Additive vasodilation and hypotensive risk, particularly orthostatic hypotension
- **Mast cell stabilizers and antihistamines (cromolyn, ketotifen, cetirizine)**: VIP modulates mast cell activity and immune response. In CIRS patients with mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS), combining VIP with mast cell drugs may produce additive or unpredictable effects on mast cell degranulation. Coordinate with your treating physician
- **Immunosuppressants**: VIP shifts immune balance from Th1 to Th2. Adding immunosuppressive drugs may cause excessive immune modulation. Monitor for increased infection susceptibility
- **Insulin and diabetes medications**: VIP regulates insulin and glucagon secretion from the pancreas. May alter glucose metabolism and require adjustment of diabetes medications
- **High-dose magnesium supplements**: Magnesium has vasodilatory effects that add to VIP's blood pressure lowering. Use caution with doses above 400mg
- **Contraindicated: Active MARCoNS nasal infection** — VIP must not be administered intranasally while MARCoNS biofilm is present. The biofilm must be eradicated and confirmed negative by deep nasal culture before starting VIP
- **Contraindicated: Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data; VIP's wide-ranging effects on vascular, immune, and neurological function pose unknown risks to fetal development
- **Contraindicated: Severe hypotension** — VIP's vasodilatory effects could cause dangerous further blood pressure drops in already-hypotensive individuals
- **Contraindicated: Active VIPoma (VIP-secreting tumor)** — additional VIP would worsen the watery diarrhea, hypokalemia, and achlorhydria associated with this condition
- **Caution: Heart failure** — vasodilation may acutely worsen hemodynamics in decompensated heart failure, though controlled vasodilation can benefit stable heart failure. Requires cardiology guidance
