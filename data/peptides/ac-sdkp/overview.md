# Ac-SDKP (Thymosin Beta-4 Fragment)

## Overview

Ac-SDKP (N-acetyl-Ser-Asp-Lys-Pro) is a naturally occurring tetrapeptide that is the active N-terminal fragment of Thymosin Beta-4 (TB-500), cleaved from the parent protein by the enzyme prolyl oligopeptidase. Endogenously, it circulates at low concentrations and is normally degraded by angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) — which is why ACE inhibitors raise its plasma levels. Research interest centers on its anti-fibrotic, anti-inflammatory, and pro-angiogenic actions in the heart, liver, kidney, and lung. It is marketed by some research-chemical vendors as an oral peptide claimed to be more bioavailable than full-length TB-500, though no published human pharmacokinetic data support oral absorption claims. The evidence base is almost entirely animal and mechanistic; human clinical data are very limited.
