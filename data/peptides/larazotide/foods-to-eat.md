# Larazotide (AT-1001)

## Foods to Eat

- **Certified Gluten-Free Whole Grains**: Rice, quinoa, buckwheat, millet, amaranth, and certified gluten-free oats. These provide fiber for microbiome health and B-vitamins often deficient in celiac patients
- **Bone Broth**: Rich in glutamine (primary fuel for enterocytes), glycine, proline, and collagen — direct building blocks for tight junction and epithelial repair. Consume 1-2 cups daily
- **L-Glutamine-Rich Foods**: Bone broth, grass-fed beef, chicken, fish, eggs, cabbage, and beets. Glutamine is the most important amino acid for intestinal barrier integrity and directly supports the tight junctions larazotide protects
- **Fermented Foods (Gluten-Free)**: Sauerkraut, kimchi, coconut kefir, water kefir, kombucha, and miso (gluten-free varieties). Beneficial bacteria produce short-chain fatty acids (butyrate) that nourish colonocytes and strengthen the gut barrier
- **Wild-Caught Fatty Fish**: Salmon, sardines, mackerel — omega-3s resolve inflammation and support intestinal epithelial healing. Essential for celiac patients with chronic mucosal inflammation
- **Cooked Vegetables**: Steamed or roasted sweet potatoes, squash, zucchini, carrots, and beets provide easily digestible nutrients, vitamin A (for epithelial repair), and soluble fiber
- **Aloe Vera Juice (Inner Fillet)**: Soothes inflamed gut mucosa, promotes epithelial repair, and provides polysaccharides that support mucosal barrier function
- **Coconut Products**: Coconut oil (antimicrobial MCTs), coconut milk, and coconut butter are anti-inflammatory, easy to digest, and naturally gluten-free
- **Bananas**: Gentle on the gut, provide prebiotic fiber (fructooligosaccharides) that feed beneficial bacteria, and contain pectin that supports mucosal integrity
- **Ginger & Turmeric**: Natural anti-inflammatories that support gut healing. Ginger also promotes gastric motility. Use fresh in cooking and teas
- **Zinc-Rich Foods**: Pumpkin seeds, grass-fed beef, and oysters. Zinc is critical for tight junction integrity and is commonly deficient in celiac patients. Zinc carnosine specifically supports gastric and intestinal lining
