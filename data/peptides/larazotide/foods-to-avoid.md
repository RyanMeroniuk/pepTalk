# Larazotide (AT-1001)

## Foods to Avoid

- **Gluten-Containing Grains**: Wheat, barley, rye, spelt, kamut, triticale, and regular oats (cross-contaminated). Gluten triggers the zonulin cascade that opens tight junctions — the exact pathway larazotide blocks. Eliminating the trigger is far more effective than blocking the pathway alone
- **Hidden Gluten Sources**: Soy sauce (use tamari), malt flavoring, modified food starch, hydrolyzed wheat protein, communion wafers, some medications and supplements (check inactive ingredients), beer, and many processed sauces/dressings
- **Conventional Dairy (Initially)**: Many celiac patients have secondary lactose intolerance due to villous atrophy damaging lactase-producing cells. Avoid dairy for the first 3-6 months of healing. Reintroduce gradually, starting with ghee, then butter, then fermented dairy (yogurt, kefir)
- **Refined Sugar**: Feeds pathogenic bacteria, promotes Candida overgrowth (common in celiac), and triggers inflammatory cytokine production. Use honey, maple syrup, or monk fruit in moderation
- **Processed Seed Oils**: Soybean, corn, canola, and sunflower oils promote omega-6-driven inflammation that impairs gut barrier recovery. Use olive oil, avocado oil, or coconut oil instead
- **Corn (for Some Celiac Patients)**: Corn proteins (zeins) can trigger cross-reactive immune responses in some celiac patients. Monitor symptoms and consider temporary elimination if healing plateaus
- **Emulsifiers & Thickeners**: Polysorbate 80, carrageenan, and carboxymethylcellulose strip the protective mucus layer and increase intestinal permeability — directly opposing larazotide's tight junction protection
- **Alcohol**: Damages intestinal epithelium, increases permeability, and depletes nutrients already deficient in celiac patients (folate, B12, zinc). Avoid during active healing
- **Processed & Ultra-Processed Foods**: Even gluten-free processed foods often contain emulsifiers, additives, and inflammatory oils. Prioritize whole, single-ingredient foods during gut healing
