# Hexarelin

## Drug Interactions & Contraindications

- **Prolactin-elevating drugs (antipsychotics, metoclopramide, domperidone, SSRIs)**: Hexarelin elevates prolactin more than any other GHRP. Combining with other prolactin-elevating medications creates high risk of clinically significant hyperprolactinemia — gynecomastia, galactorrhea, low libido, menstrual irregularity, and mood disturbances
- **Glucocorticoids (prednisone, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone)**: Hexarelin produces the strongest cortisol elevation of any GHRP. Adding exogenous glucocorticoids compounds the cortisol burden, significantly increasing risk of Cushingoid effects — muscle wasting, visceral fat gain, bone loss, immune suppression, and metabolic dysfunction
- **Cortisol-affecting medications (ketoconazole, metyrapone, mitotane)**: Drugs that alter cortisol metabolism may interact unpredictably with hexarelin's cortisol-stimulating effects. Monitor adrenal function closely
- **Diabetes medications (insulin, metformin, sulfonylureas)**: GH elevation raises blood glucose and reduces insulin sensitivity. May require dose adjustments; frequent glucose monitoring essential
- **Exogenous growth hormone (rhGH)**: Hexarelin already produces the strongest GH pulse of any secretagogue. Adding rhGH risks dangerously supraphysiological GH/IGF-1 levels
- **Other GHRPs (especially GHRP-6)**: Stacking hexarelin with other cortisol/prolactin-elevating GHRPs creates excessive hormonal burden without proportional GH benefit
- **Thyroid medications**: GH elevation alters T4-to-T3 conversion. Monitor thyroid function during use
- **Desensitization warning**: Hexarelin causes ghrelin receptor desensitization faster than any other GHRP. Continuous use beyond 4-8 weeks without cycling leads to diminishing returns that may persist for months. This is an intrinsic pharmacological limitation, not a drug interaction, but must be managed
- **Contraindicated: Active cancer or history of cancer** — hexarelin produces the highest GH/IGF-1 elevation of any secretagogue, creating the greatest theoretical risk of promoting tumor growth
- **Contraindicated: Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data; extreme GH axis manipulation poses unknown risks to fetal development
- **Contraindicated: Active pituitary tumors** — intense pituitary stimulation could accelerate tumor growth
- **Contraindicated: Prolactinoma or hyperprolactinemia** — hexarelin's prolactin-elevating effect would worsen these conditions
- **Caution: Cardiac arrhythmias** — despite hexarelin's cardioprotective CD36 binding, the potent hormonal shifts warrant caution in individuals with unstable cardiac conditions
