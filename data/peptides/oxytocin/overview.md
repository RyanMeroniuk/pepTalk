# Oxytocin

## Overview

Oxytocin is a nine-amino-acid (nonapeptide) hormone produced in the hypothalamus and released by the posterior pituitary, acting as both a circulating hormone and a central neuromodulator. It is best known for its roles in social bonding, trust, emotional connection, sexual arousal and orgasm, and the reduction of anxiety. Clinically, intravenous oxytocin is a long-established, approved drug used to induce or augment labor and control postpartum bleeding. Beyond those approved obstetric uses, intranasal oxytocin is taken off-label for social, sexual, and anxiety-related purposes, but the human evidence there is largely anecdotal and inconsistent, partly because nasal-to-brain delivery is uncertain. At high doses oxytocin can affect cardiovascular and fluid balance, warranting caution. It is mechanistically complementary to PT-141, which drives sexual arousal through a separate melanocortin pathway.
