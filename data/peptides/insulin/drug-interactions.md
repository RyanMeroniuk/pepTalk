# Insulin

## Drug Interactions & Contraindications

- **Sulfonylureas (Glipizide, Glyburide, Glimepiride)**: Caution — additive glucose-lowering greatly increases hypoglycemia risk; combined use requires careful dose adjustment and close monitoring
- **GLP-1 Agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide)**: Caution — combined glucose-lowering raises hypoglycemia risk; insulin dose reduction (often 20-50%) is typically needed when these are added
- **Metformin**: Commonly combined under medical care, but monitor for additive glucose lowering
- **Beta-Blockers**: Caution — can mask the warning symptoms of hypoglycemia (e.g., rapid heartbeat), making dangerous lows harder to detect; timing and monitoring matter
- **Corticosteroids**: Caution — raise blood glucose and increase insulin requirements; dose adjustments needed when starting or stopping steroids
- **Alcohol**: Caution — blocks hepatic glucose release and masks symptoms, sharply increasing severe hypoglycemia risk
- **SSRIs**: Caution — some can affect glucose regulation and may potentiate hypoglycemia in combination; monitor glucose
- **Thiazide Diuretics**: Caution — can raise glucose and affect potassium, complicating insulin dosing
- **Hypoglycemia (active)**: Contraindicated — administering insulin during a low can be fatal; treat the low first
- **Pregnancy / Breastfeeding**: Insulin is generally the preferred glucose-lowering therapy in pregnancy under medical supervision, but dosing must be managed carefully by a care team
- **Liver Disease / Kidney Disease**: Caution — impaired clearance prolongs insulin action and raises hypoglycemia risk; doses often need reduction and monitoring
