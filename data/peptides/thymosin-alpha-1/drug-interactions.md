# Thymosin Alpha-1

## Drug Interactions & Contraindications

- **Immunosuppressants (tacrolimus, cyclosporine, sirolimus)**: Thymosin Alpha-1 enhances T-cell function and immune activity; immunosuppressants deliberately suppress these pathways. Combining creates opposing effects and may compromise transplant outcomes or immunosuppressive therapy goals
- **Post-transplant medications**: Risk of graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) — Thymosin Alpha-1 may activate donor T-cells against host tissue. Contraindicated in recent transplant recipients on immunosuppressive regimens
- **Chemotherapy agents**: Complex interaction — Thymosin Alpha-1 is approved as a chemotherapy adjuvant in some countries and can enhance immune response during treatment, but timing and specific agent compatibility must be managed by an oncologist. May enhance or interfere with certain chemotherapy regimens
- **Interferon therapy**: Additive immune stimulation — historically combined with interferon for hepatitis treatment, but increased risk of immune-related side effects. Requires close monitoring
- **Biologic immunomodulators (rituximab, alemtuzumab)**: These deplete specific immune cell populations; Thymosin Alpha-1 stimulates immune cell production. Conflicting mechanisms require specialist coordination
- **Corticosteroids (chronic use)**: Systemic corticosteroids suppress T-cell function and cause thymic atrophy, directly opposing Thymosin Alpha-1's mechanism
- **Contraindicated**: Pregnancy and breastfeeding — reproductive toxicity data incomplete; immune modulation effects on fetal development unknown
- **Contraindicated**: Organ transplant recipients on active immunosuppression — GVHD risk documented in clinical literature
- **Contraindicated**: Hematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT) recipients — risk of immune-mediated complications
