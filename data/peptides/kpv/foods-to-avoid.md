# KPV

## Foods to Avoid

- **Gluten (Especially if Gut-Compromised)**: Gluten triggers zonulin release, opening tight junctions and increasing intestinal permeability. Even in non-celiac individuals, gluten can worsen an already-inflamed gut. Eliminate during active gut healing
- **Conventional Dairy**: Casein A1 protein and lactose can trigger inflammation and immune reactions in a compromised gut. Avoid cow's milk, cheese, and ice cream. Ghee (clarified butter) is usually tolerated as the proteins are removed
- **Refined Sugar**: Feeds pathogenic bacteria and yeast (Candida), promotes inflammatory cytokine production, and disrupts microbiome balance — all counteracting KPV's anti-inflammatory effects
- **Processed Seed Oils**: Soybean, canola, corn, and sunflower oils are high in omega-6 linoleic acid, which converts to arachidonic acid and pro-inflammatory prostaglandins, directly opposing KPV's NF-kB inhibition
- **Alcohol**: Damages gut epithelial cells, increases permeability, disrupts microbiome composition, and promotes endotoxin translocation. Eliminate completely during gut healing
- **Emulsifiers & Food Additives**: Polysorbate 80, carrageenan, carboxymethylcellulose, and other emulsifiers strip the gut mucus layer and increase intestinal permeability. Read ingredient labels carefully on packaged foods
- **Artificial Sweeteners**: Sucralose, aspartame, and saccharin alter gut microbiome composition and may increase intestinal permeability. Use stevia or monk fruit if a sweetener is needed
- **Spicy Foods (During Acute Inflammation)**: Capsaicin can irritate an already-inflamed gut lining. Reintroduce gradually as inflammation resolves
- **Raw Cruciferous Vegetables**: Raw broccoli, cauliflower, and cabbage contain difficult-to-digest fibers that can worsen bloating and irritation in a compromised gut. Cook thoroughly instead
- **Nightshades (if sensitive)**: Tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, and potatoes contain solanine and lectins that may trigger gut inflammation in sensitive individuals. Trial a 30-day removal if symptoms persist
