# Growth Hormone (Somatropin)

## Mechanisms of Action

- **Direct GH receptor activation**: Supplies the finished 191-aa hormone, which binds GH receptors throughout the body — unlike secretagogues, it does not depend on a functioning pituitary
- **Hepatic IGF-1 production**: Stimulates the liver to produce insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), which mediates most of GH's anabolic and growth effects
- **Non-pulsatile delivery**: Exogenous dosing produces a different kinetic profile than the body's natural pulses, which is part of why side effects like insulin resistance and edema can emerge
- **Lipolysis**: Promotes breakdown of stored fat, particularly visceral and abdominal fat, by stimulating hormone-sensitive lipase
- **Protein anabolism**: Increases protein synthesis and nitrogen retention, supporting lean tissue and recovery
- **Glucose/insulin antagonism**: Counter-regulatory to insulin — raises blood glucose and can reduce insulin sensitivity, the basis for its diabetogenic risk
- **Growth and tissue effects**: Drives linear bone growth (in children with open growth plates), organ growth, and collagen/connective-tissue turnover
- **No feedback suppression workaround**: Because it bypasses GHRH/somatostatin control, high exogenous doses can drive supraphysiologic IGF-1 without the body's normal braking
