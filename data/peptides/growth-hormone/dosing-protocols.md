# Growth Hormone (Somatropin)

## Dosing Protocols

- **Prescription-determined, individualized**: All dosing must be set and monitored by a physician based on indication, body weight, age, and IGF-1 response — the figures below are general reference points, not guidance for self-administration
- **Adult GH deficiency (typical)**: Often started low, around 0.2–0.4 mg/day subcutaneous, titrated to keep IGF-1 in the age-appropriate range; women and those on oral estrogen often need higher doses
- **Pediatric indications**: Weight-based dosing (commonly expressed in mg/kg/week) varies by condition and is managed by a pediatric endocrinologist
- **Administration**: Subcutaneous injection, typically once daily in the evening to loosely mimic the natural nocturnal GH rise; long-acting weekly formulations also exist
- **Devices**: Supplied in prefilled pens or cartridges (e.g., Genotropin, Norditropin, Humatrope, Omnitrope) requiring refrigeration
- **Titration by IGF-1**: Dose is adjusted to clinical response and to keep IGF-1 within the age/sex reference range, not pushed to supraphysiologic levels
- **Storage**: Refrigerate (typically 36–46°F); follow product-specific in-use stability limits
- **Caution**: Off-label/non-prescription "performance" dosing uses much higher amounts that substantially raise the risk of acromegaly-like changes, insulin resistance, and other harms
