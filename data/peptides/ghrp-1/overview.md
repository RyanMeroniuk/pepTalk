# GHRP-1

## Overview

GHRP-1 is one of the earliest members of the growth hormone releasing peptide (GHRP) family, a group of synthetic ghrelin-mimetic secretagogues that stimulate the pituitary to release growth hormone. It is a close sibling of the more widely used GHRP-2, GHRP-6, and hexarelin, sharing the same basic mechanism — binding the GH secretagogue receptor (ghrelin receptor) to trigger a GH pulse. GHRP-1 was studied primarily in older pharmacological research from the 1980s–1990s as the GHRP class was being characterized, and it has since been largely overshadowed: newer GHRPs offer better-defined dosing, cleaner side-effect profiles, or stronger appetite/GH effects, making GHRP-1 mostly niche and redundant today. Like its siblings, it tends to produce a mild bump in cortisol and prolactin alongside GH. It is not approved for human use and circulates only as a gray-market research chemical.
