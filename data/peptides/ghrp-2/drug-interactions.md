# GHRP-2 (Growth Hormone Releasing Peptide-2)

## Drug Interactions & Contraindications

- **Diabetes medications (insulin, metformin, sulfonylureas)**: GHRP-2 elevates growth hormone, which increases blood glucose and reduces insulin sensitivity. May require dose adjustments of diabetes medications; monitor blood glucose closely
- **Exogenous insulin**: GH-driven glucose elevation opposes insulin's action. Concurrent use creates unpredictable blood sugar swings and increases hypoglycemia/hyperglycemia risk
- **Glucocorticoids (prednisone, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone)**: GHRP-2 already mildly elevates cortisol. Adding exogenous glucocorticoids compounds cortisol burden, increasing risk of muscle wasting, fat gain, bone loss, and immune suppression
- **Prolactin-elevating drugs (antipsychotics, metoclopramide, SSRIs)**: GHRP-2 raises prolactin in a dose-dependent manner. Combining with other prolactin-elevating medications may cause clinically significant hyperprolactinemia (gynecomastia, low libido, menstrual irregularity)
- **Exogenous growth hormone (rhGH)**: Concurrent use may cause supraphysiological GH/IGF-1 levels, increasing risk of insulin resistance, joint pain, edema, and long-term complications
- **Thyroid medications**: GH elevation can alter thyroid hormone conversion (T4 to T3). Monitor thyroid function and adjust dosing as needed
- **Contraindicated: Active cancer or history of cancer** — GH and IGF-1 are growth factors that may promote tumor growth, angiogenesis, and cancer cell proliferation
- **Contraindicated: Pregnancy and breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data; GH axis manipulation during pregnancy poses unknown risks to fetal development
- **Contraindicated: Active pituitary tumors** — GHRP-2 stimulates the pituitary and could accelerate tumor growth
- **Caution: Intracranial hypertension** — GH elevation can worsen or precipitate this condition
