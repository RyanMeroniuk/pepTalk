# Melanotan II

## Things to Avoid

- **Tanning Beds** — absolutely avoid. Combining MT-II melanocyte stimulation with concentrated UV radiation from tanning beds dramatically increases skin cancer risk, including melanoma. MT-II does NOT make tanning beds safe
- **Prolonged Unprotected Sun Exposure** — MT-II-induced melanin is NOT equivalent to natural photoprotection. Do not use MT-II as an excuse to skip sunscreen or extend sun exposure. The combination of stimulated melanocytes and excessive UV is the highest-risk behavior with this peptide
- **Ignoring Mole Changes** — MT-II darkens existing moles and can produce new ones. Any mole that changes in size, shape, color, or becomes asymmetric requires immediate dermatological evaluation. Do not dismiss changes as "just the peptide"
- **Using MT-II with Melanoma History or High Risk** — personal or family history of melanoma is a strong contraindication. MT-II stimulates the very cells involved in melanoma development
- **Combining with PT-141** — overlapping melanocortin receptor activation increases all side effects. Do not stack these two peptides
- **High Doses** — more is not better with MT-II. Higher doses dramatically increase nausea, flushing, and side effect severity without proportional tanning benefit. Start at 0.1-0.25mg and increase gradually
- **Ignoring Priapism** — sustained erections lasting more than 4 hours are a medical emergency. Seek immediate treatment to prevent permanent damage
- **Skipping Baseline Skin Exams** — never start MT-II without a professional full-body skin check. This establishes a baseline for monitoring mole changes during use
- **Chronic Use Without Dermatological Monitoring** — if using MT-II for extended periods, schedule skin exams every 6 months minimum. Long-term melanocyte stimulation requires ongoing professional monitoring
- **Smoking** — reduces skin blood flow, impairs skin health, and increases oxidative stress on skin cells already being stimulated by MT-II
