# Thymulin

## Things to Avoid

- **Zinc Depletion** — this is the single most important thing to avoid. Without zinc, thymulin is biologically inactive. Monitor for signs of zinc deficiency: frequent infections, slow wound healing, loss of taste/smell, white spots on nails
- **Chronic Stress** — sustained cortisol accelerates thymic involution (thymus shrinkage), directly reducing the organ that produces thymulin naturally. The thymus is one of the most stress-sensitive organs
- **Sleep Deprivation** — T-cell production and immune regulation are heavily sleep-dependent. Chronic poor sleep impairs the very immune cells thymulin helps mature
- **Overtraining** — intense exercise without recovery creates immunosuppressive windows. Balance training with rest days, especially while using immune-supporting peptides
- **Smoking and Vaping** — directly damages immune cells and mucosal barriers. Tobacco smoke is particularly toxic to T-cells, undermining thymulin's differentiation support
- **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) Long-Term** — chronic PPI use significantly reduces zinc absorption, potentially rendering thymulin ineffective. Discuss alternatives with your doctor if on long-term PPIs
- **Excessive Alcohol** — depletes zinc, impairs T-cell function, and damages gut barrier integrity — a triple hit against thymulin's effectiveness
- **Environmental Toxins** — heavy metals (lead, cadmium, mercury) compete with zinc for binding sites and suppress immune function. Minimize exposure through clean water, air filtration, and avoiding contaminated food sources
