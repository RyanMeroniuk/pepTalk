# Thymulin

## Drug Interactions & Contraindications

- **Immunosuppressants (tacrolimus, cyclosporine, mycophenolate)**: Thymulin promotes T-cell differentiation and immune activation; immunosuppressants oppose this mechanism. Combining may reduce immunosuppressive efficacy or produce unpredictable immune responses
- **Zinc-depleting drugs (PPIs, thiazide diuretics, ACE inhibitors, penicillamine)**: These medications reduce zinc levels, which directly inactivates thymulin. If taking these medications, aggressive zinc monitoring and supplementation is essential — without zinc, thymulin therapy is ineffective
- **Chelation therapy (EDTA, DMSA, DMPS)**: Chelating agents bind and remove heavy metals but also deplete zinc and other essential minerals. Chelation therapy during thymulin use can render the peptide completely inactive. Separate chelation and thymulin protocols, and replenish zinc aggressively after chelation
- **Corticosteroids (chronic use)**: Systemic corticosteroids accelerate thymic involution and suppress T-cell function, directly opposing thymulin's mechanism of restoring thymic output
- **Biologic immunomodulators**: TNF inhibitors, IL-6 inhibitors, and other biologics alter immune signaling in ways that may conflict with thymulin's immune-rebalancing effects
- **High-dose iron supplements**: Iron competes with zinc for absorption; concurrent supplementation may reduce zinc bioavailability and impair thymulin activation. Separate dosing by at least 2 hours
- **Contraindicated**: Pregnancy and breastfeeding — insufficient safety data; immune modulation effects on fetal development are unknown
- **Contraindicated**: Active autoimmune flares — thymulin's immune modulation may be unpredictable during active flares and could worsen symptoms
- **Contraindicated**: Organ transplant recipients on immunosuppression — risk of immune activation against the graft
